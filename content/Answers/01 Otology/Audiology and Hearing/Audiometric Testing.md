## INTRODUCTION

Audiometric testing forms the cornerstone of otological evaluation. A systematic battery of tests — from pure tone audiometry to advanced electrophysiological assessments — enables the clinician to determine the type, degree, and site of hearing loss. This comprehensive answer covers the full spectrum of audiometric tests relevant to the DNB examination.

---

## PART A: PURE TONE AUDIOMETRY

### Definition
Pure tone audiometry (PTA) determines hearing thresholds for sinusoidal signals at specific frequencies. It is the most fundamental clinical hearing test.

### Importance
1. **Quantifies hearing loss:** Determines degree (mild 21-40 dB, moderate 41-70 dB, severe 71-95 dB, profound >95 dB)
2. **Identifies type of hearing loss:** Conductive, sensorineural, or mixed — by comparing AC and BC thresholds
3. **Characterizes configuration:** Flat, sloping, rising, notched (noise-induced), reverse slope
4. **Guides management:** Selection of hearing aids, surgical candidacy, [[Cochlear Implant|cochlear implant]] assessment
5. **Monitors progression:** Serial audiograms track disease progression or treatment response (e.g., ototoxicity monitoring, [[Otosclerosis|otosclerosis]] follow-up)
6. **Medicolegal applications:** Disability assessment, compensation claims
7. **Pre- and post-operative assessment:** Baseline and outcome documentation

### Technique
- **Equipment:** Clinical audiometer generating pure tones (250-8000 Hz, extended to 16,000 Hz for ototoxicity monitoring)
- **Transducers:**
  - **Air conduction (AC):** Supra-aural headphones (TDH-39/49/50) or insert earphones
  - **Bone conduction (BC):** Bone vibrator on mastoid process or forehead
- **Test frequencies:** Octave intervals — 250, 500, 1000, 2000, 4000, 8000 Hz; inter-octave (750, 1500, 3000, 6000 Hz) if steeply sloping
- **Procedure:** Modified Hughson-Westlake technique (ascending method): Start at 1000 Hz, decrease in 10 dB steps until inaudible, increase in 5 dB steps — threshold = lowest level heard in 2/3 ascending presentations
- **Audiogram symbols:** Right ear — O (AC), [ (masked BC); Left ear — X (AC), ] (masked BC)

### Audiogram Interpretation
- **Air-bone gap >10 dB:** Conductive component present
- **BC thresholds elevated, no air-bone gap:** Sensorineural hearing loss
- **Both AC and BC elevated with air-bone gap:** Mixed hearing loss
- **Carhart's notch:** BC dip at 2000 Hz — characteristic of otosclerosis

## PART B: MASKING

### Definition
Masking is the process of introducing noise to the non-test ear to prevent it from responding to signals presented to the test ear via cross-hearing.

### Cross-Hearing and Interaural Attenuation
- **Cross-hearing:** Sound presented to test ear reaches non-test ear by bone conduction through the skull
- **Interaural attenuation (IA):** The reduction in signal level as it crosses from one ear to the other
  - Supra-aural headphones: IA ≈ **40 dB** for AC
  - Insert earphones: IA ≈ **55 dB** for AC
  - Bone conduction: IA ≈ **0 dB** (virtually no attenuation)

### Rules/Indicators for Clinical Masking

| Rule | Situation | Action |
|------|-----------|--------|
| **Rule 1** | Unmasked AC thresholds differ by ≥40 dB (supra-aural) or ≥55 dB (insert) | Retest AC of worse ear, masking the contralateral ear |
| **Rule 2** | Unmasked BC of one ear is better than AC of either ear by ≥10 dB | Retest BC of ear with worse AC, masking contralateral ear |
| **Rule 3** | Rule 1 not applicable, but BC of ear A is ≥40 dB (supra-aural) better than unmasked AC of ear B | Retest AC of ear B, masking ear A |

**Bone conduction ALWAYS needs masking consideration** because IA for BC ≈ 0 dB.

### Types of Masking Noise
- **Narrow-band noise (NBN):** Most commonly used for pure tone masking — centred at the test frequency
- **Speech noise (speech-shaped noise):** Used for speech audiometry masking
- **White noise:** Broadband — used less commonly, less efficient

### Plateau-Seeking Method

**Principle:** Systematically increase masking noise in the non-test ear while monitoring the threshold in the test ear. The "plateau" is the range of masking levels where the masked threshold remains stable.

**Technique:**
1. Start with initial masking level = unmasked BC threshold of non-test ear + 10 dB
2. Measure threshold in test ear
3. Increase masking by 10 dB
4. Re-measure threshold
5. Repeat: if threshold remains stable despite increasing masking = **plateau found** = true threshold
6. If threshold shifts 1 dB per 1 dB increase in masking = **overmasking** (masking noise crossing to test ear)

**Graphical representation:**
- **Under-masking zone:** Initial slope where masking is insufficient (threshold still elevated)
- **Plateau:** Flat region — **true masked threshold** (minimum 3 plateau points, ideally at 20 dB range)
- **Over-masking zone:** Second 1 dB/dB slope — masking noise reaching test ear

### Masking Dilemma
Occurs when the **air-bone gap in the non-test ear is so large** that effective masking cannot be achieved without overmasking. Most common in bilateral large conductive hearing loss. Insert earphones help by increasing IA.

## PART C: IMPEDANCE AUDIOMETRY & TYMPANOMETRY

### Principle of Impedance Audiometry
- **Acoustic impedance:** The opposition offered by the middle ear to the flow of sound energy. Measured in ohms.
- **Acoustic admittance:** The reciprocal of impedance — the ease with which sound energy flows through the middle ear. Measured in mmho (millimho) or mL.
- Modern instruments measure **admittance** (compliance) rather than impedance
- The middle ear transmits maximum acoustic energy (minimum impedance) when tympanic membrane pressure equals middle ear pressure

### Tympanometry — Technology and Technique

**Components of a tympanometer:**
1. **Probe assembly** (inserted into EAC with airtight seal):
   - Loudspeaker delivering a **probe tone** (226 Hz standard; 1000 Hz for infants <6 months)
   - Microphone measuring sound pressure reflected back from TM
   - Air pump and manometer to vary EAC pressure
2. **Processing unit:** Measures admittance as a function of applied pressure

**Procedure:**
1. Airtight seal placed in EAC
2. Probe tone (226 Hz) continuously delivered
3. Air pressure varied from +200 to -400 daPa
4. Admittance measured at each pressure point
5. Peak admittance = point of optimal middle ear function

### Middle Ear Compliance — How It Is Determined
- **Static compliance:** Difference between peak admittance (at middle ear pressure) and admittance at +200 daPa (when TM is stiffened)
- Normal static compliance: **0.3-1.5 mL** (at 226 Hz probe tone)
- Measured in equivalent ear canal volume (mL)

### Tympanogram Types and Clinical Significance

| Type | Appearance | Compliance | Peak Pressure | Clinical Significance |
|------|-----------|-----------|---------------|---------------------|
| **Type A** | Normal peak at 0 daPa | Normal (0.3-1.5 mL) | -50 to +50 daPa | Normal middle ear |
| **Type As** | Low/shallow peak | **Reduced** (<0.3 mL) | Normal | **Otosclerosis**, tympanosclerosis, malleus head fixation |
| **Type Ad** | Very tall/sharp peak | **Increased** (>1.5 mL) | Normal | **Ossicular discontinuity**, flaccid TM, healed perforation |
| **Type B** | Flat — no identifiable peak | Cannot be determined | No peak | **Middle ear effusion**, TM perforation (high volume), [[Mastoid Surgery Cholesteatoma|cholesteatoma]], cerumen impaction |
| **Type C** | Peak shifted to negative pressure | Normal or reduced | **< -100 daPa** | **Eustachian tube dysfunction**, resolving/developing OME |

### Causes of Changes in Middle Ear Compliance

**Increased Compliance:**
- Ossicular chain discontinuity (most common cause)
- Flaccid/atrophic tympanic membrane
- Healed perforation with thin neomembrane

**Decreased Compliance:**
- Otosclerosis (stapes fixation)
- Tympanosclerosis
- Malleus head fixation
- Middle ear effusion (Type B)
- Adhesive otitis media

### Management of CHL with Normal Volume and Increased Compliance
This presentation (Type Ad tympanogram + conductive hearing loss) strongly suggests **ossicular chain discontinuity**.

**Evaluation:**
1. Confirm with acoustic reflex testing (absent reflexes)
2. HRCT temporal bone — evaluate ossicular chain integrity
3. Detailed history — post-traumatic, post-infective (necrosis of long process of incus)

**Management:**
- **[[Ossiculoplasty CSF Otorrhea Cochleostomy|Ossiculoplasty]]** — reconstruction of ossicular chain using autograft (incus) or prosthetic materials (TORP/PORP)
- Hearing aid as alternative if surgery not suitable or declined

## PART D: ACOUSTIC REFLEX & STAPEDIAL REFLEX DECAY

### Acoustic Reflex
- Contraction of stapedius muscle in response to loud sound (70-95 dB above threshold)
- **Pathway:** CN VIII → ventral cochlear nucleus → superior olivary complex (bilateral) → CN VII (motor nucleus) → stapedius muscle
- Bilateral pathway: stimulus in one ear produces reflex in both ears

### Stapedial Reflex Decay Test (Tone Decay at Reflex Level)

**Technique:**
1. Determine acoustic reflex threshold
2. Present a continuous tone at **10 dB above reflex threshold** for **10 seconds**
3. Monitor the impedance change (reflex amplitude) over 10 seconds
4. Test at 500 Hz and 1000 Hz

**Interpretation:**
- **Normal:** Reflex amplitude maintained throughout 10 seconds (or decays <50%)
- **Abnormal (positive decay):** Reflex amplitude decays to **<50% of initial amplitude within 5 seconds** → suggests **retrocochlear pathology**
- Most useful at 500 Hz and 1000 Hz (at higher frequencies, normal ears can show decay)

**Clinical Utility:**
- **Retrocochlear lesion screening** ([[Vestibular Schwannoma|vestibular schwannoma]])
- **Differentiates cochlear from retrocochlear** pathology
- **Sensitivity:** ~65-70% for vestibular schwannoma (lower than ABR)
- **Limitation:** Cannot be measured in ears with middle ear pathology

## PART E: COCHLEAR VS RETROCOCHLEAR DIFFERENTIATION

### Complete Site-of-Lesion Test Battery

| Test | Cochlear Lesion | Retrocochlear Lesion |
|------|----------------|---------------------|
| **SISI (Short Increment Sensitivity Index)** | Score >70% ("positive") — recruitment present | Score <30% — no recruitment |
| **Tone decay test (Carhart)** | Minimal decay (0-15 dB) | Significant decay (>30 dB) — pathological adaptation |
| **Bekesy audiometry** | **Type II** — continuous tracing intermittently drops below pulsed at high frequencies | **Type III/IV** — continuous tracing significantly below pulsed; rapid fall-off of continuous tracing |
| **Speech discrimination** | Proportionate to PTA loss | **Disproportionately poor** — "rollover" phenomenon at high intensities |
| **Acoustic reflex threshold** | Normal or elevated (recruited) | Elevated or absent |
| **Acoustic reflex decay** | Normal (no decay) | **Positive decay** (>50% amplitude loss in 5 seconds) |
| **ABR** | Normal latencies; threshold shift correlates with PTA | **Prolonged wave V latency**, prolonged I-V interval, absent waves, poor morphology |
| **OAE** | **Absent** (OHC damage) | **Present** (OHCs intact; neural problem) |

### Bekesy Audiometry Classification

| Type | Pattern | Significance |
|------|---------|-------------|
| **Type I** | Pulsed and continuous overlap throughout | Normal |
| **Type II** | Continuous drops below pulsed at high frequencies; gap <20 dB | Cochlear (recruitment) |
| **Type III** | Continuous drops rapidly below pulsed; gap >20 dB | Retrocochlear |
| **Type IV** | Continuous below pulsed at all frequencies | Retrocochlear |
| **Type V** | Pulsed below continuous (paradoxical) | Non-organic hearing loss (malingering) |

## PART F: ABR — AUDITORY BRAINSTEM RESPONSE

### Definition
ABR is the electrical activity generated by the auditory nerve and brainstem in response to brief auditory stimuli (clicks or tone bursts), recorded from scalp electrodes.

### Wave Generators (I-V)

| Wave | Generator | Approximate Latency |
|------|-----------|-------------------|
| **I** | Distal portion of CN VIII (near cochlea) | 1.5 ms |
| **II** | Proximal portion of CN VIII (near brainstem) | 2.5 ms |
| **III** | Cochlear nucleus | 3.5 ms |
| **IV** | Superior olivary complex/lateral lemniscus | 4.5 ms |
| **V** | Lateral lemniscus/inferior colliculus | 5.5 ms |

**Wave V** is the most robust and last to disappear with decreasing intensity — used for threshold estimation.

### Key Measurements
- **Absolute latencies:** Waves I, III, V
- **Interpeak intervals:** I-III (2.0 ms), III-V (2.0 ms), I-V (4.0 ms) — most important
- **Interaural wave V latency difference:** Should be <0.3 ms

### ABR Graph Description
- X-axis: Time (milliseconds, 0-10 ms)
- Y-axis: Amplitude (microvolts)
- Five (sometimes seven) positive peaks labelled I through V (VII)
- Wave V is the largest and most consistently identified
- As stimulus intensity decreases, latencies increase and amplitudes decrease
- At threshold (~10-20 dB above behavioural threshold), only wave V remains

### Clinical Applications
1. **Threshold estimation** in infants and non-cooperative patients
2. **Retrocochlear pathology screening** (vestibular schwannoma) — prolonged I-V interval
3. **Intraoperative monitoring** during CPA surgery
4. **[[Neonatal Hearing Screening OAE|Neonatal hearing screening]]** (automated ABR)
5. **ANSD diagnosis** — absent or grossly abnormal ABR with present OAEs
6. **Brainstem integrity assessment** in comatose patients
7. **Auditory maturation assessment** in premature infants

## PART G: EMG IN OTOLOGY

### Definition
Electromyography (EMG) is the recording and analysis of electrical activity generated by skeletal muscles. In otology, it is primarily used for facial nerve assessment.

### Types of EMG Recording

| Type | Technique | Application |
|------|-----------|------------|
| **Surface EMG** | Electrodes placed on skin over muscle | Screening; facial nerve monitoring during surgery (less precise) |
| **Concentric needle EMG** | Needle electrode inserted into muscle belly | Detailed analysis of motor unit potentials; facial nerve assessment in chronic palsy |
| **Single-fibre EMG** | Specialized fine needle | Assesses neuromuscular junction; detects jitter — synkinesis assessment |

### EMG Recordings in Otology

**A. Diagnostic EMG (Static):**
- Records volitional motor unit potentials at rest and during voluntary contraction
- **Normal:** Silence at rest, recruitment of motor units on contraction
- **Denervation:** Fibrillation potentials and positive sharp waves at rest (appear 2-3 weeks after injury)
- **Reinnervation:** Polyphasic motor unit potentials — may precede clinical recovery by 6-12 weeks
- **Complete denervation:** Electrical silence — no motor unit potentials, no fibrillation (end-stage)

**B. Intraoperative EMG Monitoring:**
- Real-time monitoring during mastoid surgery, parotid surgery, CPA tumour surgery
- Detects **mechanically evoked** responses when instruments approach or contact the nerve
- **Continuous free-running EMG:** Detects irritation/trauma to nerve
- **Triggered EMG:** Electrical stimulation to identify and confirm nerve location

**C. ENoG (see Facial Nerve Comprehensive answer):**
- Evoked compound muscle action potential — prognostic test in acute facial palsy

### Role of EMG in Otology — Summary
1. Prognosis of [[Facial Nerve|facial nerve palsy]] (acute and chronic)
2. Timing of surgical intervention for facial palsy
3. Intraoperative facial nerve identification and monitoring
4. Assessment of reinnervation after facial nerve repair
5. Evaluation of synkinesis in chronic facial palsy

## PART H: HEARING LOSS ASSESSMENT FOR DISABILITY

### Indian Disability Calculation Method (RPWD Act 2016)
Percentage of hearing disability is calculated using average hearing loss at specific frequencies:

**Formula (WHO/Indian method):**
1. Calculate **Pure Tone Average (PTA)** for each ear at **500, 1000, 2000, 4000 Hz**
2. **Better ear** PTA and **Worse ear** PTA determined
3. **Percentage disability = [(Better ear PTA × 5) + (Worse ear PTA × 1)] / 6**
4. This weighted average is then mapped to disability percentage using standard tables

### WHO Classification of Hearing Impairment

| Grade | PTA (Better Ear) | Degree | Disability % |
|-------|-----------------|--------|-------------|
| 0 | ≤25 dB | No impairment | 0% |
| 1 | 26-40 dB | Slight/Mild | - |
| 2 | 41-60 dB | Moderate | - |
| 3 | 61-80 dB | Severe | - |
| 4 | ≥81 dB | Profound | - |

### Indian Guidelines for Disability Certification
- **Benchmark disability:** ≥40% = eligible for disability benefits
- **Hearing disability** of 70 dB or more in better ear (PTA of 500, 1000, 2000 Hz) = significant disability
- Assessment must include PTA, impedance audiometry, and speech audiometry
- Certification by authorized ENT specialist at designated centres

## PART I: NON-ORGANIC HEARING LOSS / MALINGERING

### Tuning Fork Tests for Malingering

| Test | Technique | Interpretation |
|------|-----------|---------------|
| **Stenger test** | Two tuning forks of same frequency placed near each ear; sound presented louder in "deaf" ear | If patient claims not to hear in supposedly deaf ear but also denies hearing in good ear = malingering (based on binaural fusion — louder sound dominates perception) |
| **Chimani-Moos test** | Weber test variant in claimed unilateral deafness | If bone conduction is normal, Weber should lateralize to the "deaf" ear if it has conductive loss; failure to lateralize appropriately suggests malingering |
| **Lombard test** | Background noise (Barany noise box) presented to "deaf" ear while patient reads aloud | If voice becomes louder = positive Lombard = patient can hear in that ear (involuntary vocal intensity increase) |
| **Teal test** | Rinne test on claimed deaf ear | In true unilateral deafness, Rinne should be negative (BC > AC) if conductive, or positive (AC > BC) if SNHL |

### Audiometric Tests for Malingering

| Test | Principle | Finding in Malingering |
|------|-----------|----------------------|
| **Pure tone Stenger** | Audiometric equivalent of tuning fork Stenger; tone presented to both ears, louder in claimed deaf ear | Malingerer stops responding when signal in "deaf" ear exceeds their real threshold |
| **Bekesy audiometry** | Type V pattern | Pulsed thresholds WORSE than continuous (paradoxical — opposite of all other types) |
| **Test-retest variability** | Repeat audiogram | Inconsistent thresholds (>10 dB variability) |
| **Speech audiometry** | SRT vs PTA comparison | SRT much better than PTA (discrepancy >12 dB) |
| **OAE** | Objective test of OHC function | Present OAE with claimed severe loss = functional overlay |
| **ABR** | Objective threshold estimation | Normal ABR threshold with elevated PTA thresholds |
| **ASSR** | Objective frequency-specific thresholds | Better thresholds than claimed on PTA |
| **Cross-check principle** | Compare multiple tests | Inconsistencies across test battery |

## PART J: NPPCD

### National Programme for Prevention & Control of Deafness (NPPCD)

**Launched:** 2006-07 (pilot in 25 districts), expanded subsequently across India

**Objectives:**
1. Prevention of hearing loss from disease and environmental causes
2. Early identification, diagnosis, and treatment of ear diseases
3. Strengthening of existing inter-sectoral linkages for prevention
4. Rehabilitation of persons with hearing impairment
5. Capacity building and training of healthcare professionals

**Components:**
1. **Manpower training:** ENT surgeons, audiologists, speech therapists, district programme managers
2. **Service delivery:** Screening at PHC/CHC level, referral to district hospital for diagnosis and management
3. **Awareness generation:** IEC activities for prevention of deafness
4. **Equipment provision:** Audiometers, ENT instruments, hearing aids to district hospitals
5. **Surgical camps:** Ear surgeries at district level

**1-3-6 Rule (from JCIH Guidelines, adopted in NPPCD context):**
- Screen by **1 month** of age
- Diagnose by **3 months** of age
- Intervene (habilitation) by **6 months** of age

---

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> - **Pure tone audiometry** is the foundation of hearing assessment — determines type (conductive/SNHL/mixed), degree, and configuration
> - **Masking is essential** whenever cross-hearing is possible; **plateau-seeking method** identifies true threshold at the flat portion of the masking function
> - **Interaural attenuation:** ~40 dB for supra-aural headphones AC, ~55 dB for inserts, ~0 dB for BC
> - **Tympanometry types:** A (normal), As (stiffness/otosclerosis), Ad (discontinuity), B (effusion), C (ET dysfunction)
> - **Stapedial reflex decay >50% in 5 seconds** suggests retrocochlear pathology
> - **ABR wave generators:** I (distal VIII), II (proximal VIII), III (cochlear nucleus), IV (SOC), V (lateral lemniscus/IC)
> - **I-V interpeak interval >4.4 ms** or interaural V latency difference >0.3 ms suggests retrocochlear lesion
> - **Stenger test** is the most reliable tuning fork test for malingering; **OAE and ABR** are the most reliable objective tests
> - **Type Ad + CHL** = likely ossicular discontinuity → ossiculoplasty
> - **EMG in otology:** Primarily for facial nerve prognosis — fibrillation = Wallerian degeneration; polyphasic = reinnervation


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> 1. Always apply **masking for BC** testing — interaural attenuation is virtually 0 dB
> 2. **Carhart's notch** (2 kHz BC dip) closes after successful [[Stapes Surgery|stapedectomy]] — confirms it was an artefact
> 3. In **bilateral CHL** with large air-bone gaps, expect a **masking dilemma** — insert earphones help by increasing IA to 55 dB
> 4. **Type V Bekesy** is virtually pathognomonic of non-organic hearing loss
> 5. The **rollover index** (PI max - PI at high intensity / PI max) >0.45 suggests retrocochlear pathology
> 6. NPPCD aims to screen, diagnose, and intervene following the **1-3-6 rule** adapted from JCIH guidelines
> 7. Test-retest variability >10 dB at the same frequency should raise suspicion of **non-organic hearing loss**
> 8. Use **1000 Hz probe tone** for tympanometry in infants <6 months (226 Hz is unreliable in this age group)
