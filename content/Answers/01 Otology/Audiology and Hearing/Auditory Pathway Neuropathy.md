> - ## PART B: NEURAL PLASTICITY & NEURAL SCAVENGING *(Addresses Q123)
> - Neural plasticity (neuroplasticity) is the ability of the central nervous system to reorganize its structure, function, and connections in response to intrinsic or extrinsic stimuli, including sensory experience, injury, or deprivation.
> - | Type | Description | Example |
> - | Normal development of neural pathways shaped by sensory input during critical periods | Auditory cortex development driven by sound exposure in first years of life |
> - | Beneficial reorganization following intervention | Auditory cortex activation by electrical stimulation after [[Cochlear Implant|cochlear implantation]] |
> - | Cortical areas originally dedicated to one modality are "taken over" by another modality | Visual cortex activation by auditory stimuli in blind individuals; auditory cortex "taken over" by visual processing in prolonged deafness |
> - | Remaining functions strengthen after injury | Enhanced visual and tactile processing in deaf individuals |
> - - The period of maximum neural plasticity for auditory development is approximately **birth to 3.5-4 years
> - During this period, auditory input shapes the central auditory pathways
> - Input from the peripheral auditory system **drives development** of the central auditory system from the first years of life
> - Auditory deprivation during this critical period leads to delays in linguistic, social, and cognitive development
> - After the critical period closes, the potential for central auditory reorganization diminishes significantly
> - This is the **scientific basis for early intervention** (hearing aids, cochlear implants) — the earlier the better
> - Neural scavenging refers to the phenomenon whereby neural territory that is normally dedicated to one sensory modality is "scavenged" (appropriated) by another sensory modality when the original input is absent or deficient.
> - - In prolonged auditory deprivation (e.g., congenital deafness), the auditory cortex does not receive its expected auditory input
> - Other sensory modalities — particularly **visual** and **somatosensory** — expand their cortical representations into the "unused" auditory cortex
> - This cross-modal reorganization is mediated by competitive Hebbian mechanisms — neurons that fire together wire together, and those that don't are scavenged
> - 1. **Cochlear implant outcomes:** If neural scavenging has occurred extensively (especially after age 3.5-4 years in pre-lingual deafness), CI outcomes are significantly poorer because the auditory cortex is no longer available for auditory processing
> - The 1-3-6 rule (screen by 1 month, diagnose by 3 months, intervene by 6 months) is designed to minimize neural scavenging
> - Bilateral CI prevents scavenging of the unstimulated contralateral auditory cortex
> - Pre-lingually deaf adults implanted late show poor speech outcomes due to extensive cross-modal plasticity
> - ## PART C: AUDITORY NEUROPATHY SPECTRUM DISORDER (ANSD) *(Addresses Q70, Q123)
> - "Auditory neuropathy" (Starr, 1996)
> - "Auditory neuropathy/dyssynchrony" (2001)
> - "Auditory neuropathy spectrum disorder" (ANSD)** (2008, Guidelines Development Conference, Lake Como)
> - 5-12% of children with severe-profound hearing loss
> - Estimated **1 in 7000 neonates** on universal hearing screening
> - ~1 in 10 children with permanent hearing loss has ANSD
> - in the auditory nerve. Possible sites of lesion:
> - | IHC damage/dysfunction with preserved OHCs | Otoferlin gene mutations (presynaptic) |
> - | Synaptic transmission failure ("synaptopathy") | Otoferlin (OTOF) mutations, ribbon synapse disorders |
> - | Demyelination or axonal loss | Charcot-Marie-Tooth, Friedreich's ataxia |
> - | Central demyelination | MS, kernicterus (bilirubin toxicity) |
> - Type I postsynaptic AN:** With vestibular and peripheral neuropathies (e.g., Charcot-Marie-Tooth)
> - Type I postsynaptic AN:** With optic nerve disorders (e.g., Leber's optic atrophy, Wolfram/DIDMOAD syndrome)
> - Type II presynaptic AN:** IHC and neurotransmitter disorder (e.g., otoferlin and GJB6 mutations)
> - AN unspecified:** Affected sites unknown
> - Hyperbilirubinaemia** (most common) — >350 µmol/L; identified in 50% of ANSD cases
> - Prematurity (45%)
> - Perinatal asphyxia/hypoxia
> - Neonatal ventilator dependency (36%)
> - Low birth weight
> - Ototoxicity (aminoglycosides) (41%)
> - Family history of hearing loss
> - ## PART D: DIAGNOSIS OF ANSD *(Addresses Q70, Q123)
> - - ✓ Normal otoacoustic emissions (OAEs)* — TEOAEs or DPOAEs
> - ✓ Normal cochlear microphonic (CM)
> - - ✓ Absent or severely abnormal ABR
> - ✓ Absent middle ear (acoustic) reflexes
> - ✓ Absent contralateral suppression of OAEs
> - + Neural synchrony abnormal
> - | **Present** (may disappear over time in progressive cases) | Confirms OHC function intact |
> - | **Present** — reverses polarity with alternating stimulus polarity | Confirms preneural (hair cell) function |
> - | **Absent or grossly abnormal** — no reproducible waveforms, or only CM visible | Confirms neural dys-synchrony |
> - | **Absent** | Neural pathway disrupted |
> - | Variable — normal to profound loss | Does not correlate with speech difficulty |
> - | **Disproportionately poor** relative to PTA | Hallmark feature — difficulty understanding words despite hearing sounds |
> - | **Absent** | Efferent pathway dysfunction |
> - | Exclude structural pathology (VS, cochlear nerve deficiency) | Essential in workup |
> - | OTOF (otoferlin), GJB2 (connexin 26), PCDH9, AUNA1 | Identifies treatable/prognostic subtypes |
> - | May show peripheral neuropathy | Syndromic ANSD (CMT, Friedreich's) |
> - CM **reverses polarity** with alternating condensation/rarefaction clicks
> - ABR wave I does NOT reverse polarity
> - Use alternating polarity stimuli: CM cancels out, revealing true ABR (or absence thereof)
> - ## PART E: MANAGEMENT OF ANSD *(Addresses Q70, Q123)
> - is essential.
> - - Some neonatal ANSD (especially hyperbilirubinaemia-related) shows **spontaneous improvement** — ABR may normalize
> - Children with hyperbilirubinaemia were more likely to improve than those without (p < 0.04 at 2 kHz)
> - Two out of four neonates in early studies showed normalization of ABR
> - Serial monitoring with ABR + OAE + behavioural audiometry during this period
> - - Appropriate for children with elevated PTA thresholds
> - Benefit may be limited** because the problem is neural synchrony, not sound amplification
> - Some patients benefit (especially those with mild-moderate losses)
> - Avoid over-amplification — OHCs are intact and vulnerable to damage
> - - **Best outcomes reported** in pre-lingual children with ANSD, especially with otoferlin (OTOF) gene mutations
> - CI bypasses the site of lesion by directly stimulating the auditory nerve
> - Important:** Confirm presence of cochlear nerve on MRI before CI (cochlear nerve deficiency can coexist)
> - Outcomes are generally good if auditory nerve is present, but variable
> - Timing:** Early implantation (ideally before age 2) maximizes outcomes due to neural plasticity
> - - Improve signal-to-noise ratio
> - Useful in educational settings even for mildly affected patients
> - - Visual cues, lip-reading, sign language as adjuncts
> - Speech and language therapy — essential component
> - - Exchange transfusion for severe neonatal jaundice (prevention)
> - Treatment of associated peripheral neuropathy
> - Genetic counselling

> - | Early CI (<2 years) | Late implantation |
> - | Present on MRI | Absent/hypoplastic |
> - | None | Cerebral palsy, autism, developmental delay (up to 54% of children with ANSD) |
> - ## PART F: NEONATAL HEARING EVALUATION TESTS *(Addresses Q111)
> - Hearing loss affects ~1-3 per 1000 neonates
> - Critical for speech/language development — the brain has maximum plasticity in first years
> - 1-3-6 Rule (JCIH):** Screen by 1 month, diagnose by 3 months, intervene by 6 months
> - | Test | What It Tests | Technique | Application |
> - | Neural pathway integrity (CN VIII to brainstem) | Automated pass/fail using scalp electrodes | **Gold standard for NICU screening**; detects ANSD (which OAE alone would miss) |
> - | Outer hair cell function | Probe in EAC records emissions | **Well-baby screening**; fast, non-invasive; misses ANSD |
> - | Threshold estimation + neural pathway assessment | Multiple intensities, frequency-specific stimuli | Definitive assessment when screening fails; provides wave morphology, latency, threshold |
> - | Frequency-specific threshold estimation | Modulated tones; computer-detected response | Objective frequency-specific audiogram; useful when ABR equivocal; can test at higher intensities than ABR |
> - | Pre-neural function | Alternating polarity clicks during ABR | Distinguishes ANSD from severe SNHL |
> - | Middle ear function | 1000 Hz probe tone (226 Hz unreliable in neonates <6 months) | Exclude middle ear effusion |
> - | Test | Age Range | Technique | Reliability |
> - | 0-6 months | Observe change in behaviour (startle, eye-widening, cessation of activity) to calibrated sounds | Low — high false positive/negative rate; screening only |
> - | 6-30 months | Head turn towards sound source reinforced by animated visual reward (light-up toy) | Good — frequency-specific; reliable from ~7 months |
> - | 2.5-5 years | Child performs play task (stacking blocks, dropping ball) in response to sound | Good — can obtain frequency-specific audiogram |
> - | >5 years | Conventional pure tone audiometry with button press | Reliable |
> - | All neonates | Only those with risk factors (family history, NICU stay, craniofacial anomalies, syndromes) |
> - | Higher — captures ~50% of HL cases that have no risk factors | Misses ~50% of cases |
> - | JCIH, AAP, NHS England | Historically used, now largely replaced by UNHS |
> - | OAE for well-baby; ABR for NICU babies | ABR preferred for high-risk |
> - For more details on OAE types, screening protocols, and UNHS vs targeted screening, see existing answer `Neonatal_Hearing_Screening_OAE_Answer.md` (Q51, Q77, Q120).
> - > [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> - The **central auditory pathway** has 7 relay stations from cochlea to auditory cortex, with tonotopic organization at every level
> - Bilateral representation** begins at the SOC — unilateral cortical lesions do not cause total deafness
> - Neural plasticity** peaks in the first 3.5-4 years (critical period) — early intervention is crucial
> - Neural scavenging** — visual/somatosensory modalities appropriate auditory cortex in prolonged deafness, reducing CI outcomes
> - ANSD diagnostic triad:** Present OAE/CM + Absent/abnormal ABR + Absent acoustic reflexes
> - ANSD is **5-12% of children with severe-profound hearing loss**; hyperbilirubinaemia is the most common risk factor (50%)
> - Cochlear implantation** provides the best outcomes for ANSD, especially in otoferlin mutations
> - Some neonatal ANSD (hyperbilirubinaemia-related) **spontaneously improves** — observe for 3-6 months
> - For [[Neonatal Hearing Screening OAE|neonatal hearing screening]], **ABR is preferred in NICU babies** (detects ANSD); **OAE** for well-baby population
> - Use **1000 Hz probe tone** tympanometry in infants <6 months
