> - ## INTRODUCTION
> - ## PART A: SURGICAL ANATOMY OF THE FACIAL NERVE *(Addresses Q15, Q86)
> - portions.
> - | ~24 mm | Courses anterolaterally through CPA cistern to porus of IAM; accompanied by vestibulocochlear nerve |
> - | 5-12 mm | Runs through IAM from porus to fundus; lies in anterior superior quadrant, anterior to Bill's bar |
> - | 3-5 mm | **Shortest and narrowest** segment (avg diameter 0.68 mm); **only segment lacking anastomosing arterial cascades** — most vulnerable to ischaemia; most commonly injured in temporal bone fractures |
> - | 8-11 mm | Runs along superior edge of medial wall of tympanic cavity; lies above oval window niche, anterior/inferior to lateral SCC prominence |
> - | 10-14 mm | **Longest** petrous segment; runs from second genu to stylomastoid foramen |
> - | Variable | Enters parotid gland; divides into temporofacial and cervicofacial trunks; gives 5 terminal branches |
> - Bill's bar (vertical crest):** Named after William Fouts House; divides IAM fundus — facial nerve lies anterior to Bill's bar in anterior superior quadrant
> - Crista falciformis (transverse septum):** Divides IAM fundus horizontally
> - Geniculate ganglion (first genu):** Sensory ganglion at the sharp 'hairpin' turn between labyrinthine and tympanic segments; cradled by superior SCC posteriorly and cochlea anteroinferiorly
> - Greater superficial petrosal nerve (GSPN):** First branch of facial nerve; important landmark in middle fossa approach
> - Cochleariform process:** Consistent landmark for proximal tympanic segment
> - Pyramidal eminence:** Useful landmark for the second genu
> - Second genu:** Runs inferolateral to lateral SCC — extremely constant relationship
> - Digastric ridge:** Points to lateral and inferior aspect of mastoid segment; a line from anterior end of digastric ridge to tip of short process of incus marks the mastoid segment path
> - Chorda tympani:** Given off ~5 mm proximal to stylomastoid foramen; serves as lateral margin of facial recess
> - — from geniculate ganglion (parasympathetic to lacrimal gland via PPG)
> - — from mastoid segment behind pyramidal eminence
> - — from mastoid segment (taste anterior 2/3 tongue + parasympathetic to submandibular ganglion)
> - — from mastoid segment
> - Labyrinthine artery (from AICA):** Supplies cisternal, meatal, and labyrinthine segments
> - Petrosal branch of middle meningeal artery:** Supplies GSPN and geniculate ganglion region
> - Facial arch (stylomastoid artery + petrosal branch of MMA):** Supplies tympanic and mastoid segments
> - The **labyrinthine segment** is supplied exclusively by meatal arteries and is therefore most susceptible to ischaemic damage
> - ## PART B: FACIAL NERVE GRADING SYSTEMS *(Addresses Q27)
> - | Normal | Normal symmetrical function in all areas |
> - | Mild dysfunction | Slight weakness noticeable only on close inspection; complete eye closure with minimum effort; slight asymmetry of smile with maximal effort; synkinesis barely noticeable |
> - | Moderate dysfunction | Obvious weakness, but not disfiguring; may not be able to lift eyebrow; complete eye closure and strong but asymmetric mouth movement with maximal effort; obvious but not disfiguring synkinesis |
> - | Moderately severe | Obvious disfiguring weakness; inability to lift eyebrow; incomplete eye closure and asymmetry of mouth with maximal effort; severe synkinesis, mass movement, spasm |
> - | Severe dysfunction | Motion barely perceptible; incomplete eye closure, slight movement of corner of mouth; synkinesis, contracture and spasm usually absent |
> - | Total paralysis | No movement, loss of tone, no synkinesis, contracture or spasm |
> - Fusion of static and dynamic criteria contributes to inter-observer disagreement
> - Risk of disagreement among examiners ranges from **13% to 44%
> - A single global grading number is inadequate — primarily reflects function of the eye
> - Regional assessment (forehead, eye, nose, mouth separately) is recommended for more precise evaluation
> - Burres-Fisch system:** Multiple measurements of movement at different facial parts; considerable overlap with H-B grades III and IV
> - Nottingham system:** Subjective estimation of movement at several facial points; correlates better with H-B than Burres-Fisch; simpler to perform
> - Sunnybrook scale:** Includes secondary effects (synkinesis, spasm) in overall score
> - Facial Clinimetric Evaluation (FaCE)** and **Facial Disability Index (FDI):** Patient-reported outcome instruments addressing quality of life
> - ## PART C: TOPODIAGNOSTIC TESTS *(Addresses Q9, Q64, Q119)
> - and **no prognostic value**.
> - | Greater petrosal nerve | Strips of paper placed in inferior conjunctival fornix for 5 minutes; compare lacrimation between eyes | >75% unilateral decrease in lacrimation, or bilateral decrease (<10 mm wetted for both sides at 5 min) |
> - | Nerve to stapedius | Impedance audiometry (see Chapter 51) | Present or absent |
> - | Chorda tympani | Tongue stimulated electrically to produce metallic taste; compare two sides | Threshold compared between sides |
> - | Chorda tympani | Wharton's ducts cannulated; salivary flow measured over time following gustatory stimulus (6% citric acid) | Reduction of 25% is considered abnormal |
> - Lesion proximal to geniculate ganglion:** Abnormal Schirmer's test + absent stapedial reflex + abnormal taste + abnormal salivation
> - Lesion between geniculate and nerve to stapedius:** Normal lacrimation + absent stapedial reflex + abnormal taste
> - Lesion between stapedius nerve and chorda tympani:** Normal lacrimation + normal stapedial reflex + abnormal taste
> - Lesion distal to chorda tympani:** All branch tests normal; only motor weakness present
> - ## PART D: ELECTRODIAGNOSTIC TESTS — ENOG & EMG *(Addresses Q9, Q64, Q119)
> - — not in incomplete paresis (which has higher probability of full recovery).
> - - A **supramaximal stimulus** is delivered to the facial nerve trunk at the stylomastoid foramen
> - The evoked biphasic **compound muscle action potential (CMAP)** is recorded using surface electrodes
> - Response of the paralyzed side is compared with the normal side (control), expressed as a **percentage of normal
> - Reduction in CMAP amplitude correlates well with histological axonal loss
> - - Not useful until the **4th day** of paralysis (Wallerian degeneration takes 3-4 days)
> - Most useful between **day 3 and 3 weeks** of paralysis
> - Not useful after 3 weeks due to desynchronization
> - - In idiopathic palsy: **>90% degeneration within 14 days** of complete paralysis indicates probable poor recovery in **>50% of cases
> - However, **67% of patients** who reach 90% degeneration threshold also have excellent outcomes without intervention
> - Fisch recommends surgical intervention within 3 weeks when traumatic injury shows **>90% amplitude reduction within 6 days
> - - Cannot distinguish between axonotmesis and neurotmesis in remaining fibres
> - Not useful in incomplete paralysis
> - No useful information in first 72 hours
> - Not useful in Ramsay Hunt syndrome (multiple sites of involvement)
> - Active motor unit potentials of orbicularis oculi and oris during rest and voluntary contraction.
> - 1. If a nerve is **in continuity** (volitional activity recorded)
> - (fibrillation potentials — arise 2-3 weeks post-injury)
> - (polyphasic potentials — may precede clinical recovery by 6-12 weeks)
> - | EMG Finding | Interpretation | Action |
> - | Regeneration occurring | Surgery NOT indicated; close follow-up |
> - | Denervation with viable motor end plates | Surgical exploration indicated (anastomosis, grafting, rerouting) |
> - | Long-term denervation; muscle replaced by fibrous tissue | Static or dynamic facial reanimation indicated |
> - | Evoked CMAP compared to normal side | Active motor unit potentials after voluntary contraction |
> - | Between 3 days and 3 weeks | Complementary to ENoG after 2 weeks; also in long-standing paralysis |
> - | >90% degeneration suggests poor prognosis | Presence of active motor potentials indicates good prognosis |
> - | Not useful (desynchronization) | Fibrillation = Wallerian degeneration; Polyphasic = reinnervation |
> - | Grade 1 | Transient compression, endoneurial oedema, focal demyelination | Conduction block; distal nerve normal |
> - | Grade 2 | Axons degenerate distally; endoneurium intact | Fibrillation; diminished to absent SNAP/CMAP |
> - | Grade 3 | Endoneurium disrupted | Fibrillation; absent SNAP/CMAP |
> - | Grade 4 | Perineurium disrupted | Fibrillation; absent SNAP/CMAP |
> - | Grade 5 | Epineurium disrupted (complete division) | Fibrillation; absent SNAP/CMAP |
> - | Grade 6 (MacKinnon-Dellon) | Combination of Grades 2-4 | Mixed pattern |
> - Recovery is typically **complete** after Grade 1 and 2 injuries
> - Grade 3 injuries recover **partially
> - Grades 4 and 5 usually require **surgical intervention
> - Axonal regrowth proceeds at ~1 mm/day
> - ## PART E: ELECTROCOCHLEOGRAPHY (ECoG) *(Addresses Q64)
> - Generated by outer hair cells; mirrors the acoustic stimulus
> - DC shift generated by hair cells; reflects cochlear mechanics
> - Generated by auditory nerve (wave I of ABR)
> - Transtympanic (TT-ECoG):** Needle electrode placed on or through the tympanic membrane onto the promontory — provides **best signal quality
> - Extratympanic (ET-ECoG):** Electrode placed in the EAC near the TM — non-invasive but smaller amplitudes
> - The **SP/AP ratio** is the key diagnostic measurement
> - Normal SP/AP ratio:** <0.40-0.45
> - Elevated SP/AP ratio (>0.45):** Suggests **endolymphatic hydrops** ([[Menieres Disease and Intratympanic Therapy|Meniere's disease]])
> - Elevated SP/AP ratio supports diagnosis of endolymphatic hydrops; used to monitor treatment response
> - Real-time monitoring of cochlear function during procedures that place hearing at risk (e.g., [[Vestibular Schwannoma|vestibular schwannoma]] surgery, [[Cochlear Implant|cochlear implantation]])
> - ECoG can provide objective hearing threshold estimation, particularly useful in medico-legal cases
> - Presence of CM with absent or grossly abnormal AP/ABR helps diagnose ANSD
> - May demonstrate electrophysiological changes during provocative testing
> - For [[VEMP and Menieres Disease|VEMP]] (Q9), see the existing comprehensive answer in `VEMP_and_Menieres_Disease_Answer.md` which covers cVEMP, oVEMP, types, technique, and clinical applications.
> - ## PART F: TEMPORAL BONE FRACTURES & TRAUMATIC FACIAL PALSY *(Addresses Q15, Q73, Q119)
> - | Type | Frequency | Description | FN Palsy Incidence |
> - | Most common (~80%) | Fracture line parallel to long axis of petrous bone | 10-25% |
> - | Less common (~20%) | Fracture line perpendicular to petrous bone | 38-50% |
> - | Significant proportion | Combined features | Variable |
> - - **Otic capsule sparing:** Better prognosis
> - Otic capsule violating:** Patients are ~2x more likely to develop facial nerve paralysis, 4x more likely to have [[CSF Rhinorrhea|CSF leak]], and 7x more likely to sustain profound hearing loss
> - Complicates approximately **7% of temporal bone fractures
> - Penetrating trauma has higher incidence (~52%)
> - Site of injury:** 66% at geniculate ganglion, 20% at second genu, 8% tympanic segment, 6% mastoid portion
> - 6% of geniculate ganglion lesions have a second site of trauma in the mastoid portion
> - Longitudinal fractures: perigeniculate region most commonly involved
> - Transverse fractures: labyrinthine and mastoid segments most commonly involved
> - | Direct trauma, impingement, possible transection | Neural oedema, haematoma, secondary ischaemia |
> - | Nerve may be directly traumatized or transected | Neural continuity maintained |
> - | Variable — 50% achieve complete/near-complete recovery without surgery | **Excellent prognosis** |
> - | HRCT + ENoG mandatory | ENoG usually not necessary |
> - | May require surgical exploration if ENoG criteria met | Conservative management; expected to recover (H-B I or II) |
> - Identifies fracture pattern, site of Fallopian canal involvement
> - May identify main location of nerve injury in complete palsy cases
> - Performed at ~10-14 days post-injury; only necessary in:
> - Immediate complete paralysis
> - Complete paralysis with uncertain history that fails to improve
> - Indicated if ENoG shows no response
> - - **No surgical intervention required** — all expected to make complete or near-complete recovery (H-B I or II)
> - Steroids: Prednisolone 1 mg/kg/day for 1-3 weeks with taper
> - Serial clinical observation
> - - Steroids initiated (if no contraindication from brain injury)
> - ENoG at 10-14 days
> - Surgical exploration indicated if:** ENoG shows **>90% denervation within 6 days** of paralysis onset AND patient is neurologically stable
> - Timing of surgery:** Early exploration preferable; within 14 days (Chang and Cass); within 30 days (May) for superior results; recovery possible even if treated 27-90 days post-trauma (Quaranta)
> - - **Longitudinal fractures with preserved hearing:** Middle fossa approach
> - Transverse/mixed fractures with preserved hearing:** Middle fossa + transmastoid exploration
> - Severe SNHL:** Translabyrinthine approach (easier, less morbidity)
> - - Decompression is usually sufficient
> - Severely crushed nerve: resect and primary anastomosis
> - Transected nerve: end-to-end anastomosis if possible (tension-free)
> - Otherwise: interposition nerve graft (great auricular or sural nerve)
> - - EMG-guided decision making:
> - Polyphasic potentials → reinnervation occurring → observe
> - Fibrillation → viable motor end plates → surgical exploration for continuity
> - Silence → static/dynamic facial reanimation
> - ## PART G: FACIAL NERVE IN MASTOID SURGERY — LANDMARKS & MANAGEMENT *(Addresses Q86)
> - | Second genu of FN runs inferolateral to LSCC — **extremely constant relationship** |
> - | Line from anterior digastric ridge to tip of short process of incus marks mastoid segment |
> - | Points to lateral and inferior aspect of mastoid segment |
> - | Consistent landmark for proximal tympanic segment, especially when other landmarks destroyed by pathology |
> - | Useful landmark for second genu |
> - | Used with chorda tympani to identify nerve during facial recess approach |
> - | Lateral margin of facial recess |
> - | Lies just posterior to second genu |
> - | Separates facial nerve from superior vestibular nerve |
> - Most common: **distal tympanic segment including second genu
> - Followed by: **mastoid segment
> - Incidence: 0.6-3.6% (0.8% in Royal College of Surgeons audit)
> - Most commonly caused by failure to identify mastoid antrum properly or being impeded by overhanging tegmen
> - The **mastoid segment is least sensitive** to nerve stimulation — requires higher settings (**0.5 mA** in mastoid surgery vs 0.05-0.1 mA in CPA tumour surgery)
> - Always avoid neuromuscular blockade after intubation
> - - Observe for a few hours — local anaesthetic effect may clear
> - Consider removing tight mastoid dressing/pack over exposed nerve
> - - Start oral steroids
> - Observe clinically
> - If progression to full paralysis → consider exploration
> - - HRCT imaging (despite possible iodine-compound artefact)
> - Immediate exploration no longer advocated** — no demonstrated difference between early vs >7-day delayed exploration (Green et al.)
> - Most important: ensure experienced surgical colleague undertakes re-exploration or transfer to regional centre
> - - Aetiology unclear — possible HSV/VZV reactivation
> - Combined prednisone and acyclovir
> - Overall prognosis appears good
> - ## PART H: FACIAL PALSY IN CHRONIC OTITIS MEDIA *(Addresses Q27)
> - through Fallopian canal dehiscences or physiologic canaliculi
> - of Fallopian canal with bone erosion and nerve involvement
> - → compression → secondary thrombosis of vasa nervorum → ischaemia and infarction
> - caused by bacterial toxins
> - Mainstay:** Antibiotic therapy
> - Steroids may be used (efficacy not supported by studies)
> - Myringotomy and ventilation tube insertion when spontaneous perforation is absent
> - Prognosis generally good
> - Mastoid exploration and facial nerve decompression is ESSENTIAL
> - CT scan to delineate site of Fallopian canal erosion
> - Surgical exploration addresses:
> - Removal of [[Mastoid Surgery Cholesteatoma|cholesteatoma]]/granulation tissue compressing the nerve
> - Decompression of the nerve within the Fallopian canal
> - Assessment of nerve integrity
> - If nerve is intact but compressed: decompression is sufficient
> - If >50% circumference disrupted: repair with direct suture or inlay graft
> - If transected: end-to-end anastomosis or interposition grafting
> - > [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> - The **labyrinthine segment** (3-5 mm) is the shortest, narrowest, and most vulnerable segment — sole arterial supply from meatal arteries
> - The **House-Brackmann** grading system (I-VI) is the most widely used but has limitations — regional assessment recommended
> - Topodiagnostic tests** (Schirmer's, stapedial reflex, taste, salivary flow) help localise the lesion but have no prognostic value
> - ENoG** is the most valuable prognostic test: >90% degeneration within 14 days in complete paralysis indicates poor prognosis in >50%
> - EMG** complements ENoG: fibrillation potentials = viable end plates; polyphasic potentials = regeneration; silence = irreversible
> - Temporal bone fractures cause FN palsy in ~7% of cases; **66% at geniculate ganglion
> - Incomplete/delayed onset palsy** has excellent prognosis — no surgery needed
> - Immediate complete palsy** with >90% ENoG degeneration within 6 days → consider surgical exploration
> - In **mastoid surgery**, the lateral SCC is the most reliable landmark for the second genu
> - In **CSOM with facial palsy**, mastoid exploration and decompression is essential
> - ECoG** (SP/AP ratio) is primarily used for Meniere's disease diagnosis and intraoperative cochlear monitoring
> - > [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - or incomplete palsy not recovering after 3-6 weeks → suspect underlying **neoplasm
> - — tumour should be suspected (6/20 cases in May's series)
> - still have excellent outcomes without surgery — the threshold is not absolute
> - is the most reliable landmark when other structures are destroyed by pathology
