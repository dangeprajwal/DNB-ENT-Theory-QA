## Part A: Physiology of Bone Conduction (Q107)

Bone-conduction hearing devices (BCHDs) deliver sound energy to both inner ears by establishing pressure differences between scala tympani and scala vestibuli, causing basilar membrane movement and neural stimulation.

### Pathways of Bone-Conducted Sound (Stenfelt Model)

| Pathway | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Outer ear (Osseotympanic)** | BC vibrations enter EAC via soft tissue and bone vibrations → transmitted to TM. Sound energy to TM changes notably if EAC is occluded (occlusion effect) |
| **Middle ear (Inertial)** | With intact ossicular chain: ossicles vibrate in phase at low frequencies but become decoupled at higher frequencies (ossicular inertia/lag relative to temporal bone) |
| **Inner ear (Compressional)** | Direct inertial forces through bone + fluid inertia → pressure differences across basilar membrane. Also cochlear compression/expansion from skull vibration |

### Key Physiological Principles
- **Transcranial transmission:** Close to 0 dB for frequencies up to 700 Hz; decreases with higher frequencies. This is why BC masking is required during audiometry
- **Percutaneous vs transcutaneous:** BC sensitivity fairly similar below 1 kHz; expected 5-15 dB improvement with percutaneous systems at higher frequencies (direct bone attachment)
- **Skin attenuation:** 10-15 dB with transcutaneous transmission; increases with skin thickness
- **Bilateral stimulation:** Low transcranial transmission is better for binaural cues; high transcranial transmission is better for SSD

## Part B: Classification and Types of Bone Conduction Devices (Q55, Q107)

### Surgical Classification

| Category | Subcategory | Devices |
|---|---|---|
| **Percutaneous** | Passive (skin-penetrating abutment) | Cochlear BAHA Connect (BI300 implant + BA400 abutment), Oticon Medical Ponto |
| **Transcutaneous** | Passive (skin drive) | Cochlear BAHA Attract, Sophono Alpha 2 MPO (Medtronic) |
| **Transcutaneous** | Active (direct drive) | MED-EL Bonebridge |

### Percutaneous Devices

**Cochlear BAHA Connect System:**
- Titanium osseointegrated fixture (BI300) implanted in mastoid bone
- Skin-penetrating abutment (BA400 with hydroxyapatite coating)
- External sound processor clips onto abutment
- Direct and efficient sound transmission to bone
- Commercially available since 1984 (Entific)
- BI300 system (2010): wider base 4.5mm, smaller threads, TioblastTM coating for faster osseointegration
- BA400 abutment (2012): hydroxyapatite coating

**Oticon Medical Ponto:**
- Similar concept: osseointegrated implant + percutaneous abutment
- Available since 2009
- Various abutment lengths for different skin thicknesses
- Tissue preservation surgery (no dermatome needed)

**Percutaneous Outcomes:**
- Skin complications classified by Holgers scale (Grade 0-4):
  - Grade 0: No reaction
  - Grade 1: Redness + slight swelling
  - Grade 2: Redness, moisture, moderate swelling
  - Grade 3: + tissue granulation
  - Grade 4: Overt infection requiring implant removal
- Children: higher complication rate; fixture loss up to 14% over 15 years
- 4 mm fixture always preferred over 3 mm (higher loss rate with shorter fixture)
- MRI compatible (titanium is non-magnetic; small artefact)

### Passive Transcutaneous Devices

**Sophono Alpha 2 MPO (Medtronic):**
- Surgically implanted plate containing twin hermetically sealed magnets
- External processor coupled via twin magnets on base plate
- Vibrations transmitted through skin into bone
- Audiological criteria: BC thresholds ≤45 dB; SSD: ≤20 dB contralateral
- Surgery: 2 mm bone beds, secured with 5 titanium screws, skin reduced to 4-5 mm
- MRI conditional to 3T (5 cm artefact)

**Cochlear BAHA Attract:**
- Uses BI300 osseointegrated implant + BIM400 magnet under skin
- External processor attached via magnetic coupling
- Advantages in children: eliminates soft-tissue complications of percutaneous devices
- Disadvantages: MRI artefact (~11.5 cm), high-frequency signal dampening through skin
- Skin thickness 3-6 mm optimal; too thick → inefficient
- MRI conditional to 1.5T

### Active Transcutaneous Device

**MED-EL Bonebridge:**
- Internal: receiver coil, magnet, demodulator + BC floating mass transducer (BC-FMT)
- External: audio processor transmits power transcutaneously via inductive link
- BC-FMT converts signals to mechanical energy; secured by 2 titanium screws
- Osseointegration of screws NOT thought to be crucial
- Audiological criteria: BC <45 dB (500 Hz-4 kHz); SSD: ≤20 dB contralateral
- Surgical placement: BC-FMT within mastoid or retrosigmoid position; compression of sigmoid sinus/dura may be necessary; "lifts" up to 4 mm available
- Requires sufficient skull growth; 3D radiological planning recommended
- MRI conditional to 1.5T (non-removable magnet)

### Non-Surgical Options
- **Softband:** Elastic band holding processor against scalp; used from ~3 months age for children too young for implantation
- **Headband:** Similar principle; available for trial
- **Sound Arc (Cochlear):** Newer trial device
- **ADHEAR (MED-EL):** Adhesive skin attachment; no surgery

## Part C: Indications for Bone Conduction Devices (Q55, Q107)

### Common Indications

**1. Conductive and Mixed Hearing Loss:**
- Congenital aural atresia / microtia
- Acquired: chronic otitis media, ossicular pathology
- Chronic discharging ear (CSOM, recurrent otitis externa)
- Cannot wear hearing aid after radical mastoid surgery
- Unilateral mixed hearing loss

**2. Single-Sided Deafness (SSD):**
- Sensorineural or conductive
- Post-traumatic hearing loss
- Functions as CROS (contralateral routing of signals)

**3. Inability to Use Conventional Hearing Aids:**
- Extremely narrow ear canal
- Blind sac ear canal closure
- Lateral temporal bone resection
- Extensive cranial base surgery

### Contraindications
- Bone disease unable to support implant
- Sensitivity/allergy to materials
- Physical, emotional or psychological disorders preventing rehabilitation

### NHS England Commissioning Criteria (2016)
1. Unilateral/bilateral conductive or mixed HL within fitting criteria AND stable BC thresholds (≤15 dB deterioration in >2 frequencies in 2 years) OR unilateral SNHL (including SSD) within criteria
2. Trialled ACHA or CROS/BiCROS for ≥4 weeks (or unable anatomically)
3. Trialled BCHD on softband/headband for ≥14 days with benefit on speech tests

## Part D: Paediatric BCD Workup and Candidacy (Q55)

### Age Considerations
| Age | Option |
|---|---|
| From ~3 months | Softband/headband trial (hearing stimulation as early as possible) |
| 2-3 years | Minimum recommended for percutaneous surgery (skull thickness adequate for osseointegration) |
| 5 years | FDA minimum age for percutaneous devices |
| Puberty | Challenges: increased soft tissue thickness; abutment length may need changing |

*Successful implantation reported in patients as young as 14 months, but complication rate inversely proportional to age at implantation.*

### Preoperative Workup in Children
1. **Audiological assessment:**
   - BC thresholds — confirm within device fitting range
   - AC thresholds — confirm conductive/mixed nature
   - Speech testing appropriate to age
   - Softband trial for minimum 14 days — demonstrate benefit on speech tests
2. **Otological assessment:**
   - Examine ears; document middle ear status
   - Consider microtia/atresia — plan BCD placement to not compromise future pinna reconstruction
   - Atresiaplasty rarely performed in UK now
3. **Imaging:**
   - CT temporal bone: assess bone thickness at implant site (minimum ~3 mm for fixture)
   - 3D reconstruction planning (especially for Bonebridge)
4. **Medical assessment:**
   - Fitness for GA
   - Syndromic associations: Treacher Collins, CHARGE, hemifacial microsomia, Down syndrome
5. **MDT assessment:**
   - Audiologist, ENT surgeon, speech therapist, teacher of the deaf, paediatrician
   - Family counselling: realistic expectations, device care, skin care (percutaneous)
6. **Decision on device type:**
   - Percutaneous (BAHA/Ponto): most efficient; skin complications
   - Passive transcutaneous (Attract/Sophono): no skin complications; less efficient at high frequencies
   - Active transcutaneous (Bonebridge): good efficiency; requires skull growth

### Surgical Considerations in Children
- Single-stage tissue-preserving surgery now standard (simple linear incision, no skin flap/dermatome)
- Longer osseointegration time recommended (thinner bone)
- May require GA (vs local in adults)
- 4 mm fixture mandatory (3 mm has higher failure rate in children)
- Bilateral fitting preferred for bilateral conductive loss (BC thresholds should not vary >20 dB at 3-4 kHz)
- Careful planning with microtia: BCD placement must not compromise future pinna reconstruction

## Part E: Middle Ear Implants — Mechanism and Indications (Q20)

### Mechanism of Action
AMEIs overcome conventional hearing aid shortcomings (distortion, feedback, occlusion effect, discomfort) by:
- Bypassing the external auditory canal
- Coupling transducer directly to ossicular chain or inner ear fluid
- No speaker for signal amplification → improved sound clarity

**Electromagnetic transduction (VSB, MET/Carina):**
- Electrical signal → electromagnetic field via transduction coil → drives magnet attached to ossicles → vibratory stimulation
- VSB: Floating Mass Transducer (FMT) — enclosed magnet vibrates within electromagnetic coil

**Piezoelectric transduction (Esteem):**
- Voltage applied to piezoceramic → proportional deformation → displacement coupled to ossicles
- Esteem: sensor on incus body (acts as microphone) + driver cemented to stapes head

### Indications for MEI
**Audiological:**
- Mild to severe SNHL with failed conventional hearing aids (primary indication)
- Mixed hearing loss (expanded indication)
- Failure causes: acoustic feedback, occlusion effect, insufficient high-frequency amplification, wearing discomfort
- Hearing loss should ideally be stable
- Codacs: specifically for profound mixed hearing loss (advanced [[Otosclerosis|otosclerosis]])

**Otological:**
- Absence of retrocochlear or central involvement
- Middle ear inflammation must be controlled
- Adequate EAC for external processor (if worn in canal)
- No skin conditions preventing external component attachment

**Patient factors:**
- Medically fit for surgery
- Appropriately counselled with realistic expectations
- Mostly adults (VSB and MET/Carina cleared for <18 years)
- *Cross-reference: `Cochlear_Implant_Comprehensive.md` Part E for MEI classification/types*

## Part F: Middle Ear Implants — Surgical Considerations (Q20)

### General Approach
Similar to [[Cochlear Implant|cochlear implant]] surgery: retroauricular transmastoidal access via facial recess. Implant bed drilled in cortical temporal bone for receiver/conductor.

### Device-Specific Surgery

**VSB:**
- Posterior tympanotomy large enough for 3 mm diamond burr
- FMT attachment clip crimped around incus long process (classic) or short process clip (newer, no posterior tympanotomy needed)
- Alternative coupling: stapes head, footplate, round window
- Ossicular chain NOT disrupted (in classic application)
- Risk: crimping too tight → incus necrosis; too loose → implant failure

**Carina:**
- Atticotomy to expose incus body and malleus head
- Laser hole in incus body → transducer probe aligned
- Subcutaneous microphone in postauricular pocket
- Fine-positioning by screw adjustment

**Esteem:**
- Large facial recess opening needed → chorda tympani resection often inevitable
- Intra-operative laser Doppler vibrometry to assess ossicular mobility
- Hypomobility → abort procedure
- Requires disarticulation of ossicular chain
- Meticulous cleaning of stapes head and incus for sensor/driver placement

**Codacs:**
- Special "retromeatal approach" to facial recess at oval window level
- Total stapes removal; conventional stapes prosthesis for implant + second prosthesis in parallel for natural sound transmission
- Electronic and mechanical testing during surgery

### Complications of MEI Surgery
| Device | Complication | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| VSB | Chorda tympani damage, FMT dislocation (especially round window coupling), aural fullness | Variable |
| Carina | Device malfunction/failure (mostly charging) | 17.6% |
| Esteem | Facial weakness; taste disturbance; explantation/revision | 8%, 30%, 13% |
| Codacs | [[Tinnitus|Tinnitus]], BC deterioration, vertigo | No major device-related AEs |

### Results
- AMEIs are considered safe alternative to conventional hearing aids in experienced hands
- Functional gain 16-48 dB compared to unaided condition (all devices similar)
- Compared to best aided pre-operative condition: positive but mostly not significant improvement
- No initial decline of BC threshold after implantation in most studies
