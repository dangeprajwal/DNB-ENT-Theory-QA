> - ## INTRODUCTION
> - ## PART A: VESTIBULAR SYSTEM PHYSIOLOGY *(Addresses Q66)
> - — provides spatial orientation cues
> - — detects angular and linear accelerations
> - — provides information about body position from joints, muscles, and skin
> - - Three paired semicircular canals detect **rotational (angular) acceleration
> - Oriented in three planes to detect all rotational movements:
> - Lateral (horizontal) canals:** Both parallel, in the lateral plane; angled ~30° above horizontal
> - RALP plane:** Right Anterior + Left Posterior canals (approximately 45° to sagittal plane)
> - LARP plane:** Left Anterior + Right Posterior canals
> - Mechanism:** During head rotation, endolymph lags behind (Newton's 1st law), deflecting the cupula, which bends hair cells
> - The SCC system is designed such that cupular deflection is **proportional to head angular velocity** (not acceleration) — the canal acts as an integrator
> - Time constant T1 ≈ 0.003 s — cupula reaches 67% of ultimate deflection in just 3 ms
> - Optimal frequency range:** 0.1-5 Hz (corresponds to natural head movements during walking/running)
> - Sustained constant velocity is no longer detected after ~30 seconds
> - - **Utricle:** Relatively horizontal orientation — primarily detects horizontal linear accelerations and head tilts
> - Saccule:** Relatively vertical (sagittal) orientation — primarily detects vertical linear accelerations
> - Mechanism:** Otolithic membrane containing otoconia (CaCO3 crystals, density ~3x endolymph) shifts with linear acceleration or gravity, deflecting hair cells
> - Einstein's equivalence principle:** Otolith organs cannot distinguish between linear acceleration and tilt — the brain resolves this ambiguity using SCC input and an internal model in the vestibulocerebellum (flocculus and nodulus)
> - (4 nuclei — superior, lateral, medial, inferior) and projects to produce three main reflexes:
> - | Vestibular nerve → vestibular nuclei → oculomotor nuclei (III, IV, VI) | Gaze stabilization during head movement; generates compensatory eye movements equal and opposite to head movement; **3-neuron arc** with shortest latency (~16 ms) |
> - | Via lateral vestibulospinal tract (from lateral vestibular nucleus/Deiters') and medial vestibulospinal tract | Postural control; maintenance of upright posture; extensor muscle tone |
> - | Via medial vestibulospinal tract (from medial vestibular nucleus) | Head stabilization on trunk during body movements |
> - - **Vestibulocerebellar connections:** Flocculus/nodulus — VOR calibration and velocity storage
> - Vestibulo-cortical connections:** Parietal cortex — conscious perception of spatial orientation and self-motion
> - Vestibulo-autonomic connections:** Suprachiasmatic nucleus (circadian rhythm), vagal centres (motion sickness)
> - The canal on the leading side is **excited** (cupula deflected towards utricle in horizontal canal)
> - The contralateral canal is **inhibited** (cupula deflected away from utricle)
> - This push-pull arrangement doubles the sensitivity and provides directional coding
> - ## PART B: EWALD'S LAWS *(Addresses Q66)
> - - Each canal generates eye movements (VOR) in the plane of that canal
> - Horizontal canal stimulation produces horizontal nystagmus
> - Posterior canal stimulation produces torsional-vertical (upbeating) nystagmus
> - Anterior canal stimulation produces torsional-vertical (downbeating) nystagmus
> - - Ampullopetal flow = **excitatory** in horizontal canal → stronger nystagmus
> - Ampullofugal flow = **inhibitory** in horizontal canal → weaker nystagmus
> - Clinical significance: In horizontal canal BPPV, the ear with the stronger geotropic nystagmus is the affected ear (particles cause ampullopetal flow on that side)
> - - Ampullofugal flow = excitatory in vertical canals
> - Ampullopetal flow = inhibitory in vertical canals
> - This explains the direction of nystagmus in posterior canal BPPV during Dix-Hallpike test
> - Explain **Alexander's law:** Peripheral vestibular nystagmus increases when looking in the direction of the fast phase
> - Help determine the **affected side** in BPPV
> - Explain the **asymmetry of caloric responses** — warm stimulation (ampullopetal flow) produces a stronger response than cold stimulation
> - The excitatory response always dominates — known as **Ewald's dominance
> - ## PART C: TYPES OF NYSTAGMUS *(Addresses Q66)
> - Alternating slow and fast phases; can be peripheral or central
> - Quasi-sinusoidal, no distinct fast phase; **always central** (but may be congenital or due to severe visual loss)
> - | Present in primary gaze; peripheral type beats away from lesion; enhances in dark/behind Frenzel glasses | Peripheral = acute vestibular imbalance (neuritis, Meniere's); Central = brainstem/cerebellar |
> - | Appears on eccentric gaze; fast phase towards direction of gaze | Central — cerebellar/brainstem (impaired neural integrator); also drug effect (alcohol, sedatives, anticonvulsants) |
> - | Provoked by change in head position (Dix-Hallpike, roll test) | BPPV (peripheral); can also be central |
> - | Elicited by moving visual stimulus (striped drum); slow phase = pursuit, fast phase = saccadic reset | Tests pursuit system + saccade system; asymmetry suggests central lesion |
> - | During rotational stimulation; fast phase in direction of rotation | VOR assessment |
> - | After sudden stop of rotation; fast phase opposite to prior rotation | Decay assessed by time constant |
> - | Induced by thermal irrigation of EAC | Assesses individual labyrinthine function (see Part E) |
> - | Fast phase downward; present in primary gaze or downgaze | Craniocervical junction pathology (Chiari malformation, MS) |
> - | Fast phase upward | Brainstem lesion (medulla, pons) |
> - | Horizontal or horizontal-torsional | Any plane (vertical, torsional, multidirectional) |

> - ## PART E: CALORIC TEST *(Addresses Q97)
> - formation in the horizontal SCC when placed vertically.
> - Supine with head raised **30 degrees** above horizontal (places horizontal SCC vertically)
> - Water at **30°C** (cold) and **44°C** (warm) — i.e., 37 ± 7°C
> - Standardized as Left cold → Right cold → Left warm → Right warm
> - Each irrigation lasts **40 seconds
> - At least **5 minutes** between irrigations for thermal equilibration
> - EOG or VOG; measure slow-phase velocity or duration of nystagmus
> - COWS mnemonic:** Cold Opposite, Warm Same
> - Cold irrigation → nystagmus beating AWAY from irrigated ear (contralateral)
> - Warm irrigation → nystagmus beating TOWARDS irrigated ear (ipsilateral)
> - Mechanism: Cold → endolymph cools and sinks (in vertical SCC) → ampullofugal flow → inhibitory; Warm → endolymph heats and rises → ampullopetal flow → excitatory
> - (LW + LC)] / (RW + RC + LW + LC) × 100
> - Canal paresis >20-25%** is considered significant
> - Indicates reduced function of one labyrinth
> - (LW + RC)] / (RW + RC + LW + LC) × 100
> - DP >20-30%** is significant
> - Indicates asymmetry in VOR — less localizing than CP
> - Can be peripheral or central
> - Aminoglycoside ototoxicity, post-meningitis (25% neurological, 25% ototoxic, 25% miscellaneous, 25% idiopathic)
> - [[Vestibular Schwannoma|Vestibular schwannoma]], [[Vestibular Neuritis|vestibular neuritis]], labyrinthine lesion
> - Less specific; peripheral or central lesion
> - Central disorder (cerebellar/brainstem); normally, fixation suppresses caloric nystagmus by ~50%
> - Vertical nystagmus in response to caloric (expected horizontal) — indicates posterior fossa disease
> - ## PART F: VEMP — PHYSIOLOGICAL BASIS *(Addresses Q30, Q97)
> - | Sternocleidomastoid (SCM) muscle | Contralateral inferior oblique muscle (beneath eye) |
> - | **Saccule** | **Utricle** |
> - | **Vestibulocollic reflex (VCR):** Saccule → inferior vestibular nerve → vestibular nucleus → medial vestibulospinal tract → SCM motor neurons | **Vestibulo-ocular reflex:** Utricle → superior vestibular nerve → vestibular nucleus → oculomotor nucleus → inferior oblique |
> - | Inhibitory (biphasic p13-n23 wave) | Excitatory (biphasic n10-p15 wave) |
> - | Air-conducted clicks/tone bursts (500 Hz optimal) or bone-conducted vibration | Same stimuli; bone-conducted vibration at Fz also effective |
> - | p13 ~13 ms, n23 ~23 ms | n10 ~10 ms, p15 ~15 ms |
> - | Ipsilateral (uncrossed) | **Contralateral** (crossed) |
> - The saccular macula is closest to the stapes footplate and is preferentially stimulated by air-conducted sound (Tullio phenomenon basis)
> - Sound stimulus → saccular hair cell activation → inferior vestibular nerve → descending pathway → ipsilateral SCM inhibition = cVEMP
> - Sound stimulus → utricular hair cell activation → superior vestibular nerve → ascending pathway → contralateral inferior oblique excitation = oVEMP
> - Enhanced (low threshold, high amplitude) cVEMP and oVEMP
> - Elevated thresholds or absent VEMP
> - Absent cVEMP (inferior nerve) or oVEMP (superior nerve) depending on division affected
> - Absent or delayed VEMP on affected side
> - Absent/reduced cVEMP due to stapes fixation (conductive component)

> - ## PART G: GERIATRIC DIZZINESS *(Addresses Q16)
> - rather than due to a single cause.
> - - **BPPV** — most common cause of vertigo in elderly; prevalence increases with age; posterior canal most common
> - Presbyvestibulopathy** — age-related decline in vestibular function; loss of hair cells and vestibular neurons
> - Meniere's disease** — may occur or recur in elderly
> - Bilateral vestibular failure** — aminoglycoside exposure, autoimmune, post-meningitis
> - - **Cerebrovascular disease** — vertebrobasilar insufficiency, posterior circulation strokes
> - Vestibular migraine** — may present de novo in elderly
> - Cerebellar degeneration** — ataxia, imbalance
> - Multiple sclerosis** — though less common to present in old age
> - - **Orthostatic hypotension** — very common in elderly; exacerbated by medications, autonomic neuropathy
> - Cardiac arrhythmias** — presyncope/syncope presenting as dizziness
> - Carotid sinus hypersensitivity
> - Aortic stenosis
> - - Antihypertensives, sedatives, anxiolytics, antidepressants, aminoglycosides, anticonvulsants, diuretics
> - Polypharmacy** is a major contributing factor in elderly dizziness
> - - **Visual decline** — cataracts, macular degeneration, glaucoma → reduced visual input for balance
> - Peripheral neuropathy** — diabetes, B12 deficiency → impaired proprioception
> - Cervical spondylosis** — altered proprioceptive input from cervical spine
> - - Sarcopenia, joint disease, deconditioning → increased fall risk
> - Reduced postural reflexes
> - - Anxiety, depression, fear of falling — can perpetuate imbalance and avoidance behaviour
> - ## PART H: EVALUATION OF THE SEVERELY DIZZY PATIENT *(Addresses Q16)
> - - Timing: Episodic vs continuous; duration of episodes
> - Triggers: Positional, Valsalva, loud sounds, head movement
> - Associated symptoms: Hearing loss, tinnitus, aural fullness, headache, neurological symptoms
> - Medications review
> - Cardiovascular history
> - Falls history

> - > [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> - The VOR is a **3-neuron arc** (Scarpa's ganglion → vestibular nuclei → oculomotor nuclei) with a latency of ~16 ms
> - The SCC system encodes **head angular velocity** (not acceleration) through the mechanics of endolymph-cupula interaction
> - Ewald's 2nd law:** In horizontal canal, ampullopetal flow is excitatory (stronger); in vertical canals, ampullofugal flow is excitatory — this is reversed
> - COWS** mnemonic for caloric: Cold Opposite, Warm Same
> - Canal paresis** formula (Jongkees): >20-25% indicates unilateral labyrinthine hypofunction
> - cVEMP** tests saccular function via vestibulocollic reflex; **oVEMP** tests utricular function via VOR
> - A **HINTS** examination (Head Impulse, Nystagmus, Test of Skew) in acute vestibular syndrome is more sensitive than early MRI for detecting posterior circulation stroke
> - Peripheral vestibular nystagmus:** unidirectional, horizontal-torsional, suppressed by fixation, linear slow-phase
> - Central nystagmus:** direction-changing, any plane, not suppressed, exponential slow-phase
> - Geriatric dizziness is **multifactorial** — BPPV, orthostatic hypotension, medications, and sensory deficits are the most common causes
