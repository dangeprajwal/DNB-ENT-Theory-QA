# Midline Granulomas of the Nose

> [!question] Questions Covered
> - Discuss the aetiology, clinical features, diagnosis, and management of midline granulomas of the nose.
*Source:** Scott-Brown's Otorhinolaryngology, 8th Edition - Volume 1, Chapter 109 (Granulomatous Conditions of the Nose)

---

## INTRODUCTION

"Midline granuloma" refers to a group of conditions causing progressive destructive lesions of the midline nasal and midfacial structures. The term "lethal midline granuloma" was historically used when the underlying aetiology was uncertain. Modern understanding has identified three principal causes of midline nasal destruction: granulomatosis with polyangiitis (GPA/Wegener's), extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma (ENKTCL — formerly called "lethal midline granuloma"), and cocaine-induced midline destructive lesion (CIMDL). Any patient with blood-stained discharge and crusting in the nose has a granulomatous condition until proven otherwise.

---

## PART A: CAUSES OF MIDLINE NASAL DESTRUCTION

| Condition | Key Feature | ANCA Pattern |
|-----------|------------|--------------|
| GPA (Wegener's) | Vasculitis + granulomas | c-ANCA (PR3) |
| ENKTCL | EBV-driven lymphoma | Negative |
| CIMDL | Cocaine abuse | p-ANCA (HNE) |

### Other Differential Diagnoses

- Infections: TB, syphilis, leprosy, rhinoscleroma, fungal infections
- EGPA (Churg-Strauss): rare midline involvement
- Sarcoidosis: does not typically cause midline destruction
- Malignancy: SCC, basal cell carcinoma

---

## PART B: GRANULOMATOSIS WITH POLYANGIITIS (GPA)

GPA is the most common cause of midline granulomatous destruction of the nose.

### Key Features

- Systemic condition: granulomatous inflammation of respiratory tract + necrotizing vasculitis of small-medium vessels + glomerulonephritis
- Incidence: 10-15 per million/year; prevalence up to 5 per 100,000 in Europe
- Mean age at diagnosis: 40-50 years; predominantly Caucasian (>90%)
- Limited/localized forms in up to 30% of cases
- Untreated: mean survival of only 5 months

### Midline Destruction in GPA

The nose and sinuses are involved in >80% of patients with symptoms of obstruction, crusting, discharge, and bleeding. Destruction of intranasal structures may follow — septum, turbinates, and sinuses can form a single large cavity. [[Septal Perforation Repair|Septal perforation]] and saddle-nose deformity from cartilage/bone collapse is characteristic. However, the overlying midfacial skin is spared (unlike ENKTCL).

Other ENT involvement:
- [[OME Pathophysiology|Otitis media with effusion]] (one-third); SNHL (35%)
- [[Subglottic Stenosis|Subglottic stenosis]] (16%; 5x more common if childhood onset)
- "Strawberry" gingival hyperplasia — pathognomonic
- Ocular involvement (50%); proptosis (2%); visual loss (8%)

### Diagnosis

| Investigation | Finding |
|--------------|---------|
| c-ANCA (PR3) | Sensitivity 91%, specificity 99% |
| Localized disease | Sensitivity falls to 60% |
| p-ANCA (MPO) | 10% of GPA patients |
| CT scan | Bone destruction + new bone formation |
| MRI | "Tramlining" from fat in sclerotic wall |
| Biopsy | Granulomas, vasculitis, necrosis |

Biopsy is non-diagnostic in up to 50% of head and neck specimens. Paranasal sinus biopsies yield more positive results than nasal specimens.

### Treatment

**Remission induction:**
- Cyclophosphamide (2 mg/kg/day, max 200 mg) + high-dose prednisolone (0.5-1 mg/kg, max 80 mg)
- Rituximab (anti-CD20): as effective as cyclophosphamide; may be superior in relapsing disease (RAVE trial)
- Methotrexate: for limited/less severe disease

**Remission maintenance:**
- Methotrexate or azathioprine (similar relapse rates to long-term cyclophosphamide)
- Mycophenolate mofetil, leflunomide as alternatives

**Local nasal treatment:** Douching, intranasal steroids, nasal lubricants

**Surgery:** Reserved for refractory cases. Saddle-nose augmentation only after minimum 1 year remission (92% success rate).

## PART C: EXTRANODAL NK/T-CELL LYMPHOMA (ENKTCL)

This condition was formerly known as "midline lethal granuloma" or "Stewart's granuloma." It is included in the granulomatous chapter due to this historical nomenclature, though it is actually a neoplastic condition.

### Key Features

- Rare non-Hodgkin's lymphoma causing midface destruction
- Caused by Epstein-Barr virus (EBV)
- Most common in south-east Asia and South America
- Peak in 5th-6th decade; male preponderance

### Clinical Features — How It Differs from GPA

ENKTCL arises in the nasal cavity and spreads to orbits, oral cavity, skin, and sinuses. The key distinguishing feature is **progressive destruction of the nasal skeleton AND overlying skin** — unlike GPA where the skin is typically spared.

### Diagnosis

| Feature | Finding |
|---------|---------|
| Histology | Polymorphic atypical cells, angiocentric necrotic growth pattern |
| IHC markers | CD56+, CD2+, cytoplasmic CD3+ |
| Granulomas | ABSENT (despite historical name) |
| Key feature | Thrombosis and necrosis common |
| Staging | Imaging + bone marrow biopsy |

Early diagnosis is critical as prognosis for widespread disease is much worse. Good quality biopsy material from beneath slough/crust is essential.

### Treatment

- Dramatic response to **[[Radiotherapy Principles Fractionation Advances|radiotherapy]]** — recommended for localized disease
- Role of chemotherapy not yet established; may be added for recurrent or disseminated disease
- Local recurrence: 31-67%
- 5-year survival: 38-45%

## PART D: COCAINE-INDUCED MIDLINE DESTRUCTIVE LESION (CIMDL)

### Key Features

- Intranasal cocaine inhalation causes mucosal inflammation
- Septal perforation in ~5% of chronic users; more aggressive midfacial destruction also occurs
- Mimics localized GPA and neoplasia clinically and histologically
- **Palatal perforation is MORE common than in GPA** (key differentiating feature)

### Pathogenesis

Cocaine is the trigger, but patients who develop CIMDL appear predisposed to produce ANCA. Nearly 90% have positive p-ANCA against human neutrophil elastase (HNE), which may increase apoptosis and local inflammatory response to injury. Over 50% also have positive PR3-ANCA, making differentiation from limited GPA difficult.

### Diagnosis — Differentiating CIMDL from GPA

| Feature | GPA | CIMDL |
|---------|-----|-------|
| ANCA pattern | c-ANCA (PR3) | p-ANCA (HNE) |
| HNE-ANCA | Negative | ~90% positive |
| Palatal perforation | Rare | Common |
| Skin destruction | Spared | Variable |
| Systemic disease | Common | Rarely systemic |
| Histology | May be identical | Vasculitis, fibrinoid necrosis |

HNE-ANCA is NOT positive in normal controls or GPA patients — this is the key distinguishing marker. Urine, blood, and hair can be tested for cocaine.

### Treatment

- **No role for immunosuppression** (unlike GPA)
- Patient must stop cocaine use to prevent progression
- Conservative: nasal douching, debridement of necrotic areas, topical/systemic antibiotics
- Surgical correction of septal perforation or nasal deformity only after minimum 6-12 months cocaine-free

---

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. "Midline granuloma" encompasses GPA, ENKTCL, and CIMDL as the three principal causes of midline nasal destruction
> 2. ENKTCL was formerly known as "lethal midline granuloma" but contains NO granulomas or giant cells histologically
> 3. c-ANCA (PR3) sensitivity and specificity for GPA are 91% and 99%; falls to 60% in localized disease
> 4. ENKTCL causes destruction of overlying skin; GPA spares the skin — key clinical differentiator
> 5. CIMDL: ~90% positive for HNE-ANCA (not found in GPA); palatal perforation more common than in GPA
> 6. Untreated GPA has mean survival of 5 months; rituximab is as effective as cyclophosphamide (RAVE trial)
> 7. ENKTCL shows dramatic response to radiotherapy; 5-year survival is 38-45%
> 8. CIMDL requires cocaine cessation — NO role for immunosuppression (unlike GPA)
> 9. CT in GPA: bone destruction + new bone formation is virtually diagnostic; MRI shows "tramlining"
> 10. Saddle-nose repair in GPA only after 1 year remission (92% success); CIMDL repair after 6-12 months cocaine-free


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - Always consider cocaine use when p-ANCA and PR3-ANCA are both positive — false-positive c-ANCA occurs with cocaine abuse
> - HNE-ANCA is the most reliable serological marker to distinguish CIMDL from GPA (positive in CIMDL, negative in GPA)
> - Nasal S. aureus carriage in GPA is associated with more frequent relapses
> - Biopsy should target tissue beneath slough and crust; paranasal sinus biopsies yield more positive results than nasal specimens in GPA
> - Not all patients with CIMDL will admit to cocaine use — drug testing (urine, blood, hair) may be necessary
