# Mucociliary Clearance, Covid-19 Anosmia and Lund-mackay Scoring

**Question:** *Q47 (Jun 2021): Discuss Covid-19 loss of smell and the role of olfactory retraining therapy. Q61 (Jun 2022): Describe the nasal cycle and physiology of paranasal sinuses; discuss the Lund-Mackay scoring system for CRS. 2015-1: Describe the mucociliary transport and clearance pathways of the nose and paranasal sinuses with diagrams.*

**Source:** Scott-Brown's Otorhinolaryngology, 8th Edition - Volume 1, Chapter 89 (Physiology of the Nose and Paranasal Sinuses), Chapter 95 (Nasal Polyposis), Chapter 110 (Abnormalities of Smell)

---

## INTRODUCTION

The nasal cavity and paranasal sinuses are lined by pseudostratified columnar ciliated epithelium that performs a critical defensive function through the mucociliary transport system. This is the primary mechanical defence of the nasal mucosa, physically cleaning inspired air and protecting the lower respiratory tract. The mucous blanket traps inhaled particles which are then propelled towards the nasopharynx by coordinated ciliary beating. Mucociliary function is regulated by the nasal cycle and maintained by the unique physiology of the paranasal sinuses. Disorders of olfaction, including post-viral anosmia from SARS-CoV-2 infection, represent an important area of rhinological practice. The Lund-Mackay scoring system provides a standardized CT-based method for quantifying sinonasal disease burden in [[CRS Nasal Polyposis Turbinate|chronic rhinosinusitis]].

## PART A: MUCOCILIARY TRANSPORT AND CLEARANCE PATHWAYS

### Structure of the Mucociliary System

The respiratory epithelium lining the nasal cavity is pseudostratified columnar ciliated epithelium containing ciliated and non-ciliated columnar cells, basal cells, and mucin-secreting goblet cells. The epithelium is located on the lamina propria and basement membrane, served by capillaries that facilitate fluid flow through the nasal mucosa.

**Ciliary structure:**
- Each ciliated cell bears 50-200 cilia
- The surface membrane of a cilium encloses an organized ultrastructure with nine outer-paired microtubules enclosing a single inner pair (9+2 arrangement)
- Outer pairs are linked to one another by nexins and to the inner pair by central spokes
- Outer pairs have both outer and inner dynein arms containing ATPase
- ATP is converted to ADP using the enzyme ATPase at the dynein arms; this Mg2+-dependent reaction produces energy for ciliary motion
- Motion occurs by sliding of outer pair microtubules against one another

**Two-layer mucous blanket:**
- **Lower sol (periciliary) layer:** Watery layer where cilia move freely during their recovery stroke
- **Upper gel (viscous) layer:** Thicker layer with viscoelastic properties that traps particles; small hooks on ciliary tips engage this layer during the effective stroke

**Ciliary beat mechanism:**
- **Effective (power) stroke:** Cilia straighten, hooks engage the upper viscous layer, propelling it posteriorly
- **Recovery stroke:** Cilia bend and swing back through the lower watery layer without engaging the gel layer
- **Metachronous movement:** Cilia at right angle to the flow direction are in phase; those in the flow direction are out of phase until the cycle completes
- This produces unidirectional mucous flow from anterior to posterior

### Clearance Pathways

From the front of the nose, mucus flows posteriorly. Mucus from the sinuses and lateral wall joins together, with most passing through the middle meatus. The majority of mucus then passes around the Eustachian tube orifice before it is swallowed.

```
          NASAL CAVITY MUCOCILIARY CLEARANCE PATHWAYS

  SINUS                  CLEARANCE ROUTE
  -----                  ---------------
  Maxillary sinus    --> Floor -> up walls -> natural ostium
                         -> middle meatus

  [[Frontal Recess Frontal Sinus Surgery|Frontal sinus]]      --> Spiral pattern along floor
                         -> frontal recess -> middle meatus

  Anterior ethmoid   --> Via infundibulum -> middle meatus

  Posterior ethmoid   --> Superior meatus
                         -> sphenoethmoidal recess

  Sphenoid sinus     --> Sphenoethmoidal recess
                         -> nasopharynx

  Lateral nasal wall  --> Posteriorly through middle meatus

  ALL PATHWAYS CONVERGE:
  Middle meatus -> around Eustachian orifice -> Nasopharynx
                                             -> SWALLOWED
```

### Key Points About Sinus Clearance

**Maxillary sinus:** Mucus clearance is always through the natural ostium, regardless of the presence of accessory ostia or surgical antrostomies. Mucus travels across the floor and up the walls to exit through the natural ostium. If an accessory ostium exists alongside the natural ostium, mucus directed towards the natural opening may re-enter through the accessory ostium, creating a recirculation phenomenon. This mucous recirculation explains the failure of inferior meatal antrostomy.

**Frontal sinus:** Clearance follows a spiral pattern in which mucus flows medially along the interfrontal septum, then laterally along the floor to the frontal recess.

**Surgical relevance:** During endoscopic sinus surgery, the goal is to establish mucociliary function that is free of recirculation effects, enable maximal delivery of topical therapy, and prevent mucocele formation. Paranasal sinuses are divided into anterior, posterior, and sphenoid compartments that serve as functional units based on mucociliary drainage pathways. Once a compartment is surgically entered, all mucosal cells within the compartment must be completely dissected to avoid disconnected cells and recirculation.

### Measurement of Mucociliary Clearance

- **Saccharin test:** A saccharin particle is placed on the anterior end of the inferior turbinate; the time to perception of sweet taste is measured. Normal: 10-15 minutes; >20 minutes is abnormal
- **Saline irrigation** has been shown to significantly improve nasal mucociliary clearance as measured by saccharin tests in healthy volunteers
- **Ciliary beat frequency (CBF) measurement:** By photometry or high-speed video microscopy
- **Radiolabelled particle clearance:** Technetium-99m labelled particles tracked with gamma camera

### Factors Impairing Mucociliary Clearance

| Factor | Mechanism |
|--------|-----------|
| Smoking | Reduces cilia number, changes mucous viscosity |
| Viral infection | Damages ciliated epithelium directly |
| Temperature extremes | <10 degrees C or >45 degrees C stops ciliary movement |
| Tonicity changes | >5% above or >2% below isotonic causes paralysis |
| pH extremes | Cilia beat above pH 6.4 to pH 8.5 |
| Ageing | Ciliary function deteriorates with age |
| Dry conditions | Stop ciliary action completely |
| Codeine/narcotics | Inhibit secretion and mucociliary clearance of sputum |
| Pregnancy | Decreased mucociliary clearance due to hormonal effects |

**Primary ciliary dyskinesia (PCD):** An autosomal recessive condition with defective ciliary structure (absent dynein arms). Kartagener syndrome is the classic triad of situs inversus, bronchiectasis, and chronic sinusitis, and should be suspected in any patient presenting with this combination.

## PART B: COVID-19 AND OLFACTORY DYSFUNCTION

### Pathogenesis of Post-Viral Anosmia

Upper respiratory viruses are the most common aetiology of permanent olfactory loss. The olfactory neuroepithelium has an ultrastructure of variable uniformity and is a pseudostratified columnar epithelium supported by a highly vascularized lamina propria. Throughout life, islands of respiratory-like epithelial metaplasia appear within the epithelium as a result of cumulative viral, bacterial, and other insults.

In post-viral anosmia, the mechanism involves damage to the olfactory neuroepithelium. Jafek and colleagues noted that patients with anosmia following viral illness had markedly reduced numbers of receptors and those receptors were abnormal compared to hyposmic patients. The olfactory nerve cells and proximal extraneural spaces can serve as conduits for movement of viruses and exogenous agents from the nasal cavity into the brain.

**In SARS-CoV-2 infection specifically:**
- The virus binds ACE2 receptors and TMPRSS2 co-receptors on the nasal epithelium
- ACE2 is highly expressed on sustentacular (supporting) cells of the olfactory epithelium
- Sustentacular cells insulate bipolar receptor cells, regulate mucus production, transport molecules across the epithelium, and detoxify and degrade odorants
- Damage to sustentacular cells disrupts olfactory neuron support and function
- Olfactory cleft mucosal inflammation and oedema contribute to conductive olfactory loss
- Direct viral neurotropism via the olfactory nerve to the olfactory bulb has been postulated
- Usually temporary (2-4 weeks) as sustentacular cells regenerate
- Persistent anosmia (>3 months) in 5-10% suggests neuronal or central involvement

### Classification of Olfactory Dysfunction

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| Anosmia | Inability to detect qualitative olfactory sensations |
| Partial anosmia | Ability to perceive some but not all odours |
| Hyposmia/microsmia | Decreased sensitivity to odours |
| Dysosmia/parosmia | Distorted perception to odour stimulation |
| Phantosmia | Dysosmic sensation in absence of stimulus |
| Hyperosmia | Increased sensitivity to odours |

**Clinical relevance of parosmia:** Dysosmia usually reflects dynamic alterations of degeneration or regeneration within the olfactory neuroepithelium over time. It implies an olfactory system that is intact at least to some degree. Patients who eventually develop anosmia commonly report weeks of dysosmia preceding the anosmia. Parosmia during recovery from Covid-19 represents aberrant olfactory neuron reinnervation and is a sign of regeneration.

### Evaluation of Post-Covid Anosmia

**History:** Determine onset (sudden vs gradual), duration, pattern (constant vs intermittent), precipitating event, and associated symptoms (discharge, nasal obstruction).

**Objective olfactory testing:**
- **UPSIT (University of Pennsylvania Smell Identification Test):** 40-item scratch-and-sniff test; commercially the most widely used olfactory test worldwide; self-administered in 10-15 minutes; classifies into normosmia, mild/moderate/severe microsmia, anosmia, and probable malingering; test-retest reliability r = 0.94
- **Sniffin' Sticks test:** Threshold, discrimination, and identification (TDI) score
- **Phenyl ethyl alcohol threshold testing:** Establishes threshold using staircase procedure

**Imaging:** Coronal CT is useful for assessing sinonasal tract inflammatory disorders and bony structures. MRI is the technique of choice for imaging olfactory bulbs, tracts, and cortical parenchyma.

### Treatment of Post-Covid Anosmia

**General principles from Scott-Brown:** The most effective treatments are for conductive anosmia. Systemic steroids can temporarily reverse conductive olfactory impairment in 83% of patients while topical steroids help in only 25%. Topical nasal steroids are often ineffectual in returning smell function because the steroid fails to reach the affected upper nasal passages. Increased efficacy occurs when nasal drops or spray are administered in the head-down Moffett's position.

**Olfactory Retraining Therapy (ORT):**
- Based on the principle of neuroplasticity: repeated deliberate exposure to odours stimulates olfactory neuron regeneration and cortical reorganization
- The olfactory neuron is one of the few neurons capable of regeneration throughout life; globose basal cells are multipotent and can give rise to new neurons
- **Protocol:** Four primary odour categories (flowery, resinous, fruity, spicy) are sniffed for 10-15 seconds each, twice daily, for a minimum of 12 weeks (ideally 6 months)
- Modified ORT involves rotation of odours every 12 weeks to prevent habituation
- Evidence supports significant improvement in olfactory scores compared to controls
- **Adjuncts:** Intranasal corticosteroids (reduce olfactory cleft inflammation), omega-3 fatty acids, and intranasal vitamin A drops (may promote olfactory neuron regeneration)

**Prognosis:** Spontaneous recovery of function varies depending on severity. For patients whose odour identification ability is less severe (e.g., UPSIT scores above 25), prognosis for complete recovery is much better than for those with complete anosmia. In one study of 542 patients, only 11.31% of anosmic and 23.31% of microsmic patients regained normal age-related function over time. Age, severity of initial loss, and duration of dysfunction were significant predictors.

**Supportive measures for complete anosmia:**
- Smoke and carbon monoxide detectors must be installed
- Electric appliances preferred over gas appliances
- Expiration dates on food must be scrutinized
- Balanced diet maintained; flavour enhancers may help

## PART C: PHYSIOLOGY OF THE PARANASAL SINUSES AND NASAL CYCLE

### Functions of the Paranasal Sinuses

The paranasal sinuses are air-filled spaces within the bones of the skull. Their functions include:

1. **Reduction of skull weight:** Hollow cavities reduce the mass of facial bones
2. **Resonance of voice:** Air-filled spaces modify vocal resonance
3. **Thermal insulation:** Protect the brain and orbits from temperature extremes
4. **Humidification and warming:** Contribute to conditioning of inspired air
5. **Absorption of trauma:** Act as crumple zones protecting vital structures
6. **Nitric oxide (NO) production:** Sinus mucosa produces high concentrations of NO, which has antimicrobial, mucociliary-enhancing, and vasodilatory properties
7. **Immunological defence:** IgA is the main immunoglobulin in nasal secretions, constituting 70% of total proteins in nasal secretions; IgA2 (dimer structure) is common in nasal secretions while IgA1 (monomeric) is frequent in serum

### The Nasal Cycle

The nasal cycle is a well-recognized physiological activity whereby each side of the nose alternates phases of congestion and decongestion. Vascular activity produces the changes, especially the volume of blood in capacitance vessels (venous sinusoids).

**Key characteristics:**
- Changes are cyclical and occur every **4 to 12 hours** with the pattern being constant for each person
- Can be demonstrated in up to **80% of adults**; not easy to demonstrate in children
- Total nasal resistance remains constant despite unilateral variation (reciprocal relationship)
- The autonomic nervous system regulates the changes

**Physiological significance:**
- Resistance and airflow leads to increased cyclical secretions in the side experiencing greatest flow
- Does not appear to affect overall olfactory sensitivity in the normal nose
- Allows cyclical mucosal rest and recovery of each side alternately
- Maintains optimal mucociliary function bilaterally

**Factors affecting the nasal cycle:**

| Factor | Effect on Nasal Cycle |
|--------|----------------------|
| Exercise | Sympathetic activation reduces nasal resistance |
| Pregnancy | Increases nasal congestion bilaterally |
| Hormones (puberty) | Affect nasal mucosal engorgement |
| Infections/allergy | Disrupt normal cyclical pattern |
| Fear, emotions, sex | Alter autonomic tone |
| Vagal over-activity | Causes nasal congestion |
| High CO2 (rebreathing) | Reduces nasal resistance |
| Hyperventilation | Increases nasal resistance |
| Antihistamines | Anti-cholinergic effect improves airway |

**Nasal resistance:**
- Contributes up to **50% of total airway resistance**
- Three key components: nasal vestibule, nasal valve, and inferior turbinates
- Nasal valve is the narrowest part (~55-83 mm^2 cross-section); velocity 12-18 m/sec during quiet breathing
- Inferior turbinate enlargement of only 1-2 mm causes significant reduction in airflow velocity (from 0.89 m/s to 0.42 m/s)

## PART D: LUND-MACKAY CT SCORING SYSTEM

### Overview

The Lund-Mackay scoring system is a CT-based radiological staging system for chronic rhinosinusitis, widely used for quantifying disease extent and facilitating communication.

### Scoring Method

The system gives a score of 0-2 depending on the absence, partial, or complete opacification of each sinus system and of the osteomeatal complex on CT scanning:
- **0** = No abnormalities (clear)
- **1** = Partial opacification
- **2** = Complete opacification
- **Osteomeatal complex:** 0 = not occluded, 2 = occluded (no score of 1)

| Sinus/Structure | Left (0-2) | Right (0-2) |
|-----------------|-----------|------------|
| Maxillary | 0 / 1 / 2 | 0 / 1 / 2 |
| Anterior ethmoid | 0 / 1 / 2 | 0 / 1 / 2 |
| Posterior ethmoid | 0 / 1 / 2 | 0 / 1 / 2 |
| Frontal | 0 / 1 / 2 | 0 / 1 / 2 |
| Sphenoid | 0 / 1 / 2 | 0 / 1 / 2 |
| Osteomeatal complex | 0 / 2 | 0 / 2 |
| **Max per side** | **12** | **12** |
| **Total maximum** | | **24** |

### Clinical Significance

- Lund-Mackay score increases with increasing grade of polyposis
- Incidentally found mucosal thickening up to 3 mm is of no clinical significance
- In the ethmoid sinuses, 1-2 mm mucosal thickening occurs in 63% of asymptomatic patients
- The concept of an incidental Lund score has been proposed, with 0-5 considered normal
- Score of 0-2 may represent incidental mucosal thickening and does not necessarily indicate CRS
- A negative CT is useful to rule out rhinosinusitis and guide search for alternative diagnosis
- CT is the most useful and cost-effective technique for assessing sinonasal inflammatory disorders

### Companion Scoring: Lund-Kennedy Endoscopic Score

A separate system for endoscopic assessment of CRS (per side):

| Parameter | Scoring |
|-----------|---------|
| Polyps | 0 = absent; 1 = in middle meatus only; 2 = beyond middle meatus |
| Oedema | 0 = absent; 1 = mild; 2 = severe |
| Discharge | 0 = none; 1 = clear, thin; 2 = thick, purulent |
| Scarring | 0 = absent; 1 = mild; 2 = severe |
| Crusting | 0 = absent; 1 = mild; 2 = severe |

- Maximum score per side = 10; total maximum = 20
- Used for pre-operative assessment, post-operative follow-up, and research standardization
- OMC obstruction may be a barometer of overall disease burden in CRS with nasal polyps (CRSwNP), as increasing Lund-Mackay scoring is associated with OMC involvement overall

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. Mucociliary clearance is the primary mechanical defence of the nasal mucosa, propelling particles posteriorly towards the nasopharynx via coordinated ciliary beating in a metachronous pattern.
> 2. The cilia have a 9+2 microtubule arrangement with dynein arms containing ATPase; they beat through a two-layer mucous blanket with an effective stroke in the gel layer and recovery stroke in the sol layer.
> 3. Maxillary sinus mucus always exits through the natural ostium regardless of accessory openings; presence of both natural and accessory ostia causes mucous recirculation.
> 4. The saccharin test measures mucociliary clearance time (normal 10-15 minutes); ciliary function ceases below 10 degrees C, above 45 degrees C, or in dry conditions.
> 5. SARS-CoV-2 causes anosmia primarily through damage to sustentacular cells expressing ACE2 receptors, with additional olfactory cleft inflammation causing conductive loss.
> 6. Olfactory retraining therapy exploits neuroplasticity through repeated exposure to four primary odour categories, twice daily for a minimum of 12 weeks.
> 7. The nasal cycle is an alternating congestion-decongestion phenomenon occurring every 4-12 hours, controlled by the autonomic nervous system, demonstrable in 80% of adults.
> 8. Paranasal sinuses produce high concentrations of nitric oxide with antimicrobial and mucociliary-enhancing properties, and nasal secretions contain IgA constituting 70% of total proteins.
> 9. The Lund-Mackay CT scoring system grades each sinus 0-2 bilaterally (OMC as 0 or 2 only) with a maximum total score of 24, and scores of 0-5 may be considered incidental.
> 10. Prognosis in post-viral anosmia depends on severity; only 11% of anosmic patients regain normal function, making early olfactory testing and retraining essential.

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - Parosmia during Covid recovery represents aberrant olfactory neuron reinnervation and is a sign of regeneration, not deterioration; patients should be reassured and encouraged to continue olfactory retraining.
> - Recirculation of mucus between natural and accessory maxillary ostia explains the failure of inferior meatal antrostomy; endoscopic surgery must address the natural ostium.
> - Primary ciliary dyskinesia should be suspected in any patient with sinusitis, bronchiectasis, and otitis media; Kartagener syndrome adds situs inversus to this triad.
> - Systemic steroids reverse conductive olfactory loss in 83% of patients compared to only 25% with topical steroids; the head-down Moffett's position improves topical delivery to the olfactory cleft.
> - Incidental mucosal thickening on CT (Lund-Mackay score 0-5) is common in asymptomatic individuals and should not be over-interpreted as CRS without correlating clinical symptoms and endoscopy.
> - The olfactory neuron is unique in its capacity for lifelong regeneration from globose basal cells, providing the biological basis for recovery from post-viral anosmia and the rationale for olfactory retraining therapy.
