# Nasal Cycle and TESPAL (functions of the Nose)

> [!question] Questions
> A) Discuss the nasal cycle. B) Describe TESPAL (functions of the nose).

**Source:** Scott-Brown's Otorhinolaryngology, 8th Edition - Volume 1, Chapter 89 (Physiology of the Nose and Paranasal Sinuses) & Chapter 90 (Measurement of the Nasal Airway)

---

## INTRODUCTION

The nose is a complex organ with multiple physiological functions beyond simple respiration. It contributes up to 50% of the total airway resistance, and its nasal mucosa has a surface area of 150 cm² with an extremely rich vascular bed. The nasal cycle is a fundamental physiological phenomenon that maintains optimal nasal function. TESPAL is a mnemonic summarizing the key physiological functions of the nose.

---

## PART A: THE NASAL CYCLE

### Definition

The nasal cycle is a well-recognized physiological activity whereby each side of the nose alternates the phases of congestion and decongestion. The changes are cyclical and occur every **4 to 12 hours**, with the pattern being constant for each individual.

### Physiology

The nasal cycle is produced by **vascular activity** — specifically changes in the volume of blood in **capacitance vessels (venous sinusoids)**. Two mechanisms influence nasal mucosal vessel capacitance:

1. **Hydrostatic pressure of blood:** Changes in filling pressure affect venous sinusoid expansion; increase in pressure expands the sinusoids
2. **Autonomic nervous system:** Controls the activity of smooth muscle in walls of arteriovenous anastomoses, leading to changes in nasal blood flow

### Autonomic Control

| System | Origin | Neurotransmitter | Effect on Nose |
|--------|--------|-----------------|---------------|
| Parasympathetic | Midbrain → CN VII → pterygopalatine ganglion | Acetylcholine (secretion); VIP (vasodilation) | Vasodilation, increased secretions, congestion |
| Sympathetic | Thoracic spinal nerves → cervical sympathetic ganglion → alongside ICA | Noradrenaline | Vasoconstriction, reduced secretions, decongestion |

- Venous pressure has a greater impact on nasal airway resistance, with nasal resistance increasing by **1.2-1.3% per mmHg** of venous pressure increase
- Arteries and arterioles cause **resistance**; venules and sinusoids give **capacitance**

### Demonstration and Prevalence

- The nasal cycle can be demonstrated in up to **80% of adults**
- Not easy to demonstrate in children
- Unilateral resistance varies up to **4-fold** during the nasal cycle (0.36-1.36 Pa/cm³/s)
- **Total nasal resistance remains stable** due to the reciprocal relationship between the two sides

### Factors Affecting the Nasal Cycle

| Factor | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| Exercise | Reduces nasal resistance (sympathetic activation, ala nasi contraction) |
| Pregnancy | Increased nasal congestion (oestrogen effect on histamine receptors) |
| Hormones (puberty) | Affect nasal mucosa; contribute to mucosal engorgement |
| Infections | Disrupt the normal cyclical pattern |
| Allergy | Increases baseline congestion bilaterally |
| Fear, emotions, sexual activity | Alter autonomic tone |
| Vagal over-activity | Causes nasal congestion |
| High CO₂ (rebreathing) | Reduces nasal resistance |
| Hyperventilation | Increases nasal resistance |
| Beta-blockers | Block sympathetic action, increase congestion |
| Antihistamines | Anti-cholinergic effect → halt parasympathetic activity → increased sympathetic tone → improved airway |
| Topical decongestants | Eliminate physiological variation; reveal fixed anatomical component |

### Physiological Significance

- The physiological significance of the nasal cycle is not completely clear
- Resistance and airflow leads to increased cyclical secretions in the side experiencing the greatest flow
- It may allow resting and recovery of the nasal mucosa on the congested side while the other side functions actively
- Topical decongestant or exercise eliminates physiological variation and allows assessment of purely anatomical factors (bone and cartilage)

## PART B: TESPAL — FUNCTIONS OF THE NOSE

TESPAL is a mnemonic summarizing the principal physiological functions of the nose:

- **T** — Temperature regulation (heat exchange)
- **E** — Esthesis (olfaction/sense of smell)
- **S** — Speech resonance (vocal resonance)
- **P** — Purification (filtration and defence)
- **A** — Airway resistance (regulation of respiratory function)
- **L** — Lacrimation/humidification (moistening inspired air)

### T — Temperature Regulation (Heat Exchange)

- The temperature of inspired air can range from −50°C to 50°C
- The nose warms and conditions inspired air to body temperature and full humidity before it reaches the lower airways
- The rich vascular bed (arteriovenous anastomoses and venous sinusoids) serves as a counter-current heat exchanger
- Different racial groups have evolved nasal anatomies suited to different environments
- During expiration, heat and moisture are recovered from expired air

### E — Esthesis (Olfaction)

- Sense of smell plays an important role in protection from danger (fire, toxic substances), forming memories, psychological wellbeing, socialization and interpersonal relationships
- Olfactory dysfunction leads to poor quality of life
- Only **10-15%** of inspired air reaches the olfactory neuroepithelium
- Turbulent airflow pattern in the nasal cavity facilitates deposition of odorant molecules onto the olfactory epithelium
- The trigeminal nerve (CN V) mediates irritation, burning, cooling and tickling sensations; induces protective reflexes (sneezing, lacrimation)

### S — Speech Resonance

- The paranasal sinuses and nasal cavity serve as resonating chambers
- Modification of vocal quality during nasal obstruction (hyponasal/denasal speech)
- Excessive nasal patency (atrophic rhinitis) causes hypernasal speech

### P — Purification (Filtration and Defence)

**Mechanical defence:**
- The turbinates organize airflow, enabling the nose to expose all inspired air to the mucosal surface
- Particles of approximately **30 µm** or larger are filtered by nasal hairs (vibrissae)
- Mucociliary clearance is the main mechanical defence
- Two-layer mucus blanket: deeper aqueous layer (ciliary motion) + superficial viscous gel layer (traps particles)
- Cilia beat at **7-16 Hz** at body temperature
- Mucus flows posteriorly toward nasopharynx where it is swallowed

**Immunological defence:**
- IgA constitutes **70%** of total proteins in nasal secretions
- IgA has two subgroups: IgA1 (monomeric, predominant in serum) and IgA2 (dimer, predominant in nasal secretions)
- IgE found on mucosal surface for immune responses
- Cell-mediated immune responses activated when mucosa is breached

### A — Airway Resistance

Nasal resistance contributes up to **50%** of total airway resistance and plays a crucial role in preventing collapse of the lower respiratory tract.

**Three key components of nasal airway resistance:**

| Component | Mechanism |
|-----------|-----------|
| Nasal vestibule | Compliant wall; collapses at 30 L/min inspiratory flow; ala nasi contraction prevents collapse |
| Internal nasal valve | Narrowest part of nasal airway; cross-sectional area 55-83 mm²; bound by upper lateral cartilage, septum, nasal floor, piriform aperture, head of inferior turbinate |
| Inferior turbinate | Erectile tissue that swells and recedes; even 1-2 mm enlargement causes significant reduction in airflow velocity (0.89 → 0.42 m/s) |

**Nasal aerodynamics:**
- At rest: normal inspiratory flow rate 5-12 L/min with pressure change of 50 Pa
- During exercise: flow rate increases up to 150 L/min
- Laminar flow at nasal vestibule → turbulent flow at nasal valve (critical for air conditioning)
- **Cottle maneuver:** Lateralizing soft tissue of nasal vestibule; positive if improves airflow

**Effects of nasal resistance:**
- Increased nasal resistance → increased negative intrathoracic pressure → enhanced pulmonary ventilation
- Decrease in nasal radius causes a **four-fold** decrease in nasal airflow (based on laminar flow equations)
- Paralysis of ala nasi ([[Facial Nerve|facial nerve palsy]]) → vestibule collapse during inspiration → increased resistance

### L — Lacrimation/Humidification

- The nose humidifies inspired air to near 100% relative humidity before it reaches the lower airways
- Mucous glands (seromucinous glands) and goblet cells provide continuous mucus secretion
- Parasympathetic stimulation (acetylcholine) induces serous secretion
- Nasolacrimal duct drains tears into the inferior meatus, contributing to nasal moisture
- Dry conditions stop ciliary action; isotonic saline preserves ciliary activity
- Cilia function optimally between pH 6.4 and pH 8.5

## PART C: EFFECT OF SMOKING AND PREGNANCY ON NASAL FUNCTION

### Smoking

- Tobacco contains hundreds of noxious chemicals that irritate the nasal lining
- Causes reduction in the number of cilia and change in mucous viscosity
- Eight hours after tobacco smoke exposure, mucociliary clearance efficiency is reduced
- Heavier smokers have more marked impact on ciliary function

### Pregnancy-Induced Rhinitis (PIR)

- Common problem with incidence of approximately **22%**
- Defined as nasal congestion not present before pregnancy, manifesting in the 2nd or 3rd trimester, lasting ≥6 weeks with no known allergic cause, resolving completely within 2 weeks of delivery
- Decreased mucociliary clearance and increased nasal resistance during pregnancy
- Oestrogen has a possible role in inducing histamine receptors on epithelial cells

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. The nasal cycle alternates congestion/decongestion every 4-12 hours; demonstrated in 80% of adults; driven by venous sinusoid capacitance changes
> 2. Unilateral resistance varies up to 4-fold with the nasal cycle; total nasal resistance remains stable due to reciprocal relationship
> 3. Autonomic control: sympathetic (noradrenaline → vasoconstriction); parasympathetic (acetylcholine/VIP → vasodilation/secretion)
> 4. TESPAL: Temperature, Esthesis, Speech, Purification, Airway resistance, Lacrimation/humidification
> 5. Nose contributes up to 50% of total airway resistance; three components: vestibule, nasal valve, inferior turbinate
> 6. Internal nasal valve is the narrowest part of the nasal airway (55-83 mm² cross-section); most turbulent airflow occurs here
> 7. Even 1-2 mm enlargement of the inferior turbinate significantly reduces airflow velocity from 0.89 to 0.42 m/s
> 8. IgA constitutes 70% of total proteins in nasal secretions; IgA2 (dimer) predominates in nasal secretions
> 9. Cilia beat at 7-16 Hz at body temperature; stop at temperatures below 10°C and above 45°C
> 10. Pregnancy-induced rhinitis: 22% incidence; resolves within 2 weeks of delivery; oestrogen-mediated histamine receptor induction


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - Topical decongestants eliminate physiological nasal cycle variation — applying them before rhinomanometry reveals the fixed anatomical component of resistance
> - The Cottle maneuver is a simple bedside test for nasal valve collapse: lateralizing vestibular soft tissue is positive if it improves airflow
> - Facial nerve palsy causes ala nasi paralysis → nasal vestibule collapse during inspiration → increased nasal resistance
> - Venous pressure has greater impact on nasal resistance than arterial pressure (1.2-1.3% increase per mmHg venous pressure)
> - A four-fold decrease in nasal radius causes a four-fold decrease in nasal airflow — explains why small mucosal swelling causes disproportionate obstruction
