# Sentinel Lymph Node Biopsy in Head and Neck Cancer

> [!question] Questions Covered
> - "Describe sentinel lymph node biopsy — technology, technique, indications, and applications in head and neck cancer. Discuss the role of SLNB in the management of the N0 neck in oral cavity SCC."
*Source:** Scott-Brown's Volume 3, Chapters 18 (Metastatic Neck Disease), 26 (Pathology of Head and Neck Malignancies), 94 (Benign and Malignant Conditions of the Skin — Melanoma)

---

## Introduction

Sentinel lymph node biopsy (SLNB or SNB) is a diagnostic technique used to assess the presence of occult metastatic disease in the clinically N0 neck. The concept is based on the principle that identification of the echelon (first-draining) lymph nodes and assessing them for metastatic spread will provide information regarding the status of the rest of the neck. If the sentinel nodes are negative, the rest of the neck is unlikely to harbour metastases, thereby avoiding the morbidity of elective [[Neck Dissection|neck dissection]]. While the technique is considered standard for melanoma and breast cancer, its application in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma has been evolving, with NICE guidelines now recommending it for T1/T2 N0 oral cavity SCC.

---

## Part A — Concept and Rationale

### The Sentinel Node Concept

The sentinel lymph node is the first node in the lymphatic drainage basin to which a primary tumour drains. The concept was originally described for melanoma and is based on the observation that lymphatic metastasis is an orderly, sequential process — tumour cells spread first to the sentinel node(s) before involving other nodes. If the sentinel node is free of tumour, the rest of the lymph node basin is very likely to be free as well.

### Rationale in Head and Neck Cancer

In the N0 neck, the risk of occult metastasis is 20–30% for oral cavity SCC, and the general consensus is to treat the neck electively when the risk exceeds 15–20%. Traditional management involves either elective neck dissection (END) or elective neck irradiation. However, these treat 70–80% of patients unnecessarily, exposing them to the morbidity of surgery or radiation without therapeutic benefit. SLNB aims to identify the 20–30% who truly have occult disease and need treatment, while sparing the majority from unnecessary intervention.

## Part B — Technology and Technique

### Tracer Injection

Prior to surgery, four sites around the tumour are injected with a **radioactive-labelled colloid tracer** (typically 99mTc-labelled nanocolloid). The tracer enters the lymphatic system and drains to the first echelon lymph nodes. In some protocols, a **blue dye** (patent blue V or isosulfan blue) is also injected as a visual tracer — this is the **dual-tracer technique**.

### Lymphoscintigraphy

Following tracer injection, **gamma camera imaging** (lymphoscintigraphy) is performed to map the radioactive lymph nodes. This identifies the number, location, and level of sentinel nodes. SPECT-CT may be combined for more accurate anatomical localization, particularly useful in the complex head and neck lymphatic drainage.

### Intraoperative Detection

During surgery, the surgeon uses a **hand-held gamma radiation detection probe** to confirm the location of sentinel lymph nodes transcutaneously. The probe guides the surgeon to the "hot" nodes, which are then harvested through a small incision in the neck. Up to **four sentinel nodes** may be found in each case.

### Pathological Processing

The harvested sentinel nodes undergo a much more comprehensive analysis than routine histopathology:

| Feature | Routine Histopathology | SNB Histopathology |
|---------|----------------------|-------------------|
| Sectioning | 1–2 representative levels | Step-serial at **125 micrometer** intervals |
| Staining | H&E only | H&E + **immunohistochemistry** |
| IHC markers | Not routinely used | Pan-cytokeratin (AE1/AE3) |
| Sensitivity | Standard | Enhanced — detects micrometastases |

This intensive processing samples the entire lymph node, as missing a small deposit could result in a wrong decision to not treat the neck.

## Part C — Indications and Patient Selection

### Primary Indications in Head and Neck

| Indication | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| Oral cavity SCC | T1/T2 cN0 — **NICE-recommended** |
| Cutaneous melanoma | Breslow >1 mm, stage 1B and above |
| Oropharyngeal SCC | Emerging role — evidence growing |
| Laryngeal SCC | Technically difficult — tracer injection around laryngeal tumours is cumbersome |

### NICE Recommendations (2016)

Based on a systematic review and an original health economic model, NICE guidelines made two explicit recommendations for T1/T2 N0 oral cavity cancer:

1. **Surgical management of the neck should be offered to all patients**
2. **Sentinel lymph node biopsy should be offered instead of END** unless cervical access is needed at the same time (e.g., for free-flap reconstruction requiring recipient vessels)

### Contraindications

- Clinically or radiologically positive neck (N+ disease)
- Prior neck surgery or radiation (alters lymphatic drainage)
- Need for cervical access for flap reconstruction (END preferred)
- Tumours where adequate tracer injection is difficult (larynx)

## Part D — Evidence and Outcomes

### Sensitivity and Predictive Value

A **pooled meta-analysis** of non-randomized trials demonstrated that SNB has a **sensitivity of 93%** for oral cavity cancer. This means approximately 7% of patients with a negative SNB may have false-negative results (occult disease missed).

A negative sentinel lymph node biopsy has a **high negative predictive value of 95%** — the neck can be safely monitored, avoiding elective neck dissection.

### Comparison with Elective Neck Dissection

| Parameter | END | SLNB |
|-----------|-----|------|
| Morbidity | Higher (shoulder, nerve) | Lower — small incision |
| Staging information | Full specimen analysis | Intensive node analysis |
| Over-treatment | 70–80% treated unnecessarily | Avoids unnecessary treatment |
| Sensitivity | Gold standard | 93% (meta-analysis) |
| False negative | Not applicable | ~7% |
| NICE recommendation | Standard if SLNB unavailable | Preferred for T1/T2 N0 oral cavity |

### Positive SLNB

A positive sentinel lymph node requires a **completion neck dissection**, which is performed at a second operation. This selectively treats only those patients with confirmed occult metastasis.

## Part E — Challenges in Head and Neck

### Specific Concerns

Unlike melanoma and breast cancer where SLNB is well-established, head and neck application raises specific concerns:

- **Variable lymphatic drainage:** The head and neck has complex, sometimes unpredictable lymphatic channels with potential for skip metastases and collateral pathways
- **Operator dependence:** Recognized learning curve — surgeon experience significantly affects outcomes
- **Facial nerve risk:** Assessment of parotid sentinel nodes carries an inherent risk of facial nerve damage
- **Violation of oncologic planes:** The harvesting procedure may violate tissue planes that could complicate subsequent completion dissection
- **Site limitations:** Tracer injection around laryngeal tumours is technically cumbersome; the technique works best for accessible oral cavity tumours

### SLNB in Melanoma (Head and Neck)

For cutaneous melanoma of the head and neck, SLNB is offered for stage 1B disease or above. The sentinel lymph node is detected using dual-tracer technique (blue dye + radioactive tracer). The procedure has established **prognostic significance** but to date there has been no proven impact on melanoma-specific survival. If a sentinel node is involved, completion lymphadenectomy of levels I–V is performed, with superficial [[Parotid Gland and Salivary Tumours|parotidectomy]] if parotid nodes are involved.

### Emerging Applications

- **Non-oral cavity HNSCC:** Oropharyngeal and other sites are under active investigation
- **SPECT-CT fusion:** Improves anatomical localization of sentinel nodes
- **ICG fluorescence:** Near-infrared fluorescence with indocyanine green is emerging as an additional real-time intraoperative detection method
- **One-step nucleic acid amplification (OSNA):** Rapid intraoperative molecular assessment of sentinel nodes being investigated

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. Sentinel node biopsy is a diagnostic technique for detecting occult metastatic disease in the clinically N0 neck
> 2. NICE guidelines recommend SLNB for T1/T2 N0 oral cavity SCC instead of elective neck dissection (unless cervical access is needed for reconstruction)
> 3. Technique involves injection of 99mTc-labelled nanocolloid ± blue dye around the tumour, gamma camera imaging, and intraoperative hand-held gamma probe detection
> 4. Up to four sentinel nodes may be identified and harvested through a small neck incision
> 5. Sentinel nodes undergo step-serial sectioning at 125 micrometer intervals with immunohistochemistry (pan-cytokeratin AE1/AE3)
> 6. Pooled meta-analysis demonstrates SNB sensitivity of 93% for oral cavity cancer
> 7. Negative predictive value is 95% — the neck can be monitored without further treatment
> 8. A positive sentinel node requires completion neck dissection at a second operation
> 9. Head and neck SLNB challenges include variable lymphatic drainage, skip metastases, learning curve, and facial nerve risk with parotid nodes
> 10. For melanoma, SLNB is offered for stage 1B and above — has prognostic significance but no proven survival benefit


> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - **SLNB avoids over-treatment** — 70–80% of patients with N0 neck do not have occult metastasis and are spared unnecessary neck dissection
> - **Dual-tracer technique** (radioactive + blue dye) improves sentinel node identification rates
> - **Step-serial sectioning at 125 micrometer intervals** is essential — routine histopathology may miss micrometastases
> - **Learning curve is recognized** — surgeon experience significantly affects outcomes; centres should have adequate training and volume
> - **If free-flap reconstruction is planned**, END is preferred over SLNB as cervical access is already needed for recipient vessels
> - **SPECT-CT** improves localization and is particularly useful in the complex head and neck lymphatic anatomy
