# Surgical Approaches to Oropharyngeal Malignancy

> [!question] Questions Covered
> - Discuss the surgical approaches to oropharyngeal malignancy. Describe mandibular swing osteotomy. Discuss transmandibular and mandible-sparing approaches for oropharyngeal malignancy.
*Source:** Scott-Brown's Volume 3, Chapter 12 (Oral Cavity Tumours — Surgical Access) and Chapter 13 (Oropharyngeal Tumours — Transoral Surgery)

---

## Introduction

Surgical access to oropharyngeal tumours may be achieved through several routes: transorally, anteriorly via mandibulotomy, inferiorly and laterally from the neck, or via the upper cheek flap for maxillary extensions. The route chosen is determined by the anatomical site, nature and extent of the pathology, and the surgical aim. For the majority of oral and posterior oral tumours, the ultimate aim is removal of the tumour with adequate margins. While per oral resection with good retraction and lighting suffices for many anterior tumours, patients with trismus, microstomia, large or posteriorly based tumours may require additional access procedures. The recent resurgent interest in transoral surgery (TLM and TORS) has added minimally invasive options for the management of T1–T3 oropharyngeal tumours, while open approaches remain relevant for advanced or extensive disease.

## PART A: Classification of Surgical Approaches

### Overview

| Approach | Access Route | Best Suited For |
|---|---|---|
| Per oral (transoral) | Through the mouth | Small anterior tumours |
| TLM (transoral laser) | Endoscopic, CO2 laser | T1–T3 tonsil and BOT |
| TORS (transoral robotic) | Da Vinci robot | T1–T3 tonsil and BOT |
| Mandibulotomy (lip split) | Paramedian osteotomy | Large/posterior oral and oropharyngeal tumours |
| Visor approach | Cervical incision, lingual release | Bilateral [[Neck Dissection|neck dissection]]s; recurrence after RT |
| Upper cheek flap | Weber-Ferguson incision | Maxillary tumour extensions |
| Lateral pharyngotomy | Through the neck | Selected lateral oropharyngeal tumours |

## PART B: Transmandibular Approach — Mandibulotomy (Mandibular Swing)

### Indications

- Large or posteriorly located oral cavity and oropharyngeal tumours where per oral resection cannot achieve adequate margins
- Tumours requiring wide access for safe resection and reconstruction
- Access to the [[Parapharyngeal Space|parapharyngeal space]], infratemporal fossa or posterior oropharynx

### Contraindications

Bone resection as a method of improving access (as opposed to oncological resection) is no longer acceptable. Mandibulotomy is the preferred access procedure.

### Pre-operative Planning

An orthopantomogram (OPG) is required to demonstrate the dental anatomy of the area, specifically the root positions of the lateral incisor and canine teeth.

### Technique

**Lip split incision:**
- The neck dissection incision is extended anteriorly to split the lip
- A vertical incision over the chin point produces a more cosmetic result than an incision around the chin prominence (which makes it more pronounced)
- A chevron between the chin prominence and vermilion aids closure and breaks up the scar
- Temporary tattoos are created with needle and ink at the vermilion border to ensure accurate reapproximation

**Osteotomy site:**
- The osteotomy should be conducted anterior to the mental foramen to preserve labial sensation
- The preferred site is the **paramedian area** (between the lateral incisor and canine) — the distance between tooth roots is greater at this site, reducing dental complications
- If the distance between lateral incisor and canine roots is insufficient, the lateral incisor should be extracted and the osteotomy conducted through the socket — this minimizes osteoradionecrosis risk if a tooth is damaged

**Osteotomy technique:**
1. Miniplates are adapted and applied to the mandible bridging the proposed osteotomy site
2. The plates are then removed
3. The osteotomy is conducted using a fine reciprocating saw blade
4. Pre-application of plates ensures maintenance of occlusion at the end of the procedure
5. A modified technique where only the buccal cortex is cut with a saw and the lingual plate fractured with a small osteotome ensures bone-to-bone contact and may lead to better osseous healing

**Muscle management:**
- The paramedian osteotomy preserves the attachments of geniohyoid, genioglossus and digastric to the major segment
- Only mylohyoid requires division to mobilize the minor segment

**Access gained:** The mandible and cheek flap may be retracted laterally and superiorly, giving excellent access to the posterior oral cavity and oropharynx.

### Complications

- The non-anatomical position adopted by the TMJ during the procedure does not give rise to long-term TMJ dysfunction
- Patients have minimal concerns regarding the lip-split scar
- Osteoradionecrosis risk if dental roots are damaged during osteotomy

## PART C: Mandible-Sparing Approaches

### 1. Transoral Approach (Per Oral)

The simplest mandible-sparing approach. Adequate for the majority of oral tumours with good retraction and lighting. Limited by mouth opening, tumour location and size.

### 2. Transoral Laser Microsurgery (TLM)

Conducted according to the principles of Steiner and Ambrosch:

- Tumours are removed in several (at least two) planned pieces following **transtumoral resection**
- Transtumoral resection eases technical difficulty (especially through a distending endoscope for BOT tumours) and facilitates greater 3D orientation
- Representative **marginal biopsies** are taken from the tumour bed — up to 8 for a standard tonsil tumour (superior, anterosuperior, posterosuperior, superior deep, inferior, anteroinferior, posteroinferior, inferior deep)
- If all marginal biopsies are clear, resection is considered complete
- Re-resection is undertaken if marginal biopsies contain microscopic disease

**Outcomes:** 3-year OS ~84.5%, disease-specific survival ~91.7% (Liverpool series, 153 patients, 66% HPV+)

**Contraindications for TLM in oropharynx:**
- [[Oral Submucous Fibrosis|Oral submucous fibrosis]] (limiting mouth opening)
- Tumours with significant parapharyngeal extension

### 3. Transoral Robotic Surgery (TORS)

Uses the da Vinci surgical system for en bloc tumour removal:

- In contrast to TLM, TORS employs a standard procedure with **en bloc resection** adhering to principles of traditional surgery
- Surgical margins are measured using standard pathology reporting protocols
- The neck dissection is usually performed as a **staged procedure** 2 weeks before primary resection to reduce pharyngocutaneous fistula risk
- During neck dissection, the ipsilateral lingual and facial arteries are identified and ligated to reduce local blood pressure and risk of major haemorrhage

**Tonsil tumours:** The resection is essentially a lateral oropharyngectomy — constrictor muscles are removed with the specimen. Resectability is confirmed on imaging.

**BOT tumours:** Tumour extending across the midline is a contraindication.

**Contraindications for TORS:**
- Parapharyngeal space extension
- Pterygoid musculature involvement
- Retropharyngeal location of internal carotid artery

**Outcomes:** 2-year locoregional control 91.8%, disease-specific survival 94.5%, OS 91% (multicentre study, 410 patients)

**Acute complications:** Haemorrhage (2.4%), fistula (2.5%), temporary [[Tracheostomy|tracheostomy]] (12%)

### 4. Visor Approach and Lingual Release

- Reserved for bilateral neck dissections or recurrence/second primary tumours in previously irradiated patients
- Avoids facial incisions — the incision is in a cervical skin crease (mastoid to mastoid)
- Visor flap elevated in the subplatysmal plane to the lower border of the mandible
- An intraoral mucosal incision in the lingual gingival sulcus from posterior molar to posterior molar
- Mylohyoid, geniohyoid, genioglossus and digastric muscles are detached from the lingual mandible, allowing the floor of mouth and tongue to 'drop' into the neck
- Care must be taken to avoid injury to lingual and hypoglossal nerves
- Reattachment of geniohyoid and genioglossus at closure is important — facilitated by drilling holes in the lower mandibular border or a small box osteotomy incorporating the muscle attachments

**Disadvantages:** Excellent access is achieved without a lip split, but the expected cosmetic advantage has not been proven and the technique is associated with significantly greater functional deficit compared to lip-split mandibulotomy.

### 5. Lateral Pharyngotomy

- Access through the neck lateral to the pharynx
- Used for selected lateral oropharyngeal tumours
- Combined with neck dissection in continuity

### 6. Upper Cheek Flap (Weber-Ferguson)

- Required for large or posterior maxillary tumour extensions
- Upper lip split extending into a lateral rhinotomy incision
- The lip split is conducted along the ipsilateral philtral column to the base of the columella
- The incision extends laterally along the floor of the nose, follows the alar crease, then continues to a point 6–8 mm medial to the medial canthus
- Cornea must be protected in all patients
- Cheek flap raised in the subperiosteal plane, preserving the infraorbital nerve unless oncologically necessary to sacrifice it
- Extensions: Diffenbach extension (subciliary incision) or Lynch extension (medial canthal incision) for greater access

## PART D: Comparison of Approaches

| Feature | Mandibulotomy | TLM | TORS | Visor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Access quality | Excellent | Good (limited by scope) | Good (3D robotic arms) | Excellent |
| Lip split scar | Yes | No | No | No |
| Bone cutting | Paramedian osteotomy | None | None | None |
| Resection method | Open, en bloc | Transtumoral, piecemeal | En bloc | Open |
| Margin assessment | Standard pathology | Marginal biopsies | Standard pathology | Standard pathology |
| Functional deficit | Moderate | Least | Low | Greatest |
| Best indication | Large/posterior tumours | T1–T3 OPSCC | T1–T3 OPSCC | Bilateral necks |

---

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. Mandibulotomy (mandibular swing) is the preferred access procedure for large or posteriorly located oral cavity and oropharyngeal tumours — bone resection for access alone is no longer acceptable
> 2. The paramedian osteotomy site (between lateral incisor and canine) is preferred because root distance is greatest and it preserves geniohyoid, genioglossus and digastric attachments
> 3. Miniplates should be adapted and applied before osteotomy to ensure accurate occlusal realignment at closure
> 4. TLM employs transtumoral resection with marginal biopsies (Steiner and Ambrosch principles) — TORS uses en bloc resection with standard pathological margin assessment
> 5. TORS neck dissection is typically staged 2 weeks before primary resection to reduce pharyngocutaneous fistula risk
> 6. The visor approach avoids facial scars but produces significantly greater functional deficit than mandibulotomy — reserved for bilateral neck dissections or recurrence after prior RT
> 7. The upper cheek flap (Weber-Ferguson) provides access to maxillary extensions — the incision follows the philtral column, nasal floor and alar crease to respect aesthetic facial units
> 8. TORS contraindications include parapharyngeal space extension, pterygoid involvement and retropharyngeal internal carotid artery


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - When performing a mandibulotomy, temporary tattoos at the vermilion border are essential for accurate lip reapproximation — this simple step significantly improves cosmetic outcome
> - If the lateral incisor and canine roots are too close together, extract the lateral incisor and osteotomize through the socket rather than risk root damage and subsequent osteoradionecrosis
> - The combination of mandibulotomy and posterior marginal mandibulectomy for large soft tissue lesions should be avoided — the increased risk of osteoradionecrosis warrants a segmental resection
> - During TLM of a tonsil tumour, up to 8 marginal biopsies should be taken — the deep bed biopsies are the most important as they represent the deep margin which is most commonly positive
> - Oral submucous fibrosis (OSMF) is a listed contraindication for transoral approaches due to restricted mouth opening — mandibulotomy or visor approach may be required
