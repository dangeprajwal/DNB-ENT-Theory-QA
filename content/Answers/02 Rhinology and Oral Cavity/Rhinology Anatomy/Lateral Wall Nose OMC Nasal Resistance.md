# Anatomy of the Lateral Wall of the Nose, OMC and Nasal Resistance

> [!question] Questions
> A) Describe the anatomy of the lateral wall of the nose. Add a note on the ostiomeatal complex (OMC). B) Discuss nasal resistance.

**Source:** Scott-Brown's Otorhinolaryngology, 8th Edition - Volume 1, Chapter 87 (Anatomy of the Nose and Paranasal Sinuses) & Chapter 90 (Measurement of the Nasal Airway)

---

## INTRODUCTION

The lateral nasal wall is the most surgically important region of the nasal cavity. It contains the turbinates, meatuses, and the drainage pathways of the paranasal sinuses. Understanding its anatomy is fundamental to endoscopic sinus surgery. The concept of the ostiomeatal complex as a key area in the pathogenesis of rhinosinusitis has driven the development of [[FESS Steps Complications|functional endoscopic sinus surgery]] (FESS).

---

## PART A: ANATOMY OF THE LATERAL NASAL WALL

### Bony Framework

The lateral nasal wall is formed by contributions from several bones:
- Maxilla (anteroinferiorly)
- Lacrimal bone (anterosuperiorly)
- Ethmoid bone (superiorly - labyrinth)
- Inferior turbinate (independent bone)
- Perpendicular plate of palatine bone (posteriorly)
- Medial pterygoid plate (posterosuperiorly)

### Turbinates

The inferior, middle and superior turbinates are internal structures found along the lateral nasal wall. The middle and superior turbinates arise from extensions of the ethmoid bone, whereas the inferior turbinate is an embryologically independent osseous structure.

**Turbinate functions:**
- Filled with vascular channels and venous sinusoids
- Warm and humidify inspired air
- Modify nasal airflow resistance
- Continuously dilate and constrict under sympathetic control
- **Nasal cycle:** Alternating congestion/decongestion every 0.5-3 hours

### Meatuses and Drainage Pathways

| Meatus | Drainage |
|--------|----------|
| Inferior meatus | Nasolacrimal duct (1 cm posterior to head of inferior turbinate; Hasner's valve) |
| Middle meatus | Maxillary sinus, anterior ethmoid sinus, frontal sinus (via OMC) |
| Superior meatus | Posterior ethmoid air cells |
| Sphenoethmoidal recess | Sphenoid sinus (medial and posterior to superior turbinate) |

### Blood Supply of the Lateral Nasal Wall

Both internal and external carotid arteries supply the lateral wall:

**Sphenopalatine artery (SPA):**
- Majority of arterial supply to turbinates and lateral wall
- Enters through the sphenopalatine foramen (just inferior to horizontal attachment of middle turbinate)
- **Crista ethmoidalis:** Small crest of perpendicular plate of palatine bone anterior to sphenopalatine foramen; consistent landmark for identifying the SPA during endoscopic dissection
- 97% of specimens have 2+ branches medial to the crista ethmoidalis

**Anterior ethmoid artery:**
- Branch of ophthalmic artery (internal carotid system)
- Traverses three compartments: orbit → ethmoid cavity → olfactory fossa → nasal cavity
- Travels obliquely posterior to anterior within skull base or mucosal mesentery
- Only 20% found within a mesentery amenable to endoscopic clipping
- External approach (modified Lynch incision) often preferred for ligation

**Other arteries:**
- Posterior ethmoid artery (superior lateral wall)
- Greater palatine artery (inferior lateral wall adjacent to palate)
- Branch of facial artery (anterior lateral wall)

### Nerve Supply

- Olfactory nerve (CN I): olfactory neuroepithelium
- Ophthalmic (V1): anterior ethmoid nerve → external nasal nerve
- Maxillary (V2): via pterygopalatine ganglion → posterior superior nasal and nasopalatine nerves
- Autonomic: sympathetic (superior cervical ganglion via deep petrosal nerve); parasympathetic (facial nerve via greater superficial petrosal nerve → vidian nerve → pterygopalatine ganglion)

## PART B: THE OSTIOMEATAL COMPLEX (OMC)

### Definition

The OMC is a functional anatomical unit in the middle meatus through which the anterior group of sinuses (maxillary, anterior ethmoid and frontal) drain into the nasal cavity. It is not a single structure but a complex of anatomical structures.

### Components of the OMC

1. **Uncinate process:** Sickle-shaped bone attaching inferiorly to inferior turbinate and palatine bone; anterosuperiorly to lacrimal bone
2. **Hiatus semilunaris:** Crescent-shaped gap between uncinate process and ethmoidal bulla
3. **Ethmoidal infundibulum:** Passage between uncinate process and medial orbital wall leading to maxillary sinus ostium
4. **Ethmoidal bulla:** Largest and most consistent anterior ethmoid air cell; attached to lamina papyracea laterally
5. **Maxillary sinus ostium:** Covered by uncinate process; cannot be viewed endoscopically unless sinus has been previously opened surgically
6. **Anterior and posterior fontanelles:** Folds of mucosa covering the maxillary sinus opening together with the uncinate process

### Functional Anatomy

**Three functional sinonasal compartments:**

| Unit | Sinuses | Drainage Pathway |
|------|---------|-----------------|
| Anterior | Maxillary, anterior ethmoid, frontal | Middle meatus (OMC) |
| Posterior | Posterior ethmoid | Superior meatus |
| Sphenoid | Sphenoid sinus | Sphenoethmoidal recess |

### Uncinate Process - Superior Attachment

- Classic teaching of a single attachment pattern is inaccurate
- **>50%** of cases have multiple attachments
- Can insert into: medial orbital wall, skull base, or middle turbinate
- Superior attachment determines the frontal sinus drainage pathway
- Frontal recess is medial to the remnant uncinate process in **85%** of cases

### Clinical Significance

- Inflammation around the OMC is central to the pathogenesis of rhinosinusitis
- Obstruction at the OMC leads to impaired mucociliary clearance and secondary infection of the anterior group of sinuses
- Accessory ostia in the fontanelle may be mistaken for the true maxillary ostium
- Failure to connect the true ostium with the surgical cavity results in **mucous recirculation** (mucous directed to natural ostium, re-entering through accessory opening)

### The Paranasal Surgical Box

Endoscopic sinus surgery is based on identification of key fixed anatomical landmarks that define the **paranasal surgical box:**

**Horizontal component:**
- Medial: middle turbinate
- Lateral: medial orbital wall (lamina papyracea)
- Superior: skull base
- Posterior: sphenoid sinus

**Vertical component (for frontal recess dissection):**
- Medial: middle turbinate and intersinus septum
- Lateral: lamina papyracea
- Anterior: nasofrontal beak
- Posterior: skull base

**Three key surgical landmarks:**
1. **Maxillary sinus roof/orbital floor** (first landmark) - provides safe working distance from skull base; always below level of skull base and sphenoid planum (mean distance 10.1 mm and 11.0 mm respectively)
2. **Medial orbital wall** (second landmark) - identified after ethmoidal bulla removal; transition from orbital floor to medial wall clearly delineated
3. **Sphenoid sinus/posterior skull base** (third landmark) - posterior skull base at its lowest within the horizontal surgical box

## PART C: NASAL RESISTANCE

### Definition

Nasal resistance to airflow is the opposition to the passage of air through the nasal passages, determined by the cross-sectional area of the nasal airway and the physical properties of airflow (laminar vs turbulent).

### Factors Determining Nasal Resistance

**Anatomical factors (fixed):**
- Cartilage and bone structure (septum, turbinates, pyriform aperture)
- Cross-sectional area of the nasal valve (narrowest point)
- Determines the "anatomical" nasal patency

**Physiological factors (variable):**
- Nasal cycle (0.5-3 hour reciprocal congestion/decongestion)
- Mucosal engorgement from venous sinusoids
- Environmental exposure (temperature, humidity)

### Physics of Nasal Airflow

- Relationship between trans-nasal pressure and flow is **curvilinear** (not linear)
- At low flow rates: predominantly laminar flow
- At higher pressures: turbulent flow causes flow limitation
- Nasal alar collapse limits flow during rapid/maximal inspiratory manoeuvres
- Single numerical value cannot describe the whole pressure-flow curve
- Resistance measured at a **standardized sample pressure point**

### Normal Values

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Mean total nasal resistance (adults) | 0.23 Pa/cm^3/s (range 0.15-0.39) |
| Upper limit of normal | 0.3 Pa/cm^3/s |
| Cole's obstruction threshold | >0.25 Pa/cm^3/s |
| Maximum (infants) | ~1.2 Pa/cm^3/s |
| Adult value achieved at | 16-18 years |
| Unilateral variation (nasal cycle) | 0.36-1.36 Pa/cm^3/s (up to 4-fold) |

### Nasal Valve

The nasal valve is the region of greatest resistance:
- **Internal nasal valve:** Angle between upper lateral cartilage and septum (10-15°); cross-sectional area ~40-60 mm^2
- **External nasal valve:** Nasal vestibule bounded by alar cartilage, columella, and nasal floor
- The inferior turbinate is the single largest contributor to nasal airway resistance

### Decongestion and Anatomical Patency

- Topical nasal decongestant or exercise eliminates physiological variation
- Allows assessment of purely anatomical factors (bone and cartilage)
- Broms provided predictive values for height and nasal resistance in the decongested nose
- Useful for assessing nasal skeletal stenosis

### Clinical Applications of Nasal Resistance Measurement

- Assessment of nasal obstruction severity
- Pre- and post-operative evaluation of septal/turbinate surgery
- Nasal provocation testing (allergy)
- Research into nasal physiology and pharmacology
- Medicolegal assessment

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. The middle and superior turbinates arise from the ethmoid; the inferior turbinate is an embryologically independent bone
> 2. OMC is a functional unit comprising uncinate, hiatus semilunaris, ethmoidal infundibulum, bulla, and maxillary ostium
> 3. Crista ethmoidalis is the key landmark for identifying the sphenopalatine artery; 97% have 2+ branches medial to it
> 4. Uncinate process has multiple superior attachments in >50% of cases; determines frontal sinus drainage pathway
> 5. Frontal recess drainage is medial to the uncinate in 85% of cases
> 6. Maxillary sinus roof/orbital floor is always below skull base (mean 10.1 mm) - staying below this level avoids skull base injury
> 7. Three key surgical landmarks: orbital floor, medial orbital wall, sphenoid/posterior skull base
> 8. Nasal cycle causes up to 4-fold variation in unilateral resistance; total resistance is more stable
> 9. Mucous recirculation occurs when the true maxillary ostium is not connected with the surgical cavity
> 10. Nasal valve is the region of greatest resistance; inferior turbinate is the single largest contributor


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - The anterior ethmoid artery is only found within an accessible mesentery in 20% of cases - external ligation via Lynch incision is often the preferred approach
> - The transition point from orbital floor to medial orbital wall is a critical surgical landmark during ethmoidectomy
> - Decongestion before rhinomanometry eliminates physiological variation and reveals the fixed anatomical component of resistance
> - One cannot simply determine nasal resistance from the slope of the pressure-flow graph due to the curvilinear relationship
> - Posterior ethmoid and sphenoid compartments have completely separate drainage pathways from the anterior group despite sharing a common name
