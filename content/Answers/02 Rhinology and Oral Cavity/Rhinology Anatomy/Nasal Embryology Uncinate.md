> - ## PART C: SURGICAL ANATOMY OF THE UNCINATE PROCESS
> - that is the first structure removed during endoscopic sinus surgery. Its attachments are as follows:
> - Inferior attachment:** To the inferior turbinate and palatine bone
> - Anterosuperior attachment:** To the lacrimal bone
> - Posterosuperior attachment:** VARIABLE (discussed below)
> - rather than a single attachment pattern. Classic teaching that describes three distinct attachments of the uncinate process which determines the direction of the frontal sinus drainage pathway is **neither surgically relevant nor accurate**.
> - The uncinate process inserts onto the **medial orbital wall in 85% of cases
> - Thus, the frontal recess drainage pathway is **medial to the uncinate process in 85%** of cases
> - An uncinate process with an isolated attachment to either the skull base or middle turbinate (without attachment to the medial orbital wall) occurs in **only 15%** of cases
> - This 15% attachment pattern leads to a surgically obvious frontal drainage pathway located **lateral to the uncinate** that is easily identified at the time of surgery
> - The surgical rule: the frontal recess is medial to the remnant uncinate process or 'vertical bar' in 85% of cases, with other attachments representing easy surgical arrangements

> - CROSS-SECTIONAL VIEW
> - The true maxillary ostial opening is **covered by the uncinate process** and cannot be viewed endoscopically in a sinus cavity that has not been previously opened surgically
> - The uncinate, together with a fold of mucosa called the **anterior and posterior fontanelle**, covers the opening to the maxillary sinus
> - Accessory ostia** may be present in the fontanelle that can be mistaken for the true maxillary ostium
> - Failure to correctly identify the true ostium and connect it with the common sinus cavity results in **mucous recirculation** (mucous directed towards the natural opening along the mucociliary drainage pathway re-enters the sinus through the accessory ostium)

> - ## PART E: INTRAOPERATIVE CSF LEAK DURING FESS - RECOGNITION AND MANAGEMENT
> - Lateral lamella of the cribriform plate** (thinnest point of the anterior skull base)
> - Anterior skull base** where weakened by the anterior ethmoid neurovascular bundle
> - Posterior ethmoid** where there may be confusion regarding the relationship between the last posterior ethmoid cell and the sphenoid sinus
> - Intraoperative signs:** Clear watery fluid in the surgical field that does not match the expected bleeding pattern; may see pulsatile flow
> - Posturally provoked:** Leaning forward may increase drainage
> - Confirmatory test:** Beta-2 transferrin assay (excellent sensitivity, specificity and positive/negative predictive values); the beta-2 transferrin isoform is present only in CSF, perilymph and aqueous humour
> - Alternative test:** Beta-trace protein (considered even better than beta-2 transferrin in some European studies)
> - Intrathecal fluorescein:** Bright green coloration confirms active leak site and can confirm adequacy of repair
> - CSF leaks complicating sinus surgery diagnosed intraoperatively should be repaired under the same anaesthetic using local intranasal tissue.
> - 1. Identify the exact site of leakage
> - - **Small defect (<1 cm):** Free tissue grafts (fat, fascia) using **Bath Plug technique** (fat plug as inlay + overlay mucosal graft); technically straightforward with excellent results
> - Medium defect (1-3 cm):** **Underlay + overlay graft** with or without bone/cartilage buttress; bone or cartilage grafts are helpful for medium-sized defects to provide structural rigidity
> - Large defect (>3 cm):** **Vascularized [[Pedicled Flaps Skin Grafts|pedicled flap]]** (Hadad-Bassagasteguy nasoseptal flap based on nasoseptal artery) with rigid buttress (semirigid bioabsorbable plate in patients with elevated BMI); multilayer skull base reconstruction supported by inflated Foley catheter balloon
> - - Gelfoam placed as a 'break-layer' under the repair to isolate from shearing forces during pack removal
> - Merocel pack for support
> - Fibrin glue to reinforce closure (shown to enhance graft adherence and higher burst pressures in animal models)
> - - Head elevation
> - Lumbar drain: controversial for routine use; employed when intrathecal fluorescein is used and in high-flow or large defects
> - Avoid positive pressure mask ventilation (risk of pneumocephalus)
> - Avoid nose blowing, straining, sneezing with mouth closed, CPAP
> - Pneumococcal vaccination if antibody titres are low
> - Prophylactic antibiotics: no strong evidence base
> - - 90% success rate at initial endoscopic repair attempt
> - Up to 97% success with revision surgery
> - Spontaneous leaks have highest recurrence rate (21%, up to 46% with raised intracranial pressure)
> - > [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> - > [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - The frontal sinus is the last paranasal sinus to develop and is absent at birth; it is visible radiologically by age 8 but significant pneumatization does not occur until early adolescence, continuing until 18 years.
> - Mucous recirculation occurs when the true maxillary ostium is not connected with the surgically created antrostomy; the true ostium lies hidden behind the uncinate process and must be identified during FESS.
> - The sphenoid sinus has variable pneumatization described with reference to the sella turcica (sellar 90%, pre-sellar 9%, conchal 1%); the conchal type has trabecular bone between sinus and sella, making transphenoidal approaches more difficult.
> - The agger nasi cell is the anterior-most ethmoid cell and its degree of pneumatization influences the position of the superior uncinate process and the thickness of the nasofrontal beak; it is the key landmark for all frontal recess approaches.
> - Beta-2 transferrin is the confirmatory test for [[CSF Rhinorrhea|CSF rhinorrhoea]]; it is present only in CSF, perilymph and aqueous humour, providing excellent sensitivity and specificity for the diagnosis of CSF leaks.
> - The five ethmoturbinal lamellae (agger nasi/uncinate, bulla ethmoidalis, basal lamella, superior turbinate, supreme turbinate) are the embryological basis of all ethmoid air cells and define the sequential landmarks encountered during complete sphenoethmoidectomy.
