# Surgical Landmarks of the Pituitary Fossa

> [!question] Questions
> Describe the developmental anatomy and surgical landmarks of the pituitary fossa relevant to endoscopic transsphenoidal surgery.

**Source:** Scott-Brown's Otorhinolaryngology, 8th Edition - Volume 1, Chapter 54 (Developmental Anatomy of the Pituitary Fossa)

---

## INTRODUCTION

The pituitary gland or hypophysis cerebri is an ovoid body measuring approximately 8 mm anteroposteriorly, 12 mm transversely and 4 mm in height, weighing approximately 100-500 mg by puberty. It doubles in size during pregnancy. The adenohypophysis constitutes two-thirds of the volume. Knowledge of the detailed anatomy and surgical landmarks of the pituitary fossa is essential for the endoscopic pituitary surgeon.

---

## PART A: EMBRYOLOGY

The adenohypophysis is derived from the placodal ectoderm of the stomodeum roof. The neurohypophysis is derived from an evagination of the floor of the forebrain and the third ventricle.

A saccular depression in the roof of the stomodeum (Rathke's pouch) evaginates and remains in close contact with the ventral surface of the forebrain:

- **Anterior part** of Rathke's pouch becomes the pars anterior
- **Posterior part** (in contact with forebrain) becomes the pars intermedia
- The **forebrain evagination** becomes the neurohypophysis
- The **hypophyseal cleft** separates pars anterior from pars intermedia (vestige of Rathke's pouch)
- Remnants of Rathke's pouch may persist in the nasopharyngeal roof as a **pharyngeal pituitary**
- The **cavernous sinus** is derived from the primary head vein

**Diagram Description - Embryological Development:**
```

       |
  Infundibulum --- Pars tuberalis
       |
  Pars anterior     Pars nervosa
  Pars posterior
  Pars intermedia

```

## PART B: PITUITARY GLAND ANATOMY

### Two Major Parts

**Adenohypophysis:**
- Pars anterior, pars intermedia, pars tuberalis
- Highly vascular; epithelial cells in follicles and cords with vascular sinuses

**Neurohypophysis:**
- Pars posterior, infundibular stem, median eminence
- Axons from supraoptic and paraventricular hypothalamic nuclei

### Cell Types of the Adenohypophysis

| Cell Type | Staining | Hormone Secreted |
|-----------|----------|-----------------|
| Somatotrophs | Acidophilic | Growth hormone (GH) |
| Mammotrophs | Acidophilic | Prolactin (PRL) |
| Corticotrophs | Basophilic | ACTH (from POMC) |
| Thyrotrophs | Basophilic | TSH |
| Gonadotrophs | Basophilic | FSH and LH |
| Chromophobes | - | Stem cells/degranulated |
| Folliculostellate | - | Supporting cells |

### Blood Supply

The arterial supply is from single inferior hypophyseal arteries and several superior hypophyseal arteries on each side, all branches of the internal carotid artery:

- Superior hypophyseal arteries supply the median eminence and infundibulum
- Inferior hypophyseal arteries form an arterial ring around the infundibulum, supplying the neurohypophysis
- Adenohypophysis has no direct arterial supply; supplied by long and short portal vessels from median eminence and lower infundibulum
- Hormones secreted by exocytosis into sinusoids; secretion regulated by releasing factors from median eminence via portal system

**Neurohypophysis secretes:**
- Anti-diuretic hormone (ADH/vasopressin)
- Oxytocin

## PART C: SELLA TURCICA AND BONY LANDMARKS

The sella turcica (Turkish saddle) is a deep depression in the body of the sphenoid bone. It contains the hypophysis cerebri, dural layers and the intercavernous sinuses. The hypophysis fills 50-85% of the fossa.

### Bony Landmarks

**Diagram Description - Posterosuperior View of Sella Turcica:**
```
        Tuberculum sellae
              |
   Middle clinoid --- Carotid artery
   process              groove
        |
   Optic canal --- Anterior clinoid
                    process
        |
   Posterior clinoid --- Dorsum sellae
   process
        |
       Clivus --- Lingula
```

- **Optic canal:** Between roots of lesser wing and body of sphenoid; descends slightly anterolaterally; contains optic nerve, ophthalmic artery, meninges
- **Anterior clinoid process:** Posterior projection from lesser wing of sphenoid
- **Tuberculum sellae:** Midline ridge in the anterior slope of the sella
- **Dorsum sellae:** Posterior wall of the sella; superolateral projections form posterior clinoid processes
- **Groove for internal carotid artery:** Lateral to sella, formed as artery turns anteriorly from foramen lacerum
- **Middle clinoid process:** Small elevation in median edge of carotid groove; in ~10% of skulls, joined to anterior clinoid forming carotico-clinoid foramen
- **Lingula:** Small projection on posterolateral aspect of carotid groove
- **Clivus:** Body of sphenoid behind dorsum sellae and basioccipital bone; upper pons lies adjacent

### Rare Variants
- **Tinea interclinoidea** (~2%): Bony bridge joining anterior and posterior clinoid processes
- **Sella spine** (~1%): Bony spicule projecting from junction of anterior and inferior sella walls; represents ossified remnant of notochord

## PART D: ENDOSCOPIC ANATOMY OF THE SPHENOID AND SELLA

### Sphenoid Sinus Pneumatization (Hammer and Radberg Classification)

| Type | Frequency | Description |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| Sellar | ~80% | Well pneumatized; sella bulges into posterosuperior sphenoid |
| Pre-sellar | ~20% | Posterior wall at plane of anterior sella wall |
| Conchal | ~2% (up to 28% in Asians) | No pneumatization of sphenoid |
| Post-sella | Subgroup of sellar | Entire sella floor protrudes into sphenoid roof |

### Intra-sphenoid Sinus Septae

- Standard textbook midline septum found in only **14%** of individuals
- Single septum in 48%, two septae in 48%
- Midline posterior attachment in only 14%
- In **87% of cases**, at least one septum inserts posteriorly close to the carotid eminence
- Care needed when drilling down septae during surgical access

### Carotid Arteries and Optic Nerves (Posterior Sphenoid Wall)

In a well-pneumatized sphenoid, key landmarks visible on the posterior sphenoid wall:

1. **Pituitary fossa** - midline bulge in posterosuperior sphenoid
2. **Paraclival carotid (a)** - inferolateral to fossa
3. **Cavernous carotid (b)** - lateral to fossa; C-shaped curve as artery ascends
4. **Optic nerve (c)** - superolateral to fossa (visible in only a small proportion)

### Opticocarotid Recesses

- **Lateral opticocarotid recess (LOCR):** Bounded by optic nerve superomedially and carotid artery inferomedially; corresponds to the **anterior clinoid process** intracranially
- **Medial opticocarotid recess (MOCR):** Bounded by optic nerve superolaterally, carotid inferomedially, and pituitary fossa medially; corresponds to the **medial clinoid** intracranially

### Surgical Dimensions (Cadaveric Studies)

- Inter-optic nerve distance at carotico-optic recess: **12 mm**
- Width of pituitary fossa between carotid prominences: **21 mm**
- Height of anterior sella wall: **8 mm**
- Height of pituitary gland inside fossa: **7 mm**
- Separation of gland superior aspect to optic chiasm: **5 mm**
- Bony covering over sella: usually **<0.5 mm** (may be absent with macroadenoma)
- Dehiscence over carotid artery: up to **5% of cases**

## PART E: RELATIONS OF THE PITUITARY FOSSA

**Superior:**
- Diaphragmatica sella (dural roof)
- Pituitary stalk and infundibulum
- Optic chiasm: anterior to infundibulum, 5-10 mm above diaphragm
- Anterior cerebral artery and anterior communicating artery (anterior to optic chiasm)
- Internal carotid/middle cerebral artery (lateral to infundibulum)
- Third ventricle (above infundibulum)
- CSF surrounds above structures above the diaphragmatica sella

**Posterior:**
- Lamellar bone of the dorsum sella
- Dura mater of the posterior fossa
- Basilar artery (behind dura)

**Lateral:**
- Upper part of cavernous sinus containing:
  - Horizontal portion of internal carotid artery
  - Oculomotor (III), trochlear (IV), and abducens (VI) nerves

**Anterior and Inferior:**
- Sphenoid sinus
- Intra-sphenoid sinus septum

---

## PART F: CAVERNOUS SINUSES

The cavernous sinuses lie on the lateral aspect of the sphenoid body and are the lateral relations of the sella turcica. They are plexiform in nature - an intertwined network of thin-walled venous channels of varying sizes.

**Dimensions:** Approximately 1 cm wide and 2 cm long; wider at roof than base.

**Extent:** From apex of petrous temporal bone to superior orbital fissure.

### Internal Carotid Artery in the Cavernous Sinus

The artery enters posteroinferiorly from the carotid canal, turns anteriorly passing horizontally through the sinus, then turns superiorly to enter the anterior cranial fossa medial to the anterior clinoid processes. The horizontal portion is the lateral relation of the hypophysis. The artery may be 1-7 mm from the gland.

- **Medial wall** of cavernous sinus is much thinner than lateral wall
- The pituitary capsule is the **only barrier** to adenoma spread from the fossa
- **No thick dural sheet** exists between the sinus and the gland

### Neural Relations in the Cavernous Sinus

- **Oculomotor nerve (III):** Large, easily recognizable; located superiorly closest to optic nerve
- **Trochlear nerve (IV):** Smaller; just below oculomotor nerve
- **Abducens nerve (VI):** Most medial; inferior to carotid artery; runs inferolaterally to carotid in its horizontal portion
- **Ophthalmic division (V1):** Closely related to lateral wall
- **Maxillary division (V2):** Occasionally visible
- **Sympathetic plexus:** Runs with the carotid artery

### Dural Reflections

**Diaphragmatica sella:**
- Sheet of dura forming roof of sella between clinoid processes
- Central defect (foramen diaphragmatis) for pituitary stalk; variable diameter up to 5 mm
- Dura, arachnoid, and pia fuse with pituitary capsule at the foramen
- No subdural or subarachnoid spaces in the fossa; therefore **no CSF in normal pituitary fossa**

**Intercavernous venous sinuses:**
- Connect the two cavernous sinuses across the floor of the pituitary fossa
- Variable arrangement with multiple interconnections
- Surgeon encounters these beneath the bony sella floor during endoscopic approach

---

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. Sphenoid sinus pneumatization: sellar type (~80%), pre-sellar (~20%), conchal (~2%); conchal type much more common in Asian populations (up to 28%)
> 2. Intra-sphenoid septae are highly variable: standard midline septum in only 14%; in 87% at least one septum inserts close to the carotid eminence posteriorly
> 3. LOCR corresponds to anterior clinoid process; MOCR corresponds to medial clinoid intracranially
> 4. Inter-optic nerve distance is 12 mm; pituitary fossa width between carotid prominences is 21 mm
> 5. Bony covering over sella is <0.5 mm; carotid artery dehiscence occurs in up to 5%
> 6. The medial wall of the cavernous sinus is thin; pituitary capsule is the only barrier to adenoma spread
> 7. The pituitary fossa normally contains no CSF, but does contain intercavernous venous sinuses
> 8. The abducens nerve runs inferolateral to the carotid in its horizontal portion (most vulnerable nerve during surgery)
> 9. Understanding the variable tortuous course of the carotid artery in the sphenoid and cavernous sinus is the key to performing safe pituitary surgery
> 10. Adenohypophysis has no direct arterial supply; relies on portal venous system from median eminence


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - Pre-operative imaging must define sphenoid pneumatization, septae orientation, and carotid course before transsphenoidal surgery
> - In pre-sellar type sphenoid, intra-operative navigation may be needed to delineate the anterior sella wall
> - Drilling intra-sphenoid septae requires extreme care due to frequent insertion near the carotid eminence
> - Macroadenomas can increase the inter-carotid distance up to 3 cm, paradoxically making surgical access easier
> - The lateral opticocarotid recess is the key endoscopic landmark; recognizing it confirms the position of the anterior clinoid process
> - The pharyngeal pituitary (remnant of Rathke's pouch in the nasopharynx roof) may be a source of ectopic pituitary tumours
