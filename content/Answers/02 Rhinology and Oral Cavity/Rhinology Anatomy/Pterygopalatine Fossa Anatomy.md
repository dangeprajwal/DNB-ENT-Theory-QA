# Anatomy of the Pterygopalatine Fossa

> [!question] Questions
> Describe the anatomy of the pterygopalatine fossa with diagrams.

**Source:** Scott-Brown's Otorhinolaryngology, 8th Edition - Volume 2, Chapter 98 (Skull Base Anatomy) & Volume 1, Chapter 87 (Anatomy of the Nose and Paranasal Sinuses)

---

## INTRODUCTION

The pterygopalatine fossa (PPF) is a small, cone-shaped depression located deep to the infratemporal fossa, posterior to the maxilla near the orbital apex. It is a critical anatomical crossroad in the skull base, serving as a major neurovascular junction with communications to the nasal cavity, orbit, oral cavity, middle cranial fossa and infratemporal fossa. A thorough understanding of its anatomy is essential for endoscopic sinus surgery, management of [[Epistaxis Management|epistaxis]], [[Juvenile Nasopharyngeal Angiofibroma|juvenile nasopharyngeal angiofibroma]] surgery and skull base procedures.

---

## PART A: BOUNDARIES AND RELATIONS

### Shape and Location

The pterygopalatine fossa is an inverted pyramidal space situated between the posterior wall of the maxillary sinus anteriorly and the pterygoid process of the sphenoid bone posteriorly. It lies deep to the infratemporal fossa and medial to the zygomatic arch.

### Boundaries

- **Anterior wall:** Posterior wall (infratemporal surface) of the maxilla
- **Posterior wall:** Pterygoid process of the sphenoid bone and greater wing of the sphenoid
- **Medial wall:** Perpendicular plate of the palatine bone (lateral nasal wall)
- **Roof:** Body of the sphenoid bone (inferior surface)
- **Lateral:** Opens into the infratemporal fossa via the pterygomaxillary fissure (no bony lateral wall)
- **Floor:** Tapers to the greater palatine canal

---

## PART B: COMMUNICATIONS

The pterygopalatine fossa has several important communications to other parts of the skull base, making it a critical surgical landmark:

| Communication | Via | Leads To |
|--------------|-----|----------|
| Infratemporal fossa | Pterygomaxillary fissure | Laterally |
| Nasal cavity | Sphenopalatine foramen | Medially |
| Orbit | Inferior orbital fissure | Anteriorly |
| Middle cranial fossa | Foramen rotundum | Posterosuperiorly |
| Middle cranial fossa | Pterygoid (Vidian) canal | Posteriorly |
| Oral cavity (palate) | Greater palatine canal | Inferiorly |
| Pharynx | Palatovaginal canal | Posteroinferiorly |

The sphenopalatine foramen is formed by the sphenopalatine notch of the palatine bone in articulation with the sphenoid bone. The crista ethmoidalis is a small crest of the perpendicular plate of the palatine bone located anterior to the sphenopalatine foramen and serves as a consistent and reliable landmark to identify vessels during endoscopic dissection.

---

## PART C: CONTENTS

The small pterygopalatine fossa contains three major categories of structures:

### 1. Arterial - Terminal Third of the Maxillary Artery

The internal maxillary artery enters the pterygopalatine fossa through the pterygomaxillary fissure and terminates here as its third (pterygopalatine) part, giving off several important branches:

- **Sphenopalatine artery** - Exits through the sphenopalatine foramen to supply the lateral nasal wall and nasal septum (posterior septal artery). The sphenopalatine artery is the major arterial supply to the turbinates and lateral nasal wall.
- **Greater palatine artery** - Descends through the greater palatine canal to supply the hard palate; a branch enters the nasal cavity through the incisive canal to supply the anteroinferior septum
- **Lesser palatine arteries** - Supply the soft palate and tonsil
- **Pharyngeal branch** - Passes through the palatovaginal canal
- **Infraorbital artery** - Enters the orbit through the inferior orbital fissure
- **Posterior superior alveolar artery** - Supplies the maxillary teeth and sinus

### 2. Neural - Maxillary Nerve (V2)

The maxillary nerve enters the pterygopalatine fossa through the foramen rotundum from the middle cranial fossa. Within the fossa it gives off:

- **Infraorbital nerve** - Passes through the inferior orbital fissure into the orbit and exits through the infraorbital foramen to supply the cheek, upper lip and side of the nose
- **Zygomatic nerve** - Enters the orbit via the inferior orbital fissure
- **Posterior superior alveolar nerve** - Supplies the maxillary teeth
- **Ganglionic branches** - Connect to the pterygopalatine (sphenopalatine) ganglion
- **Posterolateral nasal nerves** - Supply the lateral nasal wall and turbinates, arising from the sphenopalatine foramen

### 3. Pterygopalatine (Sphenopalatine) Ganglion

The pterygopalatine ganglion lies just in front of the pterygoid canal within the fossa. It is the largest parasympathetic ganglion in the head.

**Roots of the ganglion:**

- **Parasympathetic root (motor):** Greater superficial petrosal nerve (from nervus intermedius of facial nerve via geniculate ganglion) - preganglionic parasympathetic fibres synapse in the ganglion
- **Sympathetic root:** Deep petrosal nerve (from the sympathetic plexus around the internal carotid artery) - postganglionic fibres pass through the ganglion without synapsing
- **Sensory root:** Ganglionic branches from the maxillary nerve (V2) - pass through without synapsing

The greater superficial petrosal nerve and deep petrosal nerve unite to form the nerve of the pterygoid canal (Vidian nerve), which runs anteriorly through the pterygoid canal to reach the pterygopalatine ganglion.

**Branches from the ganglion:**

- Nasal branches (posterolateral and posteroseptal) via sphenopalatine foramen
- Greater and lesser palatine nerves via palatine canals
- Pharyngeal branch via palatovaginal canal
- Orbital branches via inferior orbital fissure

**Note on autonomic function:** Presynaptic parasympathetic fibres travel along the Vidian nerve and synapse within the sphenopalatine ganglion to innervate the nasal mucosa via postsynaptic fibres, regulating nasal secretions. Postganglionic sympathetic fibres pass through the ganglion and terminate in the nasal mucosa, regulating vascular tone and turbinate congestion.

### 4. Fat

The remaining space in the pterygopalatine fossa is occupied by fat.

## PART D: CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE

**Epistaxis management:** The sphenopalatine artery can be ligated endoscopically at the sphenopalatine foramen. The crista ethmoidalis serves as a reliable landmark. The artery commonly branches lateral to the crista ethmoidalis with many variations; 97% of specimens have two or more branches medial to the crista ethmoidalis. If more proximal vascular control is required, the internal maxillary artery can be ligated in the pterygopalatine fossa by removal of the posterior wall of the maxillary sinus.

**Juvenile nasopharyngeal angiofibroma:** Tumour origin is close to the sphenopalatine foramen. Tumour extension into the pterygopalatine fossa is common and staged accordingly. Endoscopic and open approaches address the pterygopalatine fossa contents during tumour removal.

**Endoscopic sinus surgery:** The pterygopalatine fossa is accessed endoscopically via the posterior wall of the maxillary sinus or through the sphenopalatine foramen. Knowledge of its communications is essential to avoid complications.

**Maxillary nerve block:** The pterygopalatine fossa approach allows blockade of V2 for facial pain management.

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. Pterygopalatine fossa is a cone-shaped depression deep to the infratemporal fossa, posterior to the maxilla near the orbital apex
> 2. Contains three main structures: terminal maxillary artery, maxillary nerve (V2), and pterygopalatine ganglion with fat
> 3. Seven communications: infratemporal fossa, nasal cavity, orbit, middle cranial fossa (x2), palate, and pharynx
> 4. Sphenopalatine foramen is formed by the sphenopalatine notch of the palatine bone with the sphenoid bone
> 5. Vidian nerve = greater superficial petrosal nerve + deep petrosal nerve; runs through the pterygoid canal
> 6. Pterygopalatine ganglion is the largest parasympathetic ganglion of the head; only parasympathetic fibres synapse here
> 7. Sphenopalatine artery is the main blood supply to the lateral nasal wall and turbinates
> 8. Crista ethmoidalis is the reliable landmark for endoscopic sphenopalatine artery ligation
> 9. Greater palatine artery supplies the hard palate and enters the nasal cavity via the incisive canal
> 10. Tumour extension into the PPF is a key staging criterion for juvenile nasopharyngeal angiofibroma


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - The pterygopalatine fossa is a surgical crossroad - understanding its communications is critical for safe endoscopic surgery
> - 97% of specimens have two or more branches of the sphenopalatine artery medial to the crista ethmoidalis
> - Parasympathetic fibres regulate nasal secretions; sympathetic fibres regulate vascular tone and congestion
> - The PPF has no bony lateral wall - it opens directly into the infratemporal fossa via the pterygomaxillary fissure
> - The posterior septal artery (branch of sphenopalatine artery) is the basis of the nasoseptal flap for endoscopic skull base reconstruction
