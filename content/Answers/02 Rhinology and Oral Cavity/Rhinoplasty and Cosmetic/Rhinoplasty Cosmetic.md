# Rhinoplasty — Assessment, Saddle Nose, Reduction and Augmentation

> [!question] Questions Covered
> - "Classify saddle nose deformity and discuss management of acute and late saddle nose. Describe nasofacial angles used in rhinoplasty assessment and management of bulbous nasal tip. Discuss counselling for rhinoplasty, reduction rhinoplasty steps, and augmentation rhinoplasty indications and techniques."
*Source:** Scott-Brown's Volume 3, Chapters 80 (Rhinoplasty Following Nasal Trauma), 81 (Pre-operative Assessment for Rhinoplasty), 82 (External Rhinoplasty)

---

## Introduction

Rhinoplasty is one of the most demanding operations in facial plastic surgery, requiring a thorough understanding of nasal anatomy, facial aesthetics, and a wide repertoire of surgical techniques. Pre-operative assessment involves systematic analysis of the face and nose using standardized angles and proportions described by Powell and Humphrey. Saddle nose deformity, whether from trauma, prior surgery, or systemic disease, requires careful classification to guide the choice of reconstructive technique. Reduction rhinoplasty addresses the over-projected or humped nose, while augmentation techniques are essential for reconstruction of the under-projected or collapsed dorsum. Patient counselling is paramount to establish realistic expectations and achieve satisfactory outcomes.

---

## Part A — Saddle Nose Deformity — Classification and Management

### Classification

Saddle nose deformity results from loss of dorsal height due to loss of septal support, whether from trauma, iatrogenic causes, granulomatous disease, or cocaine abuse. It is classified by the location and severity of the dorsal depression:

| Type | Description | Graft Needed |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| Minimal | Shallow supratip depression | Septal/conchal cartilage |
| Moderate | Middle third collapse, ULC loss | Spreader grafts + dorsal onlay |
| Major | Full dorsal collapse, skin damage | Costal cartilage L-strut |
| Severe | Total framework loss + skin scar | Rib graft framework + flap |

The deformity is further characterized by the **third of the nose affected** — upper third (nasal bone depression), middle third (upper lateral cartilage collapse with loss of septal height), or lower third (loss of tip projection from septal angle damage).

### Management of Acute Saddle Nose

Early rhinoplasty is indicated for acute saddle deformity to reconstruct septal height. Key principles include:

- **Open approach** with full septal dissection provides best control
- **Fixation of dorsal keystone area** using drill holes in nasal bones and suture fixation to hold the perpendicular plate and quadrilateral cartilage together
- **Repair of upper lateral cartilages** to the reconstructed dorsal septum
- **Bilateral spreader grafts** (often asymmetric) provide secure compression and stabilize the dorsal line
- Simple oblique fractures can be released and fragments secured with batons of perforated perpendicular plate

### Management of Late Saddle Nose

Once scarring and fibrosis are established, reconstruction is more challenging:

- The key structure to repair is the **septum** — create a stable dorsal line connected to nasal bones, then correct anterior and posterior septal angles
- If cartilage is scarred or poor quality, **replace the dorsal or caudal septum** with grafts
- **Graft materials hierarchy:** septal cartilage (first choice) → conchal cartilage → costal cartilage
- **Costal cartilage:** choose horizontal 6th or 7th rib via medial inframammary approach for straighter grafts; laminate two pieces with opposite curvature to reduce warping
- A new **L-shaped strut** can be created from two or three individual cartilage pieces locked together with PDS sutures
- **Septal extension graft** fixed between extended spreader grafts provides stable support
- The most important fixation point is to the **nasal spine** — use a specific drill hole for firm suture fixation

## Part B — Nasofacial Angles in Rhinoplasty Assessment

### Facial Analysis

Analysis begins with assessment of facial symmetry and proportions using:

- **Rule of Thirds** (horizontal): trichion to glabella, glabella to subnasale, subnasale to menton — should be approximately equal
- **Rule of Fifths** (vertical): face divided into five equal widths, each approximately equal to one eye width; alar base equals intercanthal distance
- The nose ideally occupies **one-third of facial length** and **one-fifth of facial width**

### Powell and Humphrey Facial Aesthetic Triangle

| Angle | Normal Range | Landmarks |
|-------|-------------|-----------|
| Nasofrontal | 115–135° | Glabella–nasion–dorsum |
| Nasofacial | 30–40° | Dorsum relative to facial plane |
| Nasomental | 120–132° | Nasion–tip–pogonion |
| Mentocervical | 80–95° | Glabella–pogonion–cervical pt |
| Nasolabial | 90–95° (M), 95–105° (F) | Columella–lip junction |

### Key Nasal Measurements

- **Tip projection (Goode's ratio):** Line from alar–facial groove to tip should be 0.55–0.60 of the nasion-to-tip distance. Less = underprojection; more = overprojection
- **Nasal length:** Nasion to tip = stomium to menton = TS × 1.6
- **Columellar show:** Ideally 3–5 mm visible caudal septum on lateral view
- **Tip-defining points:** Four points identified by light reflection — two domes, supratip, and infratip
- **Tip rotation:** Described by the nasolabial angle — ideal is 90–95° in men, 95–105° in women

### Bulbous Nasal Tip — Definition and Management

A bulbous tip is characterized by wide, round, poorly defined lower lateral cartilages with loss of the normal tip-defining light reflex. The tip configurations include: normal, boxy, bifid, bulbous, and amorphous.

**Surgical techniques for bulbous tip:**

- **Cephalic trim:** Conservative resection of cephalic portion of lower lateral cartilage — absolute minimum 6 mm residual cartilage must be preserved
- **Domal suturing (transdomal/interdomal):** Narrows the dome angle and increases tip definition; avoid aggressive suturing which can cause pinching
- **Tip grafts:** Shield graft, cap graft, or onlay graft for added definition
- **Lateral crural repositioning:** Rather than resecting, reorientate the lateral crus so the caudal edge lies at a higher level — preferred by many surgeons over cephalic trim
- **Columellar strut:** Provides midline support between medial crura, enhances tip projection
- **Open approach** allows accurate anatomical analysis and precise cartilage modification

## Part C — Counselling, Reduction and Augmentation Rhinoplasty

### Pre-operative Counselling

The rhinoplasty improvement scale helps manage patient expectations — moving up **one point** on a five-point scale is realistic; anything more is unlikely. Key counselling points:

- Document whether primary concern is **functional, aesthetic, or both**
- Standardized clinical rhinoplasty photographs (frontal, lateral, oblique, basal, head-down view)
- Discuss facial asymmetries pre-operatively — patients may be unaware and may attribute post-operative asymmetry to surgery
- Assess skin quality using **Fitzpatrick classification** (Types I–VI): thin skin is unforgiving (shows irregularities), thick sebaceous skin limits tip refinement
- Classify nasal deviations by thirds: C-shaped, one-sided, or S-shaped

### Reduction Rhinoplasty Steps

1. **Approach selection:** Endonasal (closed) for simple reduction; external (open) for complex deformities requiring precise visualization
2. **Dorsal hump reduction:** Resect bony and cartilaginous components; powered instruments (Piezo device) allow accurate reduction under direct vision
3. **Spreader grafts:** Placed between dorsal septum and upper lateral cartilage to prevent inverted-V deformity after hump removal and maintain nasal valve
4. **Osteotomies:** Lateral (low-to-low or low-to-high) and medial oblique osteotomies to close the open roof created by hump reduction; infracture to narrow the bony vault
5. **Tip refinement:** Cephalic trim, domal suturing, shield graft as needed
6. **Splinting:** External splint for one week; internal splint if septal work performed

### Augmentation Rhinoplasty

**Indications:**
- Saddle nose deformity (post-traumatic, post-surgical, systemic disease)
- Ethnic rhinoplasty requiring dorsal augmentation
- Under-projected nasal tip
- Revision rhinoplasty with cartilage depletion

**Graft Materials:**

| Material | Source | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|----------|--------|------------|---------------|
| Septal cartilage | Nasal septum | Ideal match, no donor site | Limited quantity |
| Conchal cartilage | Ear concha | Good for battens/tip | Curved, less rigid |
| Costal cartilage | 6th-7th rib | Abundant supply, rigid | Warping, donor morbidity |
| Irradiated rib | Tissue bank | Readily available | Resorption risk |

**Techniques:**
- **Solid implant:** Carefully chamfered edges, placed in snug pocket to prevent displacement; open approach allows suture stabilization
- **Diced cartilage in fascia (DCF):** Finely diced auricular cartilage placed into temporalis fascia wrapper and injected onto dorsum — reduces visibility and warping
- **Spreader grafts:** Unilateral or bilateral for correction of dorsal deviation and nasal valve support
- **Alloplastic materials:** Reserved for cases with minimal residual autologous cartilage; risk of infection and extrusion must be discussed

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. Powell and Humphrey described the facial aesthetic triangle with four key angles: nasofrontal (115–135°), nasofacial (30–40°), nasomental (120–132°), mentocervical (80–95°)
> 2. Nasolabial angle is 90–95° in males and 95–105° in females — defines tip rotation
> 3. Goode's ratio for tip projection is 0.55–0.60 of the nasion-to-tip distance
> 4. Saddle nose classification is based on location (upper, middle, lower third) and severity (minimal to severe)
> 5. Costal cartilage is the graft of choice for major saddle nose reconstruction — laminating two pieces with opposite curvature minimizes warping
> 6. Absolute minimum 6 mm of residual lower lateral cartilage must be preserved during cephalic trim
> 7. The most important fixation point in saddle nose reconstruction is the nasal spine
> 8. The rhinoplasty improvement scale teaches patients that moving up one point is realistic
> 9. Osteotomies (lateral and medial oblique) are essential to close the open roof after dorsal hump reduction
> 10. Diced cartilage in fascia (DCF) technique is useful for dorsal augmentation in revision rhinoplasty with limited cartilage availability


> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - **A retrognathic chin gives the illusion of an overprojected nose** — genioplasty or chin implant may be needed alongside rhinoplasty
> - **Rule of thirds and fifths** provides a systematic framework for facial analysis before any nasal surgery
> - **Thin skin is unforgiving** — minor irregularities from grafts or cartilage modification are easily visible
> - **Always assess the nasal valve** in four planes (medially and laterally) — valve dysfunction is common after trauma
> - **Pre-operative photographs and documentation of asymmetry** are essential medicolegally — patients may attribute pre-existing asymmetry to the surgery
> - **Open approach** provides superior visualization for complex deformities, revision cases, and augmentation procedures
