## Introduction

Salivary gland neoplasms represent a heterogeneous group of tumours with diverse histological subtypes and biological behaviours. The WHO 2017 classification recognizes over 30 benign and 22 malignant subtypes. Approximately 80% of salivary gland tumours arise in the parotid gland, and the smaller the gland, the higher the likelihood of malignancy. The major benign tumours include pleomorphic adenoma (most common overall) and Warthin's tumour (second most common benign). Among malignancies, mucoepidermoid carcinoma (MEC) is the most common salivary malignancy, while carcinoma ex-pleomorphic adenoma (Ca ex-PA) represents a unique entity of malignant transformation within a pre-existing benign tumour. Accurate pre-operative diagnosis through FNAC, imaging, and histopathological assessment is essential for appropriate surgical planning and adjuvant treatment.

## Part A: WHO 2017 Classification of Salivary Gland Tumours

### Benign Epithelial Tumours (WHO Classification)

| Category | Key Features |
|---|---|
| Pleomorphic adenoma | Most common (50% of all SGT) |
| Warthin's tumour | 2nd most common benign; parotid |
| Myoepithelioma | Variant of PA; myoepithelial cells |
| Basal cell adenoma | Multiple subtypes recognized |
| Oncocytoma | Rare; elderly females; parotid |
| Canalicular adenoma | Minor salivary glands |
| Others | Cystadenoma, sialadenoma, ductal papillomas, sebaceous adenoma, lymphadenoma |

### WHO 2017 Malignant Epithelial Tumours (22 Recognized Types)

The WHO 2017 histologic classification of malignant salivary gland tumours includes 22 recognized phenotypes:

1. Mucoepidermoid carcinoma (MEC) -- most common salivary malignancy
2. Adenoid cystic carcinoma (AdCC)
3. Acinic cell carcinoma (AcCC)
4. Polymorphous adenocarcinoma (PAC)
5. Clear cell carcinoma (CCC)
6. Basal cell adenocarcinoma (BAC)
7. Intraductal carcinoma
8. Adenocarcinoma, NOS
9. Salivary duct carcinoma (SDC)
10. Myoepithelial carcinoma
11. Epithelial-myoepithelial carcinoma (EMC)
12. Carcinoma ex-pleomorphic adenoma
13. Secretory carcinoma
14. Sebaceous adenocarcinoma
15. Carcinosarcoma
16. Undifferentiated carcinoma
17. Large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma
18. Small cell neuroendocrine carcinoma
19. Lymphoepithelial carcinoma
20. Squamous cell carcinoma
21. Oncocytic carcinoma
22. Sialoblastoma (uncertain malignant potential)

### Histogenesis Theories

Two theories explain the complex histological appearance of salivary gland tumours:

**Multicellular theory:** Favours transformation of the entire ducto-acinar unit, requiring various differentiated cells to become de-differentiated and deranged in their growth pattern, resulting in the microscopically different components observed in various tumour types.

**Reserve cell theory (bicellular):** Both normal salivary gland units and tumour types result from differentiation of undifferentiated pluripotent precursor cells. Tumour cells originate from a problem in the normal differentiation process. The earlier in differentiation the problem occurs, the more undifferentiated and high-grade the resulting malignancy. Two types of reserve cells are distinguished:

- **Intercalated duct reserve cells** give rise to: adenoid cystic carcinoma, acinic cell carcinoma, adenocarcinoma NOS, mixed malignant tumour, and oncocytic tumours
- **Excretory duct reserve cells** give rise to: mucoepidermoid carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, and salivary duct carcinoma

### Clinical Grading of Salivary Malignancies

| Grade | Histological Types |
|---|---|
| Low | AcCC, PAC, low-grade MEC |
| Intermediate | AdCC, epithelial-myoepithelial ca |
| High | High-grade MEC, SDC, Ca ex-PA, ACNOS, undifferentiated ca |

When a two-tier system is used, the intermediate group is combined with the high-grade group because AdCC has a relentless clinical course and epithelial-myoepithelial carcinoma has a recurrence rate of 40% with DSS of only 60%.

## Part B: Warthin's Tumour (Adenolymphoma)

### Epidemiology and Clinical Features

Warthin's tumour (also known as adenolymphoma or papillary cystadenoma lymphomatosum) accounts for 20% of benign parotid tumours and is rare outside the parotid gland.

- **Peak incidence:** 7th decade of life
- **Sex ratio:** Male:female ratio of 1.6:1 (previously thought to be 10:1, now more equal)
- **Smoking:** Strong association; incidence is doubled and multicentric in smokers
- **Racial predilection:** Comparatively rare in Afro-Caribbeans
- **Viral association:** EBV has been implicated in the genesis of bilateral Warthin's tumours
- **Radiation exposure:** Follow-up of atomic bomb survivors and Chernobyl disaster shows increased incidence of both benign (Warthin's and PA) and malignant salivary tumours
- **Bilaterality:** Not infrequently bilateral; multicentric in 4-10% of cases

### Histopathology

Warthin's tumour is composed of ciliated, bi-layered oncocytic epithelium supported by reactive lymphoid stroma. The cystic areas contain amorphous debris. It can undergo infarction, degeneration, and metaplastic change either spontaneously or secondary to manipulation (e.g., FNAB, core biopsy).

Benign oncocytic epithelial inclusions are commonly seen in intra-parotid and para-parotid lymph nodes. These inclusions probably account for the tumour's propensity for multicentricity and bilaterality, and explain its rarity in the submandibular gland which contains no lymphoid tissue.

### Origin and Nature

There is controversy regarding the exact nature of Warthin's tumours:

- Some studies suggest a polyclonal origin for the epithelium, suggesting a non-neoplastic origin
- Other work suggests a clonal origin associated with a fusion oncogene transformation
- The conclusion is that Warthin's tumour arises from salivary duct inclusions within intra-parotid lymph nodes, analogous to branchial cysts arising from epithelial inclusions within cervical lymph nodes

### Malignant Transformation

Carcinomatous change in Warthin's tumours has been reported only exceedingly rarely and may in some cases be secondary to irradiation. Reported carcinomas include adenocarcinomas, squamous cell, or undifferentiated types. This may not represent true malignant transformation but rather dual pathology or erroneous pathology.

### [[PET CT Nuclear Medicine Scans|PET-CT]] Considerations

PET-CT has high false-positive rates in salivary gland disease. Common benign tumours including Warthin's tumours and pleomorphic adenomas demonstrate increased FDG uptake, which can be mistaken for malignancy.

### Management

- **Surgical:** Partial superficial parotidectomy is the standard treatment
- **Conservative observation:** Given the following factors, patients with Warthin's tumours can be managed conservatively with observation only, provided a diagnostic FNAC result is obtained:
  - No significant chance of malignant transformation
  - Often occurs in the older age group
  - Tumours may not grow or grow very slowly
  - Bearing in mind possible multifocality

## Part C: Mucoepidermoid Carcinoma (MEC)

### Epidemiology

- Most common malignant salivary gland neoplasm overall (12-29% of all salivary malignancies)
- Most common malignancy at the intraoral minor salivary gland subsite
- Represents 16-37% of all MiSGCs, but 36-59% of intraoral MiSGCs
- More frequent in females; peaks in the 5th decade
- Major risk factor: previous therapeutic radiation exposure (latent period 7-32 years)
- CMV infection may have a tenuous link to MEC development

### Histopathology

The tumour is triphasic, comprising three cell types:

- **Mucus-secreting cells** (goblet cell mucocytes)
- **Epidermoid cells** (usually without keratinization)
- **Intermediate cells** (non-descript cells of intermediate size and shape)

Low-grade tumours are macrocystic and microcystic with plentiful mucocytes, fewer epidermoid cells, and infrequent intermediate cells. High-grade tumours are more solid, contain fewer mucinous elements with a preponderance of atypical epidermoid cells, and are easily mistaken for SCC.

### Grading Systems

| System | Approach | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Healey 3-tier | Cell types | Low 6%, intermediate 20%, high 78% recurrence |
| Evans 2-tier | Solid component | >90% solid = high-grade |
| Ciccolallo 2-tier | Epithelial cells | >90% epithelial = high-grade |

The AFIP also proposed a quantitative 3-tier system based on point scoring (intracystic component <20%, neural invasion, necrosis, mitoses >4/10HPF, anaplasia) with grades: low (0-4 points, 90% 10-year OS), intermediate (5-6 points, 70% OS), and high (7-14 points, 25% OS).

Low-grade tumours have a 5-year survival of 96%, whereas high-grade tumours show a death rate 10 times this. Recent large studies find no prognostic difference between low- and intermediate-grade MEC.

### Molecular Biology

The CRTC1-MAML2 (previously MECT1-MAML2) fusion oncogene, resulting from t(11;19)(q14-21;p12-13), is present in >80% of MEC cases. It was initially thought exclusive to low-grade MEC; however, high-grade fusion-positive MEC cancers associated with advanced-stage lethal disease have now been described. CRTC3-MAML2 is a less common variant (<10%).

Additional molecular features:
- H-RAS mutations in almost half of MECs; frequency parallels tumour grade
- Cyclin D1 frequently overexpressed; high expression follows inactivation of SFRPs by hypermethylation
- HER-2 overexpressed in 30% of MEC

### Management of MEC

**Surgical treatment** is the primary modality:

- **Parotid MEC:** Superficial or total parotidectomy depending on tumour extent and location; facial nerve preserving approach whenever possible
- **Submandibular MEC:** Complete gland excision within a level I-II-III [[Neck Dissection|neck dissection]]
- **Minor salivary gland MEC:** Wide local resection with tumour-free margins

**Adjuvant therapy decision-making:**

- **Low-grade MEC without adverse features:** Surgery alone is sufficient. Only this subgroup can be managed with surgery alone without post-operative [[Radiotherapy Principles Fractionation Advances|radiotherapy]]
- **Intermediate-grade MEC:** Many patients will have additional negative prognostic factors (involved surgical margins, soft tissue invasion) that indicate adjuvant radiotherapy
- **High-grade MEC:** Post-operative radiotherapy is indicated. Occult metastasis rate is significant (high-grade MEC together with SDC, SCC, and undifferentiated carcinoma results in a 68% occult rate in one study). Consider elective neck dissection or elective radiotherapy to the neck
- **High-risk major salivary gland carcinomas:** Two reports document the benefit of post-operative platinum-based concomitant chemoradiation

**Neck management:**

- cN+ neck: Comprehensive modified radical neck dissection (levels I-V)
- cN0 neck with high-risk features (tumour >4cm, high-grade histology): Elective neck treatment (surgery or radiotherapy). Risk factors: tumour size >4cm and high clinical grade imply 20% and 49% risk for occult nodal metastasis respectively

## Part D: Carcinoma Ex-Pleomorphic Adenoma (Ca ex-PA)

### Epidemiology and Pathogenesis

- Accounts for 5-25% of all SGC; approximately 12% of all salivary malignancies and 6.2% of all pleomorphic adenomas
- Tends to arise in major glands, commonly the parotid
- Risk of developing carcinoma increases with time a pleomorphic adenoma exists -- up to 10% by 15 years
- Risk factors: older age (mean 61 years), male sex, prolonged tumour duration, multiple recurrences
- In recurrent pleomorphic adenoma: rate of carcinoma ranges from 7% to 16%
- This is why optimal initial surgery for PA is so much stressed upon in trainee surgeons

### Macroscopic Appearance

Tumours tend to be larger than their benign counterpart, poorly circumscribed, firm, tan-white, and extensively infiltrative. They can occasionally appear well circumscribed and encapsulated. The "ghost" of a pre-existing adenoma is often identified, sometimes alluded to by a dense, occasionally mineralized scar. The malignant component tends to radiate centrifugally from this nidus.

### Histopathology

The malignant element typically comprises high-grade adenocarcinoma or salivary duct carcinoma, often with squamoid differentiation. Other specific subtypes of salivary carcinoma can occur. The benign pleomorphic adenoma component can be difficult to identify and may require extensive sampling.

### WHO 2005 Sub-classification by Invasion

| Category | Invasion | Prognosis |
|---|---|---|
| Intracapsular carcinoma | Non-invasive in PA | Favourable |
| Minimally invasive | <4-6 mm invasion | Favourable |
| Widely invasive | Beyond capsule | Poor prognosis |

In principle, intracapsular and minimally invasive categories have a favourable prognosis compared to widely invasive, although the precise cut-off point to define a category of invasion with minimal metastatic potential is controversial.

### Molecular Biology

- PLAG-1 is a specific proto-oncogene found in a large percentage of pleomorphic adenomas (~30%), transcribed and overexpressed following a t(3;8)(p21;q12) chromosome translocation resulting in beta-catenin-promoter swapping
- H-RAS mutations observed in carcinoma ex-pleomorphic adenoma

### Survival

The MSKCC cause-specific survivals for Ca ex-PA are:
- 40% at 5 years
- 24% at 10 years
- 19% at 15 years

### Management

**Surgical treatment:**

- Radical surgery is the mainstay; total or radical parotidectomy for parotid lesions
- Facial nerve: Pre-operatively functioning VII nerve -- preserve unless directly invaded by or surrounded by tumour. If nerve is pre-operatively paralysed, resection is required
- If facial nerve trunk or branch is sacrificed: frozen section of cut margins is recommended to exclude skip metastases. Immediate cable grafting with greater auricular nerve (GAN) or sural nerve provides optimal functional outcomes (House-Brackmann grade III-IV in 69-78%)
- Neck dissection: For high-grade tumours and clinically N+ disease

**Post-operative radiotherapy:**

- Indicated for all but intracapsular variants
- Radiotherapy is indicated in advanced stage disease (stage III-IV) and presence of adverse prognostic factors (positive margins, PNI, LVI, high grade)
- IMRT is now the standard of care, replacing conformal wedged-pair beams

**Importance of prevention:**

The high rate of malignant change in recurrent PA (7-16%) underlines the importance of adequate initial surgery for pleomorphic adenoma to prevent future carcinoma ex-PA.

## Part E: Milan System for Reporting Salivary Gland Cytopathology (MSRSGC)

*Note: The Milan system was published after the 8th edition of Scott-Brown's and is not covered in the textbook, but is commonly asked in examinations. The following is standard knowledge supplemented by FNAC data from the textbook.*

### Six-Tier Classification

| Category | Description | ROM (%) | Management |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | Non-diagnostic | ~25 | Repeat FNAC |
| II | Non-neoplastic | ~10 | Clinical follow-up |
| III | AUS (atypia of undetermined significance) | ~20 | Repeat FNAC or surgery |
| IVa | Benign neoplasm | ~5 | Surgery |
| IVb | SUMP (salivary neoplasm of uncertain malignant potential) | ~35 | Surgery |
| V | Suspicious of malignancy | ~60 | Surgery |
| VI | Malignant | ~90 | Surgery with staging |

ROM = Risk of Malignancy; AUS = Atypia of Undetermined Significance; SUMP = Salivary gland neoplasm of Uncertain Malignant Potential.

The Milan system standardizes FNAC reporting for salivary gland lesions, analogous to the Bethesda system for thyroid cytopathology. Each category carries an associated risk of malignancy and recommended management pathway.

### FNAC of Salivary Gland Tumours (from Scott-Brown's)

FNAC is an important tool in the diagnosis of salivary neoplasms:

- **Accuracy:** 79% for differentiating between benign and malignant lesions. On systematic review, concordance with histology is 80% in cancers and 95% in benign tumours
- **Ultrasound-guided FNAC (UgFNAC):** Improves accuracy and is recommended. Helps with diagnosis of the primary tumour and synchronous staging of the neck
- **French series (1355 FNACs):** 80.5% true positive, 4.6% suspicious, 11.9% false negative, 3% uninterpretable
- **Mandatory removal:** Even if FNAC suggests benign disease, removal of the tumour for further histopathology analysis remains mandatory
- **Core biopsy:** Can improve diagnostic accuracy further; sensitivity 100%, specificity 92% when used as second-line investigation or when FNAC shows possible malignancy
- **Immediate on-site processing** of cellular quality should be provided; if unsatisfactory, FNAC can be repeated, resulting in higher diagnostic accuracy

## Part F: Staging and Management Principles of Salivary Malignancy

### UICC TNM 8th Edition -- Major Salivary Glands

**T Classification:**
- T1: Tumour 2 cm or less, without extraparenchymal extension (EPE)
- T2: Tumour >2-4 cm, without EPE
- T3: Tumour >4-6 cm, and/or EPE
- T4a: Tumour invades skin, mandible, ear canal, and/or VII nerve involvement
- T4b: Tumour invades skull base, and/or pterygoid plates, and/or encases carotid artery

EPE is defined as clinical/macroscopic invasion only; microscopic extension alone does not constitute EPE.

**N Classification:**
- N1: Single ipsilateral node, 3 cm or less, without extranodal extension (ENE)
- N2a: Single ipsilateral node >3-6 cm, without ENE
- N2b: Multiple ipsilateral nodes <6 cm
- N2c: Bilateral or contralateral nodes <6 cm
- N3a: Node(s) >6 cm, without ENE
- N3b: Single or multiple nodes, with ENE

**Stage Grouping:**
- Stage I: T1 N0 M0
- Stage II: T2 N0 M0
- Stage III: T3 N0 M0, or T1-T3 N1 M0
- Stage IVa: T4a N0-N2 M0, or T1-T3 N2 M0
- Stage IVb: T4b any N M0, or any T N3 M0
- Stage IVc: any T any N M1

### Facial Nerve Preservation Principles

- Most pre-operatively intact nerves can be dissected macroscopically free from the tumour
- VII nerve branches are only to be resected when pre-operatively paralysed or peri-operatively invaded by or surrounded by tumour
- There is consensus that microscopic disease left behind on a spared nerve branch can be controlled by post-operative radiotherapy
- Nerve sacrifice in borderline instances often induces disproportionate morbidity at the expense of minor gain in tumour control
- When resection is performed: frozen section of cut margins is recommended. Immediate cable grafting (GAN or sural nerve) results in optimal functional outcomes
- EMG signs of reinnervation appear after 4.5 months; first movements around 6th month; final function (HB grade III-IV) realized by 2 years
- Radiotherapy does not impair final results of cable grafting (HB III-IV in 69% irradiated vs 78% non-irradiated)

### Post-operative Radiotherapy Indications

Post-operative radiotherapy (IMRT as standard of care, 60 Gy) is indicated for:
- Advanced stage disease (stage III and IV)
- Positive or close surgical margins
- Perineural invasion (especially named nerve involvement)
- Lymphovascular invasion
- High-grade histology
- Recurrent disease
- pN+ disease
- Bone and muscle invasion

Post-operative radiotherapy can only be omitted for stage I-II lesions in AcCC and low-grade MEC if complete resection does not reveal other adverse pathological factors.

### Chemotherapy

- Salivary gland cancers were previously believed to be radioresistant; this is no longer accepted
- Chemotherapy remains palliative; temporary complete remission in approximately 20-25% of patients
- Cisplatin-based regimens are most studied (response rate 25% in AdCC meta-analysis)
- For high-risk major salivary gland carcinomas, post-operative platinum-based concomitant chemoradiation may be beneficial

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. The WHO 2017 classification recognizes 22 malignant salivary gland tumour subtypes, each with distinct histological features, molecular profiles, and biological behaviours.
> 2. Warthin's tumour is the second most common benign parotid tumour, strongly associated with smoking, and can be managed conservatively with observation given a diagnostic FNAC in appropriate patients.
> 3. Mucoepidermoid carcinoma is the most common salivary gland malignancy; only low-grade MEC without adverse prognostic features can be managed with surgery alone without post-operative radiotherapy.
> 4. The CRTC1-MAML2 fusion oncogene is present in more than 80% of MECs and was initially thought exclusive to low-grade tumours, but high-grade fusion-positive lethal cases have been described.
> 5. Carcinoma ex-pleomorphic adenoma carries a poor prognosis (MSKCC: 40% survival at 5 years, 19% at 15 years), and the risk increases with duration of the pre-existing PA to 10% at 15 years.
> 6. Optimal initial surgery for pleomorphic adenoma is critical to prevent future carcinoma ex-PA, given the 7-16% malignant transformation rate in recurrent PA.
> 7. The facial nerve should be preserved unless pre-operatively paralysed or directly invaded; microscopic residual disease on a spared nerve can be controlled with post-operative radiotherapy.
> 8. The Milan system provides a standardized six-tier classification for salivary gland FNAC reporting with associated risk of malignancy and management recommendations for each category.
> 9. Post-operative radiotherapy (IMRT) significantly improves locoregional control in advanced stage (III-IV) salivary malignancies and those with adverse pathological features.
> 10. TNM staging is a strong independent prognostic factor in salivary gland cancers, often overruling histological grade in multivariate analysis.

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - **The smaller the salivary gland, the more likely a tumour is malignant:** parotid 15-32%, submandibular 41-45%, sublingual 70-90%, minor salivary glands approximately 80%.
> - **PET-CT is misleading in salivary gland disease** because Warthin's tumours and pleomorphic adenomas both show increased FDG uptake, leading to false-positive results for malignancy.
> - **High-grade MEC mimics SCC histologically** and requires exhaustive sampling augmented by mucin histochemistry for accurate diagnosis; this diversity also makes FNAC interpretation challenging.
> - **For Ca ex-PA, always look for the "ghost"** of the pre-existing adenoma, sometimes identified by a dense, mineralized scar from which the malignant component radiates centrifugally.
> - **Elective neck treatment decision-making** hinges on tumour size >4 cm (20% occult rate) and high-grade histology (49% occult rate); age >54, perilymphatic spread, and extraparotid extension together confer a 95% risk of occult nodal disease.
> - **The reserve cell theory** provides a rational framework for understanding salivary tumour histogenesis and predicting tumour behaviour based on the cell of origin within the ducto-acinar unit.
