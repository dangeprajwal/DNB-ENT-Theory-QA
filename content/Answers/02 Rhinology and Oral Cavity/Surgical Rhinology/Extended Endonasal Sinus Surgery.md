# Extended Endonasal Sinus Surgery: Approaches

> [!question] Questions
> Describe the extended endonasal sinus surgery approaches.

**Source:** Scott-Brown's Otorhinolaryngology, 8th Edition - Volume 1, Chapters 114 & 116

---

## INTRODUCTION

A paradigm shift has occurred in skull base surgery with the introduction of endoscopic techniques. Endoscopic endonasal surgery (EES) has been extended beyond sinus surgery to include extrasellar regions of the ventral skull base. The primary principle of EES is utilization of the endonasal corridor to provide optimal access with the least morbidity. The endoscope provides superior visualization with panoramic views and angled visual pathways. EES is team surgery, requiring the coordination of two specialties (rhinology and neurosurgery). The sphenoid sinus is the epicentre of endonasal skull base approaches since it is the starting point for skull base experience and contains critical structures (optic nerves, internal carotid arteries).

---

## PART A: CLASSIFICATION OF APPROACHES

Endonasal approaches to the ventral skull base are classified based on their orientation in sagittal and coronal radiological planes relative to the sphenoid sinus.

### Sagittal Plane Approaches

| Approach | Region Accessed |
|----------|----------------|
| Transfrontal | Anterior to cribriform plates |
| Transcribriform | Cribriform plate and olfactory area |
| Transplanum (suprasellar) | Planum sphenoidale to tuberculum |
| Transsellar | Sella turcica |
| Transclival (superior) | Posterior clinoid |
| Transclival (middle) | Mid-clivus |
| Transclival (inferior) | Foramen magnum |
| Transodontoid | Craniovertebral junction |

### Coronal Plane Approaches

| Approach | Region Accessed |
|----------|----------------|
| **Anterior cranial fossa** | |
| Supraorbital | Orbital roof to mid-orbit |
| Transorbital | Orbital contents |
| **Middle cranial fossa** | |
| Medial cavernous sinus | Cavernous sinus |
| Medial petrous apex | Petrous apex |
| Suprapetrous | Middle fossa, lateral cavernous sinus |
| Infratemporal | Infratemporal skull base |
| **Posterior cranial fossa** | |
| Transcondylar | Occipital condyle |
| Infrapetrous | Inferior petrous bone |
| [[Parapharyngeal Space|Parapharyngeal space]] | Parapharyngeal region |

The anterior cranial base extends from the sella to the [[Frontal Recess Frontal Sinus Surgery|frontal sinus]] in the midline and laterally across the orbital roof. Sagittal plane modules (transfrontal, transcribriform, transplanum) can be combined for complete access to the anterior cranial fossa.

## PART B: INDICATIONS

| Approach | Indications |
|----------|------------|
| Transfrontal | Chronic sinusitis, mucocele, osteoma, nasal dermoid |
| Transcribriform | Meningoencephalocele, olfactory groove meningioma, olfactory schwannoma, olfactory neuroblastoma, sinonasal malignancy |
| Transplanum | Pituitary adenoma, craniopharyngioma, meningioma |
| Transsellar | Pituitary adenoma, Rathke's cleft cyst, craniopharyngioma |
| Supraorbital | Osteoma, meningioma, sinonasal malignancy |

**Sinonasal malignancies suitable for EES:** Olfactory neuroblastoma (aesthesioneuroblastoma), neuroendocrine carcinoma, sinonasal undifferentiated carcinoma (SNUC), squamous cell carcinoma, adenocarcinoma, adenoid cystic carcinoma, and melanoma. En bloc resection is not necessary as long as final resection margins are negative.

**Operability is determined by:** Biological behaviour of the tumour (diagnosis), stage (extent), and patient factors (age, medical co-morbidities). Brain invasion is not necessarily a sign of inoperability. Tumour involvement of cerebral vessels indicates inoperability for cure.

## PART C: SURGICAL TECHNIQUES

### General Setup and Principles

- Head immobilized with 3-point fixation (Mayfield clamp) for intra-operative navigation and stability
- Head extended for anterior access, slightly rotated and laterally flexed to align the surgical corridor
- Intra-operative navigation registered; neurophysiological monitoring (somatosensory-evoked potentials for cortical function, EMG of extraocular muscles if CN III/IV/VI dissection anticipated)
- Nasal decongestion with 0.05% oxymetazoline; perioperative third-generation cephalosporin prophylaxis
- Standard equipment includes endoscopic sinus surgery instruments, bipolar electrocautery, endonasal drill with 4 mm diamond bits, Doppler probe for vessel identification

### 1. Transfrontal Approach

**Key landmarks:** Foramen cecum, crista galli, frontal sinus

**Limits:** Anterior table (anterior), posterior table (posterior), crista galli (medial), lacrimal fossa (lateral), posterior table midpoint (superior)

**Technique:** Draf 3 frontal sinusotomy is the foundation. Nasofrontal recess identified bilaterally; anterior ethmoid artery identified at the posterior limit. Anterior tip of the middle turbinate resected to the first olfactory fibre. Superior septal window created for binarial access. Floor of frontal sinuses drilled anterior to the crista galli. Posterior table thinned and elevated to expose dura.

### 2. Transcribriform Approach

**Key landmarks:** Crista galli, olfactory filia, olfactory bulbs, lateral lamella

**Limits:** Frontal sinus (anterior), planum/posterior ethmoid artery (posterior), crista galli (medial), orbit (lateral)

**Technique:** Often combined with transfrontal and transplanum approaches. Bilateral ethmoidectomies performed, middle turbinates resected. Large tumours debulked first. Anterior and posterior ethmoidal arteries identified at skull base, transected on the orbital side to prevent retro-orbital haematoma. Skull base bone thinned with drill and elevated. Dura incised laterally; falx detached from crista galli and transected posteriorly.

### 3. Transplanum Approach

**Key landmarks:** Posterior ethmoid artery, olfactory tracts

**Limits:** Cribriform plate (anterior), superior intercavernous sinus (posterior), optic canals (posterolateral), orbital apex (lateral)

**Technique:** Bilateral ethmoidectomies and wide sphenoidotomy. Sphenoid rostrum resected for binarial access. Bone of planum outlined with drill and dissected from underlying dura anterior to posterior. Optic canals decompressed medially to the optic strut when needed. Dura incised carefully to avoid cortical vessel injury.

### 4. Supraorbital Approach

**Key landmarks:** Anterior and posterior ethmoid arteries

**Limits:** Frontal sinus (anterior), optic canal (posterior), midplane of orbit (superolateral)

**Technique:** Coronal plane approach to the orbital roof. Complete sphenoethmoidectomy, medial orbital wall removed to skull base level. Periorbita dissected from orbital roof to mid-plane of orbit. Binarial approach with superior septal defect enhances exposure. Medial orbital roof drilled and removed. Posteriorly can extend to anterior clinoid for 270-degree decompression of the optic canal.

### 5. Suprasellar Approach

**Key landmarks:** Optic canals, tuberculum, ophthalmic arteries, medial optic-carotid recess (mOCR), medial clinoid

**Limits:** Planum sphenoidale (anterior), optic canals and paraclinoidal ICA (lateral)

**Technique:** Combined with transsellar and transplanum approaches. Sella bone thinned with drill and removed to margins of cavernous sinus. Posterior planum thinned. Tuberculum strut disconnected laterally and removed as single piece. Dura incised above and below the superior intercavernous sinus (SIS). SIS cauterized and transected.

## PART D: PRE-OPERATIVE PLANNING AND IMAGING

**CT scan:** Better for bone erosion and sinus anatomy; obtained with skull base format for intra-operative navigation; CT angiography for ICA identification.

**MRI:** Better for soft-tissue invasion; differentiates tumour from obstructed secretions; multiplanar capabilities. Both CT and MRI are complementary and obtained in most cases.

**Key radiological features to assess:**

- Orbit: breach of lamina papyracea, loss of orbital fat planes, extraocular muscle involvement
- Septum: erosion (implications for access and nasoseptal flap use)
- Skull base: position, ethmoid artery location, defects suggesting intracranial extension
- Sphenoid sinus: pneumatization, Onodi cells, septation relation to ICA/optic nerve
- Paranasal sinus: osteitic changes (possible tumour origin), widened foramina (perineural invasion)

## PART E: RECONSTRUCTION

### Principles

Vascularized tissue is associated with significantly lower post-operative [[CSF Rhinorrhea|CSF leak]] rates for larger defects. All dural breaches should be repaired to avoid meningoencephalocele and ascending meningitis.

### Nasoseptal Flap (Hadad-Bassagasteguy Flap)

The workhorse for endoscopic skull base reconstruction. Based on the posterior septal artery (branch of sphenopalatine artery). Raised at the beginning of the procedure to preserve vascular pedicle. Superior incision runs 1 cm below skull base; inferior incision along posterior choana to nasal floor junction. Flap stored in nasopharynx during the procedure.

### Pericranial Flap

Used when nasoseptal flap is unavailable (septal involvement by tumour). Harvested via bicoronal scalp incision. Pedicled on supraorbital vessels. Passed into the nasal cavity through a bone window at the nasion (approximately 2.0 cm x 0.5 cm) below the skull base level.

### Other Local Vascularized Flaps

Middle turbinate, inferior turbinate, and nasal septal flaps based on terminal branches of the sphenopalatine artery. Regional options include temporoparietal fascial flap.

### Reconstruction Technique

Inlay fascial graft placed between brain and dura. Vascularized flap covers entire defect. Surrounding mucosa removed for flap-to-bone/dura contact. Covered with Surgicel, tissue glue, and Gelfoam. Support provided by Foley catheter or Merocel tampons.

---

## PART F: POST-OPERATIVE CARE AND COMPLICATIONS

**Post-operative care:** CT brain within 8 hours for haemorrhage/pneumocephalus. Visual assessment after optic nerve dissection. Diabetes insipidus monitoring after suprasellar approach. Antibiotics continued for duration of nasal packing. Packing removed at 5-7 days.

**Complications:** CSF leak, meningitis, tension pneumocephalus, intra-cranial haemorrhage, retro-orbital haematoma (from ethmoidal artery retraction into orbit), visual loss, diabetes insipidus, panhypopituitarism, carotid artery injury.

---

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. EES classification: sagittal plane (transfrontal to transodontoid) and coronal plane (anterior, middle, posterior cranial fossa)
> 2. Sphenoid sinus is the epicentre of all endonasal skull base approaches
> 3. Draf 3 frontal sinusotomy is the foundation for transfrontal and transcribriform approaches
> 4. Ethmoidal arteries must be transected on the orbital side with a stump for cauterization to prevent retro-orbital haematoma
> 5. En bloc resection is not necessary - negative final margins are the goal
> 6. Brain invasion does not preclude operability; cerebral vessel involvement does
> 7. Nasoseptal flap (based on posterior septal artery) is the workhorse for reconstruction
> 8. Pericranial flap is the alternative when nasoseptal flap is unavailable
> 9. Intra-operative navigation, somatosensory-evoked potentials, and EMG monitoring are standard
> 10. CT and MRI are complementary - CT for bone, MRI for soft tissue differentiation


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - Always raise the nasoseptal flap at the beginning of the procedure before septectomy - even if a CSF leak is not anticipated, preserve the pedicle
> - The medial optic-carotid recess (mOCR) is the critical landmark where the ICA courses medially before passing under the optic nerve
> - Preserve at least 1.5 cm of posterosuperior septal mucosa in the olfactory cleft region for preservation of smell
> - The anterior ethmoid artery is located between the 2nd and 3rd ethmoid lamella; navigation can locate it in a coronal plane tangential to the posterior globe surface
> - Copious warm saline irrigation (40 degrees C) for 10+ minutes may be needed to confirm complete haemostasis of brain surface bleeding
