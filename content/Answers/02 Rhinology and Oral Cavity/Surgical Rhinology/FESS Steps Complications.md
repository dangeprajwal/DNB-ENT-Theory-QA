# Functional Endoscopic Sinus Surgery (FESS) - Steps and Complications

> [!question] Questions Covered
> - Describe the steps and complications of FESS.
*Source:** Scott-Brown's Otorhinolaryngology, 8th Edition - Volume 1, Chapter 98 (Surgical Management of Rhinosinusitis) & Chapter 87 (Anatomy of the Nose and Paranasal Sinuses)

---

## INTRODUCTION

FESS is the surgical standard of care for [[CRS Nasal Polyposis Turbinate|chronic rhinosinusitis]] refractory to maximal medical therapy. The concept ranges from simple middle meatal antrostomy and anterior ethmoidectomy ("mini-FESS") to complete fronto-spheno-ethmoidectomy ("full-house FESS"). Endoscopic sinus surgery is an exercise of anatomical dissection around fixed anatomical landmarks. The goal is the creation of a new functional sinus cavity that re-establishes mucociliary clearance and enables maximal delivery of topical therapy.

---

## PART A: PRE-OPERATIVE PREPARATION

### Indications for Surgery

- CRS (with or without nasal polyps) refractory to maximal medical therapy (minimum 4 weeks topical steroids + nasal irrigation)
- Complications of ARS (orbital/intracranial abscess, Pott's puffy tumour)
- Mucocele
- [[AFRS Fungal Ball|Fungal rhinosinusitis]] (allergic or invasive)

### Imaging

- High-resolution CT in three planes (coronal, axial, parasagittal) is **mandatory**
- Images must be available to surgeon throughout surgery
- Pre-operative identification of anatomical risk factors: dehiscent lamina papyracea, angulated lateral lamella of cribriform plate, carotid artery dehiscence, Onodi cell with exposed optic nerve

### Anaesthesia

- General anaesthesia in most Western centres (laryngeal mask or endotracheal tube)
- **TIVA** (total intravenous anaesthesia) preferred by many surgeons; BIS monitoring recommended to avoid awareness
- Target systolic MAP ~90 mmHg; HR ~60 bpm for optimal surgical field
- Reverse Trendelenberg position (10-30° head elevation); cerebral perfusion pressure remains constant up to 30°

### Intranasal Preparation

- Topical decongestion: Moffat's solution (cocaine + adrenaline + sodium bicarbonate) on neuro-patties, or co-phenylcaine (equally effective)
- Local anaesthetic infiltration: 1-2 ml of 1% lignocaine with 1:80,000 adrenaline to middle turbinate, septum, frontal process of maxilla
- Allow time for vasoconstrictors to establish local effect while systemic effects dissipate

### Eye Preparation

- Traditionally eyes left open with lubrication; many surgeons now accept limited or complete taping
- Must allow rapid access to globe for intraoperative orbital assessment

## PART B: SURGICAL STEPS

### Step 1: Uncinectomy

1. Identify free edge of the boomerang-shaped uncinate process using Freer's elevator
2. **Inferior uncinectomy:** Use paediatric or adult back-biting forceps (sickle knife on inferior portion is discouraged due to risk of orbital penetration)
3. **Superior uncinectomy:** Sickle knife can be used safely superiorly (protected by hard bone of frontal process of maxilla); back-biter is acceptable alternative
4. Remove uncinate using angled through-biting forceps or dislocate forward with double right-angled ball probe then remove with microdebrider
5. Remove rough edges with 2 mm Kerrison's punch
6. Address the **horizontal part of the uncinate** extending inferior to the natural maxillary ostium
7. Dissect bone of horizontal portion free from mucosa; stretch natural ostium open with angled probe (avoid removing mucosa for primary surgery)
8. For advanced/revision disease: create large middle meatal antrostomy

### Step 2: Removal of the Ethmoidal Bulla

1. Natural ostium of the bulla sits posteromedial to its anterior face
2. Locate using double right-angled ball probe or 45° antral curette
3. Fracture the anterior face forwards
4. Remove bulla using microdebrider, aiming for complete removal of all partitions between lamina papyracea and middle turbinate
5. When removing bulla superiorly, identify **anterior ethmoidal artery** location (usually in suprabulla recess; rarely in anterior bulla wall)
6. Remove other anterior ethmoidal cells similarly
7. Prefer **through-biting instruments** over standard Blakesley-Wells forceps (latter tear mucosa leaving exposed bone)

### Step 3: Posterior Ethmoidectomy

1. **Ground lamella** of the middle turbinate defines the junction between anterior and posterior ethmoid
2. After reference to CT, perforate the ground lamella in the **inferomedial quadrant** (avoids skull base and lamina papyracea)
3. Use maxillary sinus roof as guide to superior limit of dissection (always below skull base; mean distance 10.1 mm)
4. Remove partitions between posterior ethmoid cells using microdebrider, Kerrison's punch or through-biting instruments
5. **Beware Onodi cells:** Optic nerve may traverse through a posterior ethmoid cell; surgeon must always know their exact location

### Step 4: Sphenoidotomy

1. Natural sphenoid ostium located in sphenoethmoidal recess, medial to superior turbinate, at height of antral roof
2. If endoscope cannot pass medial to superior turbinate: resect part of middle turbinate with back-biting forceps to enter superior meatus
3. Locate forward projection of superior turbinate from within posterior ethmoid
4. Resect inferior third of superior turbinate to access sphenoethmoidal recess
5. If natural ostium still not located: make artificial opening through posterior ethmoid then extend medially to incorporate natural ostium
6. Perforate ground lamella of superior turbinate in **inferomedial portion** to avoid skull base injury

### Step 5: [[Frontal Recess Frontal Sinus Surgery|Frontal Sinus]] Surgery

1. **Agger nasi cell** is key landmark; present in vast majority of cases
2. Using 2 mm 45° forward-angled Kerrison's punch in axilla of middle turbinate, remove anterior portion of agger nasi
3. Remove posterior wall and roof of agger nasi with curettes and angled instruments to expose frontal recess
4. Remove remaining fronto-ethmoidal cells with through-biting instruments, curettes, or angled microdebrider blades
5. **CRITICAL:** Avoid circumferential mucosal injury in the frontal recess (will cause stenosis and recurrent frontal sinusitis)
6. Identify position of anterior ethmoidal artery at all times
7. Address intersinus septal cells and supraorbital ethmoid cells (require angled endoscopes and curved microdebrider blades)
8. Advanced frontal instruments: giraffe forceps, 3.5 mm Hosemann punch

## PART C: POST-OPERATIVE MANAGEMENT

- **Regular review:** 7-10 days post-op, then weekly/fortnightly; expect full healing by 6 weeks
- **Saline irrigations:** High-volume; Ringer's lactate shown to be superior to isotonic/hypotonic saline (RCT)
- **Debridement:** Controversial; RCTs show no difference in outcomes between regular debridement vs no debridement
- **Antibiotics:** No evidence that prophylactic post-operative antibiotics improve outcomes; targeted therapy only if mucopus found at surgery
- **Steroids:** For CRSwNP, oral prednisolone 25 mg daily for 10 days; for extensive eosinophilic disease, 3 weeks or longer (tapered)
- **Long-term:** Topical steroid sprays/irrigations; may need indefinite use in eosinophilic CRS
- Full-house FESS with post-operative steroid irrigations significantly reduces recurrence in CRSwNP

## PART D: COMPLICATIONS

Reported complication range: **0.3-22.4%** (median 7.0%). Most are minor. Intra-operative image guidance significantly reduces complication rates (meta-analysis).

### Haemorrhage

**Sphenopalatine artery (SPA) injury:**
- Risk areas: aggressive resection of horizontal part of middle turbinate; extending sphenoid ostium inferiorly
- Management: topical vasoconstrictors (1:80,000 adrenaline ± cocaine); dedicated suction bipolar diathermy; formal SPA ligation if needed

**Anterior ethmoidal artery injury:**
- Risk area: suprabulla recess; frontal recess dissection
- Must identify and avoid in all cases where bulla elevated to skull base
- May need external exploration via Lynch incision for clipping

**General bleeding management:**
- Monopolar diathermy used with **caution** (heat dissipation can damage optic nerve)
- Topical haemostats: Surgiflo, Floseal
- **If bloody field cannot be controlled: STOP and plan elective second-stage surgery**

### Orbital Injury

**Lamina papyracea breach:**
- Can occur with first sickle knife incision through inferior uncinate
- Also high risk during middle meatal antrostomy with long thin infundibulum
- If orbital fat exposed: recognize immediately; no repair usually needed
- Check eye for intra-orbital haemorrhage (proptosis)
- Management of orbital haematoma: remove lamina papyracea + incise orbital periosteum ± lateral canthotomy/cantholysis

**Medial rectus muscle damage:**
- Occurs when surgeon fails to recognize orbital fat exposure and continues manipulation
- **Microdebrider** associated with increased risk of medial rectus injury
- Little that can be done to repair transected muscle; late oculoplastic surgery may be needed

### Optic Nerve Injury

- Risk areas: posterior ethmoid (Onodi cell), sphenoid sinus
- Most at risk if optic nerve on mesentery within Onodi cell
- Management: immediate systemic steroids + urgent ophthalmological consultation

### [[CSF Rhinorrhea|CSF Leak]]

- Estimated prevalence: **0.5%** of sinus surgical cases
- Highest risk area: **vertical lamella of cribriform plate** (especially if angled rather than vertical)
- Bloody surgical field increases risk
- Often identified immediately and repaired endoscopically
- Repair options: fat plugs, artificial materials, homografts, covering mucosal grafts
- Fibrin glue greatly facilitates CSF leak closure

### Carotid Artery Injury

- Most serious complication; can be catastrophic
- Risk: sphenoid surgery with bony dehiscence over artery
- Pre-operative identification of dehiscence reduces risk
- Management: immediate haemostatic resuscitation; pack sphenoid; direct repair (J-suture or muscle pack) or endovascular stent placement

| Complication | Incidence | Key Risk Factor |
|-------------|-----------|----------------|
| Minor (crusting, synechiae) | 5-15% | Mucosal stripping, inadequate post-op care |
| Haemorrhage (significant) | 0.5-2% | Uncontrolled bleeding, SPA/AEA injury |
| Orbital injury | 0.5-1% | Lamina papyracea breach, microdebrider |
| CSF leak | 0.5% | Cribriform plate injury, angled instruments |
| Optic nerve injury | Rare | Onodi cell, sphenoid surgery |
| Carotid artery injury | Very rare | Sphenoid surgery, bony dehiscence |
| Death | <0.01% | Carotid injury, massive haemorrhage |

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. CT scans in three planes are mandatory and must be available throughout the operation
> 2. Uncinectomy: sickle knife on inferior uncinate is discouraged (orbital penetration risk); use back-biting forceps
> 3. Ground lamella perforation: always in the inferomedial quadrant to avoid skull base and lamina papyracea
> 4. Maxillary sinus roof/orbital floor is always below the skull base - use as guide to superior limit of posterior ethmoid dissection
> 5. Circumferential mucosal injury in the frontal recess will cause stenosis and recurrent frontal sinusitis
> 6. Complication range: 0.3-22.4% (median 7.0%); image guidance significantly reduces complications
> 7. CSF leak prevalence: 0.5%; vertical lamella of cribriform plate is the highest risk area
> 8. If bloody field encountered and cannot be controlled: abandon procedure and plan second-stage surgery
> 9. Microdebrider is associated with increased risk of medial rectus injury; recognize orbital fat immediately
> 10. Through-biting instruments preferred over Blakesley-Wells forceps (latter tear mucosa leaving exposed bone)


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - Reverse Trendelenberg position produces 38.3% reduction in nasal blood flow vs only 4.7% from greater palatine canal injection
> - Ringer's lactate irrigations post-operatively are superior to isotonic or hypotonic saline (RCT evidence)
> - Eosinophils can persist in tissues up to 3 weeks; extended steroid course (3+ weeks, tapered) may be logical for extensive eosinophilic disease
> - Cognitive dissonance (Festinger): surgeons remember successes and repress failures - objective outcome measurement is essential
> - Revision rate: 19% at 5 years; 21% for CRSwNP vs 15% for CRSsNP (Royal College of Surgeons national audit)
