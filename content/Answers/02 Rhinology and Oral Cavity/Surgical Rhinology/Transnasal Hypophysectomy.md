# Transnasal Hypophysectomy

> [!question] Questions Covered
> - Describe in detail the technique of transnasal (endoscopic transsphenoidal) hypophysectomy.
*Source:** Scott-Brown's Otorhinolaryngology, 8th Edition - Volume 1, Chapters 54 (Pituitary Fossa), 84 (Pituitary Tumours) & 85 (Recurrent Pituitary Tumours)

---

## INTRODUCTION

Pituitary adenomas are the third most common intracranial tumour. Endoscopic transsphenoidal surgery has become the standard approach for the surgical management of pituitary adenomas. Gross total removal rates with the endoscopic endonasal approach (EEA) range from 77-96%, with the main factor limiting total resection being frank invasion of the cavernous sinus. Recurrence rates vary between 7-21%, with regrowth after incomplete removal reported in 39-75% of cases.

---

## PART A: INDICATIONS

### Indications for Surgery

- **Non-functioning adenomas** with mass effect (visual field defects, headache)
- **Cushing's disease** (ACTH-secreting): selective adenomectomy is first-line
- **Acromegaly/gigantism** (GH-secreting): when systemic effects persist
- **Prolactinomas:** Only when dopamine agonists fail or are not tolerated (microprolactinomas respond to medical therapy)
- **TSH-secreting adenomas**
- **Pituitary apoplexy** with visual compromise
- **Recurrent/residual tumours** after prior surgery

### Contraindications

- Active untreated sinonasal infection
- Pseudoaneurysm of ICA (must be excluded before revision surgery)
- Tumour confined to lateral cavernous sinus (consider radiation treatment)

---

## PART B: PRE-OPERATIVE EVALUATION

### Endocrine Assessment

Complete endocrine evaluation to establish baseline:
- Prolactin, GH, IGF-1
- Cortisol, ACTH
- TSH, free T3, total T4
- Testosterone, LH, FSH
- Assessment for diabetes insipidus (DI)

Patients with hypopituitarism require appropriate replacement, particularly if cortisol is low. If cortisol is normal, no steroid stress dose during anaesthesia (to avoid confusing post-operative values).

### Ophthalmological Assessment

- Formal visual field testing (Humphrey/Goldmann)
- Visual acuity, pupil reactions
- Establishes baseline for post-operative comparison

### Imaging

- **MRI (dedicated sellar and parasellar):** Tumour location, status of medial cavernous sinus wall, distance to optic apparatus, stalk deviation, degree of scarring
- **CT:** Bony landmarks, sphenoid pneumatization, septae orientation
- **CT angiography (CTA):** Required for revision surgery to exclude pseudoaneurysm; localizes ICAs
- **Image fusion:** MRI + CTA fusion provides optimal navigation for complex tumours

### Nasal Endoscopy

- Assesses sinonasal cavity for surgical approach
- Evaluates reconstructive options (nasoseptal flap availability)
- Rules out active infection

---

## PART C: SPHENOID SINUS ANATOMY (SURGICAL LANDMARKS)

### Pneumatization Types

| Type | Frequency | Surgical Implication |
|------|-----------|---------------------|
| Sellar | ~80% | Well pneumatized; sella bulges into sphenoid; ideal for surgery |
| Pre-sellar | ~20% | Posterior wall at anterior sella plane; may need navigation |
| Conchal | ~2% (up to 28% Asians) | No pneumatization; thick bone to drill |

### Critical Landmarks on Posterior Sphenoid Wall

1. **Pituitary fossa:** Midline bulge in posterosuperior sphenoid
2. **Paraclival carotid prominence:** Inferolateral to fossa
3. **Cavernous carotid prominence:** Lateral to fossa (C-shaped curve)
4. **Optic nerve prominence:** Superolateral to fossa
5. **Lateral opticocarotid recess (LOCR):** Between optic nerve and carotid; corresponds to anterior clinoid process
6. **Medial opticocarotid recess (MOCR):** Corresponds to medial clinoid

### Intra-sphenoid Septae

- Standard midline septum in only **14%** of cases
- In **87%**, at least one septum inserts near the carotid eminence posteriorly
- **Must use drill** (not rongeur) to remove septae safely to avoid fracturing ICA canal

### Key Measurements

- Inter-optic nerve distance: 12 mm
- Pituitary fossa width between carotid prominences: 21 mm
- Bony covering over sella: usually <0.5 mm
- Carotid artery dehiscence: up to 5% of cases

## PART D: SURGICAL TECHNIQUE

### Patient Positioning

- Supine with head slightly tilted to the right
- Reverse Trendelenberg position
- Head on horseshoe head holder (or Mayfield clamp if cavernous sinus invasion requiring EMG monitoring)
- Navigation mask and transmitter placed on patient's face
- Abdomen and lateral thigh prepared for potential fat or fascia lata graft

### Step 1: Nasal Phase (Creating the Sinonasal Corridor)

1. Topical decongestion (cocaine/adrenaline or co-phenylcaine)
2. Local anaesthetic infiltration of middle turbinate, septum, frontal process of maxilla
3. **Rescue flap** elevated: mucosa over sphenoid rostrum incised from lateral nasal wall at sphenoid ostium level, carried over the rostrum and anteriorly into septal mucosa (one-third to half of septum)
4. This preserves the nasoseptal flap pedicle (posterior septal artery from sphenopalatine artery) for potential use

### Step 2: Sphenoidotomy

1. Sphenoid ostium identified medial to the superior turbinate at level of antral roof
2. Sphenoid rostrum/keel completely removed or drilled down
3. All bony septations within the sphenoid sinus removed using **high-speed drill** (NOT rongeur - septae often insert on dehiscent ICA)
4. Wide exposure achieved from lateral wall to lateral wall with visualization of the LOCRs

### Step 3: Sellar Exposure

1. Face of the sella drilled until wall is very thin
2. Kerrison rongeur used as **dissector** to separate and lift bone off dura (avoid biting)
3. Bone that does not fracture with gentle pull indicates need for further drilling
4. Enough bone removed to expose the **"four blues":**
   - Lateral: walls of the cavernous sinuses (2)
   - Superior: superior intercavernous sinus
   - Inferior: inferior intercavernous sinus
5. Drilling of sellar floor provides ability to retract ICA laterally

### Step 4: Dural Opening

1. Feather blade (Mizuho) used to make incision starting in centre of sella, carried vertically downwards
2. Dura separated from underlying gland/tumour with ball probe
3. Incision extended laterally with 45° angled microscissors
4. **Superior cuts must be parallel to sphenoid floor** (avoid violation of arachnoid membrane of diaphragma sellae and subsequent [[CSF Rhinorrhea|CSF leak]])
5. Navigation and endonasal acoustic Doppler sonography used to confirm anatomy

### Step 5: Tumour Removal

**Soft tumours:**
- Two-suction technique for dissection and debridement

**Fibrous tumours:**
- Microscissors or side-cutting power instrument (Myriad, NICO)
- Ultrasonic aspirators are aggressive and may draw neurovascular structures

**Solid/extracapsular tumours:**
- Extracapsular dissection attempted if not invading cavernous sinus
- Must find compressed pituitary gland early to avoid inadvertent resection
- Cotton tip applicator used as atraumatic retractor to protect arachnoid membrane and identify tumour-gland interface

### Step 6: Reconstruction

**If no CSF leak:**
- Rescue flap repositioned; absorbable packing reapproximates pedicle to bony rostrum

**If CSF leak present (multilayer technique):**

| Layer | Material | Position |
|-------|----------|----------|
| 1st (subdural/epidural inlay) | Collagen matrix (Duragen) or fascia lata | Extends 5-10 mm beyond dural margins |
| 2nd (epidural inlay/extracranial onlay) | Acellular dermis or fascia lata | Between dura and skull base |
| 3rd (free tissue graft) | Free mucosal graft or free fat graft (abdomen) | Onlay |
| 4th (bolster) | Gelatin sponge squares + expandable sponge or Foley catheter balloon | Support for reconstruction |

- Nasoseptal flap (Hadad-Bassagasteguy) used for large defects requiring vascularized reconstruction
- All nasal packing removed 3-5 days after procedure

---

## PART E: POST-OPERATIVE MANAGEMENT

- Endocrine monitoring: cortisol levels, fluid balance (DI assessment)
- Visual field assessment: within 24-48 hours
- Nasal care: saline irrigations, no nose blowing
- Lumbar drain: considered in high-flow CSF leaks
- MRI: at 3 months post-operatively to assess residual tumour

### Complications

| Complication | Details |
|-------------|---------|
| CSF leak | 0.5-5%; multilayer repair; may need lumbar drain |
| Diabetes insipidus | Transient in 15-30%; permanent in 1-5% |
| Hypopituitarism | Risk increases with extent of resection |
| ICA injury | Catastrophic; pack sphenoid; endovascular stent |
| Visual deterioration | From haematoma or direct injury; urgent intervention |
| Meningitis | <1%; prophylactic antibiotics not routine |
| Sinonasal crusting | Common; managed with irrigation |
| [[Epistaxis Management|Epistaxis]] (SPA injury) | Rare; may need SPA ligation |

---

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. Endoscopic endonasal approach achieves gross total resection in 77-96% of primary pituitary adenomas
> 2. Frank cavernous sinus invasion is the main factor limiting total resection
> 3. Sphenoid septae must be drilled (not rongeured) as 87% insert near the carotid eminence
> 4. The "four blues" must be exposed: lateral cavernous sinus walls + superior and inferior intercavernous sinuses
> 5. Superior dural cuts must be parallel to sphenoid floor to avoid diaphragma sellae breach and CSF leak
> 6. Rescue flap technique preserves the nasoseptal flap pedicle for potential vascularized reconstruction
> 7. Extracapsular dissection is safer than powered instrumentation for solid tumours; find the gland early
> 8. Cotton tip applicator is a practical atraumatic retractor that helps identify the tumour-gland interface
> 9. Multilayer reconstruction is critical: complete defect coverage with inlay + onlay grafts
> 10. CTA mandatory before revision surgery to exclude ICA pseudoaneurysm


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - In revision surgery, fat grafts from primary surgery become dense fibrous scar requiring sharp dissection - the two-suction technique will not work
> - The rescue flap is a modification of the nasoseptal flap that avoids donor site morbidity while preserving future flap availability
> - Bone that does not fracture with gentle Kerrison pull needs more drilling - forced biting risks ICA laceration
> - Navigation is especially useful in revision cases where normal landmarks are distorted
> - Prepare abdomen (fat graft) and lateral thigh (fascia lata) routinely in all cases
