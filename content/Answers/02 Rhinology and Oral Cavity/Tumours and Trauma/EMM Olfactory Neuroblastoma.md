# Endoscopic Medial Maxillectomy and Olfactory Neuroblastoma

> [!question] Questions Covered
> - "Classify sinonasal tumours by tissue of origin. Describe indications, procedure, and limitations of endoscopic medial maxillectomy. Discuss olfactory neuroblastoma — etiopathogenesis, clinical features, staging, and management."
*Source:** Scott-Brown's Volume 1, Chapters 114 (Endoscopic Management of Sinonasal Tumours), 116 (Extended Anterior Skull Base Approaches), 117 (Radiology)

---

## Introduction

Sinonasal tumours encompass a large and diverse group of lesions arising from any of the structures comprising the paranasal sinuses. They account for approximately 3–5% of all head and neck cancers, with an estimated incidence of sinonasal malignancies of 0.5–1 per 100,000. The endoscopic approach has emerged as a favourable treatment option for both benign and malignant sinonasal tumours, with evidence supporting equivalent survival outcomes and reduced morbidity compared to open approaches. Endoscopic medial maxillectomy is a key surgical technique for accessing maxillary sinus tumours. Olfactory neuroblastoma (esthesioneuroblastoma) is an uncommon neuroectodermal tumour arising from the olfactory epithelium that requires specialized skull base surgical approaches.

---

## Part A — Classification of Sinonasal Tumours

Sinonasal tumours are classified by tissue of origin (Table 114.1):

| Tissue of Origin | Benign | Malignant |
|-------------------|--------|-----------|
| Epithelial | IP, oncocytic papilloma, REAH, salivary adenomas | SCC, SNUC, adenocarcinoma, ACC, MEC |
| Neuroendocrine | — | Carcinoid |
| Soft tissue | Myxoma, leiomyoma, haemangioma, schwannoma, meningioma, angiofibroma | Fibrosarcoma, RMS, angiosarcoma, MPNST |
| Bone/cartilage | FD, osteoma, osteoblastoma, chondroma, ameloblastoma | Chondrosarcoma, osteosarcoma, chordoma |
| Haematological | — | Lymphoma, LCH |
| Germ cell | Dermoid cyst | Teratoma, yolk sac tumour |
| Neuroectodermal | — | Esthesioneuroblastoma |

**Epidemiology:**
- Most common benign lesion: sinonasal **osteoma** (radiological incidence 1%)
- Ossifying fibroma, fibrous dysplasia, and [[Schneiderian Papilloma|inverted papilloma]] are the next most common benign lesions
- Most common malignant type: primary **epithelial** tumours — SCC is the most common subtype, followed by adenoid cystic carcinoma and adenocarcinoma
- Among non-epithelial malignancies, **lymphoma** is the most commonly reported

## Part B — Endoscopic Medial Maxillectomy (EMM)

### Indications

- Inverted papilloma with maxillary sinus involvement
- Benign tumours of the maxillary sinus (osteoma, fibrous dysplasia)
- Selected maxillary sinus malignancies with medial wall origin
- Access to pterygopalatine and infratemporal fossa tumours
- [[Juvenile Nasopharyngeal Angiofibroma|Juvenile nasopharyngeal angiofibroma]] with maxillary extension

### Surgical Steps

The endoscopic medial maxillectomy is a stepwise procedure:

1. **Uncinectomy:** Identify and remove the uncinate process to locate the natural maxillary ostium
2. **Wide antrostomy:** Enlarge the maxillary ostium posteriorly (mega-antrostomy) for posteriorly based lesions medial to the infraorbital nerve
3. **Inferior turbinate transection:** Divide the inferior turbinate at its attachment to facilitate inferior access
4. **Nasolacrimal duct management:** In modified EMM, extend the mega-antrostomy inferiorly below Hasner's valve to the piriform aperture — this preserves the lacrimal duct. For full EMM, the nasolacrimal duct is sacrificed
5. **Pre-lacrimal approach (Zhou modification):** For anterior and lateral wall access — remove bone at the junction of anteromedial maxillary walls adjacent to the pre-lacrimal recess, preserving the nasolacrimal duct intact
6. **Tumour resection:** En bloc excision of the site of origin with a cuff of normal tissue; remainder can be debulked down to attachment points
7. **Skull base reconstruction** if dural involvement

### Improving Lateral Access

- **Transeptal approach** (Douglas technique): Non-opposing septal incisions allow use of contralateral nostril for instruments, increasing the horizontal plane of access
- Angled endoscopes (30°, 45°, 70°) and curved instrumentation enhance lateral visualization
- Two-surgeon technique provides retraction and additional instrumentation

### Limitations and Contraindications

Relative contraindications to a purely endoscopic approach include:
- Involvement of **skin and subcutaneous tissue**
- Invasion of the **nasolacrimal sac**
- **Carotid artery** involvement
- **Anterior table of the [[Frontal Recess Frontal Sinus Surgery|frontal sinus]]** invasion
- Extensive dural and brain involvement
- Tumours **lateral to the infraorbital nerve** require more extensive medial wall resection
- **Steep learning curve** — surgeons should progress from inflammatory disease to benign tumours before attempting malignant resections

## Part C — Olfactory Neuroblastoma — Etiopathogenesis and Features

### Etiopathogenesis

Olfactory neuroblastoma (ONB), also called esthesioneuroblastoma, is a rare neuroectodermal tumour arising from the **olfactory neuroepithelium** (basal reserve cells of the olfactory mucosa). It accounts for approximately 3–5% of sinonasal malignancies. The tumour shows a bimodal age distribution with peaks in the **2nd and 6th decades**. There is no significant sex predilection.

The tumour typically originates **medial to the middle turbinate** in the olfactory cleft region. It is a slow-growing tumour with a tendency for local recurrence and delayed metastasis.

### Clinical Features

- **Unilateral nasal obstruction** — most common presenting symptom
- **[[Epistaxis Management|Epistaxis]]** — frequent
- **Anosmia or hyposmia** — due to olfactory epithelium involvement
- **Rhinorrhoea** — may include [[CSF Rhinorrhea|CSF rhinorrhoea]] if skull base involved
- **Orbital symptoms:** diplopia, proptosis (lateral orbital extension)
- **Headache and personality changes** (intracranial extension)
- Advanced tumours may cause cranial nerve palsies

## Part D — ONB Staging and Grading

### Kadish Staging System

| Stage | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| A | Tumour confined to nasal cavity |
| B | Tumour extending to paranasal sinuses |
| C | Tumour extending beyond PNS (orbit, skull base, intracranial) |
| D | Cervical lymph node or distant metastases (Morita modification) |

### Hyams Histological Grading

| Grade | Architecture | Mitoses | Necrosis | Calcification |
|-------|-------------|---------|----------|---------------|
| I | Lobular, preserved | None | None | Frequent |
| II | Lobular, preserved | Present | None | Variable |
| III | Some lobular | Prominent | Present | Rare |
| IV | No lobular pattern | Frequent | Prominent | Absent |

- Low-grade (I–II): better prognosis, higher differentiation
- High-grade (III–IV): aggressive, poor prognosis, resemble other neuroendocrine carcinomas

### Dulguerov TNM Staging

- T1: nasal cavity and/or paranasal sinuses (excluding sphenoid), sparing the most superior ethmoidal cells
- T2: involving the nasal cavity and/or paranasal sinuses (including sphenoid) with extension to or erosion of the cribriform plate
- T3: extending into the orbit or protruding into the anterior cranial fossa
- T4: involving the brain

## Part E — ONB Management

### Surgical Management

**Craniofacial resection** has been the gold standard treatment, combining an anterior craniotomy with a transfacial approach for en bloc resection.

**Endoscopic endonasal surgery (EES)** is now increasingly used for selected cases:

- A **transcribriform approach** is the most common approach for ONB
- Origin medial to the middle turbinate is typical — amenable to endoscopic access
- EES is combined with transfrontal and transplanum approaches for adequate margins
- A meta-analysis of 23 studies (361 patients) demonstrated **higher published survival rates for endoscopic surgery** compared to open surgery, though endoscopic series had earlier stage disease
- **Brain invasion is not necessarily a contraindication** — if a pial or cortical margin can be achieved without sacrificing major cerebral vasculature, the tumour is operable
- Intra-operative navigation and neurophysiological monitoring are essential

### Adjuvant Therapy

- **[[Radiotherapy Principles Fractionation Advances|Radiotherapy]]:** Adjuvant radiation is recommended for Kadish B and C tumours; improves local control
- **Chemotherapy:** Reserved for advanced or unresectable disease; often combined with radiotherapy (radiochemotherapy)
- **Palliative EES:** For unresectable recurrences — minimal morbidity, opportunity for multiple outpatient procedures

### Reconstruction

- **Nasoseptal flap (Haddad):** Vascularized [[Pedicled Flaps Skin Grafts|pedicled flap]] — workhorse for anterior skull base reconstruction
- **Pericranial flap:** When nasoseptal flap is unavailable (e.g., post septectomy)
- CSF leak is the most common post-operative complication (decreased with nasoseptal flap introduction)

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. Sinonasal tumours are classified by tissue of origin into 7 categories — epithelial tumours are the most common malignant type
> 2. SCC is the most common sinonasal malignancy; osteoma is the most common benign tumour
> 3. Endoscopic medial maxillectomy provides access to maxillary sinus and pterygopalatine fossa tumours
> 4. Relative contraindications to endoscopic resection include skin invasion, carotid involvement, and extensive dural/brain involvement
> 5. The pre-lacrimal approach (Zhou) allows access to anterior and lateral maxillary walls while preserving the nasolacrimal duct
> 6. Olfactory neuroblastoma arises from olfactory neuroepithelium, typically medial to the middle turbinate
> 7. Kadish staging: A (nasal cavity), B (PNS), C (beyond PNS), D (nodes/mets — Morita modification)
> 8. Hyams grading (I–IV) is based on lobular architecture, mitoses, necrosis, and calcification
> 9. Transcribriform endoscopic approach is the standard EES technique for ONB resection
> 10. Meta-analysis shows higher published survival rates for endoscopic vs open surgery for ONB, though with stage selection bias


> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - **Always obtain imaging before biopsy** of a sinonasal mass — to avoid catastrophic complications (torrential bleeding from JNA, CSF leak from encephalocele)
> - **Origination medial to the middle turbinate** on endoscopy is typical of olfactory neuroblastoma
> - **Tumour calcification** on CT is seen with esthesioneuroblastoma, chondroma, chondrosarcoma, and osteoma
> - **En bloc excision of the site of origin** is critical — the remainder of the tumour can be debulked without compromising oncological outcomes
> - **Brain invasion does not preclude surgery** if a pial margin can be achieved without sacrificing major cerebral vasculature
> - **Two-surgeon technique** with transeptal approach significantly improves endoscopic access to lateral sinonasal tumours
