# Maxillary Sinus Malignancy

**Question:** *Discuss the etiopathogenesis, staging, and management of maxillary sinus malignancy in detail.*

**Source:** Scott-Brown's Otorhinolaryngology, 8th Edition - Volume 3, Chapter 7 (Nasal Cavity and Paranasal Sinus Malignancy)

---

## INTRODUCTION

Malignancies of the nose and paranasal sinuses are rare, accounting for approximately 3% of all head and neck cancers. Maxillary sinus tumours are the most common (55%), followed by nasal cavity (35%), ethmoid sinuses (9%) and rarely frontal and sphenoid sinuses (1%). Most patients still present with advanced disease. Only 25% of maxillary sinus carcinomas are contained within the antrum at the time of presentation.

---

## PART A: ETIOPATHOGENESIS

### Histological Types

**Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC):**
- Most common sinonasal malignancy
- Peak incidence: 7th decade; male preponderance
- 85% are well differentiated
- Transformation from Schneiderian papillomas: 14.6% incidence of SCC (Batsakis)

**Adenocarcinoma:**
- 9% of sinonasal malignancies
- Found in upper nasal cavity and ethmoid sinuses
- Subtypes: papillary (least aggressive), sessile, mucoid, intestinal, undifferentiated
- Intestinal type: most often associated with woodwork exposure

**Adenoid cystic carcinoma (ACC):**
- <5% of sinonasal malignancies
- Slow but inexorable growth with early perineural and vascular spread
- Maxillary sinus most common site; long history of facial pain

**Other types:** Olfactory neuroblastoma, SNUC, melanoma, haemangiopericytoma

### Aetiological Factors

| Factor | Associated Tumour Type | Risk |
|--------|----------------------|------|
| Hardwood dust | Adenocarcinoma (ethmoid) | 70x increased incidence |
| Nickel | SCC | 250-fold increase |
| Softwood dust | SCC | Increased risk |
| Smoking | SCC | Synergistic with wood dust |
| African mahogany (burning) | Maxillary SCC | Highest incidence (Bantu) |
| Chromium, polycyclic hydrocarbons | SCC | Occupational exposure |
| Aflatoxin, mustard gas, thorotrast | Various | Occupational/environmental |

- Hardwood exposure is a **prescribed occupational disease** under UK 1959 National Insurance Regulations (Reference D6)
- Latent interval between nickel exposure and tumour: 18-36 years (Pedersen)
- No evidence that chronic sinusitis predisposes to cancer

### Ohngren's Line

A historical classification drawing an imaginary line from the medial canthus to the angle of the mandible:
- **Above the line** (infrastructure): Worse prognosis; tumours more likely to be poorly differentiated
- **Below the line** (suprastructure): Better prognosis; more amenable to treatment

This classification was described before craniofacial resection was developed and is now largely of historical interest.

---

## PART B: PATTERNS OF SPREAD

### Local Invasion

Sinonasal carcinomas tend to fill the sinus cavity before eroding bony walls. Periosteum, perichondrium and dura act as temporary barriers due to fibroelastic connective tissue.

**Maxillary sinus tumour spread:**

| Direction | Structures Involved |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Medially | Lateral wall of nasal cavity |
| Laterally | Cheek, skin |
| Superiorly | Orbit (through inferior orbital fissure) |
| Inferiorly | Palate, alveolus |
| Posteriorly | Pterygopalatine fossa, infratemporal fossa |
| Anteriorly | Anterior maxillary wall, cheek skin |

**Diagram Description - Maxillary Sinus Spread:**
```
                    ORBIT (superior)
                        |
    NASAL CAVITY ---- MAXILLARY ---- CHEEK (lateral)
     (medial)         SINUS
                        |
                    PALATE (inferior)
                        |
          PTERYGOPALATINE FOSSA (posterior)
              |
      INFRATEMPORAL FOSSA → TEMPORAL BONE
              → MIDDLE CRANIAL FOSSA
```

### Lymphatic Drainage

- Relatively scant lymphatic drainage of the nose and sinuses
- Two pathways: anterior (facial, parotid, submandibular nodes) and posterior (retropharyngeal nodes → upper deep cervical chain)
- 10% present with nodal disease; 25-35% develop nodal disease during the course of disease
- Bilateral involvement possible for midline tumours

### Distant Metastases

- Unusual at presentation; develop in 20% of adenocarcinomas vs 10% of SCCs
- Most common sites: bone, brain, liver, lung, skin
- Usually associated with local recurrence

## PART C: TNM STAGING (MAXILLARY SINUS)

| Stage | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| T1 | Tumour limited to antral mucosa with no bone erosion |
| T2 | Bone erosion/destruction except posterior wall; includes extension into hard palate and/or middle meatus |
| T3 | Invades posterior maxillary wall, subcutaneous tissues, skin, floor/medial wall of orbit, infratemporal fossa, pterygoid plates, ethmoid sinus |
| T4a | Invades anterior orbital contents, skin of cheek, pterygoid plates, infratemporal fossa, cribriform plate, sphenoid or [[Frontal Recess Frontal Sinus Surgery|frontal sinus]]es |
| T4b | Invades orbital apex, dura, brain, middle cranial fossa, cranial nerves other than V2, nasopharynx, clivus |

### Staging Matrix

| Stage | TNM |
|-------|-----|
| I | T1 N0 M0 |
| II | T2 N0 M0 |
| III | T1/2 N1 M0 or T3 N0/1 M0 |
| IVa | T1/2/3 N2 M0 or T4a N0/1/2 M0 |
| IVb | T4b any N M0 or any T N3 M0 |
| IVc | Any T any N M1 |

## PART D: CLINICAL PRESENTATION

### Maxillary Sinus Carcinoma

- **Facial pain** with progressive sensory change (infraorbital nerve infiltration)
- **[[Epistaxis Management|Epistaxis]] and epiphora** from erosion of medial wall (nasolacrimal duct obstruction)
- **Trismus** from spread into pterygopalatine and infratemporal fossae (V2 and V3 deficits)
- **Oral mass and tooth mobility** from inferior spread
- **Proptosis and diplopia** from superior orbital spread
- **Cheek swelling/distortion** from anterolateral wall breach

### Clinical Evaluation

**Endoscopy:** Thorough nasal endoscopy mandatory. Some tumours produce polypoid mucosal reaction; casual outpatient biopsy may be non-diagnostic.

**Imaging:**
- **CT:** Bone erosion detail, skull base involvement; axial and coronal views necessary
- **MRI:** Soft tissue detail, dural/cerebral infiltration, orbital invasion assessment; T2 and diffusion-weighted sequences distinguish tumour from retained secretions
- **PET/CT:** Not usually necessary for primary staging but useful for excluding distant disease (melanoma) or identifying unknown primary
- **Arteriography:** Rarely required; considered for pre-operative embolization of vascular tumours

**Biopsy:** Mandatory. General anaesthesia preferred (reduces non-diagnostic samples). Caldwell-Luc approach should be avoided if it would seed tumour or compromise subsequent resection.

## PART E: MANAGEMENT

### General Principles

- Patients presenting with T1 tumours may be cured with surgery alone
- Majority require combination therapy: surgery + [[Radiotherapy Principles Fractionation Advances|radiotherapy]] ± chemotherapy
- Post-operative radiotherapy should start within **6 weeks** of surgery: 60-65 Gy over 30 fractions
- IMRT techniques adopted to achieve tumour coverage while meeting organ-at-risk constraints
- MDT discussion essential for treatment planning

### Surgical Procedures

**Partial maxillectomy:**
- Medial maxillectomy: clearance of lateral nasal wall including ethmoid sinuses
- Palatal resection: for tumours involving hard palate

**Total maxillectomy:**
- Total removal of upper jaw as a bony box containing the tumour

**Extended maxillectomy:**
- When tumour extends beyond upper jaw
- If involving skull base: termed **craniofacial resection**

### Surgical Approaches

| Approach | Access | Indication |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| Lateral rhinotomy | Nasal cavities, medial maxilla | Medial maxillectomy; cosmetically acceptable incision |
| Weber-Fergusson | Full anterior maxilla, orbit | Total/extended maxillectomy; transverse limb in first lid crease |
| Midfacial degloving | Bilateral maxilla, central skull base | No external incision; sublabial + intercartilaginous incisions |

### Maxillectomy Technique (Weber-Fergusson)

1. Facial skin flap raised in submuscular plane
2. **Osteotomies:** Body of zygoma, midline palate, pterygoid plates (curved osteotome)
3. Medial osteotomy through ethmoid cells and frontal process of maxilla
4. Lateral osteotomy through body of zygoma (or lateral orbital wall if zygoma involved)
5. Remaining posterior ethmoid and antral roof attachments break on mobilizing maxilla
6. Bleeding from internal maxillary artery controlled by packing, Ligaclip, diathermy

### Orbital Management

- If orbital periosteum is uninvolved: globe can be preserved
- If periosteum is obviously involved: orbital exenteration generally indicated
- Nearly all medial and inferior orbital walls can be removed without enophthalmos
- Removal of Whitnall's tubercle results in lack of globe support (correctable with temporalis muscle transposition)

### Neck Management

- Occult metastasis rate <10%
- No evidence that elective selective [[Neck Dissection|neck dissection]] improves neck control or overall survival
- Therapeutic neck dissection for clinically positive nodes

### Craniofacial Resection

For ethmoid tumours involving the cribriform plate:
- Combined intracranial and transfacial approach (Ketcham/Cheesman)
- Type 1: Craniofacial (transorbital) resection - extended medial maxillectomy
- Type 2: Standard craniofacial resection - frontal craniotomy + lateral rhinotomy
- Type 3: Extended craniofacial resection - includes orbital exenteration

### Endoscopic Resection

Entirely endoscopic approach using appropriate drills and debriders can achieve equivalent results to external approaches for selected medial maxillectomy cases.

### Adjuvant Treatment

- **Post-operative radiotherapy:** 60-65 Gy; IMRT preferred; start within 6 weeks
- **Chemotherapy:** Selected cases; platinum-based concurrent with radiotherapy for SCC and SNUC
- **Proton beam radiotherapy:** Increasing experience; may reduce radiation-related morbidity
- **5-Fluorouracil topical:** Advocated by Knegt for ethmoid adenocarcinoma
- Large volume tumours: initial (chemo)radiotherapy followed by surgery may give better results

### Reconstruction and Rehabilitation

- Low defects: dental obturators (allow visualization during surveillance)
- Extensive defects: [[Free Flaps HN Reconstruction|free flap]] reconstruction (iliac crest with internal oblique, or scapula tip with latissimus dorsi)
- Titanium implants for facial prostheses (radiotherapy not a contraindication)
- Split-skin graft to undersurface of facial skin flap; primary prosthesis secured with circumzygomatic wires

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. Maxillary sinus tumours are the commonest sinonasal malignancy (55%); only 25% are contained within the antrum at presentation
> 2. Hardwood dust exposure increases adenocarcinoma risk 70-fold; nickel increases SCC risk 250-fold; both are prescribed occupational diseases
> 3. T4b (orbital apex, dura, brain, middle cranial fossa) tumours are generally considered unresectable
> 4. Post-operative RT: 60-65 Gy over 30 fractions; should commence within 6 weeks of surgery
> 5. Weber-Fergusson approach is the standard for total maxillectomy; midfacial degloving avoids external incision
> 6. Occult cervical metastasis rate <10%; elective neck dissection not proven beneficial
> 7. Orbital exenteration indicated when orbital periosteum is obviously involved by tumour
> 8. Craniofacial resection is required when ethmoid/maxillary tumours involve the cribriform plate
> 9. CT for bone detail; MRI for soft tissue/dural involvement; T2 sequences distinguish tumour from retained secretions
> 10. Schneiderian papilloma SCC transformation rate: 14.6% (Batsakis)


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - Caldwell-Luc approach should be avoided for biopsy if it would seed tumour or compromise resection margins
> - Ohngren's line is now largely of historical interest given advances in craniofacial surgery and radiation oncology
> - Latent interval for nickel-related tumours: 18-36 years; always take detailed occupational history
> - Adenoid cystic carcinoma patients may survive for extended periods even with pulmonary metastases if the primary site remains controlled
> - Removal of Whitnall's tubercle results in loss of globe support; plan temporalis muscle transposition if lateral orbital wall must be included
