# Schneiderian Papilloma (sinonasal Papilloma)

**Question:** *Describe the etiopathogenesis, investigations and surgical management of Schneiderian tumour.*

**Source:** Scott-Brown's Otorhinolaryngology, 8th Edition - Volume 1, Chapters 99, 114 & Volume 3, Chapters 7, 26

---

## INTRODUCTION

Sinonasal papillomas arise from the ectodermally-derived ciliated epithelium of the nasal cavity, termed the Schneiderian membrane. They are relatively uncommon benign epithelial tumours but are clinically important due to their locally aggressive nature, tendency to recur and association with malignancy. Inverted papilloma is the most commonly reported benign lesion of the paranasal sinuses after sinonasal osteoma. Schneiderian papillomas are classified as epithelial tumours of the sinonasal tract and are listed separately from squamous cell papillomas of other head and neck sites.

---

## PART A: CLASSIFICATION AND ETIOPATHOGENESIS

### Three Types of Schneiderian Papilloma

There are three morphologically distinct types of papilloma arising from the Schneiderian membrane:

| Feature | Exophytic (Fungiform) | Inverted | Oncocytic (Cylindrical Cell) |
|---------|----------------------|----------|------------------------------|
| Growth pattern | Exophytic, papillomatous | Complex endo-exophytic, inverting into stroma | Exophytic fronds |
| Common site | Nasal septum, nasal vestibule | Lateral nasal wall, ethmoid, maxillary sinus | Lateral nasal wall, sinuses |
| HPV association | Yes (HPV 6, 11) | Variable (approx one-third HPV positive, HPV 6, 11) | Not associated |
| Malignant transformation | Uncommon | Increases with each recurrence | 3-17% harbour carcinoma |
| Histology | Resembles verruca vulgaris, hyperkeratotic | Non-keratinizing squamous/transitional epithelium with intra-epithelial microabscesses | Bi-layered oncocytic epithelium with microabscesses |
| Basement membrane | Intact | Distinct and intact | Intact |

### Inverted Papilloma (Most Common and Clinically Significant)

**Epidemiology:** Male predilection. Associated with malignancy - Batsakis reported a 14.6% incidence of SCC in a series of 322 patients with Schneiderian papillomas. Transformation of Schneiderian papillomas into SCC is a recognized risk.

**Site of origin:** The commonest sites of origin are the ethmoid region (48%), lateral nasal wall and maxillary sinus (28%), with the [[Frontal Recess Frontal Sinus Surgery|frontal sinus]] accounting for only 2.5%. Bilateral frontal sinus involvement is not uncommon (16%).

**Histology:** The tumour is characterized by epithelium inverting into the stroma with a distinct and intact basement membrane. The epithelium may be squamous (usually non-keratinizing), respiratory, glandular, transitional cell-like or a mixture. Numerous intra-epithelial microabscesses are characteristic and stain for macrophage markers (CD68).

### Etiopathogenesis

**Human Papillomavirus (HPV):**

HPV has been implicated in the pathogenesis. Viral DNA has been found in the adjacent normal-appearing tissue, which may account for the associated recurrence rates, although in many cases recurrence is due to residual disease. Around a third of inverted papillomas are considered HPV-positive, usually with HPV 6 and HPV 11.

**Key pathological features:**

- Locally aggressive behaviour with bone erosion
- Tendency for multicentric origin - multiple sites within the paranasal sinuses may be involved
- Synchronous or metachronous multicentric presentation
- With each recrudescence (recurrence), the likelihood of malignant transformation increases
- Recurrence rates: 3% for endoscopic management of IP in non-frontal locations; 22% for frontal sinus IP (higher due to narrow confines and proximity to skull base)

**Malignant Transformation:**

- SCC is the most common associated malignancy
- Risk increases with number of recurrences
- 14.6% incidence of SCC reported in Schneiderian papillomas
- Oncocytic papillomas: 3-17% harbour a carcinoma - judicious sampling of biopsy material is essential
- Genetic anomalies in inverted sinonasal papillomas may lead to a more rational molecular treatment plan

## PART B: INVESTIGATIONS

### Clinical Assessment

- Complete history and otorhinological examination
- Nasal endoscopy - assess location, extent, site of origin, involvement of septum, orbit, palate, nasopharynx, skull base
- Complete cranial nerve assessment
- Document likely site of origin and destruction of adjacent structures

### Radiology

**CT Scan:**

- Primary mode of imaging
- High-resolution CT with contrast, 1 mm slice thickness
- Demonstrates bone erosion and sinus anatomy
- **Focal hyperostosis (osteitic changes)** on CT is a common finding and may be helpful in identifying the site of tumour origin
- Assess for: breach of lamina papyracea, skull base defects, widened foramina (perineural invasion), septum erosion

**MRI:**

- Complementary to CT and recommended
- Superior soft-tissue resolution
- Very useful in distinguishing mucus from papilloma, especially in the frontal sinus where tumour can obstruct the outflow tract
- Characteristic **cerebriform pattern** (striations) visible on T2 fat-suppressed and post-contrast sequences
- Differentiates tumour from retained secretions
- T1- and T2-weighted images with gadolinium contrast and fat saturation

### Biopsy

- Should be performed AFTER review of relevant radiological investigation to avoid catastrophic complications (bleeding or [[CSF Rhinorrhea|CSF leak]])
- Multiple biopsies from different areas to rule out co-existing malignancy
- For oncocytic papilloma: judicious sampling is essential as 3-17% harbour carcinoma

## PART C: SURGICAL MANAGEMENT

### Principles of Surgery

The success of surgery is dependent on:

1. Defining the extent of disease
2. Identifying the site of tumour origin and attachments
3. Fully extirpating all affected tissue including removal of underlying mucoperiosteum and burring the bone at the site of origin

En bloc resection is not necessary as long as final resection margins are negative. Endoscopic surgery provides better visualization of resection margins, no transgression of normal tissue planes, absence of frontal lobe manipulation, and faster recovery.

### Endoscopic Approaches

**For lateral nasal wall and ethmoid/maxillary IP:**

- Endoscopic medial maxillectomy - provides access to the medial and posterolateral maxillary sinus
- Pre-lacrimal approach - provides excellent visualization and access to anterolateral aspects of maxillary sinus while preserving the inferior turbinate (less post-operative crusting than medial maxillectomy)
- After tumour removal, the underlying bone at the site of origin is drilled down and cauterized to reduce recurrence

**For frontal sinus IP (stepwise approach):**

| Site of Origin | Surgical Approach |
|---------------|-------------------|
| Unifocal, medial wall or lower AW/PW | Draf type IIa/b or MELP |
| Unifocal/multifocal, superior AW/PW/lateral wall | MELP with external trephine |
| Lateral to midpoint of orbit | Osteoplastic flap (OPF) with MELP and external trephine |

- MELP = Modified Endoscopic Lothrop Procedure (Draf III)
- AW = anterior wall; PW = posterior wall; MW = medial wall; LW = lateral wall

**Stepwise surgical approach for frontal sinus tumours:**

1. Endonasal (including modified Lothrop procedures)
2. Osteoplastic flap (bi-coronal incision, mid forehead, brow)
3. Above and below (endoscopic and frontal craniotomy)
4. Subcranial, subfrontal and transcranial

More aggressive primary approaches may facilitate better disease control.

### For Tumours with Skull Base Involvement

- Combined transcribriform and transplanum approaches as described for extended endonasal surgery
- Posterior septectomy with nasoseptal flap preservation
- Two-surgeon team approach for binarial access
- Intra-operative navigation for critical structure identification
- Reconstruction with nasoseptal flap if dural defect present

### Special Considerations

**Maxillary sinus IP arising from the roof (medial to infraorbital nerve):** Area of osteitis often indicates the site of origin on CT. After tumour removal, the site of origin is drilled down and cauterized.

**Anterolateral maxillary sinus origin:** Despite a previous antrostomy, the site of origin may not be accessible, leading to recurrence. Pre-lacrimal approach provides excellent access to this region.

**Nasoseptal flap:** If a CSF leak is anticipated during skull base surgery for IP, the nasoseptal flap is raised before performing septectomy and stored in the nasopharynx.

### Post-operative Follow-up

- Minimum follow-up of 3 years is recommended
- Where tumour removal was performed via MELP, direct endoscopic surveillance can be performed
- When direct visualization is not possible, MRI is recommended for surveillance
- Serial endoscopic examination for early detection of recurrence

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. Schneiderian papillomas arise from the ectodermally-derived ciliated epithelium (Schneiderian membrane) of the nasal cavity
> 2. Three types: exophytic (septal, HPV 6/11), inverted (lateral wall/ethmoid, locally aggressive), oncocytic (no HPV, 3-17% harbour carcinoma)
> 3. Inverted papilloma: epithelium inverts into stroma with intact basement membrane; intra-epithelial microabscesses are characteristic
> 4. 14.6% incidence of SCC transformation in Schneiderian papillomas (Batsakis)
> 5. Commonest sites: ethmoid (48%), lateral nasal wall/maxillary sinus (28%), frontal sinus (2.5%)
> 6. Focal hyperostosis on CT suggests site of tumour origin; MRI shows characteristic cerebriform pattern
> 7. Always review imaging BEFORE biopsy to avoid catastrophic complications
> 8. Surgical success depends on identifying site of origin, complete excision including mucoperiosteum, and bone drilling at origin
> 9. Endoscopic recurrence rate: 3% for non-frontal, 22% for frontal sinus IP
> 10. En bloc resection is not necessary - negative final margins are the goal
> 11. More aggressive primary approaches for frontal sinus IP facilitate better disease control
> 12. Minimum 3-year follow-up with endoscopic surveillance or MRI


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - HPV DNA in adjacent normal tissue explains recurrence even after apparently complete excision
> - Malignant transformation risk increases with each recurrence - thorough primary surgery is critical
> - The pre-lacrimal approach preserves the inferior turbinate and provides better access to the anterolateral maxillary sinus than medial maxillectomy
> - Cerebriform pattern on MRI (T2 fat-suppressed) is characteristic of inverted papilloma and helps differentiate from retained secretions
> - Osteitic changes on CT are a reliable indicator of the site of tumour origin and guide the extent of bone drilling
> - For oncocytic papillomas, always perform judicious sampling to exclude co-existing carcinoma
