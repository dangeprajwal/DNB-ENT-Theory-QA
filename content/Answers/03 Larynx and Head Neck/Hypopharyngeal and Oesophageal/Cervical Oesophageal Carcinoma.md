# Cervical Oesophageal Carcinoma

> [!question] Questions Covered
> - Discuss the clinical features, investigations, and management of cervical oesophageal carcinoma.
*Source:** Scott-Brown's Otorhinolaryngology, 8th Edition - Volume 3, Chapter 16 (Management of Hypopharyngeal Cancer), Chapter 47 (Anatomy of the Pharynx and Oesophagus), Chapter 53 (Oesophageal Diseases), Chapter 93 (Reconstructive Microsurgery)

---

## INTRODUCTION

Cervical oesophageal carcinoma involves the uppermost segment of the oesophagus, extending from the lower border of the cricoid cartilage (C6) to the thoracic inlet. It is intimately related to the postcricoid region of the hypopharynx superiorly, making it clinically inseparable from hypopharyngeal cancer in many cases. The oesophagus lacks a serosal covering, which facilitates direct tumour spread and complicates surgical resection. Management often requires total laryngopharyngo-oesophagectomy (TLPE) with complex reconstruction. SCC predominates in this region and is more sensitive to chemo[[Radiotherapy Principles Fractionation Advances|radiotherapy]] than adenocarcinoma.

---

## PART A: SURGICAL ANATOMY OF THE CERVICAL OESOPHAGUS

### Boundaries and Relations

- **Superior:** Lower border of cricoid cartilage at C6 (pharyngo-oesophageal junction)
- **Inferior:** Thoracic inlet (suprasternal notch, approximately T1)
- **Anterior:** Trachea (adherent via loose connective tissue)
- **Posterior:** Vertebral column (C6-C7), prevertebral fascia, longus colli muscles
- **Lateral:** Common carotid arteries within carotid sheaths; lower poles of thyroid gland
- **Left lateral:** Thoracic duct ascending to subclavian-IJV junction

The recurrent laryngeal nerves pass superiorly in the groove between trachea and oesophagus bilaterally.

### Wall Structure

| Layer | Details |
|-------|---------|
| Mucosa | Non-keratinized stratified squamous epithelium |
| Submucosa | Contains extensive lymphatic plexus |
| Muscularis propria | Proximal third: striated muscle exclusively |
| Adventitia | NO serosal covering — facilitates tumour spread |

The absence of a serosal layer is a critical feature — it allows easy direct extension and makes surgical repair after perforation difficult.

### Blood Supply and Innervation

- **Arterial:** Inferior thyroid artery (from thyrocervical trunk)
- **Venous:** Inferior thyroid veins via brachiocephalic veins
- **Motor/sensory:** Recurrent laryngeal nerves + postganglionic sympathetic fibres from middle cervical ganglia
- **Constrictions:** Narrowest point at cricopharyngeal sphincter (15 cm from incisors)

### Lymphatic Drainage

- Extensive submucosal lymphatic system within the oesophageal wall
- Cervical oesophagus drains to: lower deep cervical, paratracheal (level VI), and upper mediastinal nodes
- Intramural lymphatic channels allow longitudinal flow of lymph within the organ wall — explains skip lesions
- Some lymph may pass directly into the thoracic duct

## PART B: AETIOLOGY AND PATHOLOGY

### Risk Factors

| Factor | Details |
|--------|---------|
| Tobacco | 8.53x incidence in current smokers; air-cured tobacco higher risk |
| Alcohol | Heavy drinkers (>4 drinks/day): RR 9.03; synergistic with tobacco |
| Plummer-Vinson syndrome | 4-6% of postcricoid/cervical oesophageal cancers |
| Diet | Low BMI (<18.5); low fruits/vegetables intake |
| Occupational | Construction, formalin, coal dust, steel dust |
| Previous radiation | Associated with postcricoid/cervical oesophageal SCC |
| Hot beverages | Risk factor for oesophageal SCC |

### Pathology

- >95% squamous cell carcinoma (moderately or poorly differentiated)
- SCC predominates in cervical and upper thoracic oesophagus
- Adenocarcinoma predominates in lower oesophagus (associated with Barrett's)

### Routes of Spread

| Direction | Pattern |
|-----------|---------|
| Circumferential | Intramural spread through oesophageal wall |
| Inferior (submucosal) | Most prominent; skip lesions via submucosal lymphatic plexus; extends up to 20 mm |
| Superior | Into postcricoid/hypopharynx |
| Anterior | Into trachea, thyroid gland |
| Posterior | Prevertebral fascia (T4b — unresectable) |
| Lateral | Carotid sheath (T4b — unresectable) |
| Nodal | Paratracheal, deep cervical, upper mediastinal; 70% have nodes at presentation |
| Distant | Highest among H&N cancers (17-24%); lung > bone > liver |

## PART C: CLINICAL FEATURES AND INVESTIGATIONS

### Clinical Presentation

- **Dysphagia:** Progressive, initially to solids — typically EARLY in postcricoid/cervical oesophageal cancers
- **Significant weight loss:** Hallmark of postcricoid and oesophageal tumours
- **Odynophagia** and globus sensation
- **Hoarseness:** Late presentation (RLN involvement)
- **Referred otalgia:** Via Arnold's nerve (vagus)
- **Neck lump:** Significant proportion present with this as only complaint
- **Trotter's sign:** Loss of laryngeal crepitus — implies prevertebral fascia involvement (T4b)

### Investigations

| Investigation | Role |
|--------------|------|
| Flexible transnasal oesophagoscopy | Map lesion, lower extent, oesophageal involvement, synchronous primary |
| Direct laryngoscopy + oesophagoscopy (GA) | Biopsy, extent assessment, cricopharynx involvement |
| Barium oesophagogram | Determines lower limit of tumour |
| CE-CT with bone windows | T staging, cartilage assessment, nodal status |
| MRI | Cartilage invasion (sensitivity up to 96%) |
| [[PET CT Nuclear Medicine Scans|PET-CT]] | Distant metastases, nodal assessment, post-treatment surveillance |
| EUS (endoscopic ultrasound) | T staging depth of invasion |
| FNAC | Palpable neck nodes (open biopsy MUST be avoided) |
| Bronchoscopy | Tracheal involvement assessment |

### TNM Staging

**Hypopharynx TNM (UICC 8th Edition)** — used when tumour centred at pharyngo-oesophageal junction:

| T Stage | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| T1 | One subsite, ≤2 cm |
| T2 | >1 subsite or adjacent, >2-4 cm, no hemilarynx fixation |
| T3 | >4 cm OR hemilarynx fixation OR oesophageal extension |
| T4a | Thyroid/cricoid cartilage, hyoid, thyroid gland, oesophagus, central compartment |
| T4b | Prevertebral fascia, carotid encasement, mediastinum |

**Oesophageal TNM** — used when tumour centred in cervical oesophagus:

| T Stage | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| T1a/T1b | Lamina propria or muscularis mucosae / Submucosa |
| T2 | Muscularis propria |
| T3 | Adventitia |
| T4a | Pleura, pericardium, diaphragm |
| T4b | Aorta, vertebral body, trachea |

## PART D: MANAGEMENT

### Principles

- Cervical oesophageal carcinoma often presents at advanced stage
- Management is multimodal — surgery, CRT, or sequential IC + CRT
- Choice depends on: extent of disease, laryngeal function, fitness, oesophageal involvement

### Option 1: Definitive Chemoradiation (Organ Preservation)

**Indications:**
- Intact laryngeal framework and function
- No airway compromise or aspiration
- Total disease length does not exceed 10 cm
- Good performance status

**Protocol:**
- Concurrent CRT: cisplatin 100 mg/m² days 1, 22, 43 + IMRT
- IMRT dose: 65-66 Gy gross disease, 54 Gy prophylactic nodes, in 30 fractions
- SCC more sensitive to CRT than adenocarcinoma
- MACH-NC: absolute 5-year OS benefit of 4% for concomitant CRT in hypopharynx subset

### Option 2: Surgery

**A. Total Laryngopharyngo-oesophagectomy (TLPE)**

Indications:
- Postcricoid/cervical oesophageal cancer extending beyond the cricopharynx
- Failed organ preservation
- Non-functional larynx

Procedure:
- En-bloc removal of larynx, pharynx, and entire oesophagus
- Transhiatal oesophageal resection (avoiding thoracotomy)
- Bilateral radical [[Neck Dissection|neck dissection]] including level VI

**B. Reconstruction Options**

| Defect Type | Reconstruction | Details |
|-------------|---------------|---------|
| Partial pharyngeal | Radial forearm [[Free Flaps HN Reconstruction|free flap]] or ALT free flap | 3 cm unstretched remnant ensures normal swallow |
| Circumferential | Free jejunal graft (ideal) | Proven efficacy; speech rehabilitation problematic |
| Circumferential | Tubed radial forearm or ALT flap | Excellent swallowing/voice; risk of distal stricture (reduced with silastic stents) |
| Oesophageal | Gastric pull-up | Stomach + duodenum mobilized to neck; single-stage; mortality ~11% |
| Circumferential | Gastro-omental flap | Improved swallowing/voice; large omentum segment advantageous post-CRT |

**Gastric pull-up details:**
- Reliable, single-stage vascularized reconstruction
- Stomach sutured to oropharynx at fundus
- Minimizes stricture, fistula, and mediastinitis risk
- Higher morbidity: gastric emptying issues, regurgitation (patient must remain upright)
- Pyloroplasty essential for proper gastric drainage

**C. [[Laryngectomy Rehabilitation Voice Prosthesis|Total Laryngectomy]] + Circumferential Pharyngectomy**

- When postcricoid mucosa involved with no pharyngeal preservation possible
- Lesion must be limited above cricopharynx (normal cervical oesophageal stump)
- Reconstruct with free jejunal graft or tubed skin flap

### Neck Management

- Bilateral neck dissection (levels II-V) for manifest nodes
- Bilateral levels II-IV for advanced primary with N0 neck
- Level VI (paratracheal) nodes MUST be included

### Palliative Management

- Median survival for metastatic disease: 6-10 months
- Cisplatin/carboplatin + 5-FU + cetuximab: OS 10.1 vs 7.4 months (EXTREME trial)
- Palliative RT for local pain or bleeding
- Oesophageal stents for palliation of dysphagia (NOT in operable tumours)

## PART E: OUTCOMES AND FOLLOW-UP

### Prognosis

- Overall 5-year tumour-specific survival: <30%
- Survival of treated patients rises to ~50%
- Salvage surgery success rate: only 17.1%
- Hospital mortality: ~6% for laryngopharyngectomy; ~11% for gastric pull-up
- Pharyngeal leak rates higher in post-irradiated patients (~50%); reduced by one-third with vascularized tissue reconstruction

### Follow-up Protocol

| Period | Frequency |
|--------|-----------|
| Years 1-2 | Bimonthly |
| Year 3 | Quarterly |
| Year 4 | Biannually |
| Year 5+ | Annually |

- PET-CT at 10-12 weeks post-CRT (negative predictive value >95%)
- Monitor for second primary cancers (up to 50%; risk increases 1% annually)
- Thyroid function tests at 6 months post-RT

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. Cervical oesophagus extends from lower border of cricoid (C6) to thoracic inlet — NO serosal covering facilitates direct tumour spread
> 2. Submucosal spread is the hallmark — extends up to 20 mm inferiorly with skip lesions via submucosal lymphatic plexus
> 3. Cervical oesophagus gets blood supply from inferior thyroid artery; drains to paratracheal and upper mediastinal nodes
> 4. TLPE with gastric pull-up is the definitive surgical procedure — stomach + duodenum mobilized to neck; mortality ~11%
> 5. Free jejunal graft is ideal for circumferential pharyngeal defects; ALT flaps show higher success rate (96% vs 93%) compared to jejunum
> 6. Gastric pull-up: single-stage, reliable, minimizes stricture — but requires pyloroplasty and upright positioning
> 7. Definitive CRT used when total disease length ≤10 cm with intact laryngeal framework and function
> 8. 70% have nodal metastases at presentation; 17-24% have distant metastases — highest among all H&N cancers
> 9. Oesophageal stents palliate dysphagia in non-curative disease — should NOT be inserted in operable tumours
> 10. 3 cm unstretched pharyngeal remnant ensures normal swallowing after partial pharyngectomy


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - The oesophagus lacks a serosa — this is why tumour spread is easily facilitated and why perforation repair is more difficult than in bowel
> - Anterolateral spread from postcricoid cancer may present as thyroid swelling — can be misdiagnosed as goitre, especially in young females where both conditions are common
> - RLN runs in the tracheo-oesophageal groove bilaterally — vulnerable during cervical oesophageal surgery
> - Silastic stents with cutaneous flaps have dramatically reduced distal anastomotic stricture rates in circumferential reconstruction
> - Gastro-omental flap is becoming more popular than jejunal flap — advantageous in post-CRT patients due to large omentum segment providing vascular protection
