# Hypopharyngeal Cancer — Benign and Malignant Lesions, Management of Stage 3 Pyriform Sinus Cancer

> [!question] Questions
> Enumerate benign and malignant lesions of hypopharynx. Discuss the management of stage 3 hypopharyngeal cancer involving pyriform sinus.

**Source:** Scott-Brown's Otorhinolaryngology, 8th Edition - Volume 3, Chapter 16 (Management of Hypopharyngeal Cancer)

---

## INTRODUCTION

The hypopharynx (laryngopharynx) extends from the hyoid bone to the lower border of the cricoid cartilage. It has three subsites: pyriform sinus, postcricoid area, and posterior pharyngeal wall. Hypopharyngeal cancers are rare (<0.5% of all cancers, 3-5% of head and neck cancers) but are distinctly different from laryngeal cancers — they present late, have the highest incidence of distant metastases among head and neck cancers (17-24% at presentation), and carry a poor prognosis. The most common subsite is the pyriform sinus.

---

## PART A: LESIONS OF THE HYPOPHARYNX

### Benign Lesions
- Retention cysts
- Papillomas
- Fibromas
- Lipomas
- Haemangiomas
- Leiomyomas
- Schwannomas/neurofibromas
- Postcricoid web (Paterson-Brown-Kelly/Plummer-Vinson syndrome)
- Pharyngeal pouch (Zenker's diverticulum — at pharyngo-oesophageal junction)

### Malignant Lesions
- **Squamous cell carcinoma** (>95%) — most common; moderately or poorly differentiated
- Adenoid cystic carcinoma (from minor salivary glands)
- Mucoepidermoid carcinoma
- Other salivary gland malignancies
- Mesenchymal tumours (rare)
- Lymphoma (rare at this site)
- Metastatic disease

---

## PART B: SURGICAL ANATOMY OF PYRIFORM SINUS

The pyriform sinus is a funnel-shaped lateral channel on either side of the larynx:

- **Lateral wall:** contiguous with posterior pharyngeal wall; bound by thyrohyoid membrane — tumours can spread beyond larynx WITHOUT cartilage destruction
- **Medial wall:** forms part of the aryepiglottic fold; tumours extend anteromedially into paraglottic space causing vocal cord fixity
- **Apex:** junction of medial and lateral walls, deep and inferior; related laterally to thyroid cartilage, medially to cricoid cartilage, inferior to paraglottic space
- **Opens posteriorly** into the lumen of the pharynx

### Critical Relationships of Pyriform Apex
- Close to intrinsic laryngeal muscles, cricoarytenoid joint, and recurrent laryngeal nerve → early vocal cord impairment
- Tumours escape via cricothyroid membrane → thyroid gland involvement
- Oncological resection of apex tumours necessitates cricoid removal → NO conservation laryngeal surgery with speech preservation possible

### Lymphatic Drainage

| Subsite | Primary Drainage | Special Drainage |
|---------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Pyriform sinus body | Jugulodigastric + jugulo-omohyoid (levels II-III) | - |
| Pyriform apex | Paratracheal nodes (level VI) | - |
| Postcricoid | Paratracheal nodes (level VI) | - |
| Posterior pharyngeal wall | Retropharyngeal nodes | - |

- Up to 70% have lymph node metastases at presentation
- Contralateral occult nodal metastases: at least 50% when ipsilateral nodes are positive
- >20% have involvement of lower nodal stations (levels IV and V) when clinically node-positive

## PART C: AETIOLOGY

| Factor | Details |
|--------|---------|
| Tobacco | Current smokers: 8.53x incidence rate; air-cured > flue-cured |
| Alcohol | Heavy drinkers (>4 drinks/day): RR 9.03; dark liquors: RR 4.4x vs light |
| HPV | 10.9% of hypopharyngeal cancers; pyriform fossa highest association |
| Diet | Low BMI (<18.5) increases risk; fruits/vegetables inversely related |
| Plummer-Vinson | Associated with postcricoid cancers in women |
| Occupational | Construction, potters, butchers, formalin, coal dust, steel dust |

### Molecular Factors
- Oncogene amplification: CCND1, FGF3, FGF4 at region 11q12
- p53 overexpression in 66% — associated with IMPROVED survival
- Cyclin D1: independent prognostic value for cancer-specific survival
- Ki-67: marker of poor survival
- EGFR overexpression in 30%

## PART D: CLINICAL PRESENTATION AND STAGING

### Clinical Features
- Cardinal symptoms: odynophagia, dysphagia, hoarseness, referred otalgia, weight loss, neck lump
- Globus sensation may be the only early symptom
- Dysphagia to solids: early in postcricoid, LATE in pyriform cancers
- "Hot potato" speech: base of tongue involvement
- Trotter's sign: loss of laryngeal crepitus (exolaryngeal spread/prevertebral fascia involvement)
- Significant proportion present with palpable neck lump as ONLY complaint

### TNM Staging — Hypopharynx (8th Edition)

| T Stage | Description |
|---------|------------|
| T1 | Limited to one subsite, ≤2 cm |
| T2 | >1 subsite or adjacent site, >2 cm but ≤4 cm, without hemilarynx fixation |
| T3 | >4 cm OR hemilarynx fixation OR extension to oesophagus |
| T4a | Thyroid/cricoid cartilage, hyoid, thyroid gland, central compartment soft tissue |
| T4b | Prevertebral fascia, carotid encasement, mediastinal structures |

### Stage III = T3 N0-N1 M0, or T1-T2 N1 M0

### Investigations

| Investigation | Role |
|--------------|------|
| Flexible transnasal oesophagoscopy | Map lesion, lower extent, oesophageal involvement, synchronous primary |
| Direct laryngoscopy under GA | Biopsy + assess extent for treatment planning |
| CE-CT with bone windows | T staging, cartilage assessment, nodal status |
| MRI | Cartilage invasion assessment (sensitivity up to 96%) |
| [[PET CT Nuclear Medicine Scans|PET-CT]] | Advanced stage/borderline resectable; post-RT residual/recurrent disease |
| Barium oesophagogram | Lower limit of postcricoid cancer |
| FNAC | Palpable neck nodes (open biopsy MUST be avoided) |

## PART E: MANAGEMENT OF STAGE 3 PYRIFORM SINUS CANCER

Stage 3 hypopharyngeal cancer = T3 N0-N1 or T1-T2 N1. For T3 pyriform sinus cancer specifically (>4 cm OR hemilarynx fixation OR oesophageal extension), the management options are:

### Routes of Spread to Consider in Planning
- Lateral wall → thyroid cartilage invasion (ossified cartilages more prone)
- Medial wall → paraglottic space → vocal cord fixity
- Apex → cricothyroid membrane → thyroid gland; also superior into paraglottic space
- Submucosal spread inferiorly (especially postcricoid) with potential skip lesions

### Option 1: Organ Preservation — Chemoradiation

**Indications:**
- Functional larynx (mobile cords or impaired but not fixed)
- No airway compromise
- No significant dysphagia requiring tube feeding
- Good performance status, minimal comorbidity

**Protocol:**
- Concurrent CRT: Cisplatin (100 mg/m2 day 1, 22, 43) + IMRT (65-66 Gy gross disease, 54 Gy prophylactic nodes, in 30 fractions)
- MACH-NC meta-analysis: concurrent CRT showed 4.5% absolute survival benefit at 5 years for hypopharyngeal cancers
- Alternative: cetuximab during RT if chemotherapy contraindicated

**Induction chemotherapy:**
- TPF (docetaxel + cisplatin + 5-FU) superior to PF for laryngeal preservation
- 70% laryngeal preservation at 10 years with TPF
- May be followed by RT or CRT based on response

### Option 2: Surgery — Conservation Approaches (Selected Cases)

**Partial laryngopharyngectomy:**
- For selected early T3 with accessible tumour
- Requires: pyriform apex free, cricoarytenoid joint mobile, limited subglottic extension

**Transoral approaches (selected cases):**

| Approach | Local Control | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| TLM | T3: 75% (5-yr) | Expert centres only |
| TORS | 84% DFS at 3 yrs | High-definition 3D vision, 7 degrees of freedom |
| TOVS | 95% DFS at 3 yrs | Limited experience |

### Option 3: Surgery — [[Laryngectomy Rehabilitation Voice Prosthesis|Total Laryngectomy]] with Partial Pharyngectomy

**Indications:**
- Fixed cord / non-functional larynx
- Airway compromise
- Dysphagia requiring tube feeding
- Cartilage invasion
- Failed organ preservation

**Procedure:**
- Total laryngectomy with partial pharyngectomy (if adequate pharyngeal mucosa remains)
- Near-total laryngectomy with partial pharyngectomy (NTLP) — preserves a phonatory shunt

**If circumferential pharyngeal defect:**
- Free jejunal graft (for circumferential defects)
- Free radial forearm flap or PMMC flap (for partial defects)

**If oesophageal involvement:**
- Total laryngopharyngooesophagectomy (TLPE) with gastric pull-up

### Neck Management
- Bilateral [[Neck Dissection|neck dissection]] required in all surgically treated cases (high rate of bilateral nodal metastases)
- Must include level VI (paratracheal) nodes for apex and postcricoid tumours
- For CRT: bilateral levels II-IVa included in radiation field; level VI for apex tumours
- Post-CRT residual nodes: PET at 10-12 weeks → salvage neck dissection if positive

### Management of Recurrent/Metastatic Disease
- Salvage surgery for resectable locoregional recurrence (laryngopharyngectomy)
- Salvage success rates are low (17.1% in some series)
- Metastatic disease: median survival 6-10 months
- Cetuximab-containing regimens: improve OS from 7.4 to 10.1 months

### Follow-up Protocol
- Bimonthly for first 2 years
- Quarterly in year 3
- Biannually in year 4
- Annually thereafter
- Monitor: speech, swallowing, aspiration, pharyngeal stenosis, thyroid function (post-RT), second primary cancers (up to 50% of patients)

---

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. Hypopharyngeal cancers have the HIGHEST rate of distant metastases among H&N cancers (17-24% at presentation)
> 2. >70% have nodal metastases at presentation; contralateral occult rate is at least 50%
> 3. Pyriform sinus is the most common subsite; postcricoid is most common in FEMALES
> 4. Pyriform apex tumours: escape via cricothyroid membrane to thyroid gland; no conservation laryngeal surgery possible (cricoid must be removed)
> 5. T3 = >4 cm OR hemilarynx fixation OR oesophageal extension
> 6. MACH-NC meta-analysis: concurrent CRT gives 4.5% absolute survival benefit at 5 years
> 7. TPF induction chemotherapy: 70% laryngeal preservation at 10 years (superior to PF)
> 8. Free jejunal graft for circumferential pharyngeal defects; gastric pull-up for oesophageal involvement
> 9. TLM for T3 hypopharyngeal cancer: 75% 5-year local control (in expert centres only)
> 10. Trotter's sign (loss of laryngeal crepitus) indicates prevertebral fascia involvement — unresectable (T4b)


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - Postcricoid cancer may present as a thyroid swelling (submucosal anterolateral spread) — can be misdiagnosed as goitre; both conditions common in young females
> - Post-cricoid lesions have significant submucosal spread, more pronounced inferiorly — mandates evaluation of lower extent; skip lesions possible via submucosal lymphatic plexus
> - Open biopsy of neck nodes MUST be avoided — use FNAC only
> - CE-CT is the first choice imaging; MRI reserved for troubleshooting (especially cartilage invasion)
> - Both CT and MRI OVER-DIAGNOSE posterior tumour spread in advanced hypopharyngeal disease — correlate clinically
> - Paterson-Brown-Kelly/Plummer-Vinson syndrome: 4-6% of postcricoid cancer patients have a history of sideropenic dysphagia
