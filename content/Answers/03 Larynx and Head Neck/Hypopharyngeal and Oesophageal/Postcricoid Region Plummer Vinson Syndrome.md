# Postcricoid Region — Surgical Anatomy and Plummer-vinson Syndrome

**Question:** *Describe the surgical anatomy of postcricoid region. Describe the management of Plummer-Vinson syndrome.*

**Source:** Scott-Brown's Otorhinolaryngology, 8th Edition - Volume 3, Chapter 16 (Management of Hypopharyngeal Cancer), Chapter 47 (Anatomy of the Pharynx and Oesophagus), Chapter 49 (Causes and Assessment of Dysphagia)

---

## INTRODUCTION

The postcricoid area (pharyngo-oesophageal junction) is the anterior wall of the hypopharynx, extending from the level of the arytenoid cartilages to the inferior border of the cricoid cartilage. It is a clinically significant region because of its association with Plummer-Vinson syndrome (Paterson-Brown-Kelly syndrome) and postcricoid carcinoma. Postcricoid cancer accounts for approximately 15% of all hypopharyngeal cancers and uniquely shows a female preponderance (1.5:1), unlike other hypopharyngeal subsites which have overwhelming male predominance.

---

## PART A: SURGICAL ANATOMY OF THE POSTCRICOID REGION

### Boundaries and Relations

The postcricoid area lies behind the larynx and forms the anterior wall of the hypopharynx. Its boundaries are:

- **Superior:** Level of arytenoid cartilages and connecting folds (interarytenoid mucosa)
- **Inferior:** Lower border of cricoid cartilage lamina, where it transitions into the cricopharynx (upper oesophageal sphincter)
- **Lateral margins:** Merge with medial walls of pyriform sinuses at the level where the cricoid cartilage makes an anterior bend
- **Anterior:** Posterior plate of cricoid cartilage (posteriorly related to the cricoid lamina)
- **Posterior:** Opens into hypopharyngeal lumen

### Wall Layers

The hypopharyngeal wall consists of four layers:

1. **Non-keratinized stratified squamous epithelium** with goblet cells
2. **Pharyngobasilar fascia** — fibrous layer deep to mucosa
3. **Muscular layer** — lower fibres of middle constrictor and inferior constrictor
4. **Buccopharyngeal fascia** — outermost layer

### Critical Anatomical Relationships

| Structure | Relationship | Clinical Significance |
|-----------|-------------|----------------------|
| Cricoarytenoid joint | Immediately lateral | Tumour invasion → cord fixity |
| PCA muscle | Posterior to cricoid | Invasion → cord fixity + aspiration |
| Cricothyroid membrane | Anterolateral | Route of extralaryngeal escape |
| Thyroid gland | Anterolateral | Invaded via cricothyroid membrane |
| Cervical oesophagus | Inferior | Continuous spread + skip lesions |
| Carotid sheath | Lateral (parapharyngeal) | Route of lateral spread |

### Blood Supply, Innervation, Lymphatics

- **Blood supply:** Superior thyroid arteries with collaterals from lingual and ascending pharyngeal vessels
- **Motor + sensory:** Pharyngeal plexus (glossopharyngeal and vagus nerves)
- **Referred otalgia:** Arnold's nerve (auricular branch of vagus) provides sensory supply to external auditory canal
- **Lymphatic drainage:** Paratracheal nodes (level VI) primarily; also deep cervical chain (levels II-III)

### Lymph Node Metastasis

- Up to 70% have nodal metastases at presentation
- Paratracheal and paraoesophageal (level VI) nodes involved more commonly in postcricoid tumours
- Contralateral occult metastases: at least 50% when ipsilateral nodes are positive
- >20% have involvement of lower nodal stations (levels IV and V)

## PART B: PLUMMER-VINSON (PATERSON-BROWN-KELLY) SYNDROME

### Definition

Plummer-Vinson syndrome (PVS), also called Paterson-Brown-Kelly syndrome, is the clinical triad of:

1. **Dysphagia** (due to postcricoid web)
2. **Iron-deficiency anaemia**
3. **Atrophic gastritis**

It predominantly affects middle-aged and elderly women.

### Pathogenesis

Iron deficiency causes epithelial changes throughout the upper aerodigestive tract. The mechanism involves:

- **Hyperkeratinization** of postcricoid mucosa → web formation
- Iron is a cofactor for multiple enzymes involved in epithelial turnover
- Chronic iron depletion leads to mucosal atrophy and keratinization
- The postcricoid region is vulnerable due to its anatomical position at the pharyngo-oesophageal junction

### Clinical Features

| Feature | Details |
|---------|---------|
| Dysphagia | Progressive, initially to solids; due to postcricoid web |
| Anaemia symptoms | Fatigue, pallor, palpitations |
| Smooth tongue | Glossitis with papillary atrophy |
| Angular stomatitis | Painful fissuring at mouth corners |
| Koilonychia | Spoon-shaped nails (iron deficiency sign) |
| Brittle nails | Thin, easily breakable |
| Splenomegaly | Occasionally seen |

### Investigations

1. **Complete blood count:** Microcytic hypochromic anaemia
2. **Serum iron, ferritin, TIBC:** Low iron, low ferritin, high TIBC
3. **Barium swallow:** Demonstrates postcricoid web as a thin shelf of tissue projecting anteriorly into the lumen
4. **Direct pharyngo-oesophagoscopy:** Web may or may not be visible endoscopically; allows biopsy to rule out malignancy
5. **Flexible transnasal oesophagoscopy:** Initial assessment of web and exclusion of other pathology

### Management of Plummer-Vinson Syndrome

**Medical Management:**
- **Iron replacement therapy** is the cornerstone of treatment
- Oral ferrous sulphate 200 mg TDS (or equivalent iron preparation)
- Parenteral iron if oral therapy fails or is not tolerated
- Treat underlying cause of iron deficiency (dietary, menstrual, GI losses)
- Both dysphagia and hyperkeratinization improve with iron replacement
- Correction of associated nutritional deficiencies (vitamins B12, folate, riboflavin)

**Interventional Management (for persistent web):**
- **Endoscopic dilatation** — bougie or balloon dilatation of postcricoid web
- Usually performed under GA with rigid oesophagoscopy
- May need repeated dilatation sessions
- Biopsy of web during dilatation to rule out dysplasia/malignancy

**Surveillance:**
- Long-term follow-up is ESSENTIAL due to risk of postcricoid carcinoma
- 4-6% of postcricoid cancer patients have a history of sideropenic dysphagia
- Regular endoscopic assessment recommended
- Any recurrence of dysphagia warrants urgent re-evaluation

## PART C: POSTCRICOID CARCINOMA

### Epidemiology

- Accounts for ~15% of hypopharyngeal cancers
- **Female preponderance** (1.5:1) — unique among hypopharyngeal subsites
- Associated with Plummer-Vinson syndrome and iron deficiency
- More common in Northern Europe
- >95% are squamous cell carcinoma

### Routes of Spread

| Direction | Pattern | Clinical Effect |
|-----------|---------|----------------|
| Posterior | PCA muscle, cricoarytenoid joint | Cord fixity, aspiration |
| Anterior | Cricothyroid membrane → thyroid gland | Thyroid mass (misdiagnosed as goitre) |
| Inferior | Cervical oesophagus (submucosal) | Skip lesions possible |
| Lateral | [[Parapharyngeal Space|Parapharyngeal space]] → carotid sheath | Unresectable if carotid encased |

- Submucosal spread is a hallmark of postcricoid cancer — more pronounced inferiorly
- Skip lesions in oesophagus occur via submucosal lymphatic plexus
- Anterolateral spread may present as thyroid swelling — both conditions common in young females

### Staging (UICC TNM 8th Edition — Hypopharynx)

| T Stage | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| T1 | One subsite, ≤2 cm |
| T2 | >1 subsite or adjacent, >2-4 cm, no hemilarynx fixation |
| T3 | >4 cm OR hemilarynx fixation OR oesophageal extension |
| T4a | Thyroid/cricoid cartilage, hyoid, thyroid gland, central compartment |
| T4b | Prevertebral fascia, carotid encasement, mediastinum |

### Management

**Non-surgical (organ preservation):**
- Concurrent CRT for T1-3 with intact laryngeal framework and function
- Cisplatin 100 mg/m² days 1, 22, 43 + IMRT
- MACH-NC meta-analysis: concomitant CRT gives 4% absolute benefit at 5 years in hypopharynx subset

**Surgical options:**

| Procedure | Indication |
|-----------|-----------|
| TL + partial pharyngectomy | Interarytenoid/postcricoid involvement with adequate residual pharyngeal mucosa |
| TL + circumferential pharyngectomy | No pharyngeal mucosa can be preserved |
| TLPE + gastric pull-up | Extension beyond cricopharynx into oesophagus |

- Near-[[Laryngectomy Rehabilitation Voice Prosthesis|total laryngectomy]] (NTLP) is **contraindicated** in postcricoid involvement — makes arytenoid preservation oncologically unsafe
- A 3 cm unstretched pharyngeal remnant ensures normal swallowing
- Bilateral [[Neck Dissection|neck dissection]] including level VI required

**Reconstruction:**

| Defect | Reconstruction |
|--------|---------------|
| Partial pharyngeal | Radial forearm [[Free Flaps HN Reconstruction|free flap]] or ALT free flap; PMMC [[Pedicled Flaps Skin Grafts|pedicled flap]] |
| Circumferential | Free jejunal graft (ideal); tubed radial forearm flap |
| Oesophageal | Gastric pull-up (stomach + duodenum mobilized to neck); mortality ~11% |

### Prognosis

- Overall 5-year tumour-specific survival: less than 30%
- Highest rate of distant metastases among H&N cancers (17-24% at presentation)
- Salvage surgery success rate: only 17.1%
- Second primary cancers: up to 50% incidence (risk increases 1% annually)

---

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. Postcricoid area extends from level of arytenoid cartilages to inferior border of cricoid cartilage — forms the anterior wall of hypopharynx
> 2. Plummer-Vinson syndrome = dysphagia + iron-deficiency anaemia + atrophic gastritis; affects middle-aged/elderly women
> 3. Postcricoid web forms due to hyperkeratinization from chronic iron deficiency — treated with iron replacement + endoscopic dilatation
> 4. 4-6% of postcricoid cancer patients have history of Plummer-Vinson syndrome — long-term surveillance is mandatory
> 5. Postcricoid cancer uniquely shows female preponderance (1.5:1) among hypopharyngeal subsites
> 6. Submucosal spread is a hallmark — more pronounced inferiorly, with skip lesions possible via submucosal lymphatic plexus
> 7. Anterolateral spread may mimic thyroid swelling (goitre) — both conditions common in young females
> 8. Near-total laryngectomy is contraindicated in postcricoid involvement due to oncologically unsafe arytenoid preservation
> 9. Circumferential pharyngeal defect: free jejunal graft is ideal reconstruction; gastric pull-up for oesophageal involvement (mortality ~11%)
> 10. Paratracheal nodes (level VI) are the primary lymphatic drainage of postcricoid tumours — must be included in neck dissection


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - Trotter's sign (loss of laryngeal crepitus) indicates prevertebral fascia involvement — implies T4b unresectable disease
> - Arnold's nerve (auricular branch of vagus) explains referred otalgia in postcricoid and hypopharyngeal cancers
> - Direct pharyngo-oesophagoscopy is superior to flexible endoscopy for postcricoid assessment — web may not be visible on flexible examination
> - Both CT and MRI over-diagnose posterior tumour spread in advanced hypopharyngeal disease — always correlate clinically
> - Hypopharyngeal cancers have the highest incidence of distant metastases among all H&N cancers; >50% of treatment failures are due to distant metastases
