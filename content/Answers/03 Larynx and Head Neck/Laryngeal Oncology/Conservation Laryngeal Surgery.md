# Conservation Laryngeal Surgery for [[Laryngeal Malignancy Etiopathogenesis Staging Management|Laryngeal Carcinoma]]

**Question:** *Describe surgical anatomy of laryngeal muscles. Discuss various conservation laryngeal surgical procedures for laryngeal carcinoma.*

**Source:** Scott-Brown's Otorhinolaryngology, 8th Edition - Volume 3, Chapter 14 (Tumours of the Larynx), Chapter 27 (Open Conservation Surgery for Laryngeal Cancer)

---

## INTRODUCTION

Conservation laryngeal surgery aims for complete tumour removal while preserving the functional integrity of the larynx — separating the airway and pharynx. The cricoarytenoid unit is the basic functional unit of the larynx. Preservation of at least one functioning cricoarytenoid unit allows speech and swallowing without a permanent tracheostoma. With appropriate patient selection, partial laryngectomy can be applied in approximately 30-50% of radiorecurrent laryngeal cancers, avoiding [[Laryngectomy Rehabilitation Voice Prosthesis|total laryngectomy]].

## PART A: SURGICAL ANATOMY OF LARYNGEAL MUSCLES

### Intrinsic Muscles of the Larynx

| Muscle | Action | Nerve Supply |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| Cricothyroid | Tensor of vocal cord (tilts thyroid forward) | External branch of SLN |
| Thyroarytenoid (vocalis) | Shortens/relaxes vocal cord; forms body of cord | RLN |
| Posterior cricoarytenoid (PCA) | ONLY abductor of vocal cord | RLN |
| Lateral cricoarytenoid (LCA) | Adducts vocal cord | RLN |
| Interarytenoid (transverse + oblique) | Adducts posterior glottis | RLN (bilateral) |

### Key Anatomical Relationships

**Paraglottic space:**
- Lies lateral to the ventricle, between thyroid cartilage laterally and conus elasticus/quadrangular membrane medially
- Contains fat, connective tissue, and the internal branch of SLN
- Tumour invasion = T3 staging
- Continuous pathway for supraglottic-glottic-subglottic spread

**Pre-epiglottic space:**
- Bounded: anteriorly by thyrohyoid membrane/thyroid cartilage, posteriorly by epiglottis, superiorly by hyoepiglottic ligament
- Epiglottic foramina allow tumour penetration (NOT a barrier to spread)
- Invasion = T3 for supraglottic cancers

**Cricoarytenoid unit:**
- Arytenoid cartilage + cricoid cartilage + associated musculature + nerve supply (SLN + RLN)
- Functional unit that permits glottic closure for speech/swallowing
- At least ONE must be preserved for conservation surgery

**Anterior commissure (Broyles' ligament):**
- No perichondrium — tumour can directly invade thyroid cartilage
- Critical landmark in surgical planning

## PART B: TRANSORAL CONSERVATION SURGERY

### ELS Cordectomy Classification (Types I-VI)

| Type | Name | Depth |
|------|------|-------|
| I | Subepithelial | Through superficial lamina propria |
| II | Subligamental | Through vocal ligament |
| III | Transmuscular | Through vocalis muscle |
| IV | Total cordectomy | To thyroid perichondrium |
| Va-Vd | Extended total | + contralateral cord/arytenoid/subglottis/ventricle |
| VI | Anterior commissurectomy | AC + bilateral anterior cordectomy |

### ELS Supraglottic Resection Classification (Types I-IVb)

| Type | Procedure | Indication |
|------|----------|-----------|
| I | Limited excision | Small superficial supraglottic tumours |
| IIa-IIb | Medial supraglottic | Epiglottic surface tumours (above/below hyoid) |
| IIIa-IIIb | Medial + pre-epiglottic | Petiole/infrahyoid epiglottis extending to ventricular fold |
| IVa-IVb | Lateral supraglottic | Threefolds region; IVb includes arytenoid + pyriform wall |

### TORS (Transoral Robotic Surgery)
- High-definition 3D vision, 7 degrees of freedom
- Useful for supraglottic tumours — easier to teach and learn than TLM
- Comparable outcomes to TLM for supraglottic cancers

## PART C: OPEN CONSERVATION LARYNGEAL SURGERY

### 1. Vertical Partial Laryngectomy

**A. Anterolateral hemilaryngectomy:**
- For glottic tumours NOT involving the AC
- Removes: ipsilateral thyroid ala, true cord, portions of subglottic mucosa, false cord
- Strap muscles closed over residual perichondrium to form pseudocord
- [[Tracheostomy|Tracheostomy]] + feeding tube: 3-7 days

**B. Frontolateral hemilaryngectomy:**
- For tumour involving ipsilateral cord + AC + up to anterior 1/3 contralateral cord
- Vertical cartilage cut 1 cm paramedian on contralateral thyroid ala

**C. Anterior vertical laryngectomy:**
- For AC tumour involving no more than anterior 1/3 of one or both cords
- Two paramedian cartilage incisions (~1 cm from midline)
- Silastic keel prevents anterior web formation; removed endoscopically at 4 weeks

### 2. Supracricoid Partial Laryngectomy (SCPL)

**A. SCPL with CHEP (Cricohyoidoepiglottopexy):**
- For GLOTTIC tumours extending across midline through AC
- Can remove: both true cords, both false cords, entire thyroid cartilage, paraglottic spaces bilaterally, maximum ONE arytenoid
- Reconstruction: hyoid approximated to cricoid with 3 absorbable sutures (midline + 2 paramedian)
- Suprahyoid epiglottis preserved
- Neoglottic valve: 90-degree orientation change (AP slit → transverse/T-shape)
- Temporary tracheostomy; decannulation usually early
- NGT for 4-6 weeks; PEG in older patients or single arytenoid preservation

**B. SCPL with CHP (Cricohyoidopexy):**
- For SUPRAGLOTTIC tumours with glottic involvement/pre-epiglottic space invasion/decreased cord mobility
- Same as CHEP but epiglottic remnant is absent → increased aspiration risk, delayed swallowing

### 3. Horizontal Supraglottic Partial Laryngectomy (HSPL)

- Removes: epiglottis, (part of) hyoid, pre-epiglottic space, thyrohyoid membrane, upper half thyroid cartilage, aryepiglottic fold, part of false cords
- Transection: vallecula superiorly, ventricles inferiorly, aryepiglottic folds laterally
- Closure: base of tongue to lower thyroid cartilage + posterior false cord to pyriform mucosa
- Bilateral selective [[Neck Dissection|neck dissection]] performed simultaneously (high bilateral nodal rate)
- SLN must be identified and preserved

### 4. Supratracheal Laryngectomy (STPL)

- For laryngeal cancers with subglottic extension
- Removes entire glottic and subglottic sites + thyroid cartilage
- Spares at least one functioning cricoarytenoid unit
- Inferiorly: resection to first tracheal ring
- Reconstruction: tracheohyoidopexy (THP) or tracheohyoidoepiglottopexy (THEP)

### Anatomic Boundaries for Procedure Selection

| Parameter | Vertical Partial | Supracricoid | Supraglottic |
|-----------|----------------|-------------|-------------|
| Anterior | AC or ant 1/3 opp cord | Pre-epiglottic space | 5 mm from AC |
| Posterior | Ipsilateral vocal process | Inter-arytenoid region | Inter-arytenoid free |
| Caudal | Subglottic <5 mm post/<10 mm ant | Subglottic <5 mm post/<10 mm ant | Entrance sinus of Morgagni |
| Contraindications | Epiglottic involvement, RLN invasion, cartilage invasion | Bilateral arytenoid, base of tongue, pharyngeal wall | Bilateral arytenoid, cord impairment, tumour >3 cm |

## PART D: COMPARISON OF TREATMENT MODALITIES

| Factor | OPL | TLM | (C)RT |
|--------|-----|-----|-------|
| Oncologic results | Equivocal | Equivocal | Equivocal |
| Speech quality | Poor | Good | Good |
| Swallowing | Normal | Normal | Impaired |
| Feeding tube | Temporary (1 month) | No | 5-10% |
| Tracheostomy | Temporary (1 month) | No | Occasional |
| Treatment duration | 1 day | 1 day | 7 weeks |
| Cost | Medium | Low | Expensive |
| Repeat treatment | Only salvage TL or RT | Repeat laser possible | Only salvage TL |

### Key Surgical Goals (Three factors for functional success)
1. Adequate airway with functional neoglottic valve
2. Adequate post-operative pharyngeal anatomy for swallowing
3. Optimal laryngopharyngeal coordination (motor + sensory feedback)

---

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. Cricoarytenoid unit = arytenoid + cricoid + musculature + SLN/RLN; at least ONE must be preserved
> 2. PCA is the ONLY abductor; all other intrinsic muscles are supplied by RLN except cricothyroid (ext SLN)
> 3. SCPL-CHEP: can remove both cords, both false cords, entire thyroid cartilage — maximum ONE arytenoid
> 4. HSPL requires bilateral neck dissection simultaneously (high bilateral nodal rate from supraglottis)
> 5. Silastic keel prevents anterior web after AC resection — removed endoscopically at 4 weeks
> 6. Subglottic extension limits: <5 mm posterior, <10 mm anterior for both vertical and supracricoid
> 7. Epiglottic involvement is a contraindication for vertical hemilaryngectomy (risk of pre-epiglottic space invasion)
> 8. TLM has largely replaced OPL for early cancers — lower morbidity, better voice, lower cost
> 9. After SCPL-CHEP: neoglottic valve undergoes 90-degree orientation change; quiet aspiration present in most
> 10. Salvage OPL possible in 30-50% of radiorecurrent glottic cancers; frozen sections essential


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - Pre-operative lung function assessment is mandatory for all OPL candidates; inability to walk 2 flights of stairs predicts pulmonary complications
> - Patients treated with standard 70 Gy IMRT with limited glottic portal are better candidates for salvage partial laryngectomy than those treated with chemoradiation or extensive portals
> - SCPL-CHP (for supraglottic cancer): no epiglottic remnant → significantly more aspiration and delayed swallowing than CHEP
> - The SLN can be damaged at 4 critical steps during OPL: separating infrahyoid muscles from hyoid, trans-vallecular pharyngotomy, freeing superior horn of thyroid, releasing inferior constrictor from paraglottic space
> - Cricopharyngeal myotomy during OPL can facilitate unobstructed food passage when constrictor lumen feels narrow on finger dilatation
