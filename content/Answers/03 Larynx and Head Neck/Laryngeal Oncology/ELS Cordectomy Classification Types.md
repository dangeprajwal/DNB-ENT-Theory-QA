# ELS Classification of Cordectomy and Conservation Laryngeal Surgery

> [!question] Questions
> Discuss the ELS classification of various types of cordectomies for glottic carcinoma. Describe the indications, surgical steps and complications of Type 3 cordectomy. Also: Describe the surgical anatomy of vocal cords. Discuss the various types of Endoscopic Cordectomy in respect to [[Laryngeal Malignancy Etiopathogenesis Staging Management|laryngeal carcinoma]].

**Source:** Scott-Brown's Otorhinolaryngology, 8th Edition - Volume 3, Chapter 14 (Tumours of the Larynx), Chapter 27 (Open Conservation Surgery for Laryngeal Cancer)

---

## INTRODUCTION

The European Laryngological Society (ELS) developed a standardized classification for endoscopic cordectomy (Remacle et al., 2000) and supraglottic resection (Remacle et al., 2009). This classification allows surgical practitioners to collate data, report outcomes, and compare results across centres. The classification is based on the anatomical depth and extent of resection, ranging from subepithelial (Type I) to extended total cordectomy (Type V) and anterior commissurectomy (Type VI).

## PART A: SURGICAL ANATOMY OF THE VOCAL CORD

The vocal cord structure is essential for understanding cordectomy depth:

```
  Cross-section of Vocal Cord (Lateral to Medial)

  Thyroid Cartilage (Perichondrium)
        |
  Vocalis Muscle (Thyroarytenoid)
        |
  Vocal Ligament (Intermediate + Deep Lamina Propria)
        |
  Reinke's Space (Superficial Lamina Propria)
        |
  Epithelium (6-cell thick stratified squamous)
```

**Key anatomical points:**
- The epithelium is 6-cell thick; even CIS disrupts it enough to cause voice change
- The superficial lamina propria (Reinke's space) is a jelly-like layer critical for mucosal wave generation
- The vocal ligament (intermediate + deep lamina propria) provides structural support
- The vocalis muscle (thyroarytenoid) forms the body of the cord
- The internal perichondrium of the thyroid ala forms the deep boundary
- The vocal cord extends from the vocal process of the arytenoid posteriorly to the anterior commissure anteriorly

**Anterior commissure (AC):** The point where both vocal cords meet; Broyles' ligament attaches here. No perichondrium at AC — tumour can directly invade thyroid cartilage.

## PART B: ELS CLASSIFICATION OF CORDECTOMIES FOR GLOTTIC CARCINOMAS

### Glottic Cordectomy Types (Table 14.4)

| Type | Name | Depth of Resection |
|------|------|-------------------|
| I | Subepithelial | Through superficial lamina propria (Reinke's space) |
| II | Subligamental | Through Reinke's space + vocal ligament |
| III | Transmuscular | Through vocalis muscle |
| IV | Total cordectomy | From vocal process to AC; depth reaches internal thyroid perichondrium |
| Va | Extended (contralateral) | Includes contralateral vocal fold + AC |
| Vb | Extended (arytenoid) | Includes arytenoid cartilage |
| Vc | Extended (subglottis) | Includes subglottic mucosa |
| Vd | Extended (ventricle) | Includes ventricle + false cords |
| VI | Anterior commissurectomy | AC + bilateral anterior cordectomy; may include subglottic mucosa + cricothyroid membrane |

### ELS Classification of Supraglottic Resections (Table 14.5)

| Type | Name | Indication |
|------|------|-----------|
| I | Limited excision | Small superficial tumours anywhere on supraglottis |
| IIa | Medial supraglottic (partial pre-epiglottic) | Laryngeal surface epiglottis above hyoid; suprahyoid epiglottectomy |
| IIb | Medial supraglottic (extended) | Tumours extending below hyoid; total epiglottectomy |
| IIIa | Medial supraglottic (with pre-epiglottic space) | Tumours extending to petiole; includes pre-epiglottic space |
| IIIb | Medial supraglottic (infrahyoid) | Infrahyoid epiglottis extending to ventricular fold; dissection from thyroid cartilage |
| IVa | Lateral supraglottic | Threefolds region: free epiglottic edge, aryepiglottic fold, pharyngo-aryepiglottic fold, ventricular fold |
| IVb | Lateral supraglottic (with arytenoid) | Extends to arytenoid; includes arytenoid + medial/anterior pyriform fossa wall |

## PART C: TYPE III CORDECTOMY (TRANSMUSCULAR)

### Indications
- Glottic SCC involving the vocal cord epithelium and extending into the vocalis muscle
- Tumours that have penetrated beyond the vocal ligament but do NOT extend to the thyroid perichondrium
- Depth of invasion exceeds Type II (subligamental) but does not require complete cord removal (Type IV)

### Contraindications
- Tumour reaching thyroid perichondrium (requires Type IV)
- Anterior commissure invasion extending to contralateral cord (requires Type Va or VI)
- Arytenoid involvement (requires Type Vb)
- Subglottic extension (requires Type Vc)
- Inadequate transoral exposure
- Gross cartilage invasion (requires open surgery or [[Laryngectomy Rehabilitation Voice Prosthesis|total laryngectomy]])

### Surgical Steps

**1. Preparation:**
- General anaesthesia with jet ventilation
- Suspension microlaryngoscopy with operating microscope
- CO2 laser settings adjusted for cutting (preferred over monopolar cautery due to minimal penetration)

**2. Assessment:**
- Palpation of the vocal cord to assess depth of invasion
- Intra-operative photographs before surgery
- Determine ELS cordectomy type based on extent

**3. Resection:**
- Superior incision through the ventricle (false cord-ventricle junction)
- Inferior incision along the subglottic margin of the cord
- Anterior limit at the anterior commissure (or 1-2 mm from AC if not involved)
- Posterior limit at the vocal process of the arytenoid
- Depth of resection: through the vocalis muscle, leaving the internal thyroid perichondrium intact
- Lateral and superior margins cleared under direct vision with the laser

**4. Specimen handling:**
- Specimen orientated and mounted on suitable media (e.g. dehydrated cucumber)
- Intra-operative photographs of post-operative larynx
- Sent for histological analysis with anatomical template

**5. Margin assessment:**
- Pathologist specifies exact sites where margins are <1 mm
- If margins clear ≥1 mm: routine follow-up
- If tumour at margins despite adequate surgical margins: second look at 6-8 weeks
- If residual tumour confirmed: consider further TLM or alternative treatment via MDT

### Complications of Type III Cordectomy

**Intra-operative:**
- Airway fire (risk with CO2 laser + O2)
- Haemorrhage (rare with CO2 laser)
- Over-resection (damage to thyroid perichondrium beyond intended depth)

**Post-operative:**
- Dysphonia — more significant than Types I-II due to vocalis muscle removal; voice is breathy with wider glottal gap
- Granuloma formation at resection site
- Oedema (usually self-limiting)
- Laryngeal web formation (if near anterior commissure)

**Long-term:**
- Permanent voice change — reduced maximum phonation time, increased breathiness
- Glottal insufficiency — incomplete glottic closure during phonation
- Local recurrence — comparable rates to RT and OPL

## PART D: CONSERVATION LARYNGEAL SURGERY — OPEN PROCEDURES

For completeness, the open conservation surgery options (Chapter 27):

### Types of Open Partial Laryngectomy

| Procedure | Indication |
|-----------|-----------|
| Anterolateral hemilaryngectomy | Glottic tumour NOT involving AC |
| Frontolateral hemilaryngectomy | Glottic tumour involving ipsilateral cord + AC + up to anterior 1/3 contralateral cord |
| Anterior vertical laryngectomy | AC tumour involving no more than anterior 1/3 of one or both cords |
| SCPL-CHEP (supracricoid + cricohyoidoepiglottopexy) | Glottic tumour extending across midline through AC; both cords, both false cords, entire thyroid cartilage can be removed |
| SCPL-CHP (supracricoid + cricohyoidopexy) | Supraglottic tumours with glottic/pre-epiglottic extension |
| HSPL (horizontal supraglottic) | Supraglottic tumours within defined boundaries |

### Anatomic Boundaries for Open Partial Laryngectomy

| Parameter | Vertical Partial | Supracricoid | Supraglottic |
|-----------|----------------|-------------|-------------|
| Anterior | AC or anterior 1/3 opposite cord | Pre-epiglottic space | 5 mm margin from AC |
| Posterior | Ipsilateral vocal process | Inter-arytenoid region | Inter-arytenoid free |
| Cranial | Upper limit sinus of Morgagni | Base of tongue | Base of tongue |
| Caudal | Subglottic <5 mm post/<10 mm ant | Subglottic <5 mm post/<10 mm ant | Entrance sinus of Morgagni |
| Key contraindication | Epiglottic involvement, RLN invasion, cartilage invasion | Bilateral arytenoid, pharyngeal wall, base of tongue extension | Bilateral arytenoid, cord impairment, tumour >3 cm |

### Critical Concept: Cricoarytenoid Unit

The cricoarytenoid unit is the basic functional unit of the larynx — consisting of arytenoid cartilage, cricoid cartilage, associated musculature, and nerve supply (SLN + RLN). Preservation of at least ONE functioning cricoarytenoid unit allows organ preservation laryngeal surgery with physiologic speech and swallowing without permanent tracheostoma.

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. ELS cordectomy classification: Types I-VI; developed by Remacle et al. (2000) for standardization
> 2. Type I (subepithelial) → Type IV (total): increasing depth from epithelium to thyroid perichondrium
> 3. Type Va-Vd (extended) involve contralateral cord, arytenoid, subglottis, or ventricle respectively
> 4. Type VI (anterior commissurectomy): bilateral anterior cordectomy + AC; may include cricothyroid membrane
> 5. Type III transmuscular: resection through vocalis muscle, perichondrium intact; for tumours invading beyond vocal ligament
> 6. CO2 laser is the preferred tool; laser ablation (surface destruction) is DISCOURAGED
> 7. All specimens must be orientated and mounted; pathologist specifies margin sites <1 mm
> 8. Cricoarytenoid unit is the basic functional unit — preservation of at least one allows speech and swallowing without stoma
> 9. SCPL-CHEP: can remove both cords, both false cords, and entire thyroid cartilage; needs at least one functioning arytenoid
> 10. Supraglottic ELS classification: Types I to IVb; IVb includes arytenoid + medial pyriform fossa wall


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - The silastic keel technique is used to prevent anterior laryngeal web formation after anterior commissure resection; removed endoscopically at 4 weeks
> - SCPL-CHEP reconstruction: hyoid approximated to cricoid with three absorbable sutures (midline + 2 paramedian); suprahyoid epiglottis preserved
> - After SCPL-CHEP, the neoglottic valve orientation undergoes a 90-degree turn (from slit-like AP to transverse/T-shaped); closed by mobile arytenoid(s)
> - TORS for supraglottic tumours offers 3D vision with 7 degrees of freedom — easier to teach and learn than TLM procedures
> - Salvage partial laryngectomy after RT: radiorecurrent tumours show microscopic dispersion beyond original confines — intra-operative frozen sections are essential
