# Induction Chemotherapy and Molecular Factors in HNSCC

> [!question] Questions
> Discuss the role of induction chemotherapy in HNSCC. Describe various molecular factors for alteration in HNSCC.

**Source:** Scott-Brown's Otorhinolaryngology, 8th Edition - Volume 3, Chapter 6 (Molecular Biology of Head and Neck Cancer), Chapter 24 (Principles of Chemotherapy), Chapter 30 (Biologically Targeted Agents in Head and Neck Cancers)

---

## INTRODUCTION

Chemotherapy alone cannot cure head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC). It is used in conjunction with surgery and [[Radiotherapy Principles Fractionation Advances|radiotherapy]] to improve local control, achieve organ preservation with continued function, and decrease subclinical micrometastatic spread. Induction chemotherapy (IC) — given before definitive locoregional treatment — remains a debated topic despite landmark trials. Concurrently, advances in understanding the molecular biology of HNSCC have identified key genetic alterations driving carcinogenesis and opened avenues for targeted therapy and [[Allergic Rhinitis Immunotherapy|immunotherapy]].

---

## PART A: ROLE OF INDUCTION CHEMOTHERAPY IN HNSCC

### Rationale for Induction Chemotherapy

1. **Tumour volume reduction** before definitive RT/CRT — better blood flow, greater drug delivery, decreased hypoxic areas (reducing radioresistance)
2. **Organ preservation** — shrink tumour to avoid mutilating surgery (larynx, hypopharynx)
3. **Distant metastasis control** — since surgery and RT are locoregional, IC may treat subclinical distant disease
4. **Prognostic indicator** — response to IC acts as surrogate marker for response to subsequent treatment
5. **Selection tool** — non-responders can be redirected to surgery without delay

### Standard IC Regimens

| Regimen | Drugs | Schedule |
|---------|-------|----------|
| PF | Cisplatin 100 mg/m² + 5-FU 1 g/m²/day x4-5 days | 3-weekly x 2-3 cycles |
| TPF | Docetaxel + Cisplatin + 5-FU | 3-weekly x 3 cycles |

TPF has replaced PF as the preferred IC regimen based on TAX 323 and TAX 324 trials.

### Key Landmark Trials

| Trial | Design | Key Findings |
|-------|--------|-------------|
| VA Larynx Study (1991) | IC (PF) + RT vs TL + RT | Comparable 2-yr survival (68%); 64% larynx preservation; salvage TL: T3 29%, T4 56% |
| RTOG 91-11 | CRT vs IC+RT vs RT alone | Larynx preservation: CRT 88% > ICRT 75% > RT 70%; similar OS |
| EORTC 24891 | IC (PF) + RT vs surgery + RT (hypopharynx) | No OS difference at 10 yrs; organ preservation 42% at 3 yrs |

### TPF vs PF Trials

| Trial | Finding |
|-------|---------|
| TAX 323 (European) | TPF superior tumour response (80% vs 59%); 10-yr larynx preservation: 70.3% vs 46.5% (p=0.01); no OS difference |
| TAX 324 (North American) | TPF 5-yr survival 52% vs PF 42%; improved laryngectomy-free survival |

**Criticisms of TAX trials:**
- TAX 323: No concurrent chemotherapy given with subsequent RT
- TAX 324: Concurrent carboplatin used (not optimal high-dose cisplatin)
- TPF doses differed between trials
- TPF carries significantly higher toxicity — haematological (neutropenia, myelosuppression) and non-haematological (mucositis)
- Chemotherapy-related deaths increased with TPF

### IC Followed by CRT vs CRT Alone

| Trial | Finding |
|-------|---------|
| DeCIDE | No OS benefit of adding IC to CRT in N2/N3 disease |
| PARADIGM | No OS difference: sequential IC+CRT vs CRT alone |
| TREMPLIN | IC followed by CRT or cetuximab+RT: no difference in larynx preservation |

### MACH-NC Meta-analysis (Key Reference)

The most comprehensive evidence on chemotherapy in HNSCC — >90 trials, >16,000 patients:

| Finding | Details |
|---------|---------|
| IC vs locoregional alone | No OS benefit (though PF showed some benefit) |
| Concurrent CRT | 6.5% absolute OS benefit at 5 years |
| IC vs concurrent CRT | Concurrent CRT is superior for OS |
| IC and distant metastases | IC may reduce distant metastases more effectively than concurrent CRT |
| Age effect | Benefit of CRT NOT significant above 70 years |
| Multi-agent vs single-agent | No significant benefit of multi-agent over single-agent cisplatin in concurrent setting |

### Current Indications for Induction Chemotherapy

IC is considered in selected patients:
- **Organ preservation** in advanced laryngeal/hypopharyngeal cancer (alternative to primary surgery)
- **Bulky nodal disease** (N2b and above) where distant metastasis risk is high
- **Good performance status** with minimal comorbidities
- Patients where surgery is not appropriate or refused
- Risk: patients undergoing IC may fail to complete subsequent definitive RT due to IC-induced morbidity

### Summary of Evidence

Current evidence does NOT conclusively support IC as superior to concurrent CRT alone. The role of IC remains primarily in organ preservation protocols and in reducing distant metastasis rates. Concurrent CRT remains the standard non-surgical treatment for advanced HNSCC.

## PART B: MOLECULAR FACTORS AND ALTERATIONS IN HNSCC

### Three Major Mutagenic Processes

1. **Chemical carcinogens** — tobacco smoke, alcohol
2. **Oncogenic virus infection** — HPV (types 16, 18)
3. **Epigenetic modifications** — DNA methylation, histone modification

### Hallmarks of Cancer (Hanahan & Weinberg)

Original six (2000): self-sufficiency in growth signals, insensitivity to anti-growth signals, evading apoptosis, limitless replicative potential, sustained angiogenesis, tissue invasion and metastasis. Updated (2011) with emerging hallmarks: avoiding immune destruction, deregulating cellular energetics. Enabling characteristics: genome instability and tumour-promoting inflammation.

### Key Molecular Alterations in HNSCC

| Gene/Pathway | Alteration | Significance |
|-------------|-----------|-------------|
| TP53 | Mutated in 50-60% overall; ~80% HPV-negative; 0% HPV-positive | Most commonly mutated gene; "guardian of the genome"; loss associated with reduced OS |
| CDKN2A (p16) | Inactivated by promoter methylation in >50% HPV-negative | Negative regulator of G1-S; p16 IHC = surrogate biomarker for HPV+ disease |
| CCND1 (Cyclin D1) | Frequently amplified | Promotes RB phosphorylation → cell cycle progression; independent prognostic value |
| EGFR | Overexpressed in up to 90% | Associated with poorer prognosis; target for cetuximab |
| PIK3CA | Mutated in >30% HPV-negative, >60% HPV-positive | Constitutive activation of AKT/mTOR pathway |
| PTEN | Inactivating mutations | Opposes PIK3CA; loss → unregulated PI3K signalling |
| NOTCH1 | Inactivating mutations | Negatively regulates WNT/beta-catenin pathway |
| FAT1 | Mutated in HPV-negative | Also regulates WNT/beta-catenin pathway |
| MYC | Amplified in >10% HPV-negative | Roles in cell cycle, proliferation, metabolism |

### HPV-Positive vs HPV-Negative HNSCC

| Feature | HPV-Positive | HPV-Negative |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| TP53 mutation | 0% (E6 degrades p53) | ~80% |
| p16/CDKN2A | Overexpressed (paradoxically) | Inactivated (>50%) |
| CCND1 amplification | Rare | Common |
| PIK3CA mutation | >60% | >30% |
| TRAF3 mutation | Almost exclusive | Rare |
| FADD amplification | Rare | Common |
| Prognosis | Better | Worse |
| Cell cycle control loss | ~100% (via E6/E7) | ~100% (via mutations) |

### HPV Oncoproteins

- **E6:** Binds and degrades p53 (via ubiquitin-proteasome pathway) → loss of apoptosis and cell cycle arrest
- **E7:** Binds and inactivates pRb → constitutive E2F1 release → uncontrolled cell cycle progression
- E6 and E7 together abrogate both G1/S and G2/M checkpoints

## PART C: TARGETED THERAPY AND IMMUNOTHERAPY

### Anti-EGFR Therapy — Cetuximab

Cetuximab is a human-murine chimeric monoclonal antibody that binds the extracellular domain of EGFR.

| Trial | Design | Results |
|-------|--------|---------|
| Bonner (2006) | RT ± cetuximab (oropharynx, larynx, hypopharynx) | 3-yr OS: 55% vs 45%; LRC: 50% vs 41% |
| RTOG 0522 | CRT ± cetuximab | No benefit from adding cetuximab to CRT; more toxicity |
| EXTREME | Platinum/5-FU ± cetuximab (recurrent/metastatic) | OS: 10.1 vs 7.4 months (p=0.04) |

**Side effects:** Different from standard chemotherapy — acneiform rash (correlates with response), paronychia, radiation dermatitis, infusion reactions. Less nephrotoxicity and ototoxicity than cisplatin.

### Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors

Immunotherapy has emerged as a major new modality based on understanding of immune checkpoint pathways:

| Checkpoint | Mechanism |
|-----------|-----------|
| CTLA-4/B7 | CTLA-4 on T-cells out-competes CD28 for B7 binding → switches off T-cell activation |
| PD-1/PD-L1 | Tumour cells express PD-L1 → binds PD-1 on T-cells → prevents tumour killing |

| Agent | Trial | Key Results |
|-------|-------|-------------|
| Nivolumab (anti-PD-1) | CheckMate 141 | OS: 7.5 vs 5.1 months (HR 0.70); p16+ OPSCC: 9.1 vs 4.4 months |
| Pembrolizumab (anti-PD-1) | KEYNOTE-012 | 20% response rate; well tolerated (7.6% grade 3+ AE) |

**Immunotherapy side effects:** Entirely different from chemotherapy — autoimmune reactions: skin rash, colitis, hepatitis, endocrinopathy (thyroid, adrenal, pituitary). Pseudoprogression may precede clinical response. More common with anti-CTLA4 than anti-PD1 agents.

### Emerging Strategies

- **Checkpoint inhibitors + radiation:** RT causes immunogenic cell death; checkpoint inhibition may enhance systemic activity of local therapy
- **Targeted radiosensitizers:** ATR inhibitors, Chk1 inhibitors, Wee1 kinase inhibitors (exploit synthetic lethality in p53-deficient tumours)
- **Concept of synthetic lethality:** G1 checkpoint-deficient cancer cells (p53 mutant) rely on G2/M checkpoint; inhibiting G2/M abrogates all checkpoints in cancer while sparing normal cells

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. MACH-NC meta-analysis (>16,000 patients): concurrent CRT gives 6.5% absolute OS benefit; IC alone does NOT improve OS but may reduce distant metastases
> 2. TPF is superior to PF for IC (TAX 323/324): 10-year larynx preservation 70.3% vs 46.5% — but at cost of higher toxicity
> 3. DeCIDE and PARADIGM trials: adding IC to CRT does NOT improve OS — current evidence does not support IC over CRT alone
> 4. VA Study: IC response used as surrogate marker; responders proceed to RT, non-responders to surgery
> 5. TP53 is the most commonly mutated gene in HNSCC (~80% HPV-negative; 0% HPV-positive where E6 degrades p53)
> 6. EGFR overexpressed in up to 90% of HNSCC — cetuximab (anti-EGFR MAb) improves OS in Bonner trial and EXTREME trial
> 7. PIK3CA mutations are more common in HPV-positive (>60%) than HPV-negative (>30%) HNSCC
> 8. CheckMate 141: nivolumab (anti-PD-1) improves OS to 7.5 vs 5.1 months in recurrent/metastatic HNSCC
> 9. p16 IHC is a surrogate biomarker for HPV-positive disease; HPV-positive HNSCC has distinctly better prognosis
> 10. Loss of normal cell cycle control occurs in 90-100% of both HPV-positive and HPV-negative HNSCC through different mechanisms


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - IC with TPF should only be offered to patients with good performance status and minimal comorbidity — significant risk of neutropenic sepsis and treatment-related death
> - Cetuximab does NOT increase radiation-induced mucosal toxicity (unlike cisplatin); acneiform rash correlates with better tumour response and improved OS
> - The 12 most commonly affected genes in HNSCC: TP53, PIK3CA, CDKN2A, NOTCH1, FADD, let-7c, FAT1, CCND1, PTEN, TP63, EGFR, MYC
> - Immunotherapy side effects (autoimmune) require specialist management — can evolve into life-threatening conditions; entirely different from conventional chemotherapy toxicity profile
> - RTOG 0522 showed that adding cetuximab to cisplatin-based CRT provides NO benefit and increases toxicity — triple combination is NOT recommended
