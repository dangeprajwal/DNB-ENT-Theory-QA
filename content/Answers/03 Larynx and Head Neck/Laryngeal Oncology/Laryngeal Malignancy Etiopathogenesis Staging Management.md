# Laryngeal Malignancy - Etiopathogenesis, Staging and Management

> [!question] Questions Covered
> - Describe the etiopathogenesis and clinical presentation of laryngeal malignancy. Staging and management of laryngeal malignancy.
*Source:** Scott-Brown's Otorhinolaryngology, 8th Edition - Volume 3, Chapter 2 (Aetiology of Head and Neck Cancer), Chapter 4 (Staging), Chapter 14 (Tumours of the Larynx), Chapter 27 (Open Conservation Surgery for Laryngeal Cancer)

---

## INTRODUCTION

Laryngeal malignancy is predominantly squamous cell carcinoma (>95%). It accounts for approximately 2% of all cancers worldwide. Approximately 60% arise in the glottis, 35% in the supraglottis, and 5% in the subglottis. Glottic cancers present early due to voice changes, while supraglottic cancers present late with a higher rate of nodal metastasis. Untreated, the prognosis is poor, but early glottic carcinoma has one of the best cure rates among head and neck cancers.

---

## PART A: ETIOPATHOGENESIS

### Risk Factors

| Risk Factor | Details |
|------------|---------|
| Tobacco | RR 15.5 (men), 12.4 (women) |
| Alcohol | Synergistic with tobacco; >50% additive risk |
| Combined | 50% greater risk than additive effect alone |
| HPV | Only 5-30% of laryngeal cancers (unlike oropharynx) |
| GERD/LPR | Independent risk factor |
| Occupational | Nickel, mustard gas, asbestos, sulphuric acid |

- Supraglottic patients are more likely to be heavy drinkers than glottic/subglottic patients
- Postcricoid carcinoma is associated with Paterson-Brown-Kelly/Plummer-Vinson syndrome (sideropenic dysphagia)
- Immunosuppression (HIV, organ transplant) increases risk

### Premalignancy — Laryngeal Dysplasia

- Incidence: 2-10 per 100,000
- WHO 4th Edition (2017): two-tier system — low-grade SIL and high-grade SIL (based on amended Ljubljana Classification)
- Malignant transformation rates: 11-25%
- Management: complete excision (cold steel or CO2 laser); vocal cord stripping NOT recommended
- Recurrent focal severe dysplasia should be managed as T1 laryngeal carcinoma

## PART B: CLINICAL PRESENTATION

### Glottic Cancer
- Hoarseness is the CARDINAL symptom — even CIS produces voice change (affects 6-cell thick epithelium and superficial lamina propria)
- Any hoarseness persisting >3 weeks requires urgent referral
- Advanced lesions: decreased maximum phonation time, breathiness with cord fixation, dyspnoea, stridor, haemoptysis
- Referred otalgia (via vagal complex) — sinister sign of deep invasion
- Neck nodes are rarely the presenting complaint; if present, signify deep invasion

### Supraglottic Cancer
- Early: globus sensation, foreign body sensation, paraesthesia
- Exophytic tumours: haemoptysis
- Increasing bulk: "hot potato" voice
- Extension to cords: hoarseness
- Lateral extension: referred otalgia, odynophagia, dysphagia
- May be asymptomatic until large — first presentation with neck lump (cervical nodal metastasis) is common

### Subglottic Cancer
- Early: vague "globus" sensation
- Glottic/RLN involvement: hoarseness, diplophonia
- Circumferential progression: progressive dyspnoea, stridor
- Should be considered in differential of "idiopathic" cord paralysis
- Direct thyroid extension may mimic thyroid isthmus lesion

## PART C: STAGING (TNM 8th Edition)

### Glottic Cancer Staging

| T Stage | Description |
|---------|------------|
| T1a | Limited to one vocal cord, normal mobility |
| T1b | Both vocal cords, normal mobility |
| T2 | Extends to supraglottis/subglottis AND/OR impaired cord mobility |
| T3 | Cord fixation AND/OR paraglottic space/minor thyroid cartilage erosion |
| T4a | Through thyroid cartilage OR beyond larynx (trachea, soft tissues, strap muscles, thyroid, oesophagus) |
| T4b | Prevertebral space, mediastinum, or carotid encasement |

### Supraglottic Cancer Staging

| T Stage | Description |
|---------|------------|
| T1 | Limited to one subsite, normal cord mobility |
| T2 | >1 adjacent subsite or extends outside supraglottis, without fixation |
| T3 | Cord fixation AND/OR invades postcricoid, pre-epiglottic, paraglottic space, minor thyroid cartilage erosion |
| T4a | Through thyroid cartilage AND/OR beyond larynx |
| T4b | Prevertebral space, mediastinum, or carotid encasement |

### Stage Grouping

| Stage | TNM |
|-------|-----|
| I | T1 N0 M0 |
| II | T2 N0 M0 |
| III | T1-T2 N1, or T3 N0-N1 |
| IVA | T1-T3 N2, or T4a N0-N2 |
| IVB | Any T N3, or T4b Any N |
| IVC | Any T Any N M1 |

## PART D: MANAGEMENT

### Assessment
- Flexible nasal laryngoscopy: assess cord mobility, panoramic view
- Cross-sectional imaging (CT/MRI): mandatory for all tumour stages
- MRI: higher sensitivity for cartilage invasion than CT
- Assessment under GA: rigid endoscopy + biopsy; determine transoral resectability

### Early Glottic Cancer (T1-T2) — Treatment Recommendations

| T Stage | 1st Choice | 2nd Choice | 3rd Choice |
|---------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| T1a | TLM | RT | OPL |
| T1b | RT or TLM* | OPL | - |
| T2a | RT or TLM* | OPL | - |
| T2b | (C)RT | TLM | OPL |

*Based on patient choice, local expertise, tumour and patient factors

### Comparison of Treatment Modalities

| Factor | OPL | TLM | (C)RT |
|--------|-----|-----|-------|
| Oncologic results | Equivocal | Equivocal | Equivocal |
| Speech quality | Poor | Good | Good |
| Swallowing | Normal | Normal | Impaired |
| Feeding tube | Temporary (1 month) | No | 5-10% |
| [[Tracheostomy|Tracheostomy]] | Temporary (1 month) | No | Occasional |
| Treatment duration | 1 day | 1 day | 7 weeks |
| Cost | Medium | Low | Expensive |

### T3 Laryngeal Cancer — Management Options

**Non-surgical (organ preservation):**
- CRT: Concurrent cisplatin (100 mg/m2 day 1, 22, 43) + RT (65-66 Gy/30 fractions IMRT)
- VA Study (1991): Induction chemo + RT vs TL + RT — comparable 2-year survival (68%); 64% larynx preservation
- RTOG 91-11: CRT superior to ICRT and RT alone for larynx preservation (88% vs 75% vs 70%)
- Long-term follow-up: higher non-cancer deaths in CRT arm (30.8% vs 20.8% ICRT vs 16.9% RT)

**Surgical:**
- OPL/TLM for selected T3 with functioning larynx (comparable outcomes to CRT)
- [[Laryngectomy Rehabilitation Voice Prosthesis|Total laryngectomy]]: indicated for laryngopharyngeal dysfunction, fixed cord, cartilage invasion

**Key principle:** Organ preservation is NOT appropriate when the larynx is non-functional (airway compromise, tube feeding needed, cartilage invasion).

### T4 Laryngeal Cancer
- Primary surgery (total laryngectomy) + adjuvant RT is standard
- Surgery + adjuvant RT shows superior OS compared to CRT in T4N0 disease
- Bilateral [[Neck Dissection|neck dissection]] (levels II-IV) required
- Post-operative RT for adverse features: positive margins, extracapsular spread

### Salvage Surgery for Recurrent Disease
- Salvage total laryngectomy is the standard for post-RT recurrence
- 30-50% of radiorecurrent glottic carcinomas may be suitable for salvage partial laryngectomy
- Radiorecurrent tumours often exhibit microscopic dispersion far beyond original tumour confines
- Intra-operative frozen section analysis is essential

---

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. 60% of laryngeal cancers arise in the glottis, 35% supraglottis, 5% subglottis
> 2. Hoarseness >3 weeks = urgent referral; even CIS produces voice change due to disruption of superficial lamina propria
> 3. Supraglottic cancers have >60% positive nodal rate in advanced disease vs 20% for advanced glottic cancers
> 4. T1a glottic cancer: first choice is TLM; T1b/T2a: RT or TLM; T2b: (C)RT first choice
> 5. TLM offers good speech, no feeding tube, no tracheostomy, low cost — largely replaced OPL for early cancers
> 6. RTOG 91-11: CRT is superior to ICRT and RT alone for larynx preservation (88% vs 75% vs 70%) with similar survival
> 7. Long-term RTOG 91-11 data: higher non-cancer death rate in CRT arm (30.8%) — significant toxicity concern
> 8. T4 disease with extralaryngeal spread: primary surgery + adjuvant RT is the standard of care
> 9. Organ preservation is contraindicated when larynx is non-functional (airway compromise, aspiration, tube feeding)
> 10. Salvage total laryngectomy: standard for post-RT recurrence; radiorecurrent tumours show unpredictable microscopic spread


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - Subglottic cancer should be considered in differential of "idiopathic" vocal cord paralysis, especially in high-risk individuals
> - The incidence of thyroid gland invasion is 10.7% overall; significantly higher with subglottic tumours (RR 7.5) — thyroidectomy reserved for at-risk cases, not routine
> - SEER data suggests decline in 5-year survival for T3N0M0 laryngeal cancer correlating with increased use of CRT and decreased TL — surgery may be superior for locoregional control
> - The VA trial included 48% patients with mobile cords and a proportion of T2 — results should not be generalized to all advanced cancers
> - Laryngeal dysplasia: CO2 laser excision preferred; vocal cord stripping is NOT recommended due to high risk of vocal cord damage
