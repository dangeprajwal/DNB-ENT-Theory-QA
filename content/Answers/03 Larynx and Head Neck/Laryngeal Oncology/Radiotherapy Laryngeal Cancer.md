# [[Radiotherapy Principles Fractionation Advances|Radiotherapy]] in Laryngeal Cancer

**Question:** *Discuss the role of radiotherapy in the management of laryngeal cancer. Describe various fractionation schedules and complications.*

**Source:** Scott-Brown's Otorhinolaryngology, 8th Edition - Volume 3, Chapter 14 (Tumours of the Larynx), Chapter 19 (Principles and Practice of Radiotherapy)

---

## INTRODUCTION

Radiotherapy is a cornerstone in the management of laryngeal cancer, used as primary treatment for early disease, as concurrent chemoradiation (CRT) for organ preservation in advanced disease, and as adjuvant therapy post-operatively. Megavoltage photons (4-6 MV) from linear accelerators cause ionization, producing secondary electrons and hydroxyl radicals that cause DNA double-strand breaks — the principal mechanism of cell kill. The therapeutic ratio depends on differential repair capability between tumour and normal tissues.

---

## PART A: INDICATIONS FOR RADIOTHERAPY BY T STAGE

### Glottic Cancer

| Stage | First Choice | Second Choice | RT Details |
|-------|-------------|---------------|------------|
| T1a | TLM | RT | Primary site only; no nodal treatment |
| T1b | RT or TLM | OPL | Two lateral fields, 4x4 to 5x5 cm |
| T2a | RT or TLM | OPL | Larger fields for T2; may add nodal coverage |
| T2b | (C)RT | TLM, then OPL | Impaired cord mobility → treat aggressively |
| T3 | CRT | OPL or TL | Bilateral nodal coverage mandatory |
| T4a | Surgery (TL) + adjuvant RT/CRT | — | T4 responds poorly to RT; chondronecrosis risk |

- T1 glottic: very low nodal risk → primary site treatment alone (no nodal irradiation)
- T2 with impaired cord mobility behaves like T3 with fixation — warrants concurrent CRT

### Supraglottic Cancer

| Stage | First Choice | Second Choice | RT Details |
|-------|-------------|---------------|------------|
| T1 | RT or TLM/TORS | OPL | Bilateral nodal coverage (levels II-IVa) |
| T2 | RT or TLM/TORS | OPL | Bilateral nodes — high occult metastasis rate |
| T3 | CRT | OPL or TL | Bilateral neck included |
| T4a | Surgery + adjuvant RT/CRT | — | Primary surgery preferred |

Supraglottic cancers ALWAYS require bilateral nodal treatment due to high risk of bilateral microscopic lymph node metastasis (>60%).

## PART B: RADIATION TECHNIQUES

### Conventional RT (Early Glottic Cancer)

- **Megavoltage photons:** 4-6 MV
- **Field design:** Two lateral parallel-opposed fields
- **Field size:** 4x4 cm to 5x5 cm (covers laryngeal skeleton from thyroid notch to inferior border of cricoid)
- **Target:** Primary tumour only (no nodal coverage for T1 glottic)
- **Fractionation (UK):** 50-55 Gy in 16-20 fractions over 3-4 weeks

### 3D Conformal RT (3D-CRT)

- CT-based volumetric planning
- Beam shaping with multi-leaf collimators
- Better dose conformality than conventional 2D planning
- Used for more advanced tumours requiring nodal coverage

### Intensity-Modulated RT (IMRT)

IMRT is now routinely used in most UK and US centres for advanced laryngeal cancer.

**Advantages:**
- Dose conformality around irregular target volumes
- Differential dose delivery to different target volumes simultaneously (simultaneous integrated boost)
- Parotid gland sparing → reduced xerostomia
- Reduces dose to pharyngeal constrictors → reduced late dysphagia
- Dysphagia-optimized IMRT (Do-IMRT) under evaluation (UK DARS trial)

**IMRT dose levels for advanced laryngeal cancer:**

| Target Volume | Two-dose Level | Three-dose Level |
|--------------|---------------|-----------------|
| Gross disease (GTV) | 65-66 Gy in 30 fractions | 70 Gy at 2.12 Gy/fraction |
| High-risk microscopic | — | 60 Gy |
| Prophylactic nodes | 54 Gy in 30 fractions | 54 Gy |

**Nodal coverage:**
- N0 neck: bilateral levels II-IVa at prophylactic dose (54 Gy)
- N+ neck: levels Va, Vb also at risk; if level IIa involved → level Ib and retrostyloid space (VIIb) added
- PARSPORT trial: parotid-sparing IMRT reduced grade 3-4 xerostomia from 83% to 29% at 24 months (p=0.0001)

### Types of IMRT Delivery

- **Fixed-beam step-and-shoot:** Delivered from 5-7 set beam angles with gantry stationary
- **Dynamic arc IMRT (VMAT):** Delivered while gantry rotates continuously — faster treatment

## PART C: FRACTIONATION SCHEDULES

### Key Radiobiological Concepts

- **Alpha/beta ratio:** SCC of head and neck ≈10 Gy; late mucosal damage ≈3 Gy
- **Accelerated repopulation:** Tumour cell repopulation begins at ~21 days (kick-off time, Tk); cell turnover time (Tp) = 3 days
- **Prolonging overall treatment time reduces efficacy** — must be avoided
- **BED (Biologically Effective Dose):** Allows comparison of different fractionation schedules
- **Acute mucosal BED >61 Gy10** is likely intolerable (severe mucositis)

### Fractionation Types

| Type | Definition | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Conventional | 2 Gy/fraction, 5 days/week | 70 Gy in 35 fractions over 7 weeks |
| Hypofractionation | >2 Gy/fraction | 50-55 Gy in 16-20 fractions (early glottic) |
| Hyperfractionation | <2 Gy/fraction (dose-escalated) | 84 Gy in 70 fractions over 46 days (2/day) |
| Accelerated | Reduced OTT (<46 days) | DAHANCA: 68 Gy in 34# over 38 days (6/week) |
| Very accelerated | Markedly reduced OTT | CHART: 54 Gy in 36# over 12 days (3/day) |

### Key Fractionation Trials

| Trial | Schedule | Results |
|-------|----------|---------|
| DAHANCA | 68 Gy in 34# (6 days/week vs 5) | 12% locoregional control advantage at 5 years |
| CHART | 54 Gy in 36# over 12 days (3/day) | Very accelerated; requires total dose reduction |
| MARCH meta-analysis | All altered fractionation trials | Hyperfractionation: ~8% absolute OS benefit at 5 years |
| Yamazaki RCT | T1N0 glottic: 2 Gy vs 2.25 Gy/fraction | 5-yr local control: 77% vs 92% (no toxicity difference) |
| ART DECO | Dose-escalated RT + chemo | NO improvement in LC; increased toxicity |

**Key finding from MARCH meta-analysis:** Only hyperfractionated dose-escalated RT showed survival benefit (~8% at 5 years) — same magnitude as adding concurrent chemotherapy.

**Important:** Two trials of accelerated fractionation + chemotherapy vs conventional fractionation + chemotherapy showed NO additional benefit from acceleration — suggesting accelerated repopulation may already be compensated by concurrent chemotherapy.

## PART D: CONCURRENT CHEMORADIATION

### Standard Protocols

| Regimen | Schedule | Evidence |
|---------|----------|---------|
| High-dose cisplatin | 100 mg/m² days 1, 22, 43 of RT | Standard of care; RTOG 91-11 |
| Weekly cisplatin | 40-50 mg/m² weekly during RT | Similar efficacy, better compliance, less toxicity |
| Carboplatin/5-FU | Weeks 1 and 5 during RT | Alternative for cisplatin-intolerant |
| Cetuximab + RT | Weekly cetuximab during RT | For patients with PS 0-1 unfit for chemotherapy |

### Key CRT Evidence

| Trial | Finding |
|-------|---------|
| RTOG 91-11 | CRT: 88% larynx preservation vs ICRT 75% vs RT 70%; similar OS |
| RTOG 91-11 long-term | Higher non-cancer deaths in CRT arm (30.8% vs ICRT 20.8% vs RT 16.9%) |
| RTOG 91-11 toxicity | CRT: 43% severe late complications; 18% pharyngeal dysfunction; 25% laryngeal dysfunction |
| MACH-NC | Concurrent CRT: 6.5% absolute OS benefit at 5 years; benefit NOT significant >70 years |
| Bonner trial | Cetuximab + RT: 3-yr LRC 47% vs 34%; 5-yr OS 46% vs 36% |
| RTOG 0522 | Adding cetuximab to CRT: NO benefit; MORE toxicity |

### Cetuximab

- Monoclonal anti-EGFR antibody; used when chemotherapy contraindicated
- Does NOT increase radiation-induced mucosal toxicity (unlike cisplatin)
- Side effects: acneiform rash (correlates with response), hypersensitivity reactions, paronychia
- RTOG 0522: triple combination (cisplatin + cetuximab + RT) NOT recommended

## PART E: POST-OPERATIVE (ADJUVANT) RADIOTHERAPY

### Indications for Adjuvant RT After Laryngectomy

| Factor | Details |
|--------|---------|
| pT4 tumours | All T4 disease |
| Close margins | 1-5 mm from tumour |
| Positive margins | <1 mm from tumour |
| Involved node >3 cm | Large nodal disease |
| Multiple positive nodes | High recurrence risk |
| Extracapsular spread (ECS) | Most important factor |
| Perineural invasion | Relative indication |
| Lymphovascular invasion | Relative indication |

### Adjuvant CRT vs RT Alone

- **EORTC 22931 and RTOG 9501:** Patients with ECS and/or microscopically positive margins benefit significantly from post-operative CRT over RT alone (improved OS and DFS)
- Post-operative CRT has ~2% treatment-related death rate
- NOT recommended in patients >70 years or with poor performance status
- **Timing:** Post-operative RT should start within 5 weeks (35 days), no later than 6 weeks (42 days); total surgery-to-RT-completion should not exceed 11 weeks

### Adjuvant RT Doses

- Primary site: minimum 57.6 Gy; up to 63 Gy to areas of ECS
- Prophylactic dose: 50 Gy in 25 fractions controls microscopic disease in >90% of cases

## PART F: COMPLICATIONS OF RADIOTHERAPY

### Acute Complications

| Complication | Details |
|-------------|---------|
| Mucositis | Peak 1-2 weeks post-treatment; grade 3-4 in ~88% with CRT vs 20-30% with RT alone |
| Dysphagia | May require temporary tube feeding |
| Skin reactions | Erythema, desquamation |
| Taste disturbance | Usually recovers |
| Xerostomia (acute) | Begins within first week |
| Hair loss | Within radiation field |
| Treatment-related death | 0-7% RT alone; 0-14% with CRT |

### Late Complications

| Complication | Details |
|-------------|---------|
| Xerostomia | Most common complaint; after 70 Gy, flow drops to ~20% baseline; largely irreversible; slight recovery at 12-18 months |
| Late dysphagia | Up to 50% with CRT; correlates with mean dose to pharyngeal constrictors (50-60 Gy critical) |
| Radiation fibrosis | Neck stiffness, trismus |
| Osteoradionecrosis | Mandible, temporal bone; treatment: debridement, HBO, vascularized [[Free Flaps HN Reconstruction|free flap]] |
| Hypothyroidism | Monitor thyroid function at 6 months; thyroxine replacement 1-2 mcg/kg |
| Pharyngeal stenosis | Stricture formation |
| Dental caries | Fluoride gel application recommended |
| SNHL | IMRT reduces cochlear dose |
| Chondronecrosis | Especially T4 tumours treated with RT |
| Radiation-induced malignancy | 0.3-3% beyond 5 years |
| Carotid artery stenosis | Long-term vascular complication |

### Xerostomia Prevention/Management

| Strategy | Evidence |
|----------|---------|
| IMRT (parotid-sparing) | PARSPORT: grade 3-4 xerostomia 29% vs 83% at 24 months |
| Amifostine | Reduces grade 2-3 xerostomia by 76% (acute) and 67% (late); side effect: hypotension (62%) |
| Submandibular gland transfer | To submental space: 81% had no/minimal xerostomia |
| Pilocarpine | 15-30 mg/day; limited evidence |
| Salivary substitutes | Transient benefit only |

---

## PART G: POST-TREATMENT SURVEILLANCE

- **[[PET CT Nuclear Medicine Scans|PET-CT]] timing:** 10-12 weeks post-CRT (highest sensitivity; earlier = more false positives)
- Residual neck nodes (N2b, N2c, N3): PET-CT → salvage [[Neck Dissection|neck dissection]] only if positive/equivocal
- PET-CT negative predictive value >95%
- Follow-up: bimonthly x2 years → quarterly year 3 → biannually year 4 → annually thereafter
- Monitor: thyroid function, swallowing, aspiration, dental health, second primary cancers

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. Early glottic RT: two lateral fields (4x4 to 5x5 cm), 50-55 Gy in 16-20 fractions over 3-4 weeks; NO nodal treatment needed
> 2. IMRT is the standard technique for advanced laryngeal cancer — allows parotid sparing and differential dose delivery
> 3. PARSPORT trial: parotid-sparing IMRT reduced grade 3-4 xerostomia from 83% to 29% at 24 months
> 4. MARCH meta-analysis: hyperfractionated dose-escalated RT provides ~8% absolute OS benefit at 5 years — same as concurrent chemotherapy
> 5. Accelerated repopulation begins at day 21 (Tk); prolonging overall treatment time reduces RT efficacy
> 6. RTOG 91-11: CRT achieves 88% larynx preservation but 30.8% non-cancer deaths long-term; 43% severe late complications
> 7. MACH-NC: concurrent CRT gives 6.5% absolute OS benefit; NOT significant above 70 years
> 8. Post-operative RT should start within 5 weeks; surgery-to-RT-completion should not exceed 11 weeks total
> 9. EORTC 22931 + RTOG 9501: post-operative CRT superior to RT alone for ECS and positive margins
> 10. PET-CT at 10-12 weeks post-CRT for residual neck assessment — earlier scanning gives false positives


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - Yamazaki RCT: 2.25 Gy/fraction gives 92% local control vs 77% with 2 Gy/fraction for T1N0 glottic cancer — with no difference in toxicity; hypofractionation may be superior for early glottic disease
> - Cetuximab does NOT increase radiation-induced mucosal toxicity but causes acneiform rash; RTOG 0522 showed NO benefit of adding cetuximab to cisplatin-based CRT
> - After 70 Gy, salivary flow drops to ~20% of baseline and is largely irreversible — IMRT is essential for parotid sparing
> - T4 tumours invading cartilage respond poorly to RT — risk of non-functional larynx, aspiration, chondronecrosis, and gastrostomy dependence; primary surgery preferred
> - Late dysphagia correlates with mean dose to pharyngeal constrictors (50-60 Gy range is critical) — dysphagia-optimized IMRT is under evaluation
