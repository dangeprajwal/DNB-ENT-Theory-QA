# Recurrent Respiratory Papillomatosis of the Larynx

> [!question] Questions
> Clinical features, staging and management of a case of recurrent respiratory papillomatosis of the larynx.

**Source:** Scott-Brown's Otorhinolaryngology, 8th Edition - Volume 2, Chapter 32 (Juvenile-onset RRP); Volume 3, Chapter 14 (Tumours of the Larynx)

---

## INTRODUCTION

Recurrent respiratory papillomatosis (RRP) is a potentially life-threatening disease characterized by development of papillomata anywhere in the respiratory tract, from the nasal vestibules to the terminal bronchi. It is caused by HPV types 6 and 11, with HPV 11 associated with more aggressive disease. RRP has a bimodal age distribution: juvenile-onset (JORRP, peak 3-4 years) and adult-onset (peak 20-30 years). Boys and girls are equally affected in JORRP, while adult-onset shows a male preponderance (3:2).

---

## PART A: AETIOLOGY AND PATHOGENESIS

### HPV Biology
- HPV types 6 and 11 are the causative agents
- HPV replicates by episomal maintenance in basal epithelial layer
- Replication occurs only in granular and cornified layers of terminally differentiated epithelium
- Predilection for squamociliary junctions (areas where squamous epithelium meets ciliated epithelium)
- Predominant sites: tonsillar pillars, uvula, vocal folds, laryngeal commissure

### Risk Factors for JORRP

| Factor | Risk |
|--------|------|
| Maternal genital warts | 231x relative risk |
| Latent/active HPV in cervical swabs | 10-25% of women of childbearing age |
| Vaginal delivery to affected mother | Only 1 in 400 infants develops JORRP |
| Prolonged delivery (>10 hours) | 2x increased risk |
| Young mother | Part of susceptibility triad |
| Low socioeconomic status | Part of susceptibility triad |
| HLA-DRB1*0301 | Associated with severe disease; reduced IFN-gamma expression |
| Caesarean section | Does NOT reduce risk |

### Pathology
- Macroscopic: pedunculated or sessile papillomas
- Microscopic: exophytic projections of keratinized squamous epithelium with fibrovascular core
- Koilocytes (vacuolated cells with clear cytoplasmic inclusions) indicate viral infection
- Varying degrees of dyskeratosis, parakeratosis, dysplasia

## PART B: CLINICAL FEATURES

### Presentation
- Hoarseness and stridor are the cardinal symptoms
- Also: chronic cough, paroxysms of choking, recurrent respiratory infections, failure to thrive
- May be misdiagnosed as asthma, laryngitis, bronchitis, or croup
- Delay of diagnosis up to 8 years possible

### Natural History
- Extremely variable course
- Most children require debulking at 2-3 month intervals during active disease
- Severe disease may require weekly intervention
- Median debulking procedures: 7-13 (range: 2 to 402 in one series of 118 patients)
- Tendency for remission in early teenage years (attributed to hormonal changes at puberty)
- Adult-onset in women: exacerbations during pregnancy
- Remission NOT related to HPV clearance — viral DNA persists in mucosa

### Poor Prognostic Signs
- Onset before age 3 years
- HPV-11 infection (more aggressive than HPV-6)
- Extralaryngeal spread

### Extralaryngeal Spread
- Approximately 1/3 of patients develop extralaryngeal disease
- Tracheal involvement in ~1/4 of patients
- Distal bronchopulmonary involvement: 4-11% with long-standing disease
- Factors: subglottic papillomas, [[Tracheostomy|tracheostomy]], long disease duration
- Can cause obstructive pneumonias, cavitary lesions, fever, sepsis, atelectasis

### Malignant Degeneration
- Rare but universally fatal; ~20 paediatric cases reported
- Higher incidence with younger onset (2 vs 6 years; P=0.009) and tracheal involvement
- HPV oncoproteins E6 and E7 inactivate p53 and pRb tumour suppressors
- In children: malignancy usually develops in bronchopulmonary tree (unlike adults where laryngeal)

## PART C: STAGING — DERKAY SCORING SYSTEM

### Scoring Per Anatomical Site
- **0** = none
- **1** = surface lesion
- **2** = raised lesion
- **3** = bulky lesion

### Sites Assessed

| Region | Sites |
|--------|-------|
| Larynx | Epiglottis (lingual/laryngeal), aryepiglottic folds R/L, false cords R/L, true cords R/L, arytenoids R/L, anterior commissure, posterior commissure, subglottis |
| Trachea | Upper/middle/lower thirds |
| Bronchi | Right/Left |
| Other | Tracheostomy stoma, nose, palate, pharynx, oesophagus, lungs |

Total score = sum of all site scores; higher score = more extensive disease

## PART D: MANAGEMENT

### Principles
- No curative treatment exists — goal is to maintain airway patency and preserve voice
- Treatment confined to epithelial layer depth
- Viral typing at initial pathology workup (HPV 11 = aggressive)
- Multidisciplinary approach; regular biopsies to monitor for dysplasia

### Surgical Treatment — Comparison

| Method | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|--------|-----------|--------------|
| Powered microdebrider | **Gold standard**; minimal trauma, no thermal damage, less vocal fold fibrosis, shorter procedure | No haemostasis |
| Cold steel (microflap) | No thermal damage, no vapour plume | No direct haemostasis |
| CO2 laser | Good haemostasis, ablates papillomas effectively | Late complications 13-45% (fibrosis, stenosis, webbing); infectious vapour plume |
| KTP laser (532 nm) | Fibre-delivered, good haemostasis, office-based use | Limited data |
| Pulsed dye laser (585 nm) | Photoangiolysis of microcirculation, minimal fibrosis, fibre-delivered | Less effective for large exophytic lesions |
| Coblation (40-70°C) | Low-heat, minimal invasive | Limited data; 3% of UK RRP treatment |

### Office-Based Management (Paradigm Shift)
- Initial theatre control with angiolytic lasers → office maintenance
- KTP laser 532 nm: small fibre (300 microns), portable, variable power
- Technique: blanching before complete ablation → suction → re-treat if necessary
- Bevacizumab injection: 25 mg subepithelial after ablation (10-minute awake procedure)
- Reduces GA burden and voice complications significantly

### Adjuvant Therapies

| Agent | Mechanism | Evidence |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| Cidofovir (intralesional) | Inhibits viral DNA polymerase | 1 mg/mL, 2-weekly x4 then extend intervals; stage decreased 9.2→3.4; dose <3 mg/kg; Level 3 evidence |
| Bevacizumab (Avastin) | Anti-VEGF monoclonal antibody | 25 mg/mL x3 injections at 2-3 week intervals; dramatic reduction in surgical frequency; some achieve total eradication |
| Interferon-α | Antiviral, antiproliferative, immunomodulatory | 2-5 MU/m² SC; RCT: significant reduction in growth first 6 months only; side effects: pancytopenia, hepatorenal failure |
| Indole-3-carbinol | Alters oestrogen metabolism to antiproliferative | From cruciferous vegetables; 21/33 partial/total response |
| Bevacizumab + KTP laser | Combined angiolytic approach | Office-based; reduces procedure frequency; some disease-free |

### Tracheostomy Considerations
- Creates iatrogenic squamociliary junction (additional papilloma predilection site)
- Evidence of extensive tracheobronchial seeding
- VERY LAST RESORT — but may be life-saving in severe disease
- Facilitates longer intervals between debulking

### HPV Vaccination
- Gardasil (quadrivalent: HPV 6, 11, 16, 18) → nonavalent (adds 31, 33, 45, 52, 58)
- 3 intramuscular injections: initial, 2 months, 6 months
- No live virions; induces antibody response
- Preventive role: Australia (2007) nationwide programme → significant decrease in HPV 6/11/16/18
- Therapeutic role: mixed results; only one entirely juvenile-onset study showed no change; other mixed studies more promising
- May have both preventive AND therapeutic potential

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. HPV types 6 and 11 cause RRP; HPV 11 is more aggressive than HPV 6
> 2. Caesarean section does NOT reduce risk of JORRP; only 1 in 400 infants to affected mothers develops disease
> 3. Powered microdebrider is the GOLD STANDARD for surgical management — less vocal fold fibrosis than CO2 laser
> 4. CO2 laser late complications: 13-45% (vocal fold fibrosis, stenosis, glottic webbing, arytenoid fixation)
> 5. Cidofovir: intralesional injection at 1 mg/mL; dose should be <3 mg/kg; no evidence of promoting dysplasia
> 6. Bevacizumab: 25 mg subepithelial injection; dramatic reduction in surgical frequency; some patients achieve total eradication
> 7. Malignant degeneration: rare but universally fatal; ~20 paediatric cases; all associated with HPV E6/E7 inactivating p53/pRb
> 8. Extralaryngeal spread in ~1/3 of patients; tracheostomy creates additional squamociliary junction for papilloma growth
> 9. Derkay staging: scores 0-3 per site (none → bulky); total score guides disease severity assessment
> 10. Remission in teenage years but NOT due to HPV clearance — viral DNA persists; hormonal changes may play a role


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - HLA-DRB1*0301 is associated with severe JORRP in two independent large studies — reduced IFN-gamma expression may explain severity
> - Microdebrider produces better voice outcomes than CO2 laser (lower jitter, shimmer, and perceptual scores)
> - CO2 laser vapour plume carries significant viral load — effective plume extractor is essential
> - Office-based KTP laser + bevacizumab paradigm shift: initial theatre control → office maintenance, reducing GA burden
> - Cimetidine (40 mg/kg) has shown remarkable improvement in one case of very advanced JORRP with tracheobronchial-pulmonary involvement — mechanism is immunomodulatory
