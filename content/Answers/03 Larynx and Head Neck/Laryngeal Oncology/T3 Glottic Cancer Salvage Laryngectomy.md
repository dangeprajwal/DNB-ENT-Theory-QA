# T3 Glottic Cancer — Pattern of Spread, Management and Salvage Laryngectomy

> [!question] Questions
> Discuss the pattern of spread of glottic laryngeal cancer. Describe the management of T3 glottic cancer as per the latest guidelines. Discuss the role of salvage laryngectomy in management of such a lesion.

**Source:** Scott-Brown's Otorhinolaryngology, 8th Edition - Volume 3, Chapter 14 (Tumours of the Larynx), Chapter 27 (Open Conservation Surgery)

---

## INTRODUCTION

T3 glottic cancer is defined as tumour limited to the larynx with vocal cord fixation AND/OR paraglottic space invasion with minor thyroid cartilage erosion. Management of T3 disease is one of the most debated topics in head and neck oncology. While organ preservation with concurrent chemoradiation (CRT) is widely used, large database analyses suggest declining survival rates with increased non-surgical treatment, and primary surgery may offer superior locoregional control in selected patients.

---

## PART A: PATTERN OF SPREAD OF GLOTTIC CANCER

### Local Spread

| Direction | Structures Involved | Clinical Significance |
|-----------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Anterior | AC → contralateral cord | No perichondrium at Broyles' ligament; direct cartilage invasion |
| Posterior | Arytenoid, cricoarytenoid joint, posterior commissure | Cord fixation; contraindication for hemilaryngectomy if RLN invaded |
| Lateral | Thyroid cartilage, paraglottic space | Paraglottic invasion = T3; extralaryngeal = T4 |
| Superior | Ventricle, false cords, supraglottis | Transglottic spread |
| Inferior | Subglottis, trachea, cricothyroid membrane | Thyroid gland invasion (RR 7.5 for subglottic tumours) |

### Mechanisms of Vocal Cord Fixation (T3)
- Mass effect on the cord from bulky tumour
- Invasion of vocalis muscle/intrinsic laryngeal muscles
- Cricoarytenoid joint invasion/fixation
- Recurrent laryngeal nerve invasion (contraindication for hemilaryngectomy)

### Important Spread Concepts

- **T2 with impaired cord mobility** has reduced local control similar to T3 with fixation — should be treated more aggressively
- **Transglottic cancer** spans all three laryngeal subsites (said to originate from ventricle); by definition ≥T3
- Subglottic extension: 50% of subglottic tumours invade cricoid; 75% already extralaryngeal at diagnosis
- Thyroid gland invasion: overall 10.7%; thyroidectomy reserved for at-risk cases (subglottic primary/extension)

### Lymphatic Drainage — Glottic Cancer

- T1 glottic: very small risk of occult nodes → primary site treatment alone suffices
- T2 glottic: ~7% occult nodal risk → no consensus on neck treatment
- Advanced glottic: ~20% positive nodal rate (much lower than supraglottic >60%)
- Fixed cord (T3) tumours: higher rate of occult cervical metastases → neck must be addressed

## PART B: MANAGEMENT OF T3 GLOTTIC CANCER

### The Controversy

No study has shown improvements in local control and survival compared to [[Laryngectomy Rehabilitation Voice Prosthesis|total laryngectomy]] for T3 disease. The landmark trials (VA Study, RTOG 91-11) established organ preservation, but with important caveats:
- VA Study: 48% of patients had mobile cords; majority were T3 (not T4)
- RTOG 91-11: 42% had mobile cords; included T2 patients who could have had partial laryngectomy
- Neither trial had a partial laryngectomy arm

### Option 1: Concurrent Chemoradiation (CRT)

**Indications:**
- Good performance status, minimal comorbidity
- Functioning larynx (no airway compromise)
- No significant dysphagia requiring tube feeding
- No cartilage invasion

**Protocol:**
- Cisplatin 100 mg/m² on days 1, 22, 43 concurrent with IMRT
- IMRT dose: 65-66 Gy gross disease, 54 Gy prophylactic nodes, in 30 fractions
- Alternative: weekly cisplatin 40-50 mg/m² (similar efficacy, better compliance, less toxicity)
- Cetuximab during RT if chemotherapy contraindicated (improves 3-yr LRC: 47% vs 34%; 5-yr OS: 46% vs 36%)

**Key Evidence:**

| Trial | Finding |
|-------|---------|
| VA Study (1991) | IC+RT vs TL+RT: comparable 2-yr survival (68%); 64% larynx preservation |
| RTOG 91-11 | CRT > ICRT > RT alone for larynx preservation (88% vs 75% vs 70%) |
| RTOG 91-11 long-term | Higher non-cancer deaths in CRT arm (30.8% vs 20.8% ICRT vs 16.9% RT) |
| RTOG 91-11 toxicity | CRT: 43% severe late complications; 18% pharyngeal dysfunction; 25% laryngeal dysfunction |

### Option 2: Open Partial Laryngectomy / TLM

**Indications:**
- T3 tumour within boundaries for conservation surgery
- Adequate pulmonary reserve (can walk 2 flights of stairs)
- Patient rejects TL, unfit/rejects CRT
- Available surgical expertise

**Procedures:**
- SCPL-CHEP for tumours crossing AC with cord fixation (local control up to 98%)
- TLM in expert centres (relatively immature for T3)
- Salvage partial laryngectomy for radiorecurrent T3

**Evidence:**
- Systematic review (Thomas et al.): pooled local control at 24 months for OPL in 5061 patients — results comparable to CRT
- Both OPL and TLM are efficient organ-preserving methods for T3 (systematic review)
- Best organ preservation and survival rates, lowest recurrence rates seen with OPL (but with selection bias)

### Option 3: Total Laryngectomy

**Indications:**
- Non-functional larynx (airway compromise, aspiration, tube feeding)
- Fixed cord with dysfunctional larynx
- Gross cartilage invasion
- Patient preference
- Failed organ preservation

**Evidence from database analyses:**

| Study | Finding |
|-------|---------|
| Hoffman et al. (NCDB) | 5-yr survival: surgery+RT 65.2% vs CRT 59.2% vs RT alone 42.7% |
| Chen et al. (SEER) | CRT use increased from <7% to 45% (1985-2007); TL decreased from 42% to 32%; 4-yr survival: TL 51% vs CRT 48% |
| Megwalu et al. (SEER) | Surgical therapy: better 2-yr and 5-yr DSS and OS (P<.001) |
| Meta-analysis | TL better 2-yr OS (OR 2.79) and 5-yr OS (OR 1.52) vs non-surgical |

### Larynx Preservation Consensus Panel Recommendations

Organ preservation is NOT appropriate when:
- Larynx is non-functional (airway compromise, aspiration)
- Dysphagia requiring tube feeding
- Gross cartilage invasion (T4)
- Primary endpoint should be SURVIVAL AND FUNCTION, not just larynx preservation
- Meaningful functional endpoint: laryngooesophageal dysfunction-free survival at 2 years

## PART C: SALVAGE LARYNGECTOMY

### Role and Indications
- Salvage total laryngectomy (STL) is the standard of care for recurrent laryngeal cancer after primary RT or CRT
- Required in patients who fail organ preservation protocols
- VA Study: salvage laryngectomy rates for T3 (29%) vs T4 (56%); higher in glottic cancer with fixed cords

### Challenges of Salvage Surgery

**Radiorecurrent tumours:**
- More aggressive behaviour than primary tumours
- Often recur at higher T-stage than original tumour
- Microscopic tumour deposits dispersed far beyond original confines (Zbaren et al.)
- Lymphatic drainage unpredictable in irradiated field
- [[Wound Healing Phases Types Factors|Wound healing]] significantly compromised

**Wound complications:**
- Pharyngocutaneous fistula: significantly higher risk post-CRT
- Delayed healing due to radiation fibrosis and poor vascularity
- Higher complication rates after CRT than after RT alone

### Salvage Partial Laryngectomy (Selected Cases)

- 30-50% of radiorecurrent GLOTTIC cancers may be suitable
- Stringent selection criteria:
  - Tumour within original confines (no progression on imaging)
  - Adequate endoscopic and CT assessment
  - Patient fit for post-operative rehabilitation
- Salvage vertical hemilaryngectomy: for selected small recurrences
- Salvage SCPL-CH(E)P: local control rates of 83.3% for larger lesions
- Intra-operative frozen section analysis is ESSENTIAL

### Salvage for Supraglottic Cancer
- Only 30% may be suitable for salvage conservation surgery after RT
- Higher radiation dose to base of tongue and pharyngeal constrictors → more fibrosis
- Increased aspiration and fistula risk
- Most require salvage total laryngectomy
- Poor oncologic results especially after salvage HSPL

### Post-Salvage Management
- Adjuvant re-irradiation is limited; may consider reirradiation in select cases
- Regular follow-up with endoscopy and imaging
- Vigilance for stomal recurrence (especially post-CRT salvage)

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. T3 glottic = cord fixation AND/OR paraglottic space invasion with minor thyroid cartilage erosion
> 2. RTOG 91-11: CRT achieves 88% larynx preservation vs 75% (ICRT) vs 70% (RT alone) — BUT higher non-cancer deaths (30.8%) in CRT arm
> 3. Large database analyses (SEER, NCDB) consistently show superior survival with surgical therapy for advanced laryngeal cancer
> 4. Organ preservation is contraindicated with non-functional larynx, airway compromise, cartilage invasion, tube feeding
> 5. Recommended functional endpoint: laryngooesophageal dysfunction-free survival at 2 years
> 6. VA Study: salvage TL rates lower for T3 (29%) than T4 (56%); glottic cancer with fixed cord had highest salvage rate
> 7. Radiorecurrent tumours show microscopic dispersion far beyond original confines — intra-operative frozen sections are mandatory
> 8. 30-50% of radiorecurrent glottic cancers can undergo salvage partial laryngectomy with stringent selection
> 9. SCPL-CHEP salvage: local control 83.3%; vertical hemilaryngectomy for selected smaller recurrences
> 10. CRT should NOT be used as "one size fits all" for T3 cancers — individualized approach recommended


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - The VA trial and RTOG 91-11 both had >40% of patients with mobile cords — results should not be generalized to all T3 disease
> - T2 tumours with impaired cord mobility behave like T3 with fixation (similar local control rates) — treat aggressively
> - Weekly cisplatin (40-50 mg/m²) is an acceptable alternative to 3-weekly high-dose (100 mg/m²) — similar efficacy, better compliance, less toxicity
> - Cetuximab does NOT increase radiation-induced mucosal toxicity (unlike cisplatin); main side effects: acneiform rash, hypersensitivity
> - Post-CRT residual neck disease (N2b, N2c, N3): PET scan at 10-12 weeks → salvage [[Neck Dissection|neck dissection]] only if positive/equivocal
