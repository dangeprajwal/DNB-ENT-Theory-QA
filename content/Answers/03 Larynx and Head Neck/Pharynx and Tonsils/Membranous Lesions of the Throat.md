# Membranous Lesions of the Throat

> [!question] Questions
> Enumerate the causes of membranous lesions in the throat. How would you differentiate between them?

**Source:** Scott-Brown's Volume 2, Chapter 29 (Acute Laryngeal Infections) and Volume 3, Chapter 51 (Pharyngitis)

---

## Introduction

Membranous lesions of the throat refer to the formation of a pseudomembrane or membrane on the pharyngeal, tonsillar or laryngeal mucosa. These membranes may be composed of necrotic tissue, fibrinous exudate, bacteria, or desquamated epithelium. The clinical significance lies in differentiating between the various causes, as some — particularly diphtheria — are life-threatening and require urgent specific treatment. A high index of suspicion is essential, especially in unvaccinated individuals, immigrants from endemic areas, and immunocompromised patients.

---

## PART A: Causes of Membranous Lesions

### Infective Causes

| Cause | Type of Membrane |
|---|---|
| Diphtheria | Grey, tough, adherent pseudomembrane |
| Infectious mononucleosis | Grey-white, false membrane |
| Acute follicular tonsillitis | White-yellow exudates in crypts |
| Vincent's angina | Grey-green, necrotic membrane |
| Candidiasis (thrush) | White, curd-like pseudomembrane |
| Scarlet fever | Yellow membranous film |
| Secondary syphilis | Grey-white 'snail-track' ulcers |

### Non-Infective Causes

- Agranulocytosis
- Leukaemia
- Aplastic anaemia
- Chemical or thermal burns
- Post-radiation mucositis

---

## PART B: Differentiation of Key Causes

### 1. Diphtheria

- **Causative organism:** Corynebacterium diphtheriae — gram-positive, non-motile pleomorphic bacillus
- **Membrane:** Characteristic fibrinous grey pseudomembrane on the tonsils, often spreading to other areas of the pharynx and larynx
- **Key feature:** Attempts to remove the pseudomembrane cause bleeding from the pharyngeal mucosa (membrane is adherent to underlying tissue)
- **Associated features:** Bull-neck appearance (marked cervical lymphadenopathy + cellulitis), sore throat, fever, toxaemia
- **Complications:** Toxic myocarditis (2nd week — may be fatal), peripheral neuritis (palatal paralysis with nasal regurgitation), acute airway obstruction from laryngeal involvement
- **Diagnosis:** Culture on Loeffler medium shows 'Chinese character' appearance; Elek test for toxigenicity; rapid immunochromatographic strip (ICS) and PCR for rapid diagnosis
- **Treatment:** Antitoxin on clinical suspicion (do NOT await microbiological confirmation — delay increases mortality); high-dose benzyl penicillin; airway management as needed

### 2. Infectious Mononucleosis (EBV)

- **Membrane:** Grey false membrane lining the oropharynx; may cover all lymphoid tissue of Waldeyer's ring
- **Key feature:** Extensive false membranes with significant risk of respiratory obstruction; does NOT bleed on removal (unlike diphtheria)
- **Associated features:** Classical triad of sore throat, fever and cervical lymphadenopathy; splenomegaly (50%); hepatomegaly (10%); petechiae on soft palate; rubelliform rash if ampicillin is given
- **Diagnosis:** Monospot test (sensitivity 63–95%); gold standard is IgM antibody to EBV viral capsid antigen; lymphocyte/WBC ratio >0.35 suggestive
- **Treatment:** Symptomatic; steroids for airway obstruction; ampicillin-based antibiotics must be avoided

### 3. Acute Follicular Tonsillitis (GABHS)

- **Membrane:** White-yellow necrotic or purulent exudates in tonsillar crypts; does not form a continuous membrane
- **Key feature:** Exudates are confined to the tonsillar crypts and are easily removed without bleeding
- **Associated features:** Sore throat, fever, tender jugulodigastric lymph nodes, pharyngeal erythema, scarlet fever rash in some cases
- **Diagnosis:** Clinical; Centor score helps differentiate bacterial from viral; RADT or throat culture if needed
- **Treatment:** Penicillin (drug of choice); supportive care with analgesia

### 4. Vincent's Angina (Acute Necrotizing Ulcerative Gingivitis)

- **Causative organisms:** Borrelia vincenti (spirochaete) and Fusobacterium species (fusiform bacilli) — synergistic infection
- **Membrane:** Grey-green necrotic membrane, usually unilateral, covering an ulcer on the tonsil or gingiva
- **Key feature:** Foul-smelling breath (foetor oris); membrane is friable and reveals a bleeding ulcer when removed
- **Associated features:** Seen in debilitated, malnourished or immunocompromised patients; poor dental hygiene; painful, bleeding gums
- **Diagnosis:** Smear shows spirochaetes and fusiform bacilli on gram stain
- **Treatment:** Penicillin or metronidazole; dental hygiene; nutritional support

### 5. Candidiasis (Thrush)

- **Causative organism:** Candida species (most commonly C. albicans)
- **Membrane:** White, curd-like pseudomembranous patches on the pharyngeal mucosa, tongue and buccal mucosa
- **Key feature:** Patches can be scraped off to reveal a raw, erythematous base; not adherent
- **Associated features:** Common in neonates, immunocompromised (HIV, chemotherapy), patients on prolonged antibiotics or inhaled corticosteroids, diabetics
- **Diagnosis:** Clinical; confirmed by culture or KOH preparation showing hyphae and spores
- **Treatment:** Topical antifungals (nystatin); systemic fluconazole for severe or recurrent cases

### 6. Scarlet Fever

- **Causative organism:** GABHS producing erythemogenic (erythrogenic) exotoxin
- **Membrane:** Yellow membranous film covering tonsils and pharynx
- **Key feature:** Pathognomonic 'strawberry tongue' (desquamation of lingual papillae); punctate erythematous 'sandpaper' rash with reddened flexor creases and circumoral pallor
- **Associated features:** Fever, sore throat, cervical lymphadenopathy
- **Treatment:** Penicillin

### 7. Secondary Syphilis

- **Causative organism:** Treponema pallidum
- **Membrane:** Grey-white 'snail-track' ulcers covered by a grey-white membrane; membrane scraped off reveals a pink base with no bleeding
- **Key feature:** Lesions are highly infectious; occur 4–6 weeks after primary chancre; more common in oral cavity than oropharynx
- **Associated features:** Fever, generalized lymphadenopathy, mucocutaneous rash, malaise
- **Diagnosis:** Darkfield microscopy of smear; serological tests (VDRL/RPR for screening; treponemal EIA for confirmation)
- **Treatment:** Penicillin 2.4 mega units IM

## PART C: Key Differentiating Features

| Feature | Diphtheria | IM | Tonsillitis | Vincent's |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Membrane colour | Grey | Grey-white | White-yellow | Grey-green |
| Adherence | Very adherent | Moderate | Easily removed | Friable |
| Bleeds on removal | Yes | No | No | Yes (ulcer) |
| Distribution | Tonsils → spreads | Waldeyer's ring | Tonsillar crypts | Unilateral |
| Bull neck | Yes | No | No | No |
| Foetor | Mild | Mild | No | Severe |
| Splenomegaly | No | Yes (50%) | No | No |

---

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. Diphtheria produces a grey, adherent pseudomembrane that bleeds on attempted removal — the hallmark differentiating feature
> 2. In diphtheria, antitoxin should be given on clinical suspicion — do not await microbiological confirmation as delay increases mortality
> 3. Infectious mononucleosis produces grey false membranes that may cover the entire Waldeyer's ring — ampicillin must be avoided (90% rash)
> 4. Vincent's angina is a synergistic infection of spirochaetes and fusiform bacilli — characterized by foul-smelling breath and a necrotic, friable membrane
> 5. Candida pseudomembrane is white and curd-like, easily scraped off to reveal a raw erythematous base
> 6. Scarlet fever produces a yellow membranous film with pathognomonic 'strawberry tongue' and 'sandpaper' rash
> 7. Secondary syphilis produces 'snail-track' ulcers that are highly infectious — pink base with no bleeding when scraped
> 8. The Centor score helps differentiate GABHS pharyngitis from viral pharyngitis but cannot provide a precise diagnosis
> 9. Diphtheria remains important in unvaccinated individuals and travellers from endemic areas
> 10. Agranulocytosis and leukaemia must be considered in the differential diagnosis of pharyngeal membranes — check full blood count


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - Any pharyngeal membrane in an unvaccinated child or a traveller from an endemic area should be treated as diphtheria until proven otherwise
> - The 'Chinese character' appearance on Loeffler medium culture is characteristic of Corynebacterium diphtheriae
> - In infectious mononucleosis, nasopharyngeal lymphoid tissue is present in 92% of patients (unlike acute tonsillitis) — flexible nasendoscopy aids differentiation
> - Toxic myocarditis from diphtheria occurs in the 2nd week and may be fatal — bed rest is essential until the danger has passed
> - When in doubt about the nature of a pharyngeal membrane, take a swab, start penicillin, and arrange haematological investigation to exclude blood dyscrasias
