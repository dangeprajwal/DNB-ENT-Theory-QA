# Thornwaldt's Cyst

**Question:** *Discuss the aetiology, clinical features and treatment of Thornwaldt's cyst.*

**Source:** Scott-Brown's Volume 2, Chapter 23 (Congenital Nasal Masses and Cysts)

---

## Introduction

A Thornwaldt cyst (also spelled Tornwaldt) is a cystic lesion arising from the pharyngeal recess or bursa, which sits in the midline of the posterior wall of the nasopharynx. The pharyngeal bursa ends adjacent to the adenoids and is lined by the pharyngeal mucous membrane. Cystic transformation of this recess was first described by Thornwaldt in 1885 and the condition bears his name. It is a relatively uncommon but important lesion in the differential diagnosis of nasopharyngeal masses and midline posterior nasopharyngeal pathology.

---

## PART A: Aetiology and Embryology

### Embryological Basis

The pharyngeal bursa is a persistent remnant of the connection between the notochord and the pharyngeal endoderm. During embryological development, the cranial tip of the notochord comes into contact with the pharyngeal endoderm in the region of the future nasopharynx. As the notochord regresses, a small mucosal pouch or recess (the pharyngeal bursa) may persist in the midline of the posterior nasopharyngeal wall.

### Pathogenesis

- The pharyngeal bursa becomes obstructed, leading to accumulation of secretions and cystic dilatation
- Infection or inflammation of the cyst contents produces the clinical syndrome described by Thornwaldt
- The cyst is lined by respiratory-type epithelium (ciliated columnar) continuous with the pharyngeal mucosa
- Adhesion to the cervical vertebral periosteum may occur in long-standing cases

---

## PART B: Clinical Features

### Symptoms

- **Nasal obstruction** — from mass effect in the nasopharynx
- **Occipital pain/headache** — possibly from adhesion to the cervical vertebral periosteum or inflammation
- **Aural fullness** — may result from Eustachian tube dysfunction due to proximity of the cyst
- **Post-nasal discharge** — from cyst contents draining into the nasopharynx
- **Halitosis** — from infected cyst contents
- The cyst rarely causes significant obstruction in neonates

### Signs

- Smooth, rounded, midline mass in the posterior nasopharyngeal wall
- Located deep to the adenoids in children
- May be an incidental finding on MRI or CT performed for other indications

---

## PART C: Diagnosis

### Endoscopy

Endoscopic examination of the nasopharynx confirms the diagnosis, revealing a smooth, rounded, midline cystic swelling in the posterior nasopharyngeal wall.

### Imaging

- **CT scan** — demonstrates the cystic lesion in the midline of the posterior nasopharynx and any adhesion to the cervical vertebrae
- **MRI** — provides excellent soft-tissue detail; typically shows high signal on T2-weighted images consistent with fluid contents; helps differentiate from other nasopharyngeal lesions including malignancy

### Differential Diagnosis

- Adenoid cyst or retention cyst
- [[Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma|Nasopharyngeal carcinoma]]
- Minor salivary gland tumour
- Rathke's pouch cyst
- Branchial cleft cyst

---

## PART D: Treatment

### Conservative

- Asymptomatic Thornwaldt cysts discovered incidentally require no treatment
- Initial management of symptomatic cases may include nasal decongestants

### Surgical Treatment

- **Endoscopic marsupialization** — the recommended surgical approach; performed under endoscopic guidance
- **Endoscopic excision** — complete removal of the cyst
- **Total clearance** — if complete excision is required and the cyst has significant adhesion to the cervical vertebrae, a palatal approach may be necessary

### Prognosis

- Recurrence after adequate marsupialization or excision is uncommon
- Prognosis is excellent as this is a benign condition

---

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. Thornwaldt's cyst arises from cystic transformation of the pharyngeal recess (bursa) in the midline of the posterior nasopharyngeal wall
> 2. The pharyngeal bursa is a persistent remnant of the notochord–pharyngeal endoderm connection
> 3. Clinical features include nasal obstruction, occipital pain, aural fullness and post-nasal discharge
> 4. The cyst rarely causes significant obstruction in neonates
> 5. Endoscopic examination confirms the diagnosis — smooth, midline, cystic nasopharyngeal mass
> 6. CT and MRI demonstrate the cyst and any adhesion to the cervical vertebrae
> 7. Endoscopic marsupialization or excision under endoscopic guidance is the recommended treatment
> 8. Total clearance may require a palatal approach if there is significant vertebral adhesion


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - Always consider Thornwaldt's cyst in the differential diagnosis of midline posterior nasopharyngeal masses — it can be easily overlooked, especially when obscured by adenoid tissue in children
> - The association with occipital pain is distinctive and should raise suspicion — this is thought to be related to adhesion to the cervical vertebral periosteum
> - MRI is the best imaging modality to differentiate Thornwaldt's cyst from nasopharyngeal carcinoma — the cyst shows typical fluid signal characteristics
> - Asymptomatic cysts discovered incidentally on cross-sectional imaging require no treatment — reassurance is sufficient
