# Velopharyngeal Insufficiency

> [!question] Questions
> What is velopharyngeal insufficiency? Discuss the aetiology, assessment and management of velopharyngeal insufficiency. How would you manage rhinolalia aperta? Describe the surgical anatomy of the nasopharyngeal inlet in this context.

**Source:** Scott-Brown's Volume 2, Chapter 18 (Cleft Lip and Palate), Chapter 17 (Speech and Language), Chapter 26 (The Adenoid and Adenoidectomy)

---

## Introduction

Velopharyngeal insufficiency (VPI) is the inability of the velopharyngeal sphincter to achieve adequate closure between the oropharynx and the nasopharynx during speech and swallowing. This results in hypernasal speech (rhinolalia aperta) and nasal regurgitation of fluids. VPI can occur in children with a repaired cleft palate, those with a submucous cleft, children without any obvious palatal abnormality, and as a complication of adenoidectomy. About 20% of cleft palate children have persistent speech disorder following surgery, falling into the worst category of intelligibility.

---

## PART A: Surgical Anatomy of the Nasopharyngeal Inlet

### The Velopharyngeal Sphincter

The velopharyngeal port is the communication between the oropharynx and the nasopharynx. Closure of this port is essential for normal speech (to prevent nasal escape of air during production of oral consonants) and for swallowing (to prevent nasal regurgitation). The sphincter comprises:

- **Soft palate (velum):** The primary mobile structure that elevates and posteriorly contacts the posterior pharyngeal wall
- **Levator veli palatini:** The key muscle responsible for velar elevation; its normal insertion is a transverse sling across the midline of the soft palate
- **Tensor veli palatini:** Tenses the soft palate; has a less important role in closure
- **Palatopharyngeus:** Forms the posterior tonsillar pillar; contributes to medial movement of the lateral pharyngeal walls
- **Superior constrictor:** Contributes to Passavant's ridge — a bulge of the posterior pharyngeal wall that assists closure
- **Musculus uvulae:** Adds bulk to the nasal surface of the velum, assisting seal

### Passavant's Ridge

A horizontal ridge on the posterior pharyngeal wall formed by contraction of the superior constrictor and palatopharyngeus muscles. This ridge assists velopharyngeal closure by providing a shelf against which the elevated soft palate can seal.

### Adenoid Pad

In children, the adenoid occupies the posterior nasopharyngeal wall and effectively reduces the distance the soft palate must travel to achieve closure. This is why adenoidectomy can unmask pre-existing palatal dysfunction.

## PART B: Aetiology

### Causes of VPI

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Cleft palate (repaired) | About 20% have persistent VPI |
| Submucous cleft palate | Bifid uvula, zona pellucida, notch |
| Post-adenoidectomy | Incidence 1 in 1500 to 1 in 10,000 |
| Palatal paralysis | Cerebral palsy, neurological causes |
| Craniofacial surgery | Midfacial osteotomy |
| Congenital short palate | Non-cleft velopharyngeal dysfunction |

### Post-Adenoidectomy VPI

- Clinically significant incidence: 1 in 1500 to 1 in 3000
- Stigmata of submucous cleft (bifid uvula, zona pellucida, hard palate notch) must be examined at the time of adenoidectomy
- VPI occurring after adenoidectomy will spontaneously resolve in approximately 50% of cases
- Risk is minimized by partial adenoidectomy under direct vision, preserving a buttress of adenoid tissue around the lateral margins at the velopharyngeal junction

## PART C: Clinical Features and Assessment

### Hypernasal Speech (Rhinolalia Aperta)

The velopharyngeal sphincter remains open or partly open during speech, allowing air to escape into the nasal cavity. Clinical features include:

- Hypernasal resonance during speech
- Nasal emission of air during consonant production
- Nasal regurgitation of fluids on swallowing
- Compensatory nasal grimacing as the child attempts to correct nasal escape
- Reduced intelligibility

### Clinical Tests

**Cold spatula test:** Placing a cold steel spatula under the nostrils and observing the misting pattern during speech. Misting when the child speaks a phrase with no nasal sounds (e.g. 'Katie's sister was six yesterday') is suggestive of palatal dysfunction. Some nasal escape during nasal sounds (mm, nn, ng) is normal.

### Multidisciplinary Assessment

VPI assessment is best performed in a multidisciplinary environment. The assessment includes:

- **Oral examination:** Integrity and movement of the soft palate, tonsils, presence of submucous cleft stigmata
- **Nasal examination:** Assessment of nasal patency
- **Standardized speech tests:** Articulation and intelligibility assessment by speech and language therapy
- **Flexible nasoendoscopy:** Fast becoming the investigation of choice — directly visualizes velopharyngeal port closure; may provide closer correlation with VPI severity
- **Speech videofluoroscopy:** Also widely used; provides information about lateral wall movement; alternative if nasoendoscopy is not tolerated. Research has not shown superiority of one over the other.

## PART D: Management

### Conservative Management

- **Speech therapy:** First-line management; focuses on improving articulatory patterns and reducing compensatory behaviours
- **Watchful waiting:** Post-adenoidectomy VPI resolves spontaneously in approximately 50% of cases
- In children with submucous cleft palate, management is indicated only if symptoms of VPI occur

### Surgical Management

Management decisions depend on the underlying symptoms and the size and location of the velopharyngeal gap.

**1. Palatoplasty (Palatal Re-repair):**
- Indicated when there is evidence of anterior insertion of the levator muscles (as a re-repair) or in submucous cleft
- Mainly used where the velopharyngeal gap is small
- Lower morbidity than pharyngoplasty

**2. Pharyngoplasty:**
- Alters the shape of the velopharyngeal port to allow closure on speech
- Two types:
  - **Posterior pharyngeal flap:** Uses a flap from the midline of the posterior pharyngeal wall
  - **Lateral pharyngeal wall flaps (sphincter pharyngoplasty):** Employs medial transposition of flaps from the lateral pharyngeal wall
- Both types have been shown to improve speech outcomes, with normal resonance achieved in up to 85% of cases
- **Main risk:** Increased risk of [[Obstructive Sleep Apnoea|obstructive sleep apnoea]] — must be discussed with patient and family pre-operatively

### Management of Rhinolalia Aperta

- Identify and treat the underlying cause
- If due to adenoidectomy: observe for spontaneous resolution (50% resolve); if persistent, refer for multidisciplinary VPI assessment
- If due to cleft palate or submucous cleft: surgical correction (palatoplasty or pharyngoplasty) based on size and location of the gap
- If due to palatal paralysis: pharyngoplasty or prosthetic obturator
- Speech therapy is integral to management at all stages

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. VPI is the inability of the velopharyngeal sphincter to achieve adequate closure, resulting in hypernasal speech and nasal regurgitation
> 2. The levator veli palatini is the key muscle for velopharyngeal closure — its normal insertion is a transverse sling across the midline of the soft palate
> 3. Post-adenoidectomy VPI occurs in 1 in 1500 to 1 in 3000 procedures — 50% resolve spontaneously
> 4. Submucous cleft stigmata (bifid uvula, zona pellucida, hard palate notch) must be excluded before adenoidectomy
> 5. Partial adenoidectomy under direct vision, preserving tissue at the velopharyngeal junction, reduces VPI risk
> 6. Flexible nasoendoscopy is fast becoming the investigation of choice for VPI assessment
> 7. Pharyngoplasty achieves normal resonance in up to 85% of cases but increases the risk of obstructive sleep apnoea
> 8. Palatoplasty is preferred for small velopharyngeal gaps and has lower morbidity than pharyngoplasty
> 9. The cold spatula test is a useful bedside screening tool — misting during non-nasal speech suggests palatal dysfunction
> 10. About 20% of cleft palate children have persistent speech disorder despite surgical repair


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - Bifid uvula is present in 59% of submucous cleft palate cases and is an important clinical marker to check before adenoidectomy
> - Adenoidectomy in children with undiagnosed submucous cleft is one of the most avoidable causes of iatrogenic VPI — always examine the palate pre-operatively
> - [[Tonsillectomy|Tonsillectomy]] may affect the resonance of the singing voice — parents and children who sing should be informed
> - Adenoid hyponasal speech rarely constitutes an indication for adenoidectomy on its own — adenoids regress naturally and parents should be counselled about this
> - The risk of OSA after pharyngoplasty must be weighed against the speech benefit — pre-operative sleep study is advisable
