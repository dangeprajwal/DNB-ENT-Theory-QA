# Physiology of Swallowing, Videofluoroscopy and FEES

> [!question] Questions Covered
> - Describe the phases of swallowing. Discuss swallowing assessment in a case of dysphagia in a head and neck cancer patient. Describe Videofluoroscopy. What is FEES? *Source:** Scott-Brown's Otorhinolaryngology, 8th Edition - Volume 3, Chapter 48 (Physiology of Swallowing), Chapter 49 (Causes and Assessment of Dysphagia), Chapter 50 (Functional Investigations of Upper GI Tract), Chapter 55 (Rehabilitation of Swallowing Disorders)

## INTRODUCTION

Swallowing (deglutition) is a complex neuromuscular process requiring coordinated activity of muscles in the oral cavity, pharynx, larynx, and oesophagus. Normal swallowing is part reflex and part voluntary, controlled by cortical centres, the brainstem central pattern generator, and peripheral cranial nerves. Videofluoroscopic swallowing study (VFSS) and fibreoptic endoscopic evaluation of swallowing (FEES) are the two main instrumental assessments. Understanding swallowing physiology and its assessment is essential for managing dysphagia in head and neck cancer patients.

## PART A: PHASES OF SWALLOWING

### Four Phases

| Phase | Duration | Voluntary/Reflex | Key Events |
|-------|----------|-----------------|------------|
| Oral preparatory | Variable | Voluntary | Mastication, bolus formation, saliva mixing |
| Oral transit | 1-2 seconds | Voluntary | Tongue propels bolus posteriorly to oropharynx |
| Pharyngeal | ~1 second | Reflex | Airway protection + bolus transport through pharynx |
| Oesophageal | 10-15 seconds | Reflex (peristalsis) | Bolus transported to stomach |

### Pharyngeal Phase — Critical Events (Sequential)

1. **Nasopharyngeal closure** — soft palate elevates against posterior pharyngeal wall (Passavant's ridge)
2. **Laryngeal elevation** — hyoid and larynx move superiorly and anteriorly
3. **Epiglottic deflection** — epiglottis inverts to cover laryngeal inlet
4. **Vocal fold adduction** — true cords, false cords, and aryepiglottic folds close (three tiers of closure)
5. **Pharyngeal peristalsis** — sequential contraction of superior, middle, and inferior constrictors (stripping wave)
6. **Cricopharyngeal relaxation** — UES opens due to muscle relaxation + mechanical pull from laryngeal elevation
7. **Bolus transit** through relaxed cricopharyngeus into oesophagus

### Neural Control

- **Cortical control:** Bilateral but asymmetric; primary motor cortex (precentral gyrus), supplementary motor area, anterior cingulate cortex, insula
- **Brainstem:** Central pattern generator (CPG) located in dorsal and ventral medullary groups — coordinates reflex pharyngeal swallow
- **Sensory afferents:** Cranial nerves V, VII, IX, X, XII — provide feedback for bolus detection and swallow initiation
- **Motor efferents:** CN V3 (mastication), VII (lips), IX and X (pharynx, larynx), XII (tongue)
- **Respiration-swallowing coordination:** Swallowing interrupts expiratory phase; a brief apnoeic period (~0.5-1.5 seconds) protects the airway

## PART B: SWALLOWING ASSESSMENT IN HNC

### Clinical Assessment

- **History:** Type of dysphagia (solids vs liquids vs both), onset (acute vs gradual), aspiration symptoms (coughing, wet voice, recurrent chest infections), weight loss
- **Examination:** Oral cavity inspection, cranial nerve assessment (V, VII, IX, X, XII), flexible nasoendoscopy to assess laryngeal function, vocal cord mobility, pharyngeal pooling
- **Bedside swallow evaluation:** Observation of swallowing with different consistencies; screening tool — NOT definitive

### Instrumental Assessment

| Investigation | Best For |
|--------------|---------|
| VFSS (Videofluoroscopy) | Gold standard for oral/pharyngeal phase dynamics; quantifies aspiration |
| FEES | Bedside assessment; direct visualization of pharyngeal residue; biofeedback |
| Manometry | Oesophageal motility and sphincter function |
| Transnasal oesophagoscopy | Direct oesophageal visualization without sedation |

## PART C: VIDEOFLUOROSCOPIC SWALLOWING STUDY (VFSS)

Also called modified barium swallow (MBS) — the gold standard for swallowing assessment.

### Technique

- Patient seated upright in lateral and anteroposterior views
- Swallows various consistencies mixed with barium (thin liquid, thick liquid, puree, solid)
- Real-time fluoroscopic recording at 25-30 frames/second
- Radiation exposure is low but present
- Performed jointly by speech-language therapist and radiologist

### What VFSS Demonstrates

- Oral preparatory and oral transit phases
- Timing and completeness of pharyngeal swallow
- Laryngeal elevation and epiglottic inversion
- Cricopharyngeal opening
- Aspiration (before, during, or after swallow) — classified by Penetration-Aspiration Scale
- Pharyngeal residue (vallecular and pyriform)
- Compensatory manoeuvres can be trialled during the study

### Limitations

- Requires radiology suite (not portable)
- Radiation exposure (limits duration)
- Barium is not representative of food consistency
- Cannot assess mucosal detail or sensation
- Operator-dependent interpretation

## PART D: FIBREOPTIC ENDOSCOPIC EVALUATION OF SWALLOWING (FEES)

### Definition

FEES is a bedside assessment of swallowing using a flexible nasendoscope passed transnasally to the level of the oropharynx/hypopharynx to directly observe the pharyngeal phase of swallowing with dyed food and liquid boluses.

### Indications

- Dysphagia assessment in any patient (neurological, post-surgical, HNC)
- Bedside/ICU assessment (patient too unwell for radiology suite)
- Post-operative HNC swallowing assessment
- Tracheostomized patients
- Monitoring swallowing rehabilitation over time
- Biofeedback during swallowing therapy
- Assessment of laryngeal sensation (FEESST)

### Technique

1. **Equipment:** Flexible nasendoscope, video recording system, dyed boluses (green/blue food dye)
2. **Scope passage:** Transnasally — four endoscopic views assessed sequentially:
   - Nasal passage (anatomy, secretions)
   - Nasopharynx (velopharyngeal closure)
   - Oropharynx (base of tongue, vallecular anatomy)
   - Hypopharynx (pyriform sinuses, laryngeal inlet, vocal cords)
3. **Assessment of secretions:** Before bolus — pooling indicates baseline swallowing impairment
4. **Bolus trials:** Sequential presentation of dyed liquids, puree, solids
5. **Observation:** Bolus flow, premature spillage, pharyngeal residue, penetration/aspiration, response to aspiration (cough/silent)
6. **Therapeutic trials:** Compensatory manoeuvres tested in real-time

### Interpretation

- **Premature spillage:** Bolus enters pharynx before swallow trigger
- **Penetration:** Material enters laryngeal vestibule but does not pass below vocal cords
- **Aspiration:** Material passes below vocal cords (silent if no cough)
- **Residue:** Material remaining in valleculae or pyriform sinuses after swallow
- **"Whiteout":** During the pharyngeal swallow, the view is obscured by pharyngeal constriction — this is normal and expected

### FEESST (FEES with Sensory Testing)

- Calibrated air pulse delivered to aryepiglottic fold via endoscope port
- Quantitative measurement of laryngeal adductor reflex threshold
- Normal threshold: <4 mmHg; moderately impaired: 4-6 mmHg; severely impaired: >6 mmHg
- Reduced sensation correlates with aspiration risk

### FEES vs VFSS — Comparison

| Feature | VFSS | FEES |
|---------|------|------|
| Portability | Requires radiology suite | Bedside/ICU |
| Radiation | Present | None |
| Oral phase | Visualized | NOT seen |
| Pharyngeal phase | Visualized (including "during swallow") | Whiteout during swallow |
| Aspiration | Quantified (timing relative to swallow) | Detected but timing limited |
| Mucosal detail | Not assessed | Directly visualized |
| Sensation | Not assessed | FEESST available |
| Biofeedback | Not possible | Patient can view screen |
| Repeatability | Limited by radiation | Unlimited |
| Secretion assessment | Limited | Excellent |
| Hyoid/laryngeal elevation | Quantified | Cannot quantify |
| Cost | Higher | Lower |

## PART E: SWALLOWING MANOEUVRES

### Mendelsohn Manoeuvre

- **Purpose:** Prolongs laryngeal elevation and UES opening during swallow
- **Technique:** Patient consciously holds the larynx in its elevated position at the peak of the swallow for 2-3 seconds before releasing
- **Mechanism:** Sustained hyoid and laryngeal elevation → sustained cricopharyngeal opening → improved bolus clearance
- **Indication:** Reduced laryngeal elevation, poor cricopharyngeal opening, pharyngeal residue
- **Evidence:** Increases duration and extent of UES opening; improves pharyngeal pressure generation

### Other Key Swallowing Manoeuvres

| Manoeuvre | Technique | Indication |
|-----------|-----------|------------|
| Supraglottic swallow | Deep breath → hold → swallow → cough | Delayed pharyngeal swallow; aspiration during swallow |
| Super-supraglottic swallow | Deep breath → bear down → swallow → cough | As above with additional arytenoid tilting |
| Effortful swallow | Swallow with maximum tongue and throat effort | Weak pharyngeal contraction; vallecular residue |
| Chin tuck | Tuck chin to chest during swallow | Premature spillage; delayed swallow trigger |
| Head turn (to affected side) | Turn head to paralyzed side | Unilateral pharyngeal weakness |

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. Four phases of swallowing: oral preparatory, oral transit (1-2 sec), pharyngeal (~1 sec), oesophageal (10-15 sec)
> 2. Pharyngeal phase is reflex — triggered when bolus contacts posterior tongue/faucial pillars; involves nasopharyngeal closure, laryngeal elevation, vocal fold adduction, pharyngeal peristalsis, cricopharyngeal relaxation
> 3. Neural control: cortical (bilateral, asymmetric) + brainstem CPG (dorsal/ventral medullary groups) + cranial nerves V, VII, IX, X, XII
> 4. VFSS is the gold standard for swallowing assessment — provides dynamic imaging of all phases with barium
> 5. FEES is portable, radiation-free, allows bedside assessment and biofeedback — ideal for HNC and ICU patients
> 6. FEES limitation: "whiteout" during pharyngeal swallow — cannot visualize events during the swallow itself
> 7. FEESST adds quantitative laryngeal sensation testing — threshold >6 mmHg indicates severe sensory impairment
> 8. Penetration-Aspiration Scale is the standard tool for quantifying aspiration severity on VFSS/FEES
> 9. Mendelsohn manoeuvre prolongs laryngeal elevation and UES opening — most useful for pharyngeal residue and poor cricopharyngeal opening
> 10. Swallowing assessment in HNC patients should be multidisciplinary: otolaryngologist, SLT, radiologist, dietitian


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - "Whiteout" during FEES is normal — it occurs when the pharyngeal walls contract around the endoscope during the swallow; the absence of whiteout suggests poor pharyngeal contraction
> - FEES is superior to VFSS for assessing secretion management — presence of pooled secretions before any bolus trial indicates significant swallowing impairment
> - In HNC patients, FEES can be performed repeatedly during treatment to monitor progressive dysphagia from [[Radiotherapy Principles Fractionation Advances|radiotherapy]]/chemoradiotherapy without radiation burden
> - A "wet" or "gurgly" voice quality after swallowing is a clinical sign of laryngeal penetration even before instrumental assessment
> - The EAT-10 (Eating Assessment Tool) is the most widely accepted subjective dysphagia screening questionnaire — a score >3 is abnormal
