# Papillary Thyroid Cancer — Evaluation and Management

> [!question] Questions Covered
> - A 40-year-old lady presented with solitary thyroid nodule 4 cm size and multiple ipsilateral neck nodes, reported as metastatic papillary thyroid cancer on FNAC. Discuss the evaluation and further management.
*Source:** Scott-Brown's Otorhinolaryngology, 8th Edition - Volume 1, Chapter 57 (Imaging), Chapter 58 (Pathology), Chapter 62 (DTC Management), Chapter 67 (Thyroidectomy), Chapter 68 (Locally Advanced/Nodal Disease)

---

## INTRODUCTION

Papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) is the most common thyroid malignancy (80-85%). It has an excellent prognosis overall, with 10-year survival exceeding 90%. Lymph node metastases are common (30-80%) but do not significantly worsen prognosis in patients under 55 years. This case (T2N1bM0 — Stage I in a patient <55 years) requires total thyroidectomy with therapeutic lateral [[Neck Dissection|neck dissection]] and consideration for radioactive iodine ablation.

---

## PART A: PRE-OPERATIVE EVALUATION

### Confirm Diagnosis and Stage

| Investigation | Purpose |
|--------------|---------|
| FNAC of thyroid nodule | Confirms PTC (Bethesda VI); nuclear grooves, pseudoinclusions, psammoma bodies |
| FNAC of neck nodes | Confirms metastatic PTC; Tg washout from FNAC needle increases sensitivity |
| Neck ultrasound | Map all nodal levels (I-VI); assess contralateral lobe; measure tumour dimensions |
| Flexible nasendoscopy | Assess vocal cord mobility (RLN involvement) |
| CT neck + chest (non-contrast or with contrast) | Nodal extent, retrosternal extension, pulmonary metastases |
| Blood tests | TSH, FT4, thyroglobulin, anti-Tg antibodies, calcium, vitamin D |

### Ultrasound Features of PTC (4 Cardinal Signs)

1. Solid hypoechoic nodule
2. Ill-defined/irregular margins
3. Microcalcifications
4. Taller-than-wide shape

### Ultrasound Assessment of Neck Nodes

| Feature | Suspicious for Metastasis |
|---------|--------------------------|
| Shape | Round (loss of oval/kidney-bean shape) |
| Hilum | Absent fatty hilum |
| Echogenicity | Hyperechoic (like thyroid tissue) |
| Calcification | Microcalcification or coarse calcification |
| Cystic change | Cystic degeneration within node |
| Vascularity | Peripheral or chaotic vascularity |

### TNM Staging (AJCC 8th Edition)

| T Stage | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| T1a | ≤1 cm, limited to thyroid |
| T1b | >1 cm to ≤2 cm |
| T2 | >2 cm to ≤4 cm |
| T3a | >4 cm limited to thyroid |
| T3b | Gross extrathyroidal extension into strap muscles |
| T4a | Extension into subcutaneous tissues, larynx, trachea, oesophagus, RLN |
| T4b | Prevertebral fascia, carotid encasement, mediastinal vessels |

**Age-based staging (critical for PTC):**
- **<55 years:** Stage I (any T, any N, M0) or Stage II (any T, any N, M1)
- **≥55 years:** Stages I-IVB based on T, N, M

**This case:** T2 (4 cm) N1b (ipsilateral lateral nodes) M0 → **Stage I** (patient is 40 years old)

---

## PART B: SURGICAL MANAGEMENT

### Extent of Thyroidectomy

**Total thyroidectomy is indicated** for this patient because:
- Tumour is 4 cm (at the upper limit for lobectomy consideration)
- Confirmed nodal metastases (N1b) — indicates higher risk
- Total thyroidectomy enables RAI ablation and thyroglobulin monitoring
- Allows treatment of possible contralateral microscopic disease (multifocal in 20-80%)

### Neck Dissection

**Therapeutic lateral neck dissection (levels II-V):**
- Mandatory for clinically evident lateral neck disease (N1b)
- Selective neck dissection (SND) encompassing levels IIA, III, IV, Vb (minimum)
- Include levels IIB, VA if involved
- Functional/modified radical type — preserve SCM, IJV, SAN unless directly invaded

**Central neck dissection (level VI):**
- Ipsilateral level VI dissection recommended with total thyroidectomy
- Prophylactic contralateral level VI: controversial; ATA recommends consideration for T3/T4 tumours

### Surgical Technique Highlights

| Step | Key Point |
|------|-----------|
| RLN identification | Visual identification is gold standard; IONM is adjunct |
| EBSLN preservation | Ligate superior pole vessels individually on gland capsule (Joll's triangle) |
| Parathyroid preservation | Capsular dissection; individual ITA branch ligation (NOT trunk) |
| Berry's ligament | Area of maximum RLN risk — careful dissection |
| Pyramidal lobe | Must be excised completely |
| Parathyroid autotransplant | If devascularized → mince and implant into sternomastoid |

## PART C: POST-OPERATIVE MANAGEMENT

### Radioactive Iodine (RAI) Ablation

**Indications in this case:**
- N1b disease = intermediate risk → RAI recommended
- Destroys residual thyroid tissue and micrometastases
- Enables thyroglobulin monitoring (requires no residual thyroid tissue)

**Preparation:**
- TSH stimulation required: thyroid hormone withdrawal (4 weeks off T4, or 2 weeks off T3) OR recombinant human TSH (rhTSH)
- TSH target: >30 mIU/L
- Low-iodine diet for 1-2 weeks prior

**Typical dose:** 30-100 mCi (1.1-3.7 GBq) depending on risk stratification

### TSH Suppression Therapy

| Risk Category | TSH Target |
|--------------|------------|
| High risk | <0.1 mIU/L |
| Intermediate risk | 0.1-0.5 mIU/L |
| Low risk | 0.5-2.0 mIU/L |

This patient (intermediate risk — N1b) → TSH target 0.1-0.5 mIU/L

### Dynamic Risk Stratification (Follow-up)

| Response Category | Definition | Action |
|-------------------|-----------|--------|
| Excellent | Negative imaging; stimulated Tg <1 ng/mL | De-escalate TSH suppression |
| Indeterminate | Nonspecific imaging; Tg 1-10 ng/mL | Continue observation |
| Incomplete biochemical | Negative imaging; Tg >10 or rising | Consider additional RAI or observation |
| Incomplete structural | Structural evidence of disease | Surgery, RAI, or external beam RT |

### Follow-up Protocol

| Time | Investigation |
|------|--------------|
| 6-12 weeks post-op | Thyroglobulin + anti-Tg antibodies |
| 6-12 months | Neck ultrasound + stimulated thyroglobulin |
| Annually | Thyroglobulin, TSH, neck USS |
| As needed | Diagnostic whole-body scan, CT, [[PET CT Nuclear Medicine Scans|PET-CT]] |

---

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. PTC is the most common thyroid malignancy (80-85%); multifocal in 20-80%; bilateral in up to 50%
> 2. FNAC diagnostic features: nuclear grooves, intranuclear pseudoinclusions, psammoma bodies
> 3. Age-based staging: patients <55 years are Stage I (any T, any N, M0) or Stage II (M1 only) — excellent prognosis
> 4. Total thyroidectomy indicated when: tumour >4 cm, nodal metastases, planned RAI, multifocal/bilateral disease
> 5. Therapeutic lateral neck dissection (levels II-V) mandatory for clinically evident N1b disease
> 6. Prophylactic lateral neck dissection is NOT recommended in PTC
> 7. RAI ablation recommended for intermediate-risk (N1b) and high-risk disease after total thyroidectomy
> 8. Dynamic risk stratification: excellent response = negative imaging + stimulated Tg <1 ng/mL → de-escalate treatment
> 9. TSH suppression target for intermediate risk: 0.1-0.5 mIU/L
> 10. Thyroglobulin is the tumour marker for differentiated thyroid cancer — requires total thyroidectomy + RAI ablation to be meaningful


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - Tg washout from FNAC needle of suspicious neck nodes increases diagnostic sensitivity — request this when sampling lateral neck nodes in suspected PTC
> - Microcalcification within a lymph node on ultrasound is virtually pathognomonic for metastatic PTC
> - In patients <55 years, even N1b disease is Stage I — avoid over-treatment; prognosis remains excellent
> - Cystic change in a lateral neck node in a young patient should raise suspicion for metastatic PTC — always check thyroid
> - Completion thyroidectomy should ideally be performed within 6 months if initial surgery was lobectomy and final pathology indicates need for total thyroidectomy
