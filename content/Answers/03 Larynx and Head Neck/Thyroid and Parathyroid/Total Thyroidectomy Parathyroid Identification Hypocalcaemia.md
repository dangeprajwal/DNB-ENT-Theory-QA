# Total Thyroidectomy — Indications, Parathyroid Identification and Post-operative Hypocalcaemia

> [!question] Questions
> Describe indications of total thyroidectomy according to ATA guidelines. What are various methods of identification of parathyroids during thyroidectomy? Describe management of post-thyroidectomy hypocalcaemia.

**Source:** Scott-Brown's Otorhinolaryngology, 8th Edition - Volume 1, Chapter 62 (DTC Management), Chapter 67 (Thyroidectomy), Chapter 74 (Persistent/Recurrent HPT), Chapter 77 (MIP), Chapter 79 (Complications)

---

## INTRODUCTION

Total thyroidectomy is the most commonly performed definitive surgery for thyroid malignancy and selected benign conditions. The two most dreaded complications are recurrent laryngeal nerve injury and hypoparathyroidism. Identification and preservation of the parathyroid glands with intact blood supply is critical. Post-thyroidectomy hypocalcaemia (temporary 8-30%, permanent 1.7-3.5%) remains the most common metabolic complication and requires prompt recognition and management.

---

## PART A: INDICATIONS FOR TOTAL THYROIDECTOMY

### ATA Guidelines — Malignant Indications

| Indication | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| Tumour >4 cm | All DTC >4 cm regardless of other factors |
| Multifocal disease | Bilateral or multiple foci within one lobe |
| Bilateral disease | Tumour in both lobes |
| Extrathyroidal extension | Gross extension beyond thyroid capsule |
| Nodal metastases (N1) | Central or lateral neck nodes positive |
| Distant metastases (M1) | Pulmonary, skeletal, or other distant sites |
| Planned RAI ablation | Total thyroidectomy required for effective RAI |
| Childhood radiation exposure | Higher risk of multifocal and aggressive disease |
| Family history of DTC | First-degree relative with DTC |

### Benign Indications

| Indication | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| Bilateral multinodular goitre | Symptomatic or cosmetic |
| Graves' disease | Failed medical therapy; patient preference |
| Compressive symptoms | Dysphagia, dyspnoea, stridor |
| Retrosternal extension | Significant substernal component |
| Indeterminate bilateral cytology | Bilateral Thy3a/Thy3f nodules |

### When Lobectomy is Acceptable (ATA)

- Unifocal tumour 1-4 cm
- Intrathyroidal (no extrathyroidal extension)
- Clinically node-negative (cN0)
- No high-risk features (radiation history, family history)
- Papillary thyroid microcarcinoma (<1 cm) — active surveillance is also an option

## PART B: METHODS OF PARATHYROID IDENTIFICATION

### Visual Identification

| Feature | Parathyroid | Fat | Lymph Node | Thyroid |
|---------|------------|-----|------------|--------|
| Colour | Tan/caramel | Yellow | Grey-pink | Red-brown |
| Consistency | Soft | Soft | Firm | Firm |
| Bleeding | Bleeds/bruises easily when touched | Does not | Minimal | Moderate |
| Capsule mobility | Moves within own capsule on gentle pressure | — | Fixed | — |

### Anatomical Landmarks

| Landmark | Guides to |
|----------|----------|
| Inferior thyroid artery | Both glands within 2.5 cm of ITA-RLN intersection |
| Cricothyroid joint | Superior gland within 1 cm posteriorly |
| Tubercle of Zuckerkandl | Superior gland attached to cranial aspect |
| RLN | Superior gland posterior to nerve; inferior gland anterior |
| Thyrothymic tract | Inferior gland near or within this tract |

### Superior Parathyroid (PIV — from 4th pouch)

- More constant in position (shorter migration path)
- Located on posterior surface of thyroid, within 1 cm of cricothyroid joint
- **Posterior to the RLN**
- Often attached to cranial aspect of Zuckerkandl tubercle

### Inferior Parathyroid (PIII — from 3rd pouch)

- More variable in position (longer migration path with thymus)
- Near inferior thyroid pole or within thyrothymic tract
- **Anterior to the RLN**
- Related to inferior thyroid artery

### Capsular Dissection Technique

- The key preservation technique during thyroidectomy
- Glands mobilized and reflected away from thyroid while maintaining their blood supply
- Ligate inferior thyroid artery branches **individually close to the gland** (NOT trunk ligation)
- End-artery supply — any disruption causes devascularization

### Intraoperative Assessment of Viability

- Viable gland: maintains colour, bleeds when biopsied
- Devascularized gland: turns dark/dusky
- If devascularized → **autotransplant** immediately

### Autotransplantation Protocol

1. Confirm tissue is parathyroid (frozen section)
2. Divide gland into approximately 12 pieces (1 mm each)
3. Implant into a muscle pocket (usually sternomastoid)
4. Mark site with a non-absorbable suture or clip
5. Function returns in 4-6 weeks

### Search Strategy for Missing Glands

| Missing Gland | Search Locations |
|--------------|-----------------|
| Superior | Retropharyngeal/retro-oesophageal (most common ectopic), behind hyoid, carotid sheath, intrathyroidal |
| Inferior | Thyrothymic ligament, cervical thymus, anterior mediastinum, carotid sheath, intrathyroidal |

## PART C: POST-THYROIDECTOMY HYPOCALCAEMIA

### Pathophysiology

| Mechanism | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| Devascularization | Most common cause; disrupted end-artery blood supply |
| Inadvertent excision | Parathyroid removed with thyroid specimen |
| Stunning | Temporary ischaemia with subsequent recovery |
| Hungry bone syndrome | Rapid calcium influx into bone (especially in thyrotoxicosis/HPT) |

### Risk Factors

- Graves' disease
- Thyroid cancer with central [[Neck Dissection|neck dissection]]
- Substernal goitre
- Reoperation/revision thyroidectomy
- Surgeon inexperience

### Incidence

| Type | Incidence |
|------|----------|
| Temporary hypoparathyroidism | 8-30% (resolves within 6 months) |
| Permanent hypoparathyroidism | 1.7-3.5% (persists beyond 6 months) |

### Clinical Features

- **Perioral and digital paraesthesia** — earliest symptom
- Muscle cramps and spasms
- **Chvostek sign:** Facial muscle twitching on tapping facial nerve anterior to ear
- **Trousseau sign:** Carpopedal spasm with BP cuff inflation above systolic for 3 minutes
- Tetany, laryngospasm
- Seizures (severe)
- QT prolongation on ECG

### Management Algorithm

| Corrected Ca (mmol/L) | Symptomatic | Asymptomatic |
|----------------------|-------------|-------------|
| >2.15 | Milk | Nil |
| 2.00-2.15 | Calcichew 1g BD | Nil |
| 1.90-2.00 | Calcichew 1g TDS + alfacalcidol 1 mcg OD | Calcichew 1g TDS |
| 1.80-1.90 | Calcichew 1g TDS + alfacalcidol 1 mcg BD | Calcichew 1g TDS + alfacalcidol 1 mcg BD |
| <1.80 | **IV calcium gluconate** 10-30 mL over 10-15 min + oral Ca + alfacalcidol 1 mcg TDS | Calcichew 1g TDS + alfacalcidol 1 mcg TDS |

### Acute Management (Ca <1.75 mmol/L or symptomatic tetany)

- IV calcium gluconate 10% — 10-30 mL over 10-15 minutes
- Continuous ECG monitoring
- Avoid extravasation (tissue necrosis risk)
- Transition to oral calcium + active vitamin D as soon as possible

### Maintenance Therapy

| Agent | Dose | Notes |
|-------|------|-------|
| Calcium carbonate | 500-1000 mg TDS, titrate | First-line oral supplement |
| Calcium citrate | Equivalent doses | For patients with reflux/gastric bypass |
| Alfacalcidol (1-alpha) | 0.25-1 mcg/day | Active vitamin D analogue |
| Calcitriol | 0.25-0.5 mcg/day | Alternative active vitamin D |

### Prevention Strategies

- Pre-operative vitamin D assessment and replacement if deficient
- Capsular dissection technique — preserve parathyroid blood supply
- Individual ITA branch ligation (NOT trunk ligation)
- Identify and preserve ALL four glands
- Autotransplant devascularized glands immediately
- Some centres give prophylactic calcium to ALL post-TT patients

### Permanent Hypoparathyroidism Management

- Lifelong oral calcium + active vitamin D
- Monitor: ionized calcium, phosphorus, PTH, 25-OH vitamin D, urinary calcium
- Urinary calcium levels increase over time → risk of nephrocalcinosis
- **Recombinant PTH (rhPTH 1-84):** Subcutaneous injection; maintains calcium, decreases phosphorus, reduces/eliminates vitamin D supplementation, improves bone turnover

---

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. ATA indications for total thyroidectomy: tumour >4 cm, multifocal/bilateral disease, extrathyroidal extension, nodal metastases, planned RAI
> 2. Lobectomy acceptable for: unifocal 1-4 cm, intrathyroidal, cN0, no high-risk features
> 3. Superior parathyroid (4th pouch): constant position, posterior to RLN, within 1 cm of cricothyroid joint
> 4. Inferior parathyroid (3rd pouch): variable position (migrates with thymus), anterior to RLN, near thyrothymic tract
> 5. Capsular dissection + individual ITA branch ligation (NOT trunk) is the key technique for parathyroid preservation
> 6. Devascularized parathyroid → autotransplant into sternomastoid: confirm on frozen section, mince into ~12 pieces
> 7. Post-thyroidectomy hypocalcaemia: temporary 8-30%, permanent 1.7-3.5%
> 8. Perioral paraesthesia is the earliest symptom of hypocalcaemia — do NOT wait for tetany
> 9. Acute symptomatic hypocalcaemia: IV calcium gluconate 10-30 mL over 10-15 minutes with ECG monitoring
> 10. Recombinant PTH (rhPTH 1-84) is a newer option for permanent hypoparathyroidism — reduces need for calcium/vitamin D supplementation


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - Berry's ligament is where the RLN is at maximum risk — it passes in close proximity or through this dense fibrous attachment of thyroid to trachea/cricoid
> - Tubercle of Zuckerkandl: RLN lies medial to it; superior parathyroid attached to its cranial aspect — the single most useful surgical landmark for both structures
> - Approximately 1% of parathyroid glands are intrathyroidal — if a gland cannot be found, consider intraoperative ultrasound of the thyroid specimen
> - EBSLN lies in Joll's triangle — up to 20% are subfascial and unidentifiable; always ligate superior pole vessels individually on the gland capsule
> - Hungry bone syndrome risk is highest in patients with pre-existing Graves' disease or hyperparathyroidism — anticipate aggressive calcium replacement
