# Spasmodic Dysphonia and Botulinum Toxin in ENT

> [!question] Questions
> Describe various types of spasmodic dysphonia. Discuss the clinical features and management. Mention the uses of botulinum toxin in ENT.

**Source:** Scott-Brown's Otorhinolaryngology, 8th Edition - Volume 3, Chapter 68 (Movement Disorders of the Larynx), Chapter 67 (Phonosurgery)

---

## INTRODUCTION

Spasmodic dysphonia (SD) is a voice disorder arising from a focal dystonia involving specific laryngeal muscles, reflecting central motor processing abnormalities. It is task-specific — spasm occurs only during phonation, while the larynx appears normal at rest. Originally considered a psychoneurosis, it is now established as a neurological condition. The incidence is approximately 1 per 100,000, with female predominance and average age of onset of 40 years. There is typically a long interval from onset to diagnosis (~2-3 years).

## PART A: TYPES AND CLINICAL FEATURES

### Types of Spasmodic Dysphonia

| Type | Frequency | Mechanism | Voice Quality |
|------|-----------|-----------|---------------|
| Adductor SD | ~90% | Involuntary adductor spasm of TA + LCA muscles | Strangled, staccato, voice breaks |
| Abductor SD | <10% | Involuntary abductor spasm of PCA muscle | Breathy, effortful voice onset |
| Mixed SD | Rare | Combined adductor + abductor spasms | Features of both types |

### Adductor Spasmodic Dysphonia

- Characteristic strangled, staccato voice with phonation breaks
- Breaks associated with vowels — particularly words ending with vowel followed by word starting with vowel (e.g., "we eat")
- Words with two vowels in tandem are problematic (e.g., "eighty-year eels")
- Vowels /i/ and /a/ particularly affected
- **Detection:** Counting upward from 80 — voice breaks become apparent
- Airway is NEVER compromised (adduction occurs only during phonation, never respiration)

### Abductor Spasmodic Dysphonia

- Difficulty with voice onset after voiceless consonants at beginnings of words (/w/, /b/, /s/, /t/, /p/, /f/)
- Voiceless voice breaks — breathy interruptions
- **Test phrase:** "Who has hidden Harry's hat?"
- **Detection:** Counting from 50

### Key Clinical Features

- Task-specific dystonia — overridden by laughing, chanting, singing
- Alcohol may ameliorate symptoms
- ~8% have family history of dystonia
- THAP1 gene (DYT6) mutations identified in some cases (only ~1%)
- Most cases are sporadic/idiopathic

### Diagnosis

- Made on clinical grounds — listening skills of experienced clinician
- **Nasendoscopy:** Gold standard for visualizing laryngeal spasms during connected speech
- **EMG:** Demonstrates bursts of involuntary spasms overlaid on normal interference pattern
- Multidisciplinary team: SLT, otolaryngologist, neurologist, neurophysiologist

## PART B: MANAGEMENT OF SPASMODIC DYSPHONIA

### 1. Botulinum Toxin Injection (Mainstay)

Type A most commonly used: OnabotulinumtoxinA (Botox), AbobotulinumtoxinA (Dysport), IncobotulinumtoxinA (Xeomin).

**Dose equivalence:** 1 unit Botox = 3 units Dysport = 0.75 units Xeomin

**For Adductor SD:**
- Injection into thyroarytenoid muscle via transcutaneous approach through cricothyroid membrane
- Needle advanced in midline, then angled 15 degrees cranially and 30 degrees laterally
- EMG monitoring commonly used to confirm needle position
- Low doses: 3-10 units Dysport (1-3 units Botox) into one or both TA muscles
- Dose titrated according to response
- Frequency: every 6 weeks to 6 months (variable)
- Initial 7 days of weak/breathy voice with possible liquid aspiration
- Peak benefit at 2-3 weeks
- **Success rate: 94%**

**For Abductor SD:**
- Injection into PCA muscle — more technically challenging
- Approached transcutaneously by rotating larynx or under GA
- EMG guidance essential
- Larger doses required
- Less effective than for adductor SD

### 2. Surgical Options

| Procedure | Mechanism | Details |
|-----------|-----------|---------|
| SLAD-R | Selective denervation + reinnervation | Denervate TA + LCA; ansa cervicalis anastomosis; 82% satisfaction |
| CO2 laser thyroarytenoid myoneurectomy | Muscle + nerve ablation | Endoscopic approach |
| Type 2 [[Vocal Fold Paralysis|thyroplasty]] (Isshiki) | Lateralization of vocal fold | Titanium shim at anterior commissure; done under LA |
| RLN section/crush (Dedo 1976) | Nerve disruption | Initial results promising; long-term recurrence |

### 3. Speech Therapy

Little role alone but helps manage compensatory tension patterns and optimize post-treatment voice.

## PART C: USES OF BOTULINUM TOXIN IN ENT

| Application | Target Muscle/Site | Details |
|------------|-------------------|---------|
| Adductor spasmodic dysphonia | Thyroarytenoid | Mainstay treatment; 94% success |
| Abductor spasmodic dysphonia | PCA muscle | Less effective; EMG-guided |
| Essential voice tremor | TA ± LCA | Reduces amplitude of tremor |
| Hyperfunctional dysphonia | TA | Refractory cases only |
| Sialorrhoea/drooling | Parotid and/or submandibular glands | Ultrasound-guided; lasts 3-6 months |
| Frey's syndrome | Subcutaneous parotid region | Intradermal injection; prevents gustatory sweating |
| Rhinitis (vasomotor) | Inferior turbinate | Reduces glandular secretion |
| Bruxism/TMJ dysfunction | Masseter ± temporalis | Reduces muscle hyperactivity |
| Cosmetic (facial lines) | Frontalis, corrugator, orbicularis oculi | Glabellar lines, crow's feet, forehead |
| Blepharospasm | Orbicularis oculi | Focal dystonia treatment |
| Hemifacial spasm | Facial muscles | Symptomatic relief |
| Oromandibular dystonia | Masticatory muscles | Jaw-opening or jaw-closing type |
| Cricopharyngeal achalasia | Cricopharyngeus | Treats dysphagia from CP spasm |

### Mechanism of Action

- Botulinum toxin type A cleaves SNAP-25 protein at the neuromuscular junction
- Prevents acetylcholine vesicle fusion with presynaptic membrane
- Results in temporary chemodenervation (chemical denervation)
- Effect is reversible — nerve terminal sprouts new synaptic connections
- Onset: 24-72 hours; Peak effect: 2-3 weeks; Duration: 3-6 months

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. Spasmodic dysphonia is a focal dystonia, NOT a psychoneurosis — neurological origin confirmed
> 2. Adductor SD (~90%): strangled voice, breaks on vowels; detected counting from 80
> 3. Abductor SD (<10%): breathy voice, breaks on voiceless consonants; test phrase "Who has hidden Harry's hat?"
> 4. Task-specific — overridden by laughing, singing, chanting; airway is NEVER compromised
> 5. Botulinum toxin injection is the mainstay treatment — 94% success rate for adductor SD
> 6. Dose equivalence: 1 unit Botox = 3 units Dysport = 0.75 units Xeomin
> 7. For adductor SD: transcutaneous injection through cricothyroid membrane into TA muscle; EMG monitoring used
> 8. SLAD-R (selective laryngeal adductor denervation-reinnervation) achieves 82% patient satisfaction for adductor SD
> 9. Type 2 thyroplasty (Isshiki): titanium shim separates anterior vocal folds — done under local anaesthesia
> 10. Botulinum toxin has wide ENT applications: sialorrhoea, Frey's syndrome, rhinitis, cricopharyngeal achalasia, blepharospasm


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - Mixed SD may only become apparent during treatment of what appears to be pure adductor or abductor SD
> - Abductor SD requires EMG-guided PCA injection — technically more difficult and less effective than adductor SD treatment
> - Initial 7 days of breathy/weak voice after botox injection is expected and should be counselled
> - RLN section (Dedo procedure) showed promising short-term but poor long-term results — largely abandoned in favour of SLAD-R
> - THAP1 gene (DYT6) mutation found in only ~1% of screened SD patients — routine genetic testing not recommended
