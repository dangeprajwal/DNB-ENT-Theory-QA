# Sulcus Vocalis and Reinke's Oedema

**Question:** *Discuss the aetiology, clinical features and management of sulcus vocalis. Describe Reinke's oedema.*

**Source:** Scott-Brown's Otorhinolaryngology, 8th Edition - Volume 3, Chapter 63 (Structural Disorders of the Vocal Cords), Chapter 67 (Phonosurgery)

---

## INTRODUCTION

Sulcus vocalis and Reinke's oedema are structural disorders of the vocal fold involving the superficial lamina propria (Reinke's space). While sulcus vocalis represents an invagination and loss of Reinke's space, Reinke's oedema represents chronic irreversible swelling within it. Both significantly impair the mucosal wave essential for normal phonation. Understanding of the layered structure of the vocal fold (Hirano's body-cover theory) is fundamental to managing these conditions.

---

## PART A: SULCUS VOCALIS

### Definition

A sulcus vocalis is a localized invagination of the vocal fold mucosa of varying depth. They may be described as "open cysts" and represent loss of the superficial lamina propria.

### Classification

| Type | Name | Depth | Reinke's Space | Voice Impact |
|------|------|-------|---------------|-------------|
| Type 1 | Physiological sulcus | Superficial (not beyond SLP) | Preserved | Mild; often incidental |
| Type 2a | Sulcus vergeture | Into vocal ligament | Lost focally; exists supero-laterally and infero-medially | Moderate dysphonia |
| Type 2b | Classic sulcus / Pouch type | Beyond ligament into TA muscle | Marked loss | Severe dysphonia |

### Aetiology

- **Sulcus vergeture (Type 2a):** Thought to be congenital — failure of development of Reinke's space. Becomes apparent at puberty. Can be familial.
- **Classic sulcus (Type 2b):** May represent ruptured intracordal cyst. Focal pit extending into TA muscle.
- **Mucosal bridge:** Rare variant — arises from rupture through deep aspects of two sulci/cysts forming a tubed pedicle of mucosa spanning the vocal fold surface.

### Clinical Features

- Variable degrees of dysphonia, roughness, breathiness
- Sulcus vergeture: high-pitched monotone, weak, breathy and strained voice
- Reduced vocal range and projection
- Localized absence or reduction of mucosal wave on stroboscopy
- Diagnosis may only be confirmed at microlaryngoscopy and cordotomy

### Investigations

| Investigation | Finding |
|--------------|---------|
| Laryngostroboscopy | Reduced/absent mucosal wave; sulcus may appear as linear depression |
| Microlaryngoscopy | Definitive diagnosis; palpation reveals tethering |
| Cordotomy (intra-op) | Confirms depth and type; defines plane between sulcus and ligament |

### Management

**Type 1 (Physiological sulcus):**
- Conservative: speech therapy + antireflux medication
- No surgical intervention needed

**Type 2a (Sulcus vergeture):**
- Difficult to treat surgically — extensive loss of SLP
- Best managed by **bilateral medialization procedures** (injection or [[Vocal Fold Paralysis|thyroplasty]]) rather than attempted resection
- Injection augmentation with fat or HA improves bulk and closure
- Results variable; no single treatment modality is superior

**Type 2b (Classic sulcus / Pouch type):**
- Careful microsurgical dissection of pocket off vocal ligament
- Defining the plane between base of sulcus and ligament is challenging
- Mini-microflap technique preserves overlying mucosa
- May combine with injection augmentation
- Post-operative voice therapy essential

## PART B: REINKE'S OEDEMA

### Definition

Chronic, irreversible swelling of the vocal folds due to fluid accumulation within Reinke's space (superficial lamina propria). Also called polypoid degeneration, polypoid corditis, smoker's larynx, pseudomyxoma.

### Aetiology

| Factor | Role |
|--------|------|
| Smoking | Almost exclusively in moderate-heavy smokers; primary cause |
| Voice strain | Contributory phonotrauma |
| Extraoesophageal reflux | May play a role |
| Hypothyroidism | Concomitant feature in some cases |

### Pathology

- Epithelium: non-specific changes
- Basement membrane: thickened
- Reinke's space: lakes of oedema, extravasated erythrocytes, thickening of subepithelial vessel walls
- Myxomatous degeneration of connective tissue

### Epidemiology

- Men and women affected equally (meta-analysis of 1538 cases)
- Age: 40-60 years most common
- Bilateral in 62-85% of cases
- One of the most common structural vocal fold conditions in smokers

### Grading (Tan et al.)

| Grade | Appearance |
|-------|-----------|
| 1 | Marginal edge oedema |
| 2 | Obvious sessile swelling, thrown over vocalis during phonation |
| 3 | Large bag-like swelling filled with fluid |
| 4 | Partially obstructing lesion, medial borders in contact along most of length |

### Clinical Features

- Deepening of pitch (women mistaken for man on telephone)
- Gruffness, effortful speaking
- Inability to raise pitch
- Choking episodes (grade 3-4)
- Airway obstruction (rare; grade 4)

### Management

**Conservative:**
- Smoking cessation (essential — oedema recurs within 2 years if smoking continues)
- Vocal hygiene counselling
- Treat hypothyroidism, infections, allergies, reflux
- Voice therapy: controversial but may help motivated patients

**Surgical Indications:**
- Leukoplakia requiring histological assessment
- Gross oedema causing choking or airway compromise
- Pitch elevation is the main patient requirement
- Failed conservative management

**Surgical Technique:**

```
REINKE'S OEDEMA — SURGICAL S[[Laryngectomy Rehabilitation Voice Prosthesis|TEP]]S

1. Suspension microlaryngoscopy under GA
2. Incision on LATERAL aspect of superior surface
   (preserve median vibrating edge — critical)
3. Elevate mucosa with blunt dissector
4. Aspirate/remove myxomatous contents with cupped forceps
5. Avoid damage to underlying vocal ligament
6. Replace mucosal flap; trim excess mucosa
7. Flap held by surface tension, suture, or tissue glue
8. No contraindication to bilateral same-session surgery
```

### Surgical Principles

1. Reduce bulk of mucosa (mass per unit length)
2. Obtain straight mucosal edge — avoid leaving myxomatous deposits
3. **Avoid damage to and exposure of underlying ligament** (reduces scarring and web formation)

### Patient Counselling

- Voice may not be recognized by friends/relatives post-operatively
- Singing voice may be permanently altered
- Speaking may be more effortful for up to 1 year
- Oedema will likely return within 2 years if smoking continues

## PART C: COMPARISON — SULCUS VOCALIS vs REINKE'S OEDEMA

| Feature | Sulcus Vocalis | Reinke's Oedema |
|---------|---------------|-----------------|
| Reinke's space | Lost/invaginated | Expanded/oedematous |
| Aetiology | Congenital (Type 2a); acquired (Type 2b) | Smoking (almost exclusively) |
| Gender | Equal | Equal |
| Voice change | Breathy, weak, high-pitched | Deep, gruff, low-pitched |
| Mucosal wave | Reduced/absent focally | Irregular/excessive |
| Bilateral | Variable | 62-85% |
| Surgery | Microsurgical dissection ± augmentation | Mucosal flap with aspiration |
| Prognosis | Variable; difficult | Good if smoking stopped |

---

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. Sulcus vocalis = invagination of vocal fold mucosa; classified into Type 1 (physiological), Type 2a (vergeture — congenital), and Type 2b (classic/pouch — acquired)
> 2. Sulcus vergeture is thought to be congenital (failure of Reinke's space development) — becomes apparent at puberty; can be familial
> 3. Sulcus vergeture is best treated by bilateral medialization (injection/thyroplasty) rather than attempted surgical resection
> 4. Reinke's oedema occurs almost exclusively in moderate-heavy smokers; bilateral in 62-85% of cases
> 5. Reinke's oedema graded 1-4 (Tan classification): from marginal edge oedema to partially obstructing lesion
> 6. Surgical technique for Reinke's oedema: incision on LATERAL aspect of superior surface — preserve the median vibrating edge
> 7. No contraindication to bilateral same-session surgery for Reinke's oedema
> 8. Oedema recurs within 2 years if smoking continues — counselling is mandatory
> 9. Diagnosis of sulcus may only be confirmed at microlaryngoscopy and cordotomy — stroboscopy shows reduced mucosal wave
> 10. Mucosal bridge is a rare variant — tubed pedicle from rupture of two adjacent sulci/cysts


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - Women with Reinke's oedema are commonly mistaken for men on the telephone — pitch deepening is the hallmark complaint
> - The median vibrating edge must NEVER be incised during Reinke's oedema surgery — always incise on the lateral superior surface
> - Type 2b sulcus (pouch type) may represent a ruptured intracordal cyst — always examine for associated contralateral lesion
> - Voice therapy alone cannot resolve structural sulcus (Type 2a/2b) but is essential post-operatively for optimal outcomes
> - Hypothyroidism should always be excluded in Reinke's oedema — it is a recognized concomitant feature
