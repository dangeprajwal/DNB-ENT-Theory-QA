## Introduction

The management of head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) has evolved from surgery and [[Radiotherapy Principles Fractionation Advances|radiotherapy]] alone to incorporate chemotherapy, targeted biological agents and immunotherapy. Chemotherapy can be administered at different time points in the patient pathway: as induction/neoadjuvant treatment before definitive therapy, concurrently with radiotherapy, as adjuvant therapy after surgery, or for palliation of recurrent/metastatic disease. Each strategy has distinct rationale, evidence base and toxicity profile. The development of monoclonal antibodies targeting the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) and immune checkpoint inhibitors targeting PD-1/PD-L1 and CTLA-4 pathways has opened an exciting new era in the treatment of HNSCC, particularly for recurrent and metastatic disease.

## Part A — Chemotherapy Strategies in Head and Neck Cancer

### Overview of Strategies

| Strategy | Timing | Purpose | Standard Regimen |
|----------|--------|---------|-----------------|
| Induction (neoadjuvant) | Before definitive RT/CRT | Tumour shrinkage; organ preservation; treat micrometastases | TPF (docetaxel + cisplatin + 5-FU) 3-weekly x 3 |
| Concurrent (concomitant) | Simultaneously with RT | Radiosensitization; direct tumour kill | Cisplatin 100 mg/m2 days 1, 22, 43 |
| Adjuvant | After definitive surgery | Eradicate micrometastatic disease; improve local control | Cisplatin + RT (for high-risk features) |
| Palliative | Recurrent/metastatic disease | Symptom relief; modest survival extension | Cisplatin/carboplatin + 5-FU +/- cetuximab |

### Rationale for Combination Chemotherapy

Combinations of cytotoxic agents are used rather than single agents because:
- Exposure to agents with different mechanisms of action and non-overlapping toxicities
- Reduction in the development of drug resistance
- Ability to use drugs that may be synergistic

### Concurrent Chemoradiation (CRT) — Standard of Care

Concurrent CRT is the standard of care for locally advanced H&N cancer. It is given for its direct cell-killing effect and for sensitizing cancer cells to the effects of radiotherapy. Compared with induction chemotherapy, concurrent CRT:
- Has less chance of treatment breaks
- Has been proven by the MACH-NC meta-analysis to give a **6.5% absolute survival benefit at 5 years** when added to RT alone
- This benefit was NOT seen in patients over 70 years of age

**Rationale for concurrent CRT:**
1. Chemotherapy sensitizes tumours to RT by inhibiting tumour repopulation
2. Preferentially kills hypoxic cells
3. Inhibits repair of sublethal radiation damage
4. Sterilizes micrometastatic disease outside radiation fields
5. Decreases tumour mass, improving blood supply and reoxygenation
6. RT, in turn, sensitizes tumours to chemotherapy by inhibiting repair of drug-induced damage

### Adjuvant Chemotherapy

Adjuvant chemotherapy (after definitive surgery) is NOT used as a sole modality after RT. However, when given with adjuvant radiation (adjuvant CRT), it improves local control and survival in patients with high-risk features:
- Positive surgical margins
- Nodal involvement (especially multiple nodes)
- Extracapsular spread (ECS)

Two pivotal phase III studies — RTOG 9501 and EORTC 22931 — demonstrated improved locoregional control with addition of concurrent cisplatin to adjuvant RT in high-risk patients. Post-hoc analysis confirmed that ECS and positive margins were the most important indications, with an improved 5-year survival of 13%.

## Part B — Neoadjuvant (Induction) Chemotherapy

### Definition

Neoadjuvant (induction) chemotherapy is the use of chemotherapy prior to definitive surgery or radiotherapy in patients with locally advanced head and neck cancer. It is almost always given before radiotherapy (rather than surgery), as the preparatory processes for RT (immobilization device, planning) can take place while the patient receives induction chemotherapy, bringing forward the treatment start date.

### Rationale

1. **Tumour volume reduction** — shrinks the primary tumour before definitive RT/CRT, allowing better blood flow and drug delivery, and decreasing hypoxic radioresistant areas
2. **Organ preservation** — potential to preserve a functioning larynx/hypopharynx; organ preservation is only worthwhile if the organ retains function
3. **Distant metastasis control** — since surgery and RT are locoregional treatments, induction chemotherapy can theoretically treat subclinical distant micrometastatic spread
4. **Prognostic information** — response to neoadjuvant chemotherapy acts as a surrogate marker for response to subsequent treatment (used in the VA Larynx Trial — non-responders were directed to laryngectomy)
5. **Converting inoperable to operable** — shrink bulky disease (especially fixed cervical nodes) to enable technically easier surgery

### Advantages

- Potential for organ preservation (larynx, hypopharynx)
- Reduction in distant metastasis rates (more effective than concurrent CRT for this endpoint per MACH-NC)
- Can act as biomarker of chemosensitivity — guide subsequent treatment decisions
- May enable surgery in previously inoperable cases
- Brings forward treatment start date (RT planning occurs concurrently)
- Better drug delivery to intact tumour vasculature (before surgery or RT disrupts blood supply)

### Disadvantages

- **No proven overall survival benefit** over concurrent CRT alone (MACH-NC, PARADIGM, DeCIDE)
- **Higher toxicity with TPF** — haematological (neutropenia, myelosuppression) and non-haematological (severe mucositis); increased chemotherapy-related deaths
- **Risk of failing to complete RT** — IC-induced morbidity may cause patients to have breaks in or fail to complete definitive RT, which worsens outcomes
- **Delays definitive locoregional treatment** — if the tumour is not responsive, precious time is lost
- **Selection bias** — patients suitable for IC (good PS, few comorbidities) may already have better prognosis
- **Organ preservation without function** — preserving a non-functioning organ is pointless and may lead to aspiration

### Key Evidence

| Trial | Design | Key Finding |
|-------|--------|-------------|
| VA Larynx (1991) | IC (PF) + RT vs TL + RT | Comparable 2-yr survival (68%); 64% larynx preservation |
| RTOG 91-11 | CRT vs IC+RT vs RT alone | Larynx preservation: CRT 88% > IC+RT 75% > RT 70% |
| TAX 323 (European) | TPF vs PF (induction) | TPF superior response (80% vs 59%); 10-yr larynx preservation 70.3% vs 46.5% |
| TAX 324 (N. American) | TPF vs PF (induction) | TPF 5-yr survival 52% vs PF 42% |
| DeCIDE | IC + CRT vs CRT alone (N2/N3) | No OS benefit from adding IC |
| PARADIGM | IC + CRT vs CRT alone | No OS difference |
| TREMPLIN | IC then CRT vs IC then cetuximab-RT | No difference in larynx preservation |
| MACH-NC meta-analysis | >90 trials, >16,000 patients | IC: no OS benefit; concurrent CRT: 6.5% absolute OS benefit at 5 years |

### Current Indications for Induction Chemotherapy

IC is considered in selected patients:
- Organ preservation in advanced laryngeal/hypopharyngeal cancer (when surgery not appropriate)
- Bulky nodal disease (N2b and above) where distant metastasis risk is high
- Good performance status with minimal comorbidities
- Where surgery is not feasible and conversion to operable disease is desired

## Part C — Classification of Chemotherapeutic Agents in H&N and Monoclonal Antibodies

### Classification Summary

| Class | Examples | Key H&N Indication |
|-------|---------|-------------------|
| **Platinum agents** | Cisplatin, carboplatin | Concurrent CRT (gold standard); induction; palliative |
| **Antimetabolites** | 5-Fluorouracil, methotrexate | 5-FU in PF/TPF induction; MTX for palliation |
| **Taxanes** | Docetaxel, paclitaxel | TPF induction; palliative combinations |
| **Cytotoxic antibiotics** | Bleomycin | Historical use in H&N regimens |
| **Anti-EGFR MAB** | Cetuximab | Concurrent with RT; palliative (EXTREME) |
| **Anti-PD1 MAB** | Pembrolizumab, nivolumab | Recurrent/metastatic HNSCC |
| **Anti-PD-L1 MAB** | Durvalumab | Under investigation |
| **EGFR-TKI** | Gefitinib, erlotinib | Oral agents; recurrent/metastatic disease |
| **Anti-VEGF MAB** | Bevacizumab | Investigational in H&N |

### Monoclonal Antibodies in Head and Neck Cancer

#### Sources and Types of Monoclonal Antibodies

| Type | Source | Immunogenicity | Administration |
|------|--------|---------------|----------------|
| Murine | Mouse antibodies | High (may induce immune response limiting repeat use) | Limited clinical use |
| Chimeric | Part mouse/part human | Moderate | Cetuximab (chimeric IgG) |
| Humanized | Engineered to be mostly human | Low | Bevacizumab |
| Fully human | Fully human | Minimal | Panitumumab |

#### Mechanisms of Action of Monoclonal Antibodies

**Direct effects:**
- Induction of apoptosis
- Blocking receptors needed for cell proliferation/function
- Anti-idiotype antibody formation amplifying immune response

**Indirect effects:**
- Antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) — conjugating 'killer cells' to tumour cells
- Complement-mediated cellular cytotoxicity (fixing complement leading to cytotoxicity)

#### Anti-EGFR Monoclonal Antibodies

EGFR is overexpressed in up to 90% of H&N cancers and is associated with poorer prognosis. When growth factor attaches to the receptor, cells are stimulated to divide. If the receptor is mutated, division may occur even without growth factor stimulation.

**Cetuximab (Erbitux):**
- Human-murine chimeric MAB that binds the extracellular portion of EGFR
- Binding affinity equal to the natural ligand; can effectively block EGF and TGF-alpha
- Causes EGFR internalization; targets cytotoxic immune cells towards EGFR-expressing tumour cells
- Also demonstrates anti-angiogenic effects

| Trial | Design | Key Results |
|-------|--------|-------------|
| Bonner (2006) | RT +/- cetuximab (locoregionally advanced) | 3-yr survival: 55% vs 45%; LRC: 50% vs 41% |
| RTOG 0522 | CRT +/- cetuximab | No benefit from adding cetuximab to CRT; more toxicity |
| EXTREME | Cisplatin/5-FU +/- cetuximab (recurrent/metastatic) | OS: 10.1 vs 7.4 months; improved response rate |

**Cetuximab toxicity profile:** Different from standard chemotherapy — less nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity and peripheral neuropathy; BUT gives more severe Grade 3-4 radiation dermatitis, acneiform rash (face, neck, trunk), eczema-like condition at fingertips, paronychia. Infusion-related reactions (headache, flushing, pyrexia, urticaria, bronchospasm) require pre-medication with steroids. Paradoxically, patients who develop the acne rash may have a better tumour response and improved survival.

**Other anti-EGFR MABs:** Panitumumab (fully human) and zalutumumab — both showed less encouraging results with no improvement in overall survival.

#### Anti-VEGF Monoclonal Antibodies

**Bevacizumab (Avastin):** Humanized murine MAB targeting VEGF. First anti-angiogenic drug to show survival advantage in cancer therapy. Anti-angiogenic agents tend to be cytostatic (stabilizing disease) rather than cytotoxic. In H&N cancer, clinical trials are exploring bevacizumab in combination with EGFR-targeted drugs.

## Part D — Immunotherapy in Head and Neck Cancer

### Definition

Immunotherapy is a form of cancer treatment that harnesses and enhances the body's own immune system to recognize, attack and destroy cancer cells. Unlike conventional chemotherapy (which directly damages DNA) or targeted therapy (which inhibits specific growth pathways), immunotherapy works by modulating the host immune response. In H&N cancer, the most clinically relevant immunotherapy approach involves immune checkpoint inhibitors — monoclonal antibodies that interrupt the immunosuppressive pathways (inhibitory checkpoints) used by tumour cells to evade detection and elimination by the host immune system.

### The Immune Checkpoint Concept

Normal immune homeostasis involves a balance between activating and inhibitory signals. Cancer cells exploit inhibitory checkpoints to suppress antitumour immune responses, allowing them to escape immune surveillance. The Elimination-Equilibrium-Escape model describes this process:

1. **Elimination:** Immune system recognizes and eliminates precancerous/cancerous cells
2. **Equilibrium:** Tumour cells suppress antitumour responses sufficiently to establish dormancy
3. **Escape:** Tumour cells accumulate changes (loss of tumour antigens, MHC proteins) that allow them to evade immunosurveillance; immunoediting selects less immunogenic clones; recruitment of Tregs and MDSCs further suppresses immunity

### Checkpoint Inhibitor Pathways

#### CTLA-4 Pathway (Activation Phase)

```
Antigen-Presenting Cell (APC)
          │
    B7 ───┤
          │
    ┌─────▼─────┐         ┌──────────┐
    │   CD28    │ Signal 2│          │
    │ (activates├────────►│ T-cell   │
    │  T-cell)  │         │ ACTIVATED│
    └───────────┘         └──────────┘

    ┌───────────┐         ┌──────────┐
    │   CTLA-4  │ Blocks  │          │
    │ (competes │ Signal 2│ T-cell   │
    │  with     ├────────►│INACTIVATED│
    │  CD28)    │         └──────────┘
    └───────────┘
    Anti-CTLA4 MAB (ipilimumab) blocks
    CTLA-4, allowing CD28 to activate T-cell
```

- CTLA-4 on T-cells competes with CD28 for binding to B7 on APCs
- If CTLA-4 wins, signal 2 is shut off and the T-cell is inactivated
- Anti-CTLA4 MAB (ipilimumab) blocks CTLA-4, allowing T-cell activation

#### PD-1/PD-L1 Pathway (Effector Phase)

- An activated T-cell expresses PD-1 receptors on its surface and secretes interferon-gamma (IFN-gamma)
- In response, tumour cells upregulate PD-L1 expression on their surface
- PD-L1 binding to PD-1 switches off the activated T-cell, preventing tumour cell killing
- Anti-PD1 MAB (pembrolizumab, nivolumab) or anti-PD-L1 MAB (durvalumab) block this interaction, reactivating T-cell mediated tumour killing

The likelihood of benefit from immunotherapy may correlate with the mutational burden and neoantigenic load of the tumour. Head and neck cancers (especially HPV-negative tumours) are associated with a significant number of mutations.

### Immunotherapy Agents in Head and Neck Cancer

| Agent | Target | Type | Key Evidence |
|-------|--------|------|-------------|
| Pembrolizumab | PD-1 | Humanized MAB | KEYNOTE-012: 20% RECIST response; 51% had tumour reduction; KEYNOTE-040, KEYNOTE-048 ongoing |
| Nivolumab | PD-1 | Fully human MAB | CHECKMATE-141 |
| Durvalumab (MEDI4736) | PD-L1 | Human MAB | HAWK, EAGLE trials |
| Ipilimumab | CTLA-4 | Fully human MAB | Used in melanoma; under investigation in H&N in combinations |

#### KEYNOTE-012 Trial (Pembrolizumab)

- Patients with PD-L1-positive (>1% staining) relapsed/metastatic HNSCC
- Divided into HPV+ve and HPV-ve cohorts
- Treated with pembrolizumab 10 mg/kg every 2 weeks
- Treatment beyond progression permitted if clinical benefit derived
- **Results:** 26 of 51 patients (51%) had tumour reduction; 20% achieved RECIST v1.1 criteria for response
- Subsequently, 132 patients treated with fixed dose pembrolizumab (200 mg) every 3 weeks irrespective of PD-L1 status — well tolerated (only 7.6% grade 3+ adverse effects); 18.2% partial response; 31.3% stable disease

### Side Effects of Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors

The side effects are entirely different from standard chemotherapy — by "taking the brakes off" the immune response, these agents cause autoimmune adverse reactions:

| Side Effect | Description | Frequency |
|-------------|-------------|-----------|
| Skin rash | Maculopapular, pruritic | Common |
| Colitis | Diarrhoea; can be severe | Moderate |
| Hepatitis | Transaminitis; liver dysfunction | Moderate |
| Endocrinopathy | Thyroid dysfunction (hypo/hyperthyroidism), adrenal insufficiency, pituitary dysfunction (hypophysitis) | Moderate |
| Pneumonitis | Interstitial lung disease | Less common |
| Nephritis | Interstitial nephritis | Rare |

- Autoimmune side effects are **more common with anti-CTLA4 agents** (ipilimumab) than anti-PD1/PD-L1 agents
- All autoimmune reactions require specialist management as they can evolve into serious, life-threatening conditions
- Clinical responses to immunotherapy tend to occur more slowly than with conventional chemotherapy
- **Pseudoprogression** may occur — apparent disease progression before response; treatment may be continued if clinical benefit is perceived

### Combinations of Checkpoint Inhibitors and Radiation

Radiation therapy can cause immunogenic cell death. Pre-clinical and clinical data demonstrate that immune checkpoint inhibition may enhance this effect and lead to systemic activity of a local therapy (abscopal effect). Multiple studies are combining checkpoint inhibitors with ionizing radiation — both palliative hypofractionated RT and radical standard fractionation (with/without platinum-based chemotherapy). This strategy holds promise for using intensive locoregional therapy to generate systemic protection against metastatic disease recurrence.

## Part E — Chemotherapy for Recurrent/Metastatic Disease

### Prognosis

- Locoregional recurrence reported in up to 50% of H&N cancer patients
- Median survival in metastatic disease: approximately 6-12 months
- First-line chemotherapy response rates <20% for single agents (methotrexate, docetaxel, paclitaxel)

### Key Principles

- **Patient selection is critical** — fitness, performance status, comorbidities, renal function, previous treatments and interval since last chemotherapy all influence decision-making
- **Shared decision-making** essential — balance between symptom palliation, survival extension and quality of life
- **Locoregional and metastatic disease are separate problems** — the presence of distant metastases does not necessarily contraindicate salvage treatment at the primary site

### Standard Palliative Regimens

| Regimen | Agents | Response Rate | Notes |
|---------|--------|---------------|-------|
| Cisplatin + 5-FU | Standard combination | ~40% | Most common first-line |
| Carboplatin + 5-FU | Carboplatin replaces cisplatin | ~40% | Less toxic; preferred in palliation |
| EXTREME regimen | Cisplatin/5-FU + cetuximab | Higher than PF alone | OS: 10.1 vs 7.4 months (p=0.04) |
| Cetuximab monotherapy | Cetuximab alone | 10-15% | After cisplatin/5-FU refractory |
| Pembrolizumab/nivolumab | Anti-PD1 checkpoint inhibitor | ~20% (RECIST) | After first-line progression; irrespective of PD-L1 |

- **Elderly patients** do appear to respond to platinum-based chemotherapy in the metastatic setting, in contrast to a lack of benefit when used in primary CRT regimens; however, there is more toxicity
- More toxic regimens using platinum + taxane have not improved survival compared with cisplatin + 5-FU but have higher response rates at the cost of higher toxicity

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. Four chemotherapy strategies exist in H&N cancer: induction (neoadjuvant — before definitive treatment), concurrent (simultaneously with RT), adjuvant (after surgery), and palliative (recurrent/metastatic disease)
> 2. Concurrent CRT with cisplatin is the standard of care for locally advanced HNSCC, providing a 6.5% absolute survival benefit at 5 years (MACH-NC meta-analysis); this benefit is NOT seen in patients over 70 years
> 3. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy aims to shrink tumour volume, enable organ preservation, treat subclinical distant metastases and provide prognostic information; however, no proven OS benefit over concurrent CRT alone exists (DeCIDE, PARADIGM trials)
> 4. TPF has replaced PF as the preferred induction regimen (TAX 323/324 trials) but carries significantly higher toxicity including neutropenia, severe mucositis and chemotherapy-related deaths
> 5. Adjuvant CRT improves outcomes in surgically treated patients with high-risk features — ECS and positive margins are the strongest indications (RTOG 9501, EORTC 22931)
> 6. Cetuximab is a chimeric anti-EGFR MAB that improves survival when added to RT (Bonner trial: 3-yr OS 55% vs 45%) but does NOT add benefit when combined with CRT (RTOG 0522); it is used when cisplatin is contraindicated
> 7. Immunotherapy uses immune checkpoint inhibitors (anti-PD1, anti-PD-L1, anti-CTLA4) to reactivate T-cell mediated tumour killing by blocking the PD-1/PD-L1 and CTLA-4 inhibitory pathways exploited by cancer cells to evade immune surveillance
> 8. Pembrolizumab (KEYNOTE-012): 20% RECIST response and 51% tumour reduction in PD-L1-positive recurrent/metastatic HNSCC; immunotherapy responses may be slower than chemotherapy and pseudoprogression can occur
> 9. Autoimmune side effects of checkpoint inhibitors (rash, colitis, hepatitis, endocrinopathy) are entirely different from chemotherapy toxicity and are more common with anti-CTLA4 than anti-PD1/PD-L1 agents; all require specialist management
> 10. Combination of checkpoint inhibitors with radiation is a promising strategy — radiation causes immunogenic cell death that may synergize with immune checkpoint inhibition to generate systemic antitumour activity (abscopal effect)


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - **Concurrent CRT is superior to induction + RT for organ preservation** — RTOG 91-11 showed 88% larynx preservation with CRT vs 75% with induction + RT, establishing concurrent CRT as the standard non-surgical approach
> - **The MACH-NC meta-analysis is the single most important evidence source** for chemotherapy in H&N cancer — >90 trials, >16,000 patients; confirms concurrent CRT benefit (6.5% OS) and the lack of OS benefit for induction chemotherapy (though PF showed some benefit and IC reduces distant metastases)
> - **Cetuximab's acneiform rash correlates with better tumour response** — patients who develop the rash tend to have improved overall survival; the rash can usually be managed by increasing treatment intervals and topical medications
> - **Response to induction chemotherapy is a powerful selection tool** — the VA Larynx Trial used this principle: responders proceeded to RT (organ preservation), while non-responders underwent laryngectomy without delay
> - **HPV-positive tumours have better outcomes** but at present, HPV status does NOT alter chemotherapy or RT regimens outside of clinical trials; multiple de-escalation trials are underway
> - **Objective response rates to checkpoint inhibitors are around 20%** in patients progressing on first-line chemotherapy, irrespective of PD-L1 expression status; PD-L1 expression correlates with but does not definitively predict response; no reliable biomarker for patient selection has been identified
