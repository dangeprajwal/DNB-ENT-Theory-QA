## INTRODUCTION

Head and neck oncology has undergone transformative advances in recent decades including minimally invasive transoral surgical approaches, [[Allergic Rhinitis Immunotherapy|immunotherapy]], intensity-modulated [[Radiotherapy Principles Fractionation Advances|radiotherapy]] and precision medicine. The AJCC 8th edition staging system introduced landmark changes for oral cavity cancer (incorporation of depth of invasion), oropharyngeal cancer (separate staging for HPV-positive tumours) and nodal staging (incorporation of extranodal extension). Management of the clinically negative (N0) neck in early oral cavity carcinoma remains a key debate, with recent high-level evidence now favouring elective neck dissection over observation.

## PART A: RECENT ADVANCES IN HEAD AND NECK MALIGNANCY MANAGEMENT

### 1. Transoral Surgical Approaches

**Transoral Robotic Surgery (TORS):**
- Uses the da Vinci robotic system for resection of oropharyngeal tumours
- Provides 3D magnified visualization, EndoWrist articulation (7 degrees of freedom), tremor filtration
- Primarily applied to T1-T2 oropharyngeal cancers, especially HPV-positive SCC
- Advantages: organ preservation, avoidance of mandibulotomy, reduced swallowing dysfunction, shorter hospital stay
- Allows de-escalation of adjuvant treatment in selected patients

**Transoral Ultrasonic Surgery (TOUSS):**
- Uses ultrasonically activated devices (e.g., Thunderbeat) for transoral tumour resection
- Does not require the da Vinci robotic platform — lower cost
- Provides comparable access to oropharyngeal and supraglottic tumours
- Advantages over TORS: no need for expensive robotic system, shorter setup time, adaptable to existing surgical instruments
- Disadvantages: 2D visualization (vs 3D in TORS), less degrees of instrument freedom, steeper surgeon-dependent learning curve

**Transoral Laser Microsurgery (TLM):**
- CO2 laser combined with operating microscope
- Established technique for glottic and supraglottic laryngeal cancers
- Can achieve organ preservation with excellent oncological outcomes for early-stage disease

### 2. Immunotherapy (Checkpoint Inhibitors)

The development of drugs that suppress the ability of cancer cells to avoid destruction by the immune system represents a major advance. PD-1/PD-L1 interaction inhibitors have shown significant potential:

- **Nivolumab:** PD-1 inhibitor — FDA-approved for treatment of head and neck cancer; in-depth analysis demonstrates that head and neck cancer is characterized by high levels of immune cell infiltration and immunosuppression, regardless of HPV status
- **Pembrolizumab:** PD-1 inhibitor — FDA-approved for recurrent/metastatic HNSCC
- Regulatory T (Treg) and natural killer (NK) cells have emerged as potential therapeutic targets
- Immunotherapy holds great promise — trials of nivolumab and similar agents are ongoing

### 3. Intensity-Modulated Radiotherapy (IMRT)

IMRT represents the standard of care for radiotherapy in head and neck cancer, offering:
- Conformal dose distribution with steep dose gradients
- Reduced damage to surrounding organs at risk (parotid glands, spinal cord, optic structures)
- "Dose-painting" capability allowing different dose levels to different targets simultaneously
- Improved local control rates with reduced toxicity

### 4. Molecular Targeted Therapy

- **Cetuximab (Erbitux):** Monoclonal antibody targeting EGFR — used in combination with radiotherapy or chemotherapy
- Tyrosine kinase inhibitors targeting multiple pathways
- BRAF/MEK inhibitors for BRAF-mutant tumours (e.g., anaplastic thyroid, some HNSCC)
- Molecular profiling enabling precision medicine — matching treatments to individual tumour mutations

### 5. HPV-Related Oropharyngeal Cancer

Recognition of HPV-positive oropharyngeal cancer as a distinct entity with markedly better prognosis has led to:
- Separate staging system in AJCC 8th edition (p16-positive vs p16-negative)
- De-escalation trials aiming to reduce treatment toxicity while maintaining excellent outcomes
- p16 immunohistochemistry as a surrogate marker for HPV status

### 6. Sentinel Lymph Node Biopsy (SLNB)

SLNB has emerged as an alternative to elective neck dissection in T1-T2 N0 oral cavity cancer:
- Large multicentre trial of 415 patients showed sensitivity of 86%
- NICE guidelines (2016) recommend SLNB should be offered as alternative to END unless cervical access is needed for reconstruction
- Avoids morbidity of full neck dissection in patients with truly negative necks

## PART B: AJCC 8th EDITION TNM STAGING — ORAL CAVITY

### Key Change: Depth of Invasion (DOI)

The most significant modification in the AJCC 8th edition for oral cavity tumours is the incorporation of **depth of invasion (DOI)** into T classification. DOI is measured from the level of the basement membrane of adjacent normal mucosa, NOT tumour thickness.

### Oral Cavity T Classification (AJCC 8th Edition)

| T Stage | Size | DOI |
|---------|------|-----|
| T1 | ≤2 cm | ≤5 mm |
| T2 | ≤2 cm AND DOI >5 mm OR >2 cm to ≤4 cm | ≤10 mm |
| T3 | >4 cm OR any size | >10 mm |
| T4a | Invades cortical bone, deep extrinsic tongue muscles, maxillary sinus, skin of face | — |
| T4b | Invades masticator space, pterygoid plates, skull base, encases ICA | — |

**Note:** Superficial erosion alone of bone/tooth socket by gingival primary is NOT sufficient to classify as T4a.

### N Classification (AJCC 8th Edition) — Key Change: Extranodal Extension (ENE)

The AJCC 8th edition recognizes the importance of **extranodal extension (ENE)** — previously called extracapsular spread:

| N Stage | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| N1 | Single ipsilateral node ≤3 cm, without ENE |
| N2a | Single ipsilateral node >3 cm to ≤6 cm, without ENE |
| N2b | Multiple ipsilateral nodes ≤6 cm, without ENE |
| N2c | Bilateral/contralateral nodes ≤6 cm, without ENE |
| N3a | Any node >6 cm, without ENE |
| N3b | Any node(s) with clinical ENE |

**Clinical ENE:** Deep fixation/tethering to underlying muscle or adjacent structures, or clinical signs of nerve involvement are classified as clinical extranodal extension. A patient with ENE is automatically N3b, reflecting poorer prognosis.

### Separate Staging for HPV-Positive Oropharyngeal Cancer

The AJCC 8th edition created a distinct staging system for p16-positive oropharyngeal cancer:

| Stage | TNM Combination |
|-------|-----------------|
| I | T1-T2, N0-N1, M0 |
| II | T1-T2 N2, or T3 N0-N2, M0 |
| III | T1-T3 N3, or T4 any N, M0 |
| IV | Any T, any N, M1 |

Key differences from general H&N staging: no T4b category (T4a and T4b are combined); N classification for p16-positive OPC is based on number of nodes (≤4 vs >4) in pathological staging.

## PART C: BIOMARKERS IN ORAL CANCER

### Current Biomarkers

**p53 mutations:**
- Inactivating mutations found in the majority of tobacco-related oral cavity cancers
- Loss of p53 function is an early event in oral carcinogenesis
- Found in dysplasia and can predict malignant transformation

**EGFR (Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor):**
- Overexpressed in 80-90% of HNSCC
- Target for cetuximab therapy
- Associated with poorer prognosis when overexpressed

**Cyclin D1:**
- Amplification and overexpression correlate with poor prognosis in oral SCC
- Cell cycle regulator involved in G1/S phase transition

**Histopathological biomarkers:**
- Depth of invasion (now incorporated into TNM)
- Worst pattern of invasion (WPOI): pattern 4 and 5 associated with higher recurrence and metastasis
- Perineural invasion
- Lymphovascular invasion

### Molecular Markers with Emerging Clinical Utility

- **PIK3CA mutations:** Identified in HNSCC, potential therapeutic target
- **TP53 mutations:** Common in tobacco-related cancers; less common in HPV-related OPSCC
- **CDKN2A (p16) loss:** Tumour suppressor loss in oral cavity cancer
- **Salivary biomarkers:** Emerging field — non-invasive detection of tumour-derived DNA, RNA and proteins in saliva for screening and monitoring

## PART D: CLASSIFICATION OF NECK DISSECTION

### Current Classification (AAO-HNS, 2002)

**1. Radical Neck Dissection (RND):**
- Clearance of lymph node levels I-V
- Removal of SCM, IJV, and spinal accessory nerve (SAN)
- Original Crile procedure — now rarely performed

**2. Modified Radical Neck Dissection (MRND):**
- Clearance of levels I-V with preservation of one or more non-lymphatic structures:
  - Type I: Preserves SAN
  - Type II: Preserves SAN + IJV
  - Type III: Preserves SAN + IJV + SCM (functional neck dissection)

**3. Selective Neck Dissection (SND):**
- Clearance of selected levels based on primary tumour site and patterns of metastatic spread:

| Type | Levels Cleared | Primary Site |
|------|---------------|-------------|
| Supraomohyoid (SOHND) | I-III | Oral cavity |
| Lateral | II-IV | Larynx, hypopharynx, oropharynx |
| Posterolateral | II-V + suboccipital | Posterior scalp, skin |
| Anterior | Level VI | Thyroid, subglottic, trachea |

**4. Extended Neck Dissection:**
- Removal of additional lymph node groups (retropharyngeal, parapharyngeal, mediastinal) or non-lymphatic structures (carotid artery, hypoglossal nerve, vagus nerve) not included in standard ND types

### Key Principle

SND is therapeutically equivalent to comprehensive neck dissection for elective treatment of the N0 neck, based on prospective studies. There is little justification for comprehensive neck dissection in the elective setting.

## PART E: MANAGEMENT OF THE N0 NECK IN ORAL CAVITY CARCINOMA

### The Debate

Management of the clinically negative neck (cN0) in early-stage (T1-T2) oral cavity SCC has been one of the most debated topics in head and neck surgery. Options include:
1. Elective neck dissection (END)
2. Elective neck irradiation (ENI)
3. Watch and wait policy (observation with regular follow-up)
4. Sentinel lymph node biopsy (SLNB)

### Landmark Trial: D'Cruz et al (2015)

The most significant evidence comes from the D'Cruz et al randomized controlled trial (NEJM 2015) — the largest and most robust study addressing this question:

| Group | Patients | Positive Necks | Disease-free Survival | p-value |
|-------|----------|---------------|----------------------|---------|
| END | 245 | 81 (33%) | 70% | p < 0.001 |
| Observation | 255 | 146 (57%) | 46% | — |

**Key findings:**
- Occult nodal disease rate in the END group was 30%
- Nodal relapse in the observation group was 45% — markedly higher
- Nodal relapse in observation group was more advanced and associated with higher rate of ENE
- END significantly improved overall survival and disease-free survival
- Subgroup analysis: benefit of END may not exist for tumours with DOI ≤3 mm

### Indications for Elective Neck Treatment

**Box 18.6 — Indications for elective neck treatment:**
- 15-20% or greater chance of subclinical neck disease
- Vigilant follow-up not possible
- Clinical evaluation of the neck is difficult
- Access to the neck needed for reconstruction

### NICE Guidelines (2016)

Based on systematic review and health economic model, NICE made two explicit recommendations for T1-T2 N0 oral cavity cancer:
1. **Surgical management of the neck should be offered to ALL patients**
2. **Sentinel lymph node biopsy should be offered as alternative to END** unless cervical access is needed at the same time (e.g., for free-flap reconstruction)

### Evidence Summary

| Study | Design | Patients | Outcome | Significance |
|-------|--------|----------|---------|-------------|
| Vandenbrouck (1980) | RCT | 75 | No difference | NS |
| Kligerman (1994) | RCT | 67 | END superior | p = 0.04 |
| Fakih (1989) | RCT | 70 | No difference (1-year data) | NS |
| D'Cruz (2015) | RCT | 500 | END superior | p < 0.001 |

### Extent of Neck Dissection

When END is performed for oral cavity cancer:
- **Minimum:** SND levels I-III (supraomohyoid)
- **Tongue cancers:** Add level IV (due to skip metastases to level IV in 14-19%)
- **Level V:** Not routinely indicated for mucosal primaries
- **Level IIb:** Clearance involves risk of accessory nerve traction; occult metastasis rate 3.9% for oral cavity

### Elective Neck Irradiation

Retrospective evidence suggests approximately 40-50 Gy to the clinically N0 neck will control occult metastases in 90-95% of cases. When the primary is treated with surgery, elective neck surgery is preferred as it provides histological staging data, facilitates reconstructive vessel access, and may improve survival.

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. TOUSS uses ultrasonically activated devices for transoral resection without robotic platform — lower cost alternative to TORS with comparable access but 2D visualization
> 2. Nivolumab (PD-1 inhibitor) is FDA-approved for head and neck cancer — immunotherapy represents one of the most significant recent advances in HN oncology
> 3. AJCC 8th edition introduced DOI into oral cavity T staging: T1 = ≤2 cm/≤5 mm DOI; T2 = ≤2 cm + DOI >5 mm or >2-4 cm/≤10 mm DOI; T3 = >4 cm or >10 mm DOI
> 4. Extranodal extension (ENE) is now incorporated into N staging — any node with clinical ENE is automatically N3b regardless of size
> 5. p16-positive oropharyngeal cancer has a separate staging system in AJCC 8th edition with better stage-for-stage prognosis
> 6. D'Cruz et al (2015) RCT demonstrated significant survival benefit for END over observation in T1-T2 N0 oral cavity cancer (DFS 70% vs 46%, p<0.001)
> 7. NICE (2016) recommends surgical management of the N0 neck for ALL patients with T1-T2 oral cavity cancer; SLNB is an acceptable alternative
> 8. For oral cavity N0: SND levels I-III minimum; add level IV for tongue cancers (skip metastases); level V not routinely indicated
> 9. General consensus: treat the neck when occult metastasis risk exceeds 15-20%; many oncologists treat for lesser probability (5-15%)
> 10. The threshold of 20% for elective treatment is derived from a decision analysis model where morbidity of treatment is offset by benefit — widely accepted but based on radical neck dissection, not modern SND


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - DOI ≤3 mm may not benefit from elective neck dissection (D'Cruz subgroup analysis) — this threshold is increasingly being used to refine the indication for END
> - Sentinel lymph node biopsy achieves 86% sensitivity in a large multicentre trial — an excellent option when cervical access for reconstruction is not required
> - Nodal relapse after observation is more advanced and has higher ENE rates than occult disease found at END — the "watch and wait" strategy allows a window for progression
> - For p16-positive oropharyngeal cancer, there is no T4b category — T4a and T4b have indistinguishable survival curves, prompting merger into a single T4 category
> - Buccal carcinoma has 26% occult nodal metastasis rate in cN0 patients — END recommended for tumours >T2 or thickness >5 mm
> - Elective neck irradiation (40-50 Gy) is 90-95% effective for subclinical disease but does not provide histological staging — surgery preferred when primary is managed surgically
