## Introduction

White-light endoscopy remains the cornerstone of clinical examination of the upper aerodigestive tract, but is inherently limited by its inability to detect very small epithelial changes and inability to differentiate benign from malignant lesions in vivo. Narrow band imaging (NBI) is a novel optical enhancement technology that overcomes these limitations by using filtered light to visualize mucosal and submucosal neoangiogenic microvascular patterns. Together with contact endoscopy and autofluorescence, these enhanced visualization techniques represent the newer diagnostic modalities for early detection of head and neck cancer without the need for tissue biopsy.

---

## Part A — Principle of Narrow Band Imaging (NBI)

NBI technology is based on the principle that **depth of light penetration is a function of its wavelength**, and that haemoglobin has specific absorption peaks:

1. **Optical filtering** — Special filters select only two narrow wavelength bands from white light, corresponding to the peak absorption of haemoglobin
2. **Blue light (415 nm)** — Penetrates superficial mucosa only; highlights **superficial capillary networks** as brown
3. **Green light (540 nm)** — Penetrates deeper into the submucosal layer; displays **subepithelial vessels** as cyan
4. **High-contrast image** — The combined dual-wavelength illumination produces a very high-contrast image of the tissue surface, particularly evident with **HDTV camera**

### Technical Features

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Wavelengths used | 415 nm (blue) and 540 nm (green) |
| Mechanism | Selective absorption by haemoglobin at peak wavelengths |
| Superficial view | Capillary networks (brown) — 415 nm |
| Deep view | Subepithelial vessels (cyan) — 540 nm |
| Enhancement | Requires HDTV camera for optimal contrast |
| Availability | Standard feature on some videoendoscopes (Olympus) |
| Staining required | No — non-contact, no dyes needed |
| Assessment | Office-based (clinic) or under GA |

## Part B — NBI in Diagnosis of Laryngeal Lesions

### Neoangiogenic Features on NBI

Squamous cell carcinomas arising from the upper aerodigestive tract mucosa are accompanied by **neoangiogenesis**. NBI identifies the following characteristic angiogenic features associated with precancerous and malignant mucosal lesions:

**Intraepithelial Papillary Capillary Loops (IPCLs):**

| IPCL Pattern | Significance |
|--------------|-------------|
| Type I — Regular, thin loops | Normal mucosa |
| Type II — Dilated, regular | Inflammatory/benign |
| Type III — Irregular, dilated | Low-grade dysplasia |
| Type IV — Irregular, tortuous | High-grade dysplasia / CIS |
| Type V — Destroyed, irregular | Invasive carcinoma |
| Type Va — Small dots/loops | Superficial invasion |
| Type Vb — Irregular vessels | Deep invasion |
| Type Vc — Large, irregular | Deep invasion, cartilage |

**Diagnostic Accuracy in Primary Laryngeal Lesions:**
- Sensitivity: **89%**
- Specificity: **93%**
- Higher values achieved in assessing lesions following radiation and chemoradiation therapy
- Reduces requirement for tissue biopsy in post-treatment surveillance

### NBI in CUP (Cancer of Unknown Primary)

NBI modifies the standard white light endoscope using optical filters absorbing all but two wavelengths:
- 415 nm — highlights submucosal capillaries as **brown**
- 540 nm — identifies prominent vessels as **cyan**

As SCCs are accompanied by neoangiogenesis, NBI identifies neoplastic tissue at an earlier stage than conventional endoscopy.

**Meta-analysis of 4 studies (115 CUP patients):**
- Sensitivity: **74.1%** (95% CI 52.5%–100%)
- Specificity: **94.1%** (95% CI 23.7%–100%)

NICE recommends: *"Consider using NBI (in clinic or during GA assessment) in cases where [[PET CT Nuclear Medicine Scans|PET-CT]] has failed to identify a primary site"*

## Part C — Contact Endoscopy: Technique and Findings

### Principle and Technique

Contact endoscopy allows in vivo and in situ observation of mucosal cells and microvascular networks at high magnification:

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Instrument | Contact microlaryngoscope (Karl Storz 8715 AA) |
| Diameter | 5.8 mm (standard); 4 mm (nasal/oral) |
| Magnification | 60× and 150× (adjustable) |
| First described | Desormaux, 1865 (bladder); Karl Storz laryngoscope, 1995 |
| Staining | Methylene blue for cellular observation; none for vascular observation |
| Duration of staining | 4–5 minutes |
| Anaesthesia | GA with intubation (larynx); often no anaesthesia needed (nasal, oral, oropharynx) |

### Cellular Patterns on Contact Endoscopy

**Normal squamous epithelium:**
- Polyhedric cells in continuity
- Round, dark nuclei
- Light blue cytoplasm
- Regular nucleus:cytoplasm ratio
- Homogeneous pattern

**Normal ciliated epithelium:**
- Round, dark nuclei very close to each other
- Cytoplasm limits difficult to define
- Higher density of nuclei per optic field

**Pathological Patterns:**

| Pattern | Contact Endoscopy Findings |
|---------|---------------------------|
| Chronic inflammation | Larger nuclei, increased N:C ratio, organized vascular pattern with higher density |
| Fungal infection | Small dark dots (spores), hyphae, filamentous structures; often associated with dysplasia |
| Keratosis | Cells without nuclei; amorphous or laminar structure; different degrees within same lesion |
| Dysplasia | Altered size, shape and colouration of nuclei; dyskaryosis and anisokaryosis; disorganized vascular network indicates breach of basal membrane |
| Carcinoma | Marked cellular irregularity and heterogeneity; variable N:C ratio; atypical vessels with ectasias, haemorrhages, thrombosis |
| Papilloma | Vascular axis of papillae visible; NBI enhances visualization |

### Vascular Assessment

- Most superficial microvascular network situated immediately below the basal membrane
- Vascular morphology conditioned by topography and mucosal function
- **Vocal cords** — blood vessels parallel to each other, connected by transverse anastomotic vessels (arrangement favours vibratory movements)
- **Most UADT mucosa** — vessels parallel with many anastomoses forming a plexus

**Diagnostic Accuracy:**
- Sensitivity: **90%**
- Specificity: **94%**
- 88% agreement with histopathological findings after appropriate training
- Currently confined to clinical research but shows promise for wider application

## Part D — NBI vs Contact Endoscopy: Key Differences

| Feature | NBI | Contact Endoscopy |
|---------|-----|-------------------|
| Principle | Optical filtering of white light to dual wavelengths | Direct magnification of mucosal surface |
| Contact with mucosa | No — non-contact technique | Yes — endoscope applied directly on mucosa |
| Magnification | Standard endoscopic view with enhanced contrast | 60× and 150× magnification |
| Staining required | No | Methylene blue (for cells); none (for vessels) |
| What is visualized | Mucosal and submucosal vascular patterns | Individual cells, nuclei, cytoplasm + vessels |
| Depth assessment | Superficial capillaries + subepithelial vessels | First 3 layers of epithelial cells + subepithelial vessels |
| Sensitivity | 89% (laryngeal lesions) | 90% |
| Specificity | 93% | 94% |
| Availability | Standard on some videoendoscopes | Specialized endoscope (Karl Storz) |
| Setting | Office and operating room | Primarily operating room (larynx) |
| Learning curve | Significant — must understand aberrant microvascular architecture | Requires training in cytological interpretation |
| Limitation | Cannot assess deep extent of neoplasia | Cannot substitute for histological biopsy |
| Enhancement with NBI | — | Yes — NBI illumination with contact endoscopy improves vessel visibility |
| Main use | Screening, margin assessment, post-treatment surveillance | Research tool, biopsy guidance, margin assessment |

**Combined use:** In 2007, contact endoscopy was enhanced by combining with Olympus NBI illumination system, which selectively absorbs light by haemoglobin, improving visibility of deeper vessels without methylene blue staining.

## Part E — Other Newer Diagnostic Modalities for Early H&N Cancer

### 1. Autofluorescence

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Principle | Fluorophores (collagen, elastin, mitochondria, porphyrins) emit light after absorption at specific wavelengths |
| Light source | Monochromatic blue/UV light (375–460 nm), typically laser form |
| Normal mucosa | Fluoresces **green** |
| Neoplastic mucosa | Reduced autofluorescence, emits **red-violet colour** |
| Sensitivity | 87–97% (combined with microlaryngoscopy for invasive carcinoma) |
| Specificity | 78–87% |
| Limitations | Cannot assess basal mucosal layer (restricted penetration depth); low specificity with false positives from granulation tissue, scarring, chronic inflammation |

### 2. Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT)

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Principle | Analogue of ultrasound using light instead of sound |
| Resolution | Micrometre resolution cross-sectional image (similar to vertical histological section) |
| Technique | Interferometry measures echo time delay of back-reflected light |
| Advantage | Significantly increases sensitivity for benign vs malignant and grade of precancerous lesions vs microlaryngoscopy alone |
| Limitation | Cannot yet replace excisional tissue biopsy |

### 3. Fluorescence Lifetime Imaging (FLIM)

Non-contact optical method providing complementary ultrastructural and spectral detail. Based on principle that fluorescence lifetime of a fluorophore depends on its molecular environment but not its concentration, allowing investigation of molecular effects independent of fluorophore concentration.

### Summary Comparison

| Modality | Sensitivity | Specificity | Key Advantage |
|----------|-------------|-------------|---------------|
| NBI | 89% | 93% | Non-contact, readily available, high contrast |
| Contact endoscopy | 90% | 94% | Cellular-level detail, in vivo cytology |
| Autofluorescence | 87–97% | 78–87% | Identifies invasive carcinoma well |
| OCT | Enhanced vs MLS alone | Enhanced vs MLS alone | Cross-sectional view like histology |
| White-light endoscopy | Baseline | Baseline | Standard, universally available |

## Part F — Nerve Monitoring in Laryngeal Surgery

Intraoperative nerve monitoring (IONM) is used during thyroid and laryngeal surgery to identify and preserve the recurrent laryngeal nerve (RLN):

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Principle | EMG signal detected from vocalis muscle via endotracheal tube surface electrodes upon stimulation of RLN |
| Electrode placement | Specialized EMG endotracheal tube with surface electrodes at glottic level |
| Stimulation | Direct stimulation of exposed nerve with monopolar/bipolar probe (1–2 mA) |
| Response | EMG amplitude >100 μV confirms intact nerve function |
| Loss of signal | Amplitude <100 μV or absent — indicates possible nerve injury |
| Applications | Thyroidectomy, revision thyroid surgery, laryngeal framework surgery, skull base surgery |
| IONM types | Intermittent (standard) and Continuous (C-IONM using vagal electrode) |
| Staged thyroidectomy | If signal lost on first side → consider staging second side to prevent bilateral VCP |
| Limitations | Does not prevent nerve injury; false negatives possible; EMG tube positioning critical |

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. NBI uses optical filters selecting two wavelengths — 415 nm (blue, superficial capillaries as brown) and 540 nm (green, subepithelial vessels as cyan) — based on peak haemoglobin absorption
> 2. Intraepithelial papillary capillary loops (IPCLs) are the characteristic angiogenic features on NBI — Types IV–V indicate high-grade dysplasia to invasive carcinoma
> 3. NBI achieves sensitivity 89% and specificity 93% in diagnosis of primary laryngeal lesions, with higher values in post-treatment assessment
> 4. In CUP, NBI meta-analysis (115 patients) shows sensitivity 74.1% and specificity 94.1%; NICE recommends NBI when PET-CT fails to identify primary
> 5. Contact endoscopy uses 60× and 150× magnification with methylene blue staining to visualize first 3 epithelial cell layers and subepithelial microvascular network in vivo
> 6. Contact endoscopy of vocal cords reveals parallel blood vessels with transverse anastomotic connections favouring vibratory movements — pattern disrupted in dysplasia and carcinoma
> 7. Key difference: NBI is non-contact with standard endoscopes visualizing vascular patterns; contact endoscopy requires direct mucosal application with specialized endoscope for cellular-level detail
> 8. Autofluorescence uses monochromatic blue/UV light (375–460 nm) — normal mucosa fluoresces green while neoplastic mucosa emits red-violet; sensitivity 87–97% but low specificity (false positives from scarring)
> 9. Combined contact endoscopy with NBI illumination (since 2007) enhances visualization of deeper vessels without requiring methylene blue staining
> 10. All optical enhancement techniques are limited by superficial penetration depth and cannot assess deep extent of neoplasia; they complement but do not replace histological biopsy

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - **NBI is standard feature on Olympus videoendoscopes** — no additional equipment needed; can be toggled between white light and NBI with a button press
> - **In CUP evaluation sequence**: CT/MRI → PET-CT → NBI (if PET-CT negative) → EUA with guided biopsies including bilateral [[Tonsillectomy|tonsillectomy]] and tongue base mucosectomy
> - **Learning curve is the main barrier** to NBI adoption — requires understanding of aberrant microvascular architecture patterns
> - **Contact endoscopy identifies fungal infection** (dark spores) associated with dysplastic lesions — important coexistence often missed by standard examination
> - **For post-radiation surveillance**, NBI is particularly valuable as it reduces the need for tissue biopsy in irradiated patients where healing may be compromised
> - **Nerve monitoring does not prevent injury** — it provides real-time feedback but the surgeon must integrate IONM data with anatomical knowledge for safe dissection
