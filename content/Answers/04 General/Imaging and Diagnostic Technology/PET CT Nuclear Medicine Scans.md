## Introduction

Positron emission tomography (PET), now routinely combined with computed tomography as PET-CT, represents a paradigm shift in head and neck oncological imaging by providing functional metabolic information integrated with anatomical detail. Unlike conventional CT and MRI that produce predominantly anatomical images, PET identifies pathology based on altered tissue metabolism. The radiotracer 2-[18F]fluoro-2-deoxy-D-glucose (FDG) exploits the increased glucose avidity of malignant cells — an observation first made by Otto Warburg in the 1920s. Nuclear medicine scans, including Sestamibi scintigraphy, MIBG and octreotide scanning, provide additional functional imaging modalities critical to the management of endocrine and vascular tumours of the head and neck.

## Part A — Principle and Physics of PET-CT

PET is a functional imaging technique relying on a radioactive molecule (radiotracer) that decays with **positron emission**. The process involves the following steps:

1. **Radiotracer administration** — FDG is given intravenously and enters cells via glucose transporters
2. **Metabolic trapping** — FDG is phosphorylated to FDG-6-phosphate but cannot undergo further glycolysis, causing intracellular accumulation; malignant cells trap more radiotracer due to increased glucose metabolism
3. **Positron emission** — Unstable 18F decays by positron emission; the positron travels a short distance in tissue before colliding with an electron
4. **Annihilation reaction** — Collision produces two gamma-ray photons of 511 keV each, emitted at approximately 180° to each other
5. **Detection** — Opposing detectors in the PET scanner ring detect coincident photons; a computer reconstructs images representing radiotracer uptake

**Standardized Uptake Value (SUV):**
The SUV provides a semi-quantitative index of radiotracer uptake, calculated as:

```
SUV = Tracer uptake (MBq/mL) / [Administered activity (MBq) / (Patient weight (kg) × 1000)]
```

Pre-treatment SUVmax at the primary site and nodes is an independent predictor of prognosis in H&N cancer.

### CT Component of PET-CT

The CT in PET-CT serves two functions:

| Function | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Anatomical localization | Precise location of radiotracer activity for accurate pathology identification |
| Attenuation correction | Corrects for signal reduction as photons travel through tissue |

- CT is usually performed at **lower radiation dose** and **without IV contrast** (contrast may cause PET artefacts)
- Modern systems use continuous bed motion acquisition, reducing artefacts
- PET-CT has replaced standalone PET and is more accurate diagnostically

## Part B — Applications of FDG PET-CT in H&N SCC

### 1. Occult Primary Tumours (CUP)

FDG PET-CT is a **key modality** in evaluation of the unknown primary:

- Should be included **prior to EUA** — universal agreement
- Meta-analysis (11 studies, 433 patients): overall primary detection rate **37%**, sensitivity and specificity both **84%**
- FDG PET-CT detects **31.4% more primary sites** not diagnosed at usual assessment (vs 24.5% for PET alone)
- Sensitivity and specificity up to **87% and 92%** respectively in identifying primary HNSCC
- NICE recommends FDG PET-CT as first investigation to detect primary site in CUP
- A negative PET-CT does not preclude panendoscopy and biopsies

### 2. Staging of Primary Disease

| Application | Role |
|-------------|------|
| Primary tumour | Limited role; can delineate submucosal extension (post-cricoid, tracheal) |
| Neck nodes (N+) | Limited — cannot exclude subclinical disease; reactive nodes may be FDG avid |
| Equivocal nodes | Useful for bilateral/contralateral nodal disease assessment |
| Distant metastases | **Major role** — pooled sensitivity 0.83, specificity 0.96 |
| Bone metastases | Superior to bone scan — pooled sensitivity 0.85 vs 0.55 |
| NPC staging | Highly recommended for bulky nodal disease to detect distant metastases |

NICE recommends PET-CT for **T4 nasopharyngeal/hypopharyngeal cancer** and **advanced neck disease (N3)**.

### 3. Post-Treatment Assessment

- **High NPV (>90%)** at ≥8 weeks post-CRT for residual disease assessment
- Prospective study (564 patients): PET-CT surveillance resulted in **fewer [[Neck Dissection|neck dissection]]s** (54 vs 221) with fewer complications (22 vs 85)
- PET-CT surveillance saves **£1,415 per person** with 0.07 QALY gain
- Two consecutive negative PET-CT within 6 months → **NPV 98%**
- More accurate than contrast-enhanced CT in distinguishing recurrence from post-treatment sequelae (accuracy 79% vs 61% for CT)

### 4. Recurrent Disease Detection

- Pooled sensitivity and specificity: **0.92 and 0.87** respectively
- Superior to CT and MR for distinguishing active disease from treatment sequelae
- Sensitivity 96%, NPV 99% in one prospective study of 116 patients
- Detects **19% subclinical recurrences** not found by conventional follow-up

### 5. Synchronous/Metachronous Malignancies

- Cumulative incidence of second malignancy following H&N cancer: up to **18%**
- FDG PET-CT detection rate of unexpected second primary at staging: **2–3%**
- Common sites: lung, colorectal, thyroid, upper GI tract

### 6. [[Radiotherapy Principles Fractionation Advances|Radiotherapy]] Planning

FDG PET-CT is increasingly used for RT target volume delineation — reduces inter-observer variability in GTV delineation, reduces GTV size, and identifies tumour that would otherwise be missed.

## Part C — PET-CT in Non-SCC Head & Neck Tumours

| Tumour Type | PET-CT Role |
|-------------|-------------|
| Differentiated thyroid cancer | "Flip-flop phenomenon": FDG-PET positive when 131I scan negative (dedifferentiation); sensitivity 95%, specificity 81% |
| Medullary thyroid cancer | Lesion detection probability 68% (highest among all modalities) |
| Anaplastic thyroid cancer | FDG avid; useful for staging |
| Thyroid nodules | Limited — both benign and malignant equally FDG avid |
| Salivary gland tumours | Cannot distinguish benign from malignant (both FDG avid) |
| Lymphoma | Effective except low-grade NHL and MALT lymphoma |
| Paraganglioma | FDG avid; 18F-DOPA PET useful for screening |
| Melanoma | FDG avid |
| NPC (recurrent) | PET-CT detects loco-regional and distant failures; recommended with rising EBV DNA titre |
| Parathyroid adenoma | PET-CT not recommended for PHPT localization (inferior to Sestamibi + US) |

## Part D — Limitations and Pitfalls of PET-CT

**False Positives:**
- Granulomatous disease (sarcoid, sarcoid-like reaction, TB)
- Post-treatment inflammation and sequelae
- Brown adipose tissue FDG uptake
- Dental amalgam artefacts (anterior 2/3 of mouth)
- Normal Waldeyer's ring lymphoid tissue
- Benign thyroid and salivary tumours

**False Negatives:**
- Mucinous carcinomas (gastric)
- Highly differentiated neuroendocrine carcinoma
- Adenocarcinoma within a scar
- Sub-centimetre lesions (<1 cm) — detection influenced by both size and FDG avidity
- Low-grade NHL and MALT lymphoma
- Muco-epidermoid and adenoid cystic carcinoma post-treatment

**Other Limitations:**
- Cannot exclude subclinical nodal disease (does not replace elective neck treatment)
- Radiation exposure equivalent to two whole-body diagnostic CTs
- Cost considerations limit routine surveillance use
- No universal consensus on defining "positive" vs "negative" vs "equivocal"
- HPV-positive nodal disease takes longer to involute — can cause false assessment at 12 weeks

## Part E — Modified Barium Swallow (MBS)

Modified barium swallow (videofluoroscopy) is a radiological investigation of swallowing function, distinct from PET imaging but frequently asked alongside it:

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Principle | Real-time fluoroscopic imaging during ingestion of barium-coated boluses of varying consistency |
| Purpose | Evaluates all phases of swallowing — oral preparatory, oral, pharyngeal, oesophageal |
| Key findings | Aspiration, penetration, residue, cricopharyngeal dysfunction, strictures |
| Penetration-Aspiration Scale | 8-point scale grading airway invasion (1 = no penetration, 8 = silent aspiration) |
| Advantages | Dynamic real-time assessment; evaluates all phases; can test compensatory strategies |
| Limitations | Radiation exposure; non-physiological bolus (barium); does not assess sensory function |
| Compared to FEES | MBS better for oral and oesophageal phases; FEES better for pharyngeal phase and secretion assessment |

## Part F — Nuclear Medicine Scans in Head & Neck

### Overview of Nuclear Medicine Scans

| Scan | Radiotracer | Indication in H&N |
|------|-------------|-------------------|
| FDG PET-CT | 18F-FDG | H&N SCC staging, CUP, recurrence, RT planning |
| Sestamibi (MIBI) | 99mTc-sestamibi | Parathyroid adenoma localization |
| MIBG | 123I-MIBG | Paraganglioma, neuroblastoma metastases |
| Octreotide scan | 111In-pentetreotide | Paraganglioma at multiple locations |
| 18F-DOPA PET | 18F-DOPA | Paraganglioma screening |
| Thyroid scan | 99mTc pertechnetate / 123I | Thyroid nodule functional assessment |
| RAI scan | 131I | Post-thyroidectomy remnant ablation, DTC metastases |
| Bone scan | 99mTc-MDP | Skeletal metastases, Paget's disease |
| Gallium scan | 67Ga | Necrotizing otitis externa follow-up |
| Milk scan | 99mTc | Gastro-oesophageal reflux (paediatric) |
| Salivary scintigraphy | 99mTc pertechnetate | Salivary gland function assessment |

### Sestamibi Scan — Parathyroid Adenoma

**Principle:**
99mTc-sestamibi diffuses passively through cell membranes and accumulates almost exclusively in **mitochondria**. Parathyroid adenomas have abundant oxyphil cells with high mitochondrial content, causing excessive and prolonged radiotracer retention compared to thyroid tissue.

**Technique:**
1. Patient positioned as on operating table (sandbag under shoulders)
2. Dual-phase planar images: **10 minutes** and **2 hours** after injection
3. Focus of retained radiotracer at 2 hours indicates hyperfunctioning parathyroid gland
4. Can be used for radioguided intraoperative surgery

**Sensitivity and Accuracy:**

| Modality | Sensitivity | Specificity/PPV |
|----------|-------------|-----------------|
| Sestamibi scintigraphy | 76–79% | PPV 91% |
| SPECT-MIBI | 92–98% | Improved over planar |
| SPECT-CT fusion | 88% exact localization | Better than CT or SPECT alone |
| Ultrasound | 76.1% (pooled) | — |

**Factors Influencing Sestamibi Imaging:**

| Factor | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| High calcium (>11.3 mg/dL) | >95% positive scan |
| High PTH (>200 pg/mL) | Nearly 100% sensitivity |
| Vitamin D deficiency | More positive scans |
| Small adenomas (<600 mg) | Likely negative |
| Low oxyphil cells (<20%) | Likely negative |
| Multigland disease | Major shortcoming — not identified |
| Thyroid nodules | Sensitivity drops (92% → 53%) |

**Advanced Techniques:**
- **SPECT-CT fusion** — combines functional (gamma camera) + anatomical (CT) data; exact position in 88% of cases vs 65% CT alone vs 55% SPECT alone
- **Dual isotope subtraction** — sestamibi + thyroid-specific tracer (123I or 99mTc pertechnetate); computer subtraction reveals parathyroid area
- **4D-CT** — rapid contrast uptake and washout characteristic of parathyroid adenomas; triple-phase 1.5mm axial scans at 30, 60, 90 seconds post-contrast

### Paraganglioma Imaging

| Scan | Application |
|------|-------------|
| 123I-MIBG | Demonstrates catecholamine activity in paragangliomas |
| Octreotide scintigraphy | Detects paragangliomas at multiple locations (somatostatin receptor avidity) |
| 18F-DOPA PET | Screening for multicentric paragangliomas (SDH gene mutations) |
| 18FDG-PET | Paragangliomas are FDG avid |
| MRI (head to pelvis) | Preferred for ongoing screening — no radiation exposure; achieves same result as PET |

Patients with jugular foramen paraganglioma should be screened for other paragangliomas, particularly with SDH gene mutations (typically **SDHB or SDHD**).

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. PET identifies pathology based on altered tissue metabolism using FDG, which exploits the Warburg effect of increased glucose avidity in cancer cells
> 2. Annihilation reaction produces two 511 keV gamma-ray photons at 180° detected by coincidence detectors; SUV provides semi-quantitative uptake measurement
> 3. CT component of PET-CT provides anatomical localization and attenuation correction; usually performed at low dose without IV contrast
> 4. In CUP, FDG PET-CT detects 31.4% more primary sites than conventional assessment with sensitivity and specificity up to 87% and 92%
> 5. For distant metastases detection, PET-CT has pooled sensitivity 0.83 and specificity 0.96 — significantly superior to conventional staging
> 6. Post-CRT assessment at ≥8 weeks provides NPV >90%; two consecutive negative scans within 6 months achieves NPV of 98%
> 7. "Flip-flop phenomenon" in differentiated thyroid cancer — FDG-PET positive when 131I scan negative, indicating dedifferentiation and worse prognosis
> 8. Key limitations include false positives from inflammation, sarcoidosis and brown fat; false negatives with mucinous tumours and sub-cm lesions
> 9. Sestamibi scintigraphy accumulates in mitochondria-rich oxyphil cells of parathyroid adenomas with sensitivity 76–79%; SPECT-CT fusion localizes exact position in 88%
> 10. Factors reducing Sestamibi sensitivity include small adenoma size (<600 mg), multigland disease, concurrent thyroid nodules and low oxyphil cell content


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - **PET-CT surveillance post-CRT saves £1,415 per person** and reduces unnecessary neck dissections from 221 to 54 (PET-directed approach)
> - **NICE recommends PET-CT for CUP as first investigation**, T4 NPC/hypopharyngeal, and N3 disease — not routinely for all H&N cancers
> - **A negative PET-CT does not preclude panendoscopy** — always proceed to EUA and guided biopsies in CUP
> - **For parathyroid re-exploration**, SPECT-CT and 4D-CT are critical — standard Sestamibi is poor for multigland disease
> - **Paraganglioma screening** in SDH mutation carriers: MRI head-to-pelvis preferred over PET (no radiation); 123I-MIBG and octreotide scan complement
> - **MBS and FEES are complementary** — MBS for oral/oesophageal phases, FEES for pharyngeal phase and secretion management
