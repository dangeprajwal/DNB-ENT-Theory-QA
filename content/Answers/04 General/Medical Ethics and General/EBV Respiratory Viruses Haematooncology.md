# EBV in ENT, Respiratory Viruses and ENT Manifestations of Haemato-oncological Disorders

## INTRODUCTION

Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) is a gamma-herpesvirus with diverse manifestations in ENT practice, ranging from the common infectious mononucleosis to [[Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma|nasopharyngeal carcinoma]] and lymphoma. Respiratory viruses cause the majority of upper respiratory tract infections encountered in otolaryngological practice, including acute otitis media, pharyngitis and croup. Haemato-oncological disorders have numerous ENT manifestations, ranging from [[Epistaxis Management|epistaxis]] and oral ulceration in thrombocytopenia and leukaemia to cervical lymphadenopathy in lymphoma and extra-nodal lymphoid tumours of the head and neck.

## PART A: EBV INFECTIONS IN ENT AND HEAD AND NECK

### Virology

EBV (Human Herpesvirus-4, HHV-4) is a double-stranded DNA virus of the gammaherpesvirus family. It infects B-lymphocytes and epithelial cells, establishing lifelong latency. Over 90% of adults worldwide are EBV-seropositive. The virus is transmitted through saliva (hence "kissing disease").

### ENT Manifestations of EBV

**1. Infectious Mononucleosis (Glandular Fever)**
- The most common clinical EBV manifestation in ENT
- Occurs mainly in adolescents and young adults
- Spread by close contact through saliva
- **Clinical features:** Fever, fatigue, malaise, exudative tonsillitis (often extensive false membranes), bilateral cervical lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly
- Haematological derangement: atypical lymphocytes on blood film (>10%), elevated liver enzymes
- **ENT complications:** [[Peritonsillar Abscess|Peritonsillar abscess]], airway obstruction from tonsillar hypertrophy, splenic rupture (avoid contact sports for 6 weeks)
- **Diagnosis:** Monospot test (heterophile antibody); EBV-specific IgM/IgG serology
- **Management:** Supportive; avoid amoxicillin/ampicillin (causes characteristic maculopapular rash in 90-100%); corticosteroids for severe tonsillar hypertrophy with airway compromise

**2. Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma (NPC)**
- Almost all NPC cells contain EBV — strong and consistent association
- EBV is ubiquitously found in non-keratinizing NPC (WHO Types II and III)
- EBER (EBV-encoded RNA) immunostaining detects EBV in cancer cells — used for definitive diagnosis
- EBV antibody titres (VCA IgA, EA IgA) correlate with disease stage
- **Plasma EBV DNA:** Highly sensitive (96%) and specific (93%) biomarker for NPC detection, monitoring and surveillance
- Falling titre reflects tumour response; rising titre indicates recurrence
- Nasopharyngeal brushing for EBV DNA has sensitivity of 98.9% and specificity of 99.3%

**3. Lymphoma**
- **Burkitt lymphoma:** High percentage of tumours positive for EBV, particularly in endemic (African) form — presents in head and neck as jaw mass in children
- **Hodgkin lymphoma:** EBV found in Reed-Sternberg cells in approximately 40% of cases — presents with painless cervical lymphadenopathy
- **T-NK cell lymphoma (Extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma, nasal type):** Strongly EBV-associated; presents as destructive midline nasal mass (formerly midline lethal granuloma)
- **Post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder (PTLD):** EBV-driven B-cell proliferation in immunosuppressed transplant recipients; presents with tonsillar/adenoid hypertrophy, cervical lymphadenopathy

**4. Oral Hairy Leukoplakia**
- White, corrugated, non-removable plaques on lateral tongue borders
- Almost exclusively in HIV-positive patients
- Caused by EBV replication in oral epithelium
- Marker of immunosuppression; often resolves with HAART

**5. [[Facial Nerve|Facial Nerve Palsy]]**
- EBV seroconversion associated with facial nerve palsy
- 40% of EBV-associated facial nerve palsy cases are bilateral — significantly higher bilateral rate than Bell's palsy
- Viral analysis of facial nerve specimens shows EBV alongside HSV-1 and VZV

**6. [[Sudden SNHL|Sudden Sensorineural Hearing Loss]]**
- EBV seroconversion found in approximately 6% of patients with sudden SNHL
- Postulated mechanism: viral cochleitis or vasculitis of cochlear vessels

**7. Salivary Gland Disease**
- Lymphoepithelial cyst of parotid (in HIV/AIDS)
- Warthin's tumour — EBV DNA detected in tumour tissue

## PART B: VIRUSES CAUSING RESPIRATORY ILLNESS

### Classification of Respiratory Viruses

| Virus | Family | Genome | Key ENT Manifestation |
|-------|--------|--------|----------------------|
| **Rhinovirus** | Picornaviridae | RNA | Common cold (most frequent cause); linked to AOM via Eustachian tube dysfunction |
| **Influenza A and B** | Orthomyxoviridae | RNA | Upper and lower respiratory infection; associated with secondary AOM; oseltamivir reduces AOM incidence |
| **Parainfluenza 1-4** | Paramyxoviridae | RNA | Croup (PIV-1 most common cause); laryngotracheobronchitis |
| **RSV** | Paramyxoviridae | RNA | Bronchiolitis in infants; 62% of children with RSV bronchiolitis develop AOM |
| **Adenovirus** | Adenoviridae | DNA | Pharyngoconjunctival fever; exudative tonsillitis; adenoiditis |
| **Coronavirus** | Coronaviridae | RNA | Common cold; SARS-CoV-2 (anosmia, dysgeusia) |
| **EBV** | Herpesviridae | DNA | Infectious mononucleosis; NPC; lymphoma |
| **Coxsackievirus A** | Picornaviridae | RNA | Herpangina; hand-foot-mouth disease |
| **HSV-1** | Herpesviridae | DNA | Gingivostomatitis; laryngeal herpetic lesions |
| **CMV** | Herpesviridae | DNA | SNHL (congenital); pharyngitis in immunocompromised |
| **SARS-CoV-2** | Coronaviridae | RNA | Anosmia/hyposmia, dysgeusia; pharyngitis; [[Subglottic Stenosis|subglottic stenosis]] from prolonged intubation |

### ENT Relevance of Respiratory Viruses

**Acute otitis media (AOM):**
- Respiratory viruses associated with AOM in decreasing frequency: RSV > influenza A > parainfluenza > rhinovirus > adenovirus
- 62% of children with RSV bronchiolitis develop AOM; bacteria isolated from middle ear in all cases
- Viral URTI causes Eustachian tube dysfunction, negative middle ear pressure, and secondary bacterial infection
- Oseltamivir (neuraminidase inhibitor) reduces AOM incidence during influenza epidemics
- Palivizumab: monoclonal antibody prophylaxis for severe RSV disease in high-risk infants

**Acute pharyngitis/tonsillitis:**
- Viral pharyngitis accounts for >70% of acute sore throats
- Key viral causes: rhinovirus, adenovirus (conjunctivitis common), EBV (exudative with membranes), coxsackievirus (herpangina, vesicles on soft palate), HSV (gingivostomatitis)

**Croup (acute laryngotracheobronchitis):**
- Parainfluenza virus type 1 is the most common cause
- Characteristic barking cough, inspiratory stridor, hoarse voice
- Age peak: 6 months to 3 years

## PART C: ENT MANIFESTATIONS OF HAEMATO-ONCOLOGICAL DISORDERS

### Leukaemia — ENT Manifestations

| Manifestation | Mechanism |
|---------------|-----------|
| Epistaxis | Thrombocytopenia (reduced platelet production) |
| Oral ulceration/mucositis | Neutropenia + mucosal infiltration by leukaemic cells |
| Gingival hyperplasia | Leukaemic cell infiltration (especially AML-M4/M5) |
| Cervical lymphadenopathy | Leukaemic lymph node infiltration |
| Tonsillar enlargement | Leukaemic infiltration |
| Facial nerve palsy | Rare presentation of acute leukaemia; leukaemic infiltration of temporal bone |
| Hearing loss | Leukaemic infiltration of cochlea; ototoxicity from chemotherapy |
| Lacrimal gland enlargement | Leukaemic infiltration |

### Lymphoma — ENT Manifestations

**Hodgkin lymphoma:**
- Painless cervical lymphadenopathy (most common presentation)
- Waldeyer's ring involvement is rare (unlike NHL)
- B-symptoms: fever, night sweats, weight loss >10%

**Non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL):**
- Cervical lymphadenopathy (most common)
- Extra-nodal disease in 25% of cases — head and neck sites include:
  - Waldeyer's ring (tonsil most common extra-nodal site)
  - Nasal cavity/paranasal sinuses (NK/T-cell lymphoma)
  - Salivary glands
  - Thyroid
  - Larynx (extremely rare)
  - Orbit
- Post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder (PTLD): EBV-driven; tonsillar/adenoid hypertrophy in immunosuppressed patients
- **Differential from TB:** Tissue diagnosis required; histopathological examination distinguishes lymphoma from granulomatous disease

### Multiple Myeloma (Myelomatosis)

- Jaw lesions (mandible/maxilla) — lytic bone lesions causing pathological fracture
- Rare primary presentation in head and neck
- Extramedullary plasmacytoma: soft tissue plasma cell tumour, commonly in nasal cavity, nasopharynx, paranasal sinuses

### Thrombocytopenia and Bleeding Disorders

- **Epistaxis** — most common ENT presentation of bleeding diathesis
- Causes: ITP, leukaemia, chemotherapy-induced, aplastic anaemia, DIC
- Glanzmann thrombasthenia — platelet function disorder causing recurrent epistaxis
- Assessment: FBC, clotting screen, coagulation factors; specialist haematological investigation for unusual bleeding patterns
- Persistent or asymmetric epistaxis in a child warrants haematological investigation

### Chemotherapy-Related ENT Complications

- **Mucositis:** Oral and pharyngeal ulceration from myelosuppressive chemotherapy
- **Ototoxicity:** Cisplatin (most ototoxic), carboplatin — bilateral high-frequency SNHL
- **Immunosuppression:** Oropharyngeal candidiasis, HSV reactivation
- **Neutropenic sepsis:** [[Deep Neck Space Infections|Deep neck space infection]]s, peritonsillar abscess in neutropenic patients
- **Myelosuppression:** Increased bleeding risk from any ENT procedure

### HIV — ENT Manifestations (Brief Overview)

As covered in existing files, key manifestations include OPC, oral hairy leukoplakia, Kaposi's sarcoma, cervical lymphadenopathy, NHL, parotid lymphoepithelial cysts, and viral-associated conditions (CMV, EBV, HSV).

---

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. EBV is ubiquitously found in non-keratinizing NPC — EBER immunostaining is used for definitive diagnosis; plasma EBV DNA (sensitivity 96%, specificity 93%) is the key biomarker for detection and surveillance
> 2. Infectious mononucleosis presents with exudative tonsillitis, bilateral cervical lymphadenopathy and splenomegaly — amoxicillin must be avoided (causes maculopapular rash in 90-100%)
> 3. EBV-associated facial nerve palsy has a 40% bilateral rate — significantly higher than Bell's palsy; EBV should be considered in bilateral facial palsy
> 4. Burkitt lymphoma, NK/T-cell lymphoma (nasal type), Hodgkin lymphoma and PTLD are all EBV-associated H&N malignancies — tissue diagnosis is essential to differentiate
> 5. Respiratory viruses associated with AOM in decreasing frequency: RSV > influenza A > parainfluenza > rhinovirus > adenovirus — 62% of children with RSV bronchiolitis develop AOM
> 6. Parainfluenza virus type 1 is the most common cause of croup; rhinovirus is the most common cause of the common cold
> 7. Epistaxis is the most common ENT manifestation of thrombocytopenia and leukaemia — persistent or asymmetric epistaxis in a child warrants haematological investigation
> 8. Non-Hodgkin lymphoma: 25% present with extra-nodal disease; tonsil is the most common extra-nodal H&N site; NK/T-cell lymphoma (nasal type) is strongly EBV-associated
> 9. Gingival hyperplasia is characteristic of acute monocytic/myelomonocytic leukaemia (AML-M4/M5) — may be the presenting feature; biopsy is diagnostic
> 10. Post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder (PTLD) should be suspected when an immunosuppressed transplant patient presents with tonsillar hypertrophy, adenoid enlargement or cervical lymphadenopathy — EBV-driven; management includes reduction of immunosuppression


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - When EBV is detected by EBER immunostaining in a nasopharyngeal biopsy, the differential includes NPC, NK/T-cell lymphoma and Hodgkin lymphoma — EBV presence alone does not confirm NPC; careful histological assessment is essential
> - Oral hairy leukoplakia (EBV-related) is almost pathognomonic of HIV infection — its presence in a patient not known to be HIV-positive should prompt HIV testing
> - The combination of fever, exudative tonsillitis, generalized lymphadenopathy and hepatosplenomegaly in an adolescent should prompt a Monospot test before prescribing antibiotics — ampicillin/amoxicillin rash in EBV may be mistaken for penicillin allergy, which has lifelong implications
> - Extramedullary plasmacytoma of the nasal cavity or nasopharynx should be distinguished from NHL — plasma cell markers on immunohistochemistry are diagnostic; radiation therapy is the primary treatment (unlike NHL which requires chemotherapy)
> - RSV bronchiolitis in infants causes AOM in 62% of cases via Eustachian tube dysfunction — treatment of the viral illness with supportive measures is more important than antibiotics for the associated AOM
> - Any patient with persistent unexplained cervical lymphadenopathy lasting >6 weeks should have tissue biopsy to exclude lymphoma — FNAC may be insufficient; excisional biopsy provides better tissue architecture for subtyping
