## INTRODUCTION

Hyperbaric oxygen therapy (HBOT) is the intermittent breathing of 100% oxygen at pressures greater than 1 atmosphere absolute (ATA) in a specially designed chamber. In ENT practice, its principal applications are in the management of osteoradionecrosis, [[Sudden SNHL|sudden sensorineural hearing loss]] and necrotizing otitis externa. Hypotensive anaesthesia (controlled hypotension) is a deliberate reduction in arterial blood pressure to minimize intraoperative bleeding and improve surgical field visibility, with particular applications in ear surgery and FESS. Day care surgery has become an increasingly important aspect of ENT practice, with [[Tonsillectomy|tonsillectomy]], grommets, septoplasty and other procedures performed as ambulatory procedures in appropriate patients.

## PART A: HYPERBARIC OXYGEN THERAPY (HBOT)

### Definition

HBOT involves the intermittent breathing of 100% oxygen at pressures exceeding 1 atmosphere absolute (ATA), typically 2.0-2.8 ATA, within a pressurized chamber. This results in a dramatic increase in dissolved oxygen in plasma, independent of haemoglobin binding.

### Mechanism of Action

**Oxygen physiology at elevated pressure:**
- At 1 ATA, haemoglobin is 97% saturated; dissolved oxygen in plasma is minimal (~0.3 mL/dL)
- At 3 ATA breathing 100% O2, dissolved plasma oxygen increases to ~6 mL/dL — sufficient to support tissue oxygen requirements independently of haemoglobin
- Tissue oxygen tension increases from normal (~40 mmHg) to >1000 mmHg

**Therapeutic mechanisms:**
1. **Enhanced oxygenation** of ischaemic/hypoxic tissues — overcomes tissue oxygen deficit in irradiated, infected or ischaemic tissues
2. **Neovascularization (angiogenesis)** — stimulates new blood vessel formation in irradiated tissues; fibroblast proliferation and collagen synthesis require oxygen
3. **Bactericidal effect** — direct toxicity to anaerobic organisms (Clostridium, Bacteroides); enhanced leukocyte killing of aerobic bacteria
4. **Anti-oedema effect** — vasoconstriction reduces oedema without reducing oxygen delivery (net tissue oxygenation increases despite vasoconstriction)
5. **Enhanced antibiotic efficacy** — aminoglycosides, fluoroquinolones, sulfonamides require oxygen for optimal bactericidal activity
6. **Reduction of reperfusion injury** — inhibits neutrophil adhesion to endothelium

### Indications in ENT Practice

| Indication | Mechanism/Evidence |
|-----------|-------------------|
| **Osteoradionecrosis (ORN)** | Stimulates angiogenesis in irradiated bone; adjunct to debridement; initial treatment for ORN of mandible and skull base |
| **Pre-implant irradiated jaw** | To improve vascularity before dental implant placement; evidence insufficient for routine use |
| **Sudden SNHL (idiopathic)** | Enhanced cochlear oxygenation; used when steroid therapy fails; some evidence of benefit |
| **Malignant otitis externa** | Adjunct to antibiotics + debridement in refractory cases |
| **Necrotizing fasciitis** | Adjunct therapy — bactericidal against anaerobes; enhances antibiotic efficacy |
| **Laryngeal radionecrosis** | Promotes healing of radiation-damaged laryngeal tissues |
| **[[Wound Healing Phases Types Factors|Wound healing]] post-RT** | Enhances tissue repair in irradiated surgical beds |

**Note from Scott-Brown's:** For osteoradionecrosis, initial treatment including local debridement and hyperbaric oxygen is helpful, but some patients may need definitive resection of involved bone and reconstruction with a free osteocutaneous flap. For dental implants in irradiated jaws, there is currently inadequate information to support routine HBOT use.

### HBOT Protocol

- Typically 20-30 sessions (Marx protocol: 20 sessions pre-operative, 10 post-operative for ORN)
- Each session: 90-120 minutes at 2.0-2.4 ATA
- Monoplace (single patient) or multiplace (multiple patients) chambers
- Sessions usually daily, 5 days per week

### Complications of HBOT

**Barotrauma (most common):**
- Middle ear barotrauma (otic barotrauma) — most frequent complication
- Sinus barotrauma
- Pulmonary barotrauma (rare — risk of pneumothorax)
- Dental barotrauma

**Oxygen toxicity:**
- CNS toxicity (Paul Bert effect): seizures — risk increases with pressure and duration
- Pulmonary toxicity (Lorrain Smith effect): pneumonitis with prolonged exposure
- Retinal toxicity: progressive myopia (reversible)

**Other:**
- Claustrophobia
- Fire risk (O2-enriched environment)
- Decompression sickness (extremely rare with standard protocols)

### Contraindications

- Untreated pneumothorax (absolute)
- Concomitant bleomycin or doxorubicin (pulmonary toxicity potentiated)
- Uncontrolled seizure disorder
- Severe COPD with CO2 retention
- Pregnancy (relative)
- Inability to equalize middle ear pressure (relative — may require myringotomy)

## PART B: HYPOTENSIVE ANAESTHESIA (CONTROLLED HYPOTENSION)

### Definition

Hypotensive anaesthesia (deliberate or controlled hypotension) is the intentional reduction of mean arterial blood pressure to 50-65 mmHg (or systolic BP to 80-90 mmHg) during general anaesthesia to minimize intraoperative bleeding and improve visualization of the surgical field.

### Mechanism and Methods

**Principles:**
A reduction in venous tone and backpressure reduces both capillary and venous bleeding. This can be achieved by:

| Method | Mechanism | Agent |
|--------|-----------|-------|
| **Short-acting opioids** | Greater cardiovascular stability; facilitates moderate hypotension | Remifentanil (drug of choice) |
| **Intravenous nitrates** | Venodilation reduces venous pressure and bleeding | GTN infusion |
| **Sodium nitroprusside** | Direct arterial and venous vasodilation | Nitroprusside infusion |
| **Beta-blockers** | Reduce heart rate and cardiac output | Esmolol, labetalol |
| **Patient positioning** | Reduces hydrostatic venous pressure | 25° head-up tilt (reverse Trendelenburg) |
| **Volatile agents** | Dose-dependent vasodilation | Isoflurane, sevoflurane |
| **Calcium channel blockers** | Arteriolar vasodilation | Nicardipine |
| **TIVA** | Stable cardiovascular profile | Propofol + remifentanil |

**Remifentanil** allows greater control of cardiovascular stability and facilitates moderate hypotension — it is compared favourably with nitroprusside and esmolol during [[Tympanoplasty Myringoplasty|tympanoplasty]] (Degoute et al., 2001).

### Role in ENT Practice

**Primary applications:**
1. **Middle ear/mastoid surgery** — reduces bleeding in the confined mastoid cavity; critical for visualization of facial nerve, ossicular chain and tegmen
2. **[[FESS Steps Complications|Functional endoscopic sinus surgery]] (FESS)** — reduces mucosal bleeding; improves endoscopic visualization; critical for safety near orbit and skull base
3. **Rhinoplasty/septoplasty** — reduces intraoperative blood loss
4. **Major head and neck surgery** — reduces total blood loss; may reduce transfusion requirements

### Complications

- **Cerebral ischaemia** — if MAP falls below autoregulatory range (<50 mmHg)
- **Myocardial ischaemia** — reduced coronary perfusion pressure
- **Renal failure** — reduced renal perfusion (autoregulation impaired <60 mmHg MAP)
- **Optic nerve ischaemia** — rare but devastating; related to prolonged hypotension + anaemia
- **Reactive hypertension** — on discontinuation of hypotensive agents
- **Cyanide toxicity** — with prolonged nitroprusside use

### Contraindications

Hypotension should be limited in patients with:
- Uncontrolled hypertension
- Cerebrovascular disease
- Ischaemic heart disease
- Renal impairment
- Anaemia

## PART C: DAY CARE SURGERY IN ENT

### Definition

Day care (ambulatory) surgery refers to procedures where the patient is admitted and discharged on the same day, without an overnight hospital stay.

### Principles of Safe Day Care Surgery

**1. Patient selection criteria:**
- ASA I or II (healthy or mild systemic disease)
- Age: generally >6 months to <70 years (flexible with assessment)
- BMI <40 (relative; depends on procedure)
- Controlled comorbidities (hypertension, diabetes, asthma)
- Reliable home supervision for 24 hours post-discharge
- Home within 1 hour travel of the hospital
- Responsible adult to accompany patient home

**2. Suitable ENT procedures for day care:**
- Myringotomy and grommet insertion
- Adenoidectomy
- Tonsillectomy (increasingly common; controversial)
- Septoplasty / septorhinoplasty (selected cases)
- Functional endoscopic sinus surgery (selected)
- Microlaryngoscopy and biopsy
- Excision of skin/subcutaneous lesions
- Pinnaplasty
- Nasal polypectomy
- Foreign body removal (ear, nose)

**3. Anaesthetic considerations:**
- Short-acting agents (propofol, remifentanil, desflurane)
- Effective PONV prophylaxis (ondansetron, dexamethasone)
- Multimodal analgesia (paracetamol, NSAIDs, local anaesthesia)
- Avoid long-acting opioids
- LMA preferred over ETT where appropriate

**4. Discharge criteria:**
- Stable vital signs for minimum 1 hour
- Tolerating oral fluids
- Adequate pain control with oral analgesia
- No active bleeding
- Able to mobilize independently
- Voiding normally
- Written discharge instructions provided
- Emergency contact number given
- Responsible adult present

**5. Safety infrastructure:**
- Dedicated day surgery unit with recovery facilities
- Protocols for managing complications (bleeding, airway compromise)
- Clear escalation pathways and transfer protocols
- Post-operative telephone follow-up within 24 hours
- Patient information leaflets with warning signs

### Advantages of Day Care Surgery

- Reduced hospital-acquired infections
- Cost-effective (reduced bed occupancy)
- Reduced waiting lists
- Less disruption to patient's life
- Reduced DVT/PE risk
- High patient satisfaction

### Contraindications/Exclusions

- ASA III-IV with uncontrolled disease
- Anticipated difficult airway
- High risk of post-operative haemorrhage (e.g. bleeding disorders)
- Inadequate social support at home
- Living alone with no responsible adult
- OSA (moderate-severe) — risk of post-anaesthetic airway compromise

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. HBOT involves breathing 100% O2 at >1 ATA; at 3 ATA dissolved plasma O2 increases to ~6 mL/dL — sufficient for tissue requirements independent of haemoglobin
> 2. Key ENT indications for HBOT: osteoradionecrosis (adjunct to debridement), sudden SNHL (when steroids fail), malignant otitis externa, necrotizing fasciitis — evidence for dental implants in irradiated jaws is insufficient
> 3. Middle ear barotrauma is the most common complication of HBOT; CNS oxygen toxicity (seizures) is the most serious — contraindicated with untreated pneumothorax and concurrent bleomycin
> 4. Hypotensive anaesthesia aims for MAP 50-65 mmHg; remifentanil is the agent of choice as it allows greater cardiovascular stability and control of hypotension
> 5. 25° head-up tilt reduces hydrostatic venous pressure and is a simple adjunct to pharmacological hypotension in ear and sinus surgery
> 6. Hypotensive anaesthesia is contraindicated in patients with uncontrolled hypertension, cerebrovascular disease and ischaemic heart disease
> 7. Primary ENT applications of controlled hypotension: middle ear/mastoid surgery, FESS, septoplasty — where even small amounts of bleeding significantly impair surgical visualization
> 8. Day care ENT procedures include grommet insertion, adenoidectomy, tonsillectomy (selected), septoplasty, microlaryngoscopy — patient selection (ASA I-II, responsible adult, proximity to hospital) is critical
> 9. Discharge criteria for day care surgery: stable vitals for 1 hour, tolerating oral fluids, adequate pain control, no active bleeding, responsible adult present
> 10. Short-acting anaesthetic agents (propofol, remifentanil, desflurane), effective PONV prophylaxis, and multimodal analgesia are the cornerstones of day care anaesthesia in ENT


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - HBOT for osteoradionecrosis follows the Marx protocol: 20 pre-operative sessions (to promote angiogenesis in irradiated tissue) followed by surgery, then 10 post-operative sessions — sessions are typically 90 minutes at 2.0-2.4 ATA
> - For middle ear surgery, the combination of remifentanil infusion with 25° head-up positioning and normocapnia ventilation provides optimal surgical field conditions — hypotension beyond this simple protocol is unnecessary in most cases
> - The anaesthetist should not implement hypotension during FESS or ear surgery in a patient with cardiovascular disease without careful risk assessment and agreement with the surgeon — failure to respect this principle constitutes poor professional practice
> - Day care tonsillectomy remains controversial in some settings due to the risk of delayed post-tonsillectomy haemorrhage (peak at day 5-7) — if performed, only low-risk patients (adult, no bleeding diathesis, proximity to hospital) should be selected
> - Patients requiring HBOT who cannot equalize middle ear pressure should have prophylactic myringotomy tubes inserted before starting the treatment course to prevent middle ear barotrauma
> - The evidence for HBOT in sudden SNHL is mixed — several RCTs and Cochrane reviews show some benefit when combined with systemic steroids, but it is not universally accepted as standard of care
