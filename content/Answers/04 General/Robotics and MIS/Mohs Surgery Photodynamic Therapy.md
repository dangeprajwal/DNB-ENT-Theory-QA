# Mohs Micrographic Surgery and Photodynamic Therapy for Cutaneous Malignancy

> [!question] Questions
> What is Moh's surgery? Photodynamic therapy for cutaneous malignancy of head and neck.

**Source:** Scott-Brown's Otorhinolaryngology, 8th Edition - Volume 3, Chapter 94 (Non-Melanoma Skin Cancer) and Volume 1, Chapter 50 (Laser Principles in Otolaryngology)

---

## INTRODUCTION

Non-melanoma skin cancers (NMSCs), primarily basal cell carcinoma and squamous cell carcinoma, are the commonest cancers affecting the head and neck. Mohs micrographic surgery (MMS) represents the gold standard surgical technique for high-risk cutaneous malignancies, providing the highest cure rates (98-100%) through complete margin assessment under frozen section control. Photodynamic therapy (PDT) is a non-surgical treatment modality that uses the combination of a photosensitizer drug, light and oxygen to selectively destroy tumour cells. Both techniques have established roles in the management of cutaneous malignancy of the head and neck, with Mohs surgery suited to high-risk lesions and PDT to superficial, low-risk disease and field cancerization.

---

## PART A: MOHS MICROGRAPHIC SURGERY

### Definition

Mohs micrographic surgery is a highly effective and cost-effective surgical technique that allows the assessment of 100% of the tumour margin under frozen sections. Unlike conventional excision (which examines only a sampling of the margin), Mohs surgery provides complete peripheral and deep margin assessment, ensuring no residual tumour remains before wound closure.

### Principle

The technique involves sequential stages of tumour removal with immediate histological examination of 100% of the excision margin until clear margins are achieved:

1. **Initial debulking** of the clinically visible tumour
2. The lesion is then **removed in thin layers**, maintaining tissue orientation
3. Each layer is **separated into blocks and colour-coded** for processing in the cryostat
4. Frozen sections are cut from the **undersurface** of each block, allowing examination of the entire peripheral and deep margin
5. Any area of **incompletely excised** tumour is identified and precisely mapped
6. A **further stage** is taken only from the area shown to be incompletely excised
7. This process is **repeated** until all margins are clear
8. Once adequate clearance is achieved, the defect is **closed using reconstructive surgery**

### Indications

**High-risk basal cell carcinoma (gold standard indication):**
- Poorly defined clinical margins
- Larger than 2 cm diameter
- Located on the central face (nose, periorbital, perioral, ear)
- Morphoeic or infiltrative histological subtype
- Recurrent BCC after previous treatment

**Squamous cell carcinoma:**
- High-risk features (poorly differentiated, perineural invasion)
- Areas where wider margins are technically difficult
- Lesions where excision would cause significant visual or functional impairment
- Nerve involvement (perineural spread)

**Other tumours:**
- Lentigo maligna (melanoma in situ) — Mohs with immunostaining
- Desmoplastic melanoma
- Dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans (DFSP) — offers greater chances of complete removal
- Microcystic adnexal carcinoma
- Merkel cell carcinoma (selected cases)

### Cure Rates

| Tumour Type | MMS Cure Rate | Conventional Excision |
|-------------|--------------|----------------------|
| Primary BCC | 98-100% | ~95% (with 3-4 mm margin) |
| Recurrent BCC | 94-96% | ~80-85% |
| High-risk SCC | Very high | Variable |
| DFSP | >95% | Variable (higher recurrence) |

### Advantages

- Assessment of 100% of the margin (vs <1% with conventional histological sampling)
- Highest cure rate of any treatment modality for NMSC
- Maximum tissue conservation (removes only tumour, preserving normal tissue)
- Cost-effective when reconstruction costs are considered
- Particularly suited to cosmetically and functionally sensitive areas (eyelids, nose, lips, ears)
- Can be performed under local anaesthesia as an outpatient procedure

### Disadvantages

- Requires specialized training (fellowship-trained Mohs surgeons)
- Time-consuming (each stage takes 30-60 minutes for frozen section processing)
- Requires on-site histopathology laboratory with cryostat
- Not widely available in all centres
- Not suitable for deeply infiltrating tumours extending to bone or orbit (may require additional imaging)

## PART B: PHOTODYNAMIC THERAPY — PRINCIPLES

### Definition and Mechanism

Photodynamic therapy (PDT) is a subset of photochemotherapy requiring three components to produce cell death:

1. **Photosensitizer drug** — administered topically or intravenously
2. **Light** of appropriate wavelength — delivered via laser or LED source
3. **Oxygen** — present in tissue

The photosensitizer accumulates within tumour cells and, upon activation by light of appropriate wavelength, reacts with tissue oxygen to form **singlet oxygen** — a highly reactive oxygen species that damages cell membranes and produces cell death through apoptosis and necrosis.

### Selective Tumour Targeting

PDT achieves selective tumour destruction through two mechanisms:

1. **Differential drug uptake:** The photosensitizer is retained in tumour tissue for a longer period than in normal tissue — this improves the therapeutic effect while reducing toxicity to normal tissue
2. **Accurate light application:** Light is delivered precisely to the tumour area, limiting damage to surrounding structures

### Drug-Light Interval

The period between photosensitizer administration and light application (the drug uptake period) varies between 3 and 96 hours, depending on the photosensitizer used.

### Photosensitizers Used in Clinical Practice

| Photosensitizer | Route | Clinical Use |
|----------------|-------|-------------|
| Methyl aminolevulinic acid (MAL) | Topical | Actinic keratosis, superficial BCC |
| 5-Aminolevulinic acid (5-ALA) | Topical | Actinic keratosis, BCC, H&N SCC |
| Porfimer sodium (Photofrin) | Intravenous | Oesophageal cancer, Barrett's |
| m-THPC (temoporfin/Foscan) | Intravenous | H&N squamous carcinoma |
| Dihaematoporphyrin ether | Intravenous | H&N squamous carcinoma |

## PART C: CLINICAL APPLICATIONS OF PDT IN HEAD AND NECK

### Cutaneous Malignancy

**Actinic keratosis (AK):**
- PDT with methyl aminolevulinic acid and red light source is a highly effective treatment with good overall tolerance and patient satisfaction
- Particularly useful for multiple AKs in a field (field cancerization)

**Bowen's disease (SCC in situ):**
- PDT achieves cure rates of around 90%

**Basal cell carcinoma:**
- Used for superficial and nodular BCCs not suitable for surgery
- Cure rates vary depending on photosensitizer: 75-95%
- Severity of local inflammatory reaction (erythema, erosion, scabbing) correlates with higher success rates
- NOT appropriate for morphoeic, infiltrative or high-risk BCC

### Head and Neck Squamous Cell Carcinoma

**Oral cavity and oropharynx:**
- Biel treated 217 patients with early SCC of the head and neck using PDT — 194 (89%) showed complete response, 23 (10.6%) showed partial response
- Best results obtained in oral cavity and oropharynx (11 of 13 patients with complete response)
- Karakullukcu et al.: 170 patients with early oral cavity/oropharyngeal neoplasms — 90% response rate, 70% complete response (cure) rate

**Larynx:**
- Biel treated 25 patients with early SCC of the larynx — complete response achieved despite radiation therapy having previously failed in 17 of the 25 patients

**Nasopharynx:**
- Lofgren et al.: 5 patients with circumscribed NPC — 3 of 5 had no evidence of disease at 4 years

**Oesophagus:**
- Phase II trial (218 patients): PDT vs Nd:YAG laser for palliative oesophageal treatment — equivalent dysphagia improvement but fewer perforations in PDT group (1% vs 7%)
- Overholt: 55 patients with dysplasia/early carcinoma in Barrett's mucosa — ablation achieved in 43 of 55

### Advantages of PDT

- Non-invasive, can be repeated
- Not affected by prior [[Radiotherapy Principles Fractionation Advances|radiotherapy]], chemotherapy or surgery
- Selective tumour destruction (relative sparing of normal tissue)
- Good cosmetic outcomes for superficial lesions
- Treats field cancerization (multiple lesions simultaneously)
- Can be performed as outpatient procedure
- Suitable for patients unfit for surgery

### Limitations and Side Effects

- **Limited depth of tissue penetration** — NOT effective for deeply invasive tumours
- **Skin phototoxicity** — the major side effect; patients must avoid daylight during and after treatment to prevent extensive and painful skin reactions
- Local pain at photosensitizer injection site (IV preparations)
- Local inflammatory reaction: erythema, erosion, scabbing
- Oesophageal stricture reported in 53% of Barrett's PDT patients
- Exact optimal photosensitizer-tumour-treatment protocol still being defined

## PART D: COMPARISON OF TREATMENT MODALITIES FOR CUTANEOUS MALIGNANCY

| Feature | Mohs Surgery | PDT | Conventional Excision | Radiotherapy |
|---------|-------------|-----|----------------------|-------------|
| Best for | High-risk NMSC | Superficial/low-risk NMSC | Standard NMSC | Inoperable lesions |
| Cure rate | 98-100% | 75-95% (varies) | ~95% (adequate margins) | ~90% |
| Margin control | 100% assessed | None | Sampling only | N/A |
| Tissue conservation | Maximum | Maximum | Moderate | N/A |
| Cosmesis | Excellent | Excellent | Good | Variable |
| Repeat treatment | Possible | Easily repeated | Possible | Limited |
| Field treatment | No | Yes | No | Yes |
| Deep tumours | Suitable | NOT suitable | Suitable | Suitable |
| Availability | Specialist centres | Specialist centres | Widely available | Widely available |

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. Mohs micrographic surgery allows assessment of 100% of the tumour margin under frozen sections — cure rates 98-100% for primary BCC, making it the gold standard for high-risk cutaneous malignancy
> 2. The Mohs technique involves sequential stages: debulking, thin-layer removal, colour-coded block processing in cryostat, frozen section examination of entire margin, re-excision only of positive areas until clear
> 3. High-risk BCC indications for MMS: poorly defined margins, >2 cm, central face location, morphoeic/infiltrative histology, recurrent lesion
> 4. PDT requires three components: photosensitizer drug + light of appropriate wavelength + oxygen — singlet oxygen formation causes selective tumour cell death
> 5. Photosensitizer drugs are retained in tumour tissue longer than normal tissue — this provides the selectivity of PDT; drug-light interval ranges from 3 to 96 hours
> 6. PDT for cutaneous malignancy: actinic keratosis (first-line), Bowen's disease (~90% cure), superficial BCC (75-95% cure) — NOT suitable for morphoeic/infiltrative lesions
> 7. PDT for early H&N SCC: 89% complete response rate in 217 patients; best results in oral cavity and oropharynx; effective even after failed radiotherapy in laryngeal SCC
> 8. Skin phototoxicity and limited depth of tissue penetration are the principal shortcomings of PDT — patients must avoid daylight exposure during treatment period
> 9. PDT is not affected by prior radiotherapy, chemotherapy or surgery — making it useful as salvage therapy for recurrent or multiply-treated lesions
> 10. Mohs surgery provides maximum tissue conservation in cosmetically sensitive areas (nose, eyelids, lips, ears) — the defect is reconstructed only after 100% clear margins are confirmed


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - The key advantage of Mohs surgery over conventional excision is the examination of 100% of the margin — conventional histopathology examines less than 1% of the true margin through bread-loaf sectioning, meaning residual tumour can be missed
> - Mohs surgery is particularly valuable for morphoeic/infiltrative BCC where clinical margins are poorly defined — these lesions have subclinical extension that cannot be appreciated visually, making conventional excision with predetermined margins unreliable
> - PDT has a unique role in treating field cancerization (multiple AKs, widespread Bowen's disease) — surgical excision cannot address large areas of damaged skin, whereas PDT treats the entire field simultaneously
> - PDT has been shown to produce complete responses in early laryngeal SCC even after failed radiotherapy — this makes it a potential salvage option, though long-term data are limited
> - The severity of the local inflammatory reaction after PDT (erythema, erosion, scabbing) correlates with treatment success — a poor inflammatory response predicts treatment failure and should prompt consideration of alternative therapy
> - When Mohs surgery is combined with delayed reconstruction, the defect can be temporarily dressed and reconstruction planned optimally — this is preferable to immediate reconstruction if there is uncertainty about margin clearance
