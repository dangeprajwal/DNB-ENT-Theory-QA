## INTRODUCTION

Robotic surgery represents one of the most significant technological advances in otolaryngology, head and neck surgery. The development of transoral robotic surgery (TORS) has transformed the management of oropharyngeal, supraglottic and hypopharyngeal cancers by providing a minimally invasive alternative to open surgical approaches and chemoradiation. The da Vinci Surgical System, currently the most widely used robotic platform, provides three-dimensional visualization, wristed instrumentation with 540 degrees of freedom, tremor elimination and motion scaling — features that overcome the limitations of traditional transoral surgery. Since the first robotic procedure in otolaryngology was performed at the Walter Reed Army Medical Center in 2003, TORS has demonstrated safety, feasibility and oncological equivalence to established approaches, with the potential advantage of improved functional outcomes.

## PART A: PRINCIPLES OF ROBOTIC SURGERY

### Definition

Robotic surgery is defined by the Society of American Gastrointestinal and Endoscopic Surgeons (SAGES) and the Minimally Invasive Robotic Association (MIRA) as 'a surgical procedure or technology that adds a computer technology-enhanced device to the interaction between a surgeon and a patient during a surgical operation and assumes some degree of control heretofore completely reserved for the surgeon.'

### The da Vinci Surgical System

The da Vinci Surgical System (Intuitive Surgical Inc., Sunnyvale, CA) is the most popular platform for robotic surgery. It consists of three components:

**1. Patient-side cart:**
- Deploys surgical instruments placed within the patient's body
- Houses a binocular 12 mm endoscope with dual zero or thirty degree optics
- Carries interchangeable 5 mm or 8 mm diameter instruments including atraumatic forceps, monopolar cautery, bipolar cautery, dissectors and manipulators

**2. Surgeon's console:**
- Remotely placed from the operating field
- Three-dimensional display recreating surgical anatomy in a virtual environment
- Seat for the surgeon with foot pedals controlling cautery and other instruments
- Hand controls linked to instruments in the operative field

**3. Vision cart:**
- Houses the video processor
- Screens project the procedure for the operating assistant and observers

### Key Technological Features

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| 3D visualization | Binocular stereoscopic endoscope providing depth perception |
| Wristed instruments | 540 degrees of motion at zero or thirty degree angles |
| Tremor elimination | Computer filtering removes physiological tremor |
| Motion scaling | Greater hand movements translated to precise instrument motion |
| Dual consoles | Allow two surgeons to share view and swap instrument control |
| Fibre laser | Couples visualization with precise cutting — reduced thermal damage |

### Principles of TORS Technique

The most common cutting device is the monopolar electrocautery spatula, which doubles as a dissector. Lasers have recently been adapted to work with robotic manipulators, providing more precise cutting with nominal thermal damage and reduced tissue necrosis compared to monopolar electrocautery.

**Oral retractors used in TORS:**
- Feyh-Kastenbauer (FK) retractor — commonly used
- Crowe-Davis, McIvor, or Dingman retractors — provide excellent oropharyngeal exposure
- Tubed laryngoscopes (e.g. Lindholm) significantly limit range of motion and are NOT preferred

## PART B: APPLICATIONS OF ROBOTIC SURGERY IN OTOLARYNGOLOGY

### 1. Transoral Robotic Surgery for Head and Neck Cancer

**TORS for oropharyngeal cancer** is the primary and most established application. The principle of TORS surgery is en bloc resection with a clear margin.

Key evidence:
- Weinstein et al. multicentre prospective series (177 patients): no intraoperative mortality, blood loss averaged 83 mL, positive margin rate 4.3%, gastrostomy tube dependence 5.0%, [[Tracheostomy|tracheostomy]] at last follow-up 2.3%
- White et al. (89 patients): 2-year recurrence-free survival 86.5%, no patients gastrostomy tube dependent at follow-up
- Moore et al. (45 patients): all margins negative, all 14 tracheostomies decannulated, all 30 feeding tubes eventually removed

**TORS for supraglottic cancer:** Demonstrated role in supraglottic resections.

**TORS for hypopharyngeal cancer:** Feasibility demonstrated for early T-classification hypopharyngeal lesions.

**TORS for unknown primary:** Tongue base mucosectomy using TORS has pick-up rates of 50-60% for occult primaries, even in patients showing no avidity on CT-PET imaging.

### 2. Robotic Thyroidectomy

Minimally invasive thyroid surgery approaches include:
- **Transaxillary robotic thyroidectomy:** Kang et al. reported on 200 patients using da Vinci system
- **Robotic facelift thyroidectomy:** Terris et al. demonstrated decreased operative time and outpatient thyroidectomy without drain placement
- **Meta-analysis of 5200 patients:** Equivalent adverse event and complication rate for robotic thyroidectomy compared to conventional approaches

### 3. Robotic [[Neck Dissection|Neck Dissection]]

- Minimally invasive endoscopic-robotic approaches demonstrated in cadaver models
- Retroauricular/facelift approaches show equivalent functional and oncologic outcomes to open approaches
- Robotic approach takes greater operative time (attributed to learning curve)
- TORS for retropharyngeal lymphadenectomy in papillary thyroid carcinoma demonstrated

### 4. Robotic Sleep Apnoea Surgery

Transoral robotic tongue base resection for [[Obstructive Sleep Apnoea|OSA]]:
- First described by Vicini (2010): 10 patients, mean blood loss 25 mL, operative time 45.5 min
- Friedman (2012): 40 patients — TORS achieved 60.5% reduction in AHI (statistically significant superiority over radiofrequency [32%] and coblation [37%])
- Only TORS group achieved statistically significant improvement in minimum oxygen saturation
- Vicini also demonstrated robotic geniohyoidpexy technique with hyoid bone suspension

### 5. Robotic Skull Base Surgery

Multiple approaches have been described:
- **Transoral approach:** For [[Parapharyngeal Space|parapharyngeal space]] and infratemporal fossa tumours — O'Malley et al. enrolled 10 patients with benign parapharyngeal tumours, 9 of 10 successfully completed with TORS
- **Cervical-transoral robotic surgery (C-TORS):** Access to anterior and midline skull base using combined cervical and transoral ports
- **Translabial/Caldwell-Luc approach:** Access to cribriform plate, planum sphenoidale, sella turcica, clivus
- **Transpalatal approach:** Best compromise of visualization and instrumentation for nasopharynx, clivus and anterior skull base
- Key advantage: two-handed endoscopic closure of dural defects

### 6. Robotic [[Free Flaps HN Reconstruction|Free Flap]] Inset

Robotic-assisted mobilization and inset of flaps for oropharyngeal reconstruction after TORS resection of larger tumours, providing velopharyngeal competence and reducing fistula risk.

## PART C: ROBOTIC SURGERY IN OROPHARYNGEAL CANCERS — INDICATIONS AND CONTRAINDICATIONS

### Indications for TORS in Oropharyngeal Cancer

**Patient and tumour factors:**

| Parameter | Indication |
|-----------|------------|
| Tumour stage | T1-T4a (most evidence for T1-T2) |
| Nodal stage | N0-N2b (multiple trials) |
| HPV status | Both HPV+ve and HPV-ve (role in de-escalation for HPV+ve) |
| Site | Tonsil and tongue base (primary sites) |
| Resectability | Transorally resectable with negative margins |
| Margin threshold | >2-5 mm depending on trial (see below) |

**Specific indications:**
- Early stage (T1-T2) oropharyngeal SCC — best candidates
- HPV-positive oropharyngeal cancers being considered for de-intensification of adjuvant therapy
- Unknown primary — tongue base mucosectomy for diagnosis (50-60% pick-up rate)
- Recurrent oropharyngeal cancer after prior treatment
- Supraglottic and selected hypopharyngeal cancers accessible transorally

### Contraindications for TORS

**Absolute contraindications:**
- Inadequate transoral exposure (trismus, limited mouth opening, retrognathia)
- Tumours with carotid artery encasement
- Unresectable disease (T4b — prevertebral fascia involvement, carotid encasement)
- Distant metastatic disease
- Medically unfit for general anaesthesia

**Relative contraindications:**
- T4a tumours requiring mandibulotomy for access
- Bulky nodal disease (N2c, N3) — may still require comprehensive treatment
- Prior radiation to the field (increased complication risk)
- Tumours extending to or across the midline of the tongue base (risk of airway compromise and dysphagia)
- Fixed/tethered tumours with limited mobility

### Ongoing Randomized Trials

Several trials are comparing TORS with chemoradiation for oropharyngeal cancer:

| Trial | Design | Key Feature |
|-------|--------|-------------|
| CompARE (UK) | Phase III RCT | 4 arms including TORS; intermediate and high-risk OPC |
| PATHOS (UK) | Phase II/III | HPV+ve OPC: TORS followed by risk-stratified reduced adjuvant RT |
| ADEPT | Phase III | Post-TORS: RT alone vs RT + cisplatin in p16+ with ECS |
| ORATOR | Phase II RCT | RT vs TORS + neck dissection for early stage OPC |
| ECOG 3311 | Phase II | Post-TORS risk stratification: low-dose vs standard RT for HPV+ |

### TORS vs Transoral Laser Microsurgery (TLM)

| Feature | TORS | TLM |
|---------|------|-----|
| Resection technique | En bloc resection | Follows tumour extent, may transect |
| Margin assessment | Margins on intact specimen | Marginal biopsies needed |
| Learning curve | Less steep (superior optics + ergonomics) | Steeper — line-of-sight limitations |
| Instrumentation | Wristed, 540° motion | Straight instruments |
| Visualization | 3D, magnified | 2D microscopic |

## PART D: ADVANTAGES AND DISADVANTAGES OF ROBOTIC SURGERY

### Advantages

**Over open approaches:**
- Avoids mandibulotomy or lip-split incisions
- Avoids external scars
- Reduced blood loss (mean 25-83 mL in various series)
- Shorter hospital stay
- Earlier resumption of oral diet
- Lower rates of permanent feeding tube dependence (5% in Weinstein series)
- Potential for de-escalation of adjuvant therapy (especially HPV+ve OPC)
- Better functional outcomes (swallowing, speech)

**Technological advantages:**
- Three-dimensional high-definition visualization
- 540 degrees of wristed instrumentation
- Tremor elimination and motion scaling
- Dual consoles facilitate training and mentoring
- Access to difficult-to-reach anatomical sites (tongue base, parapharyngeal space, skull base)

### Disadvantages

- **High cost:** Capital equipment (da Vinci system), maintenance, consumable instruments
- **No haptic (tactile) feedback** — surgeon relies on visual cues only
- **Size of instruments:** Current instruments may be too large for certain H&N applications, particularly skull base
- **Dependence on adequate oral exposure** — trismus or limited mouth opening excludes patients
- **Learning curve** — although less steep than TLM, still requires dedicated training
- **Lack of Level 1 evidence** — no completed randomized trials comparing TORS to chemoradiation (multiple ongoing)
- **Post-operative haemorrhage risk** — 3-8% (particularly from lingual artery territory)
- **Monopoly platform** — single manufacturer limits competitive innovation

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. The da Vinci Surgical System consists of three parts: patient-side cart (instruments + endoscope), surgeon's console (3D display + hand controls), and vision cart (video processing) — provides 540 degrees of wristed instrumentation with tremor elimination
> 2. The first robotic procedure in otolaryngology was performed in 2003 at Walter Reed Army Medical Center (vallecular cyst removal); TORS was defined by Hockstein, Weinstein and O'Malley from 2005 onward
> 3. The principle of TORS surgery is en bloc resection with a clear margin — positive margin rate 4.3% in multicentre series; 2-year recurrence-free survival 86.5%
> 4. TORS is primarily indicated for T1-T2 oropharyngeal SCC (tonsil and tongue base); also used for supraglottic, hypopharyngeal cancers, unknown primary diagnosis and OSA
> 5. Key contraindications for TORS include inadequate transoral exposure (trismus, retrognathia), carotid encasement (T4b), and distant metastases
> 6. Multiple ongoing randomized trials (CompARE, PATHOS, ADEPT, ORATOR, ECOG 3311) are comparing TORS with chemoradiation — de-escalation of adjuvant therapy for HPV+ve OPC is a major focus
> 7. Robotic thyroidectomy (transaxillary, facelift approaches) shows equivalent complication rates to conventional surgery in a meta-analysis of 5200 patients
> 8. TORS for tongue base resection in OSA achieved 60.5% AHI reduction — statistically superior to radiofrequency (32%) and coblation (37%)
> 9. TORS tongue base mucosectomy for unknown primary has pick-up rates of 50-60%, even in CT-PET negative patients
> 10. TORS advantages over open surgery include avoidance of mandibulotomy, reduced blood loss, lower feeding tube dependence; disadvantages include high cost, no haptic feedback, and dependence on adequate oral exposure

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - TORS learning curve is less steep than TLM because the superior 3D optics and wristed instrumentation compensate for the absence of direct line-of-sight — dual consoles facilitate training by allowing a mentor to share control
> - The Feyh-Kastenbauer (FK) retractor provides the best exposure for oropharyngeal TORS, whereas tubed laryngoscopes (e.g. Lindholm) significantly limit range of motion and should not be used
> - Fibre laser technology coupled to robotic instruments provides more precise cutting with nominal thermal damage compared to monopolar electrocautery — particularly useful in the tongue base where preserving neuromuscular integrity is critical for swallowing
> - The absence of haptic feedback in robotic surgery means the surgeon must rely entirely on visual cues to judge tissue resistance and depth of dissection — this is a particular challenge in skull base surgery where critical neurovascular structures are at risk
> - Post-TORS haemorrhage is the most feared complication, typically occurring on day 7-10 from the lingual artery territory — some surgeons advocate prophylactic ligation of the lingual artery during concurrent neck dissection
> - Cervical-transoral robotic surgery (C-TORS) extends the reach of TORS to the anterior and midline skull base by placing instrument ports at the posterior border of the submandibular gland — this is a cadaver-proven technique awaiting wider clinical validation
