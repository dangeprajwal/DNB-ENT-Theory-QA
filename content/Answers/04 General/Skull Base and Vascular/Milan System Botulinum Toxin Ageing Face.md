## INTRODUCTION

Standardized cytological reporting systems for salivary gland fine needle aspiration cytology (FNAC) have been developed to provide a common framework for communication between cytopathologists and surgeons. The Milan System for Reporting Salivary Gland Cytopathology (2018) is the most widely adopted such system, modelled on the Bethesda system for thyroid cytology. Knowledge of facial nerve surgical landmarks during parotidectomy is essential for safe salivary gland surgery. Botulinum toxin has become the most widely used non-surgical facial rejuvenation treatment, with applications ranging from cosmetic wrinkle reduction to management of facial synkinesis following nerve palsy. Understanding its mechanism, clinical applications and limitations is increasingly relevant to ENT practice.

## PART A: MILAN SYSTEM FOR REPORTING SALIVARY GLAND CYTOPATHOLOGY

### Background

The Milan System for Reporting Salivary Gland Cytopathology was published in 2018 to standardize the reporting of salivary gland FNAC, analogous to the Bethesda system for thyroid cytology. It provides a tiered classification with associated risk of malignancy (ROM) and recommended clinical management for each category.

### Classification Categories

| Category | Description | Risk of Malignancy | Management |
|----------|-------------|-------------------|------------|
| I | Non-diagnostic | 25% | Repeat FNAC |
| II | Non-neoplastic | 10% | Clinical and imaging follow-up |
| III | Atypia of undetermined significance (AUS) | 20% | Repeat FNAC or surgery |
| IVA | Benign neoplasm | <5% | Surgery or observation |
| IVB | Salivary gland neoplasm of uncertain malignant potential (SUMP) | 35% | Surgery |
| V | Suspicious for malignancy | 60% | Surgery |
| VI | Malignant | 90% | Surgery (definitive) |

### Key Points of the Milan System

**Category I (Non-diagnostic):** Insufficient cellular material for diagnosis. Includes acellular specimens, cyst fluid only, or predominantly blood. The relatively high ROM (25%) reflects the fact that some malignant tumours yield scant cellularity on aspiration.

**Category II (Non-neoplastic):** Includes chronic sialadenitis, lymphoepithelial cyst, retention cyst, granulomatous inflammation. Conservative management with clinical and imaging follow-up is appropriate.

**Category III (AUS):** Specimens that are not easily classified due to qualitative or quantitative limitations. Repeat FNAC is recommended; if persistent atypia, surgical excision is warranted.

**Category IVA (Benign neoplasm):** Most commonly [[Parotid Gland and Salivary Tumours|pleomorphic adenoma]] or Warthin's tumour. Superficial parotidectomy or extracapsular dissection is appropriate depending on the tumour type.

**Category IVB (SUMP):** Specimens showing features that raise concern for a low-grade malignancy but are insufficient for a definitive malignant diagnosis. Includes basaloid neoplasms and some oncocytic proliferations. Surgical excision is recommended.

**Category V (Suspicious for malignancy):** Features strongly suggestive of malignancy but insufficient for definitive classification. Surgery is recommended.

**Category VI (Malignant):** Definitive cytological diagnosis of malignancy. Includes mucoepidermoid carcinoma, adenoid cystic carcinoma, acinic cell carcinoma, salivary duct carcinoma and other high-grade malignancies.

### Comparison with Pre-Milan Reporting

Prior to the Milan system, the standardized cytological categories used in practice (as described in Scott-Brown's) were:
1. Inadequate
2. Benign
3. Atypical cells present
4. Suspicious for malignancy
5. Overtly malignant

The Milan system provides a more nuanced classification, particularly with the separation of benign neoplasms (IVA) from neoplasms of uncertain malignant potential (IVB), and the addition of associated ROM and management recommendations for each category.

## PART B: SURGICAL LANDMARKS FOR FACIAL NERVE DURING PAROTID SURGERY

### Anatomical Landmarks for Identifying the Facial Nerve

The facial nerve trunk exits the skull base through the stylomastoid foramen and enters the parotid gland, where it divides into its five terminal branches. Several surgical landmarks are used to identify and protect the facial nerve during parotidectomy:

**Primary landmarks (for the main trunk):**
1. **Tragal pointer (cartilaginous pointer):** The facial nerve trunk lies approximately 1 cm deep and inferior to the tragal pointer — the most reliable and commonly used landmark
2. **Tympanomastoid suture:** The nerve exits the stylomastoid foramen at the anterior end of this suture line; identified by following the suture line from the mastoid
3. **Posterior belly of digastric muscle:** The facial nerve lies superior and superficial to the posterior belly of digastric at the level of the stylomastoid foramen
4. **Styloid process:** The nerve exits the stylomastoid foramen anterior to the styloid process

**Secondary landmarks:**
5. **Mastoid process (tip):** The nerve lies anterior to the mastoid tip
6. **Greater auricular nerve:** Points towards the main trunk; can be traced proximally as a guide

### The Superficial Position in Children

The facial nerve is more superficial in small children because the mastoid process is not fully developed — this must be considered during first branchial cleft anomaly surgery and paediatric parotid surgery.

### Intraoperative Facial Nerve Injury — Options

If facial nerve injury occurs intraoperatively:
1. **Immediate primary repair** (end-to-end neurorrhaphy) if clean transection and tension-free approximation is possible
2. **Cable nerve grafting** (great auricular nerve or sural nerve graft) if a segment is missing and tension-free repair is not possible
3. **Cross-face nerve graft** using a redundant buccal branch from the contralateral side — for delayed reconstruction
4. **Free functioning muscle transfer** (gracilis) — for chronic cases where motor end plates have degenerated (>18 months)
5. **Intraoperative nerve monitoring** should be used to minimize injury risk

## PART C: BOTULINUM TOXIN IN TREATMENT OF THE AGEING FACE

### Mechanism of Action

Botulinum toxin type A (BTX-A) is produced by Clostridium botulinum. It is synthesized as a single-chain polypeptide of approximately 150 kDa, cleaved into:
- **Heavy chain (100 kDa):** Responsible for binding to glycoproteins on cholinergic nerve endings
- **Light chain (50 kDa):** The toxifying chain — a zinc-dependent endopeptidase

After injection into a muscle, the heavy chain binds specifically to cholinergic nerve endings. The whole molecule is internalized by pinocytosis. The light chain cleaves SNAP-25 (synaptosome-associated protein), which is essential for exocytosis of acetylcholine. This results in blockade of vesicle fusion and prevention of acetylcholine release, causing temporary muscle paresis.

**Timeline:**
- Onset of paresis: 2-5 days after injection
- Duration of effect: at least 3 months, gradually wearing off
- Low doses produce some effect; increasing doses reach a plateau beyond which further increases add no benefit

### Available Preparations

| Preparation | Manufacturer | Dose Equivalence |
|-------------|-------------|-----------------|
| OnabotulinumtoxinA (Botox/Vistabel) | Allergan | 1 unit |
| AbobotulinumtoxinA (Dysport/Azzalure) | Ipsen/Galderma | 3 units |
| IncobotulinumtoxinA (Xeomin/Bocouture) | Merz | 0.75 units |
| RimabotulinumtoxinB (Myobloc) | — | Different scale |

Units are NOT directly interchangeable between preparations.

### Cosmetic Applications in the Ageing Face

BTX-A targets hyperkinetic wrinkles — dynamic lines caused by the pull of underlying muscles of facial expression:

**Treatment areas:**
- **Glabellar lines (frown lines):** Corrugator supercilii and procerus muscles — the most commonly treated area; FDA-approved indication for Botox Cosmetic
- **Horizontal forehead lines:** Frontalis muscle
- **Periorbital lines (crow's feet):** Orbicularis oculi
- **Nasal lines (bunny lines):** Nasalis
- **Perioral lines:** Orbicularis oris
- **Platysmal bands:** Platysma

### Role in Facial Palsy and Synkinesis

BTX-A is used extensively in the management of post-paralytic facial synkinesis (abnormal involuntary facial movement accompanying voluntary movement of a different muscle group). Applications include:
- Treatment of synkinesis to improve symmetry
- Weakening the non-paralyzed side to improve facial symmetry at rest
- Used in nearly 50% of patients in specialist facial function clinics

**Preparation protocol (Dysport):** 500 units made up to 5 mL of normal saline (10 units per 0.1 mL); injected at 45 degrees using a 1 mL insulin syringe.

### Other ENT Applications of Botulinum Toxin

- **Frey's syndrome:** Subdermal injection to prevent gustatory sweating (repeated at 6-12 monthly intervals)
- **Salivary fistula/sialorrhoea:** Injection into parotid/submandibular glands under ultrasound guidance
- **[[Spasmodic Dysphonia Botulinum Toxin|Spasmodic dysphonia]]:** Injection into thyroarytenoid muscles under EMG guidance
- **Cricopharyngeal spasm:** Injection into cricopharyngeus muscle
- **Crocodile tears syndrome:** Injection into lacrimal gland

### Advantages of BTX-A in Facial Rejuvenation

- Non-surgical, minimally invasive
- Excellent safety profile
- Reversible (effects wear off in 3-4 months)
- Can be combined with fillers, chemical peels and other modalities
- Quick procedure with minimal downtime

### Limitations and Side Effects

- Temporary effect — requires repeated injections
- Variable dose-duration correlation between patients
- Risk of unintended muscle weakness (e.g. brow ptosis, diplopia if periorbital injection is misplaced)
- Bruising at injection site
- Contraindicated in neuromuscular junction disorders (myasthenia gravis)
- Cannot address volume loss, skin laxity or gravitational ptosis — fillers or surgery required

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. The Milan System has 6 categories (I-VI with IVA and IVB): Non-diagnostic (25% ROM), Non-neoplastic (10%), AUS (20%), Benign neoplasm (<5%), SUMP (35%), Suspicious (60%), Malignant (90%)
> 2. Category IVB (SUMP) is a unique Milan category — specimens raising concern for low-grade malignancy but insufficient for definitive diagnosis; surgical excision recommended
> 3. The tragal pointer is the most reliable landmark for the facial nerve trunk during parotidectomy — the nerve lies approximately 1 cm deep and inferior to it
> 4. Other facial nerve landmarks include the tympanomastoid suture, posterior belly of digastric (nerve lies superior/superficial), and styloid process
> 5. BTX-A cleaves SNAP-25 (synaptosome-associated protein) which blocks acetylcholine vesicle fusion — onset in 2-5 days, duration at least 3 months
> 6. Botox, Dysport and Xeomin units are NOT interchangeable — 1 unit Botox ≈ 3 units Dysport ≈ 0.75 units Xeomin
> 7. Glabellar lines (frown lines) are the most commonly treated cosmetic area — corrugator and procerus muscles; other areas include forehead, crow's feet, perioral lines and platysmal bands
> 8. BTX-A is used in nearly 50% of facial palsy clinic patients — for synkinesis treatment and to weaken the non-paralyzed side for improved symmetry
> 9. Salivary gland FNAC has 90-95% overall accuracy — most dependent on site of aspiration, number of needle passes, slide preparation and interpreter experience
> 10. BTX-A is also used in ENT for Frey's syndrome, sialorrhoea, spasmodic dysphonia and cricopharyngeal spasm — these are important non-cosmetic applications


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - The high ROM (25%) for Milan Category I (non-diagnostic) reflects the fact that some malignant tumours yield scant cellularity — repeat FNAC or core biopsy is essential rather than assuming a benign diagnosis
> - The facial nerve is more superficial in small children due to incomplete mastoid pneumatization — this is critically important during paediatric parotid surgery and first branchial cleft anomaly surgery
> - BTX-A effects are dose-dependent up to a plateau — increasing the dose beyond this plateau does not produce stronger effects; each patient has a consistent but individual response
> - For facial synkinesis management, BTX-A is injected into the affected muscles using a Dysport protocol of 500 units in 5 mL saline — the dose is titrated based on clinical response
> - Non-surgical facial rejuvenation requires a combination approach: BTX-A for dynamic lines, fillers for volume loss, topical retinoids for skin texture, and chemical peels for surface renewal — reliance on a single modality gives suboptimal results
> - The greater auricular nerve can be used as a secondary landmark during parotidectomy — if inadvertently transected, the severed end can form a painful stump neuroma presenting months post-operatively
