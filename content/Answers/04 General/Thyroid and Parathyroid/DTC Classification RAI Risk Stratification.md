## INTRODUCTION

Thyroid neoplasms constitute a diverse group of tumours arising from follicular epithelial cells, parafollicular C-cells, or non-epithelial elements. Differentiated thyroid cancer (DTC) — comprising papillary and follicular carcinoma — accounts for over 90% of thyroid malignancies and carries an excellent prognosis with 10-year survival exceeding 98% for Stage I–III disease. Management follows a personalized, risk-stratified approach combining surgery, radioactive iodine (RAI) ablation, and TSH suppression. Dynamic risk stratification after initial treatment guides the intensity of follow-up and the need for further intervention.

## PART A: CLASSIFICATION OF THYROID NEOPLASMS

### WHO Classification (4th Edition, 2017)

The World Health Organization classification of thyroid tumours is comprehensive and includes benign, borderline, and malignant entities:

**Benign Tumours:**
- Follicular adenoma (normofollicular, microfollicular/foetal, macrofollicular/colloid, solid, trabecular, organoid subtypes)
- Hyalinizing trabecular tumour

**Borderline/Uncertain Malignant Potential:**
- Follicular tumour of uncertain malignant potential (FT-UMP)
- Well-differentiated tumour of uncertain malignant potential (WDT-UMP)
- Non-invasive follicular thyroid neoplasm with papillary-like nuclear features (NIFTP)

**Malignant Epithelial Tumours — Follicular Cell Origin:**
- Papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) — 80-85% of thyroid cancers
  - Variants: classical, follicular variant, encapsulated, microcarcinoma, columnar cell, oncocytic
- Follicular thyroid carcinoma (FTC)
  - Subtypes: minimally invasive, encapsulated angioinvasive, widely invasive
- Hürthle (oncocytic) cell carcinoma
- Poorly differentiated thyroid carcinoma (insular carcinoma)
- Anaplastic (undifferentiated) thyroid carcinoma

**Malignant Epithelial Tumours — C-Cell Origin:**
- Medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC)
- Mixed medullary and follicular thyroid carcinoma

**Other Malignant Tumours:**
- Squamous cell carcinoma
- Mucoepidermoid carcinoma
- Sclerosing mucoepidermoid carcinoma with eosinophilia
- Mucinous carcinoma
- Intrathyroid thymic carcinoma
- Spindle epithelial tumour with thymus-like differentiation (SETTLE)

**Non-Epithelial Tumours:**
- [[Glomus Tumour|Paraganglioma]]
- Mesenchymal/stromal tumours
- Primary thyroid lymphoma (MALT lymphoma, DLBCL)

### Simplified Clinical Classification

| Category | Types | Frequency |
|----------|-------|-----------|
| Differentiated | PTC, FTC, Hürthle cell | >90% |
| Medullary | MTC (sporadic, familial) | 5-8% |
| Poorly differentiated | Insular carcinoma | 1-2% |
| Undifferentiated | Anaplastic carcinoma | <2% |
| Other | Lymphoma, SCC, sarcoma | <2% |

## PART B: TNM STAGING OF DTC (AJCC 8th Edition)

The TNM classification guides treatment decisions and prognostication:

| T Stage | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| T1a | ≤1 cm, limited to thyroid |
| T1b | >1 cm to ≤2 cm, limited to thyroid |
| T2 | >2 cm to ≤4 cm, limited to thyroid |
| T3a | >4 cm, limited to thyroid |
| T3b | Gross extrathyroidal extension into strap muscles only |
| T4a | Extension into subcutaneous tissues, larynx, trachea, oesophagus, RLN |
| T4b | Invades prevertebral fascia, encases carotid or mediastinal vessels |

**Nodal staging:** N0 = no nodal metastasis; N1a = Level VI/VII nodes; N1b = lateral cervical or retropharyngeal nodes (Levels I-V).

**Distant metastasis:** M0 = none; M1 = distant metastases.

**Age-based staging (critical concept):**
- Patients **<55 years**: Stage I (any T, any N, M0) or Stage II (any T, any N, M1) — only two stages
- Patients **≥55 years**: Stage I through IVB based on T, N, M substages

## PART C: RADIOACTIVE IODINE (RAI) THERAPY IN THYROID CANCER

### Principle

RAI (Iodine-131) exploits the unique ability of differentiated thyroid follicular cells to trap and organify iodine via the sodium-iodide symporter (NIS). After total thyroidectomy, residual thyroid tissue and iodine-avid metastases selectively concentrate RAI, leading to destruction by beta-particle emission.

### Indications for RAI Ablation

RAI ablation has undergone the most radical change in practice in recent years, moving from universal application to personalized evaluation at multidisciplinary team discussion.

**RAI indicated (high-risk features):**
- Tumour >4 cm in size
- Gross extrathyroidal spread
- Distant metastases
- These patients receive ablative dose followed by treatment dose

**RAI NOT indicated (low-risk features):**
- Unifocal or multifocal tumours <1 cm (papillary or follicular variant)
- Minimally invasive follicular cancer without angioinvasion
- No extension outside thyroid capsule

**Intermediate group (1-4 cm) — individualized decision:**
Poor prognostic features favouring RAI include:
- Aggressive histology (tall cell, poorly differentiated, diffuse sclerosing PTC)
- Widely invasive follicular thyroid cancer
- Extracapsular invasion
- Multiple involved lymph nodes or high ratio of involved to non-involved nodes
- Large size of individual involved nodes

### RAI Dosing

Two large randomized controlled trials (Mallick et al. and Schlumberger et al., both NEJM 2012) demonstrated that a **lower dose of 1.1 GBq is as effective as a higher dose of 3.7 GBq**, with fewer side effects. High treatment doses are recommended for gross residual disease or distant metastases, with repeat treatments every 6-12 months for disease not amenable to surgery.

### Preparation for RAI

A total thyroidectomy is required before RAI ablation (completion thyroidectomy if initial surgery was hemithyroidectomy). TSH stimulation is necessary for effective RAI uptake:

- **Recombinant human TSH (rhTSH):** Two injections 5 days before treatment — preferred, fewer side effects
- **Levothyroxine withdrawal:** More prolonged, greater side effects, but alternative option
- **Low-iodine diet** for 1-2 weeks prior
- Avoid iodinated CT contrast within 4 weeks of RAI (clearance period)

### Post-RAI Scan

An iodine scan is undertaken 2-10 days after treatment, assessing radioiodine uptake in the thyroid bed and detecting uptake in areas outside the neck.

### Side Effects of RAI

- Xerostomia (may increase with time due to salivary gland damage)
- Submandibular gland swelling
- Fatigue
- Amenorrhoea/oligomenorrhoea (4-10 months duration)
- Pregnancy deferral: 6 months (BTA) or 6-12 months (ATA)
- Breastfeeding: discontinue at least 8 weeks prior to RAI
- Males: defer fathering a child for 4 months; sperm banking offered (infertility possible)

### External Beam [[Radiotherapy Principles Fractionation Advances|Radiotherapy]] (EBRT)

EBRT is considered for patients with gross local tumour invasion at surgery or residual/recurrent tumour that is non-radioiodine avid. Intensity-modulated radiotherapy (IMRT) is the method of choice, reducing damage to surrounding tissues and reducing toxicity.

## PART D: POST-THYROIDECTOMY MANAGEMENT OF WELL-DIFFERENTIATED THYROID MALIGNANCY

### Immediate Post-Operative Management

After total thyroidectomy, TSH suppression should be commenced with either:
- **Levothyroxine T4** (2 mcg/kg) — standard approach
- **Liothyronine T3** (20 mcg t.d.s.) — if RAI planned in near future (shorter half-life allows quicker withdrawal)

The patient's histopathology is reviewed, and a multidisciplinary team discussion determines the need for adjuvant treatment including RAI ablation and degree of TSH suppression.

### Treatment of Papillary Thyroid Microcarcinoma (PTMC)

PTMC (≤1 cm maximum diameter) has an excellent prognosis with almost no mortality:

- **Incidentally found on histology** (post-operatively): Considered cured — discharge to GP with annual thyroid function test monitoring (hemithyroidectomy carries medium to long-term risk of hypothyroidism)
- **Identified pre-operatively** (radiologically or symptomatic): If no poor prognostic features — follow up for 5 years then discharge to lower-intensity setting
- **PTMC with aggressive features** (aggressive histology, extracapsular spread, lymph node metastasis): Manage as non-low-risk papillary thyroid cancer

### [[Neck Dissection|Neck Dissection]] Principles

- **Prophylactic lateral neck dissection:** No evidence of benefit — NOT recommended
- **Prophylactic central neck dissection:** Evidence unclear and conflicting; may benefit tumours >4 cm or with extrathyroidal extension; NOT recommended for low-risk disease or tumours <4 cm with classical PTC and no extrathyroidal extension
- **Therapeutic neck dissection:** Mandatory for clinically evident nodal disease

### RLN Management

If tumour is found encasing the recurrent laryngeal nerve during operation, the clinician should aim to preserve the nerve if it was functioning pre-operatively. A small amount of disease may be left on the nerve — there is no survival benefit from nerve sacrifice, but significant quality of life detriment from [[Vocal Fold Paralysis|vocal cord palsy]]. However, residual disease on trachea or oesophagus significantly decreases survival — partial tracheal or oesophageal resection is warranted.

## PART E: DYNAMIC RISK STRATIFICATION

Dynamic risk stratification is the cornerstone of personalized post-treatment follow-up for DTC. It is performed **9-12 months** after initial treatment (total thyroidectomy ± RAI ablation).

### Assessment

The main evaluation uses:
1. **Stimulated thyroglobulin** (via rhTSH injection or levothyroxine withdrawal)
2. **High-definition ultrasound** of the neck
3. Cross-sectional/nuclear medicine imaging if indicated

These together are more accurate than undertaking a whole-body radioiodine scan.

**Key thyroglobulin thresholds:**
- Stimulated Tg **<0.5 mcg/L** → predicts disease-free status with **98-99% accuracy**
- Stimulated Tg **>2 mcg/L** → highly predictive of **persistent disease**
- Unstimulated Tg **<0.1 mcg/L** with negative USS → very high negative predictive value in low-risk patients

**Important caveat:** Stimulated thyroglobulin can only be used in the absence of thyroglobulin antibodies and is most sensitive after both total thyroidectomy and RAI ablation. The appearance of thyroglobulin antibodies when previously absent, or their gradual rise, may indicate recurrent disease.

### Dynamic Risk Stratification Categories

| Response | Criteria | Follow-up |
|----------|----------|-----------|
| Excellent | Stimulated Tg <1; negative USS; negative imaging | 6-monthly year 1, then annual |
| Indeterminate | Stimulated Tg 1-10; non-specific USS changes; stable sub-1 cm nodes | More frequent — individualized |
| Incomplete | Stimulated Tg ≥10 or rising; persistent/new disease on imaging | Investigate; intensive follow-up |

### TSH Suppression by Response Category

| Response | TSH Target (mU/L) | Duration |
|----------|-------------------|----------|
| Excellent | 0.3-2.0 | Review at 5 years |
| Indeterminate | 0.1-0.5 | For 5-10 years |
| Incomplete | <0.1 | Indefinitely |

### Management by Response Category

**Excellent response:** Annual thyroglobulin assessments; TSH maintained in low-normal range (0.3-2.0 mU/L). After 5 years disease-free, consider follow-up in less intensive setting (e.g., nurse-led clinic).

**Incomplete response:** If USS shows recurrence → consider surgery. If USS normal → CT/MRI. If cross-sectional imaging negative → [[PET CT Nuclear Medicine Scans|PET-CT]] scan. If PET-CT also negative → I-131 scan. If surgically resectable disease identified → reoperation. If no surgically resectable disease → further RAI therapy. TSH suppression to <0.1 mU/L indefinitely.

**Indeterminate response:** Close review with serial thyroglobulin and ultrasound to detect recurrence early. TSH maintained 0.1-0.5 mU/L for at least 5-10 years.

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. WHO classifies thyroid tumours into benign (follicular adenoma), borderline (FT-UMP, NIFTP), and malignant categories including DTC, MTC, poorly differentiated, and anaplastic carcinoma
> 2. PTC accounts for 80-85% of thyroid cancers; FTC for approximately 10%; DTC overall has >98% 10-year survival for Stage I-III disease
> 3. Age-based staging: patients <55 years have only Stage I (M0) or Stage II (M1) — even with extensive nodal disease
> 4. RAI ablation is indicated for tumours >4 cm, gross extrathyroidal spread, or distant metastases; NOT indicated for unifocal/multifocal tumours <1 cm
> 5. Two large RCTs (NEJM 2012) demonstrated low-dose RAI (1.1 GBq) is as effective as high-dose (3.7 GBq) with fewer side effects
> 6. RAI preparation requires TSH stimulation via rhTSH injection or levothyroxine withdrawal; iodinated contrast clearance period is 4 weeks
> 7. PTMC found incidentally on histology = cured; PTMC identified pre-operatively without aggressive features = 5-year follow-up then discharge
> 8. Dynamic risk stratification at 9-12 months: stimulated Tg <0.5 predicts disease-free status with 98-99% accuracy; Tg >2 predicts persistent disease
> 9. TSH suppression varies by response: excellent (0.3-2.0 mU/L), indeterminate (0.1-0.5 mU/L), incomplete (<0.1 mU/L indefinitely)
> 10. Prophylactic lateral neck dissection is NOT recommended in DTC; prophylactic central neck dissection is controversial and reserved for high-risk cases (>4 cm, extrathyroidal extension)


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - Thyroglobulin is only reliable as a tumour marker after total thyroidectomy and RAI ablation, and in the absence of thyroglobulin antibodies — rising antibody titres may themselves indicate recurrence
> - A functioning RLN encased by tumour should be preserved (shave tumour off) — nerve sacrifice does not improve survival but causes significant morbidity; however, tumour on trachea/oesophagus requires resection as it decreases survival
> - Completion thyroidectomy is required before RAI if initial surgery was hemithyroidectomy
> - Pregnancy should be deferred 6-12 months after RAI; breastfeeding stopped 8 weeks before RAI; male patients should consider sperm banking
> - NIFTP (non-invasive follicular thyroid neoplasm with papillary-like nuclear features) was reclassified from carcinoma to borderline category in WHO 2017 — reducing unnecessary aggressive treatment
> - Stimulated thyroglobulin combined with high-definition neck USS is superior to whole-body radioiodine scan for post-treatment assessment
