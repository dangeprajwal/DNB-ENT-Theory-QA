## INTRODUCTION

Minimally invasive and robotic thyroid surgery represents an evolving field that aims to reduce or eliminate visible neck scarring while maintaining safety comparable to conventional thyroidectomy. The Thyroid Imaging Reporting and Data System (TI-RADS) provides a standardized ultrasound classification for thyroid nodule risk stratification. Molecular diagnostic tests are increasingly used to refine the evaluation of indeterminate thyroid nodules and guide management of differentiated thyroid cancers (DTC). These topics reflect the trend towards personalized, evidence-based thyroid cancer management.

## PART A: ENDOSCOPIC AND MINIMALLY INVASIVE APPROACHES TO THYROID SURGERY

### Historical Context

The principles of safe thyroid surgery were established by Kocher (Nobel laureate, 1909). The desire to minimize neck scarring has driven innovation — studies reveal that **71% of patients** in a thyroid clinic would prefer not to have a neck scar, a preference independent of sex and skin colour. Furthermore, professionals rate scar aesthetics more favourably than patients.

### Classification of Minimally Invasive Techniques

| Approach | Technique | Incision |
|----------|-----------|----------|
| MIVAT (Miccoli) | Gas-less, endoscopic | 2-3 cm midline neck |
| Henry technique | CO2 insufflation | 3 lateral ports along SCM |
| ABBA | Axillo-bilateral-breast | Remote (no neck incision) |
| BABA | Bilateral axillo-breast | Remote (no neck incision) |
| RAT (da Vinci) | Robotic transaxillary | 5-6 cm axillary incision |

### 1. MIVAT (Miccoli Technique)

The **minimally invasive video-assisted thyroidectomy (MIVAT)** was pioneered by Paolo Miccoli in Pisa. It is an open (gas-less) method:

**Technique:**
- A 2-3 cm midline incision placed in the standard thyroidectomy position
- A 30-degree, 4 mm endoscope provides magnified visualization
- Endoscopic dissection of the superior pole, which is delivered into the neck
- Dissection of the lower pole and completion thyroidectomy is done conventionally once the thyroid is delivered through the incision
- Total thyroidectomy is possible through a single incision
- Requires two competent assistants and video stacks

**Advantages:**
- Avoids potential complications of insufflation
- Excellent visualization of RLN, external laryngeal nerve, and parathyroid glands
- Smaller incision with better cosmetic outcome

**Limitations:**
- Suitable for nodules up to **3 cm** (total lobe volume should not exceed 20 mL)
- Equates to approximately **8% of a UK surgeon's practice**
- Steep learning curve: **>50 cases** required for expert status
- Excessive traction through small incision can cause hyperpigmentation and hypertrophic scarring
- Should be avoided in patients with thyroiditis, cancer, or previous surgery

### 2. Henry Technique

Pioneered by Jean-François Henry in Marseille, this uses the **insufflation method**:

**Technique:**
- Three lateral ports along the anterior border of sternomastoid: two 3 mm ports for instrumentation and one 1 cm port for the insufflator endoscope
- CO2 insufflation at **8 mmHg** creates working space and achieves a bloodless field
- Dissection takes place completely within the neck
- Thyroid lobe delivered through the endoscope port

**Limitations:**
- Total thyroidectomy is **NOT possible** by this method
- Technically much more challenging than Miccoli technique
- Risk of hypercarbia from CO2 insufflation
- Not widely adopted for thyroid surgery (better suited for parathyroidectomy)

### 3. Scarless in the Neck Techniques (ABBA/BABA)

These techniques were pioneered in the Far East (Japanese and Korean surgeons), driven by cultural desire to avoid neck scars:

**ABBA (Axillo-Bilateral-Breast Approach):**
- Incisions placed in the axillae and breast regions — completely avoiding the neck
- Lobectomy and total thyroidectomy achievable
- Steep learning curve; requires competent assistant working with 2D view

**BABA (Bilateral Axillo-Breast Approach):**
- Similar remote access approach via bilateral axillary and breast incisions
- Both gas-less and insufflation techniques have been used
- Risk of hypercarbia with insufflation techniques

### 4. Robotic-Assisted Thyroidectomy (RAT) — da Vinci System

RAT was pioneered by **Chung and colleagues** at Yonsei University, Seoul. By 2013, Korean surgeons had experience in **>3000 patients**.

**Da Vinci System Components:**
- Surgeon console (remote control)
- Patient-side cart with four robotic arms
- Video stack with camera control units (CCDs)
- Master-slave device — surgeon controls robotic arms from console

**Technical Advantages of da Vinci:**
- **3D visualization** (dual endoscope system) — superior depth of field
- **EndoWrist technology:** 270-degree rotation in both directions (7 degrees of freedom)
- **Tremor filtration:** Removes natural human tremor
- **Motion scaling:** Adjustable instrument movement range
- Equal dexterity with dominant and non-dominant hands

**Operative Technique:**
- **5-6 cm axillary incision** marked while patient is awake (arm in "extended salute" position)
- Dissection extends in subfascial and subcutaneous planes above pectoralis major, over clavicle
- Entry through dehiscence between sternal and clavicular heads of SCM
- Dissection under strap muscles to access thyroid
- Robot docked at right angles to operating table
- 30-degree down stereoscopic endoscope; instruments include Maryland, Debakey forceps and Harmonic shears (seal vessels up to 3 mm)
- RLN and parathyroids identified first before switching to energy device

**Indications:**
- Hemithyroidectomy for nodules up to **6 cm**
- Total thyroidectomy if contralateral lobe is near normal
- Patients with hypertrophic scarring tendency
- Patients expressing strong preference to avoid neck scar

**Contraindications:**
- Thyroid cancer (except microcarcinoma in young patients)
- Graves' disease
- BMI >30
- Degenerative shoulder pathology
- ASA category >2

**Complications:**
- Same as conventional thyroidectomy: [[Vocal Fold Paralysis|vocal cord palsy]], hypoparathyroidism, bleeding, infection
- **Additional:** Brachial plexus dysfunction (unique to RAT)
- Airway obstruction from bleeding is LESS likely than CT (capacious potential space dissipates haematoma)

**Ethical and Economic Considerations:**
- Significantly increased operative time compared to conventional
- Inpatient stay and time off work are identical to CT
- Economic justification difficult — lack of competition in robotic market
- Different healthcare system in Korea (5× reimbursement for robotic vs. conventional) has driven adoption
- UK adoption slow — niche indications; training, proctorship and credentialing are essential

## PART B: TI-RADS CLASSIFICATION AND SCORING

### Background

The Thyroid Imaging Reporting and Data System (TI-RADS) is a standardized ultrasound-based classification that categorizes thyroid nodules by their risk of malignancy. It guides the decision of whether FNAC is required and at what nodule size threshold.

### ACR TI-RADS Scoring System

Points are awarded based on five ultrasound feature categories:

**1. Composition:**
- Cystic or almost cystic: 0 points
- Spongiform: 0 points
- Mixed cystic and solid: 1 point
- Solid or almost solid: 2 points

**2. Echogenicity:**
- Anechoic: 0 points
- Hyperechoic or isoechoic: 1 point
- Hypoechoic: 2 points
- Very hypoechoic: 3 points

**3. Shape:**
- Wider than tall: 0 points
- Taller than wide: 3 points

**4. Margin:**
- Smooth: 0 points
- Ill-defined: 0 points
- Lobulated or irregular: 2 points
- Extra-thyroidal extension: 3 points

**5. Echogenic Foci:**
- None or large comet-tail artefact: 0 points
- Macrocalcification: 1 point
- Peripheral rim calcification: 2 points
- Punctate echogenic foci (microcalcification): 3 points

### TI-RADS Categories

| TI-RADS | Points | Risk | FNAC Recommendation |
|---------|--------|------|---------------------|
| TR1 — Benign | 0 | <2% | No FNAC needed |
| TR2 — Not suspicious | 2 | <2% | No FNAC needed |
| TR3 — Mildly suspicious | 3 | 5% | FNAC if ≥2.5 cm; follow if ≥1.5 cm |
| TR4 — Moderately suspicious | 4-6 | 5-20% | FNAC if ≥1.5 cm; follow if ≥1.0 cm |
| TR5 — Highly suspicious | ≥7 | >20% | FNAC if ≥1.0 cm; follow if ≥0.5 cm |

### Highly Suspicious US Features (Favour Malignancy)

The four cardinal ultrasound features suspicious for malignancy:
1. Solid hypoechoic nodule
2. Ill-defined/irregular margins
3. Microcalcifications (punctate echogenic foci)
4. Taller-than-wide shape

## PART C: APPROACH TO SUSPECTED RECURRENT THYROID CANCER

### Detection

Recurrent DTC is detected through the dynamic risk stratification follow-up protocol:
- Rising or elevated serum **thyroglobulin** (stimulated Tg >2 mcg/L → highly predictive of persistent/recurrent disease)
- Rising **thyroglobulin antibodies** (appearance when previously absent or gradual rise)
- Structural disease on **neck ultrasound** (suspicious lymph nodes or thyroid bed mass)
- Abnormal cross-sectional imaging (CT, MRI, [[PET CT Nuclear Medicine Scans|PET-CT]])

### Management of Recurrence

- **Surgically resectable disease:** Revision surgery (completion thyroidectomy, selective [[Neck Dissection|neck dissection]], or excision of recurrent nodule)
- **Non-resectable, iodine-avid disease:** Further RAI therapy
- **Non-resectable, non-iodine-avid disease:** Consider external beam [[Radiotherapy Principles Fractionation Advances|radiotherapy]], tyrosine kinase inhibitors (sorafenib, lenvatinib for DTC), or clinical trials
- **TSH suppression:** Maintain at <0.1 mU/L indefinitely for patients with incomplete response
- **Follow-up:** Serial thyroglobulin monitoring, periodic neck USS, cross-sectional imaging as needed

## PART D: MOLECULAR DIAGNOSTICS IN DIFFERENTIATED THYROID CANCER

### Overview

Understanding of the molecular pathogenesis of thyroid cancer has progressed rapidly. Genetic aberrations and alterations in signalling pathways have reshaped insights into thyroid cancer aetiology and management. Molecular testing has a dual role: (1) refining the diagnosis of indeterminate thyroid nodules on FNAC, and (2) prognostication and treatment planning in established DTC.

### Key Oncogenes in Thyroid Cancer

**1. BRAF (V600E) Mutation:**
- Serine/threonine kinase in the MAPK pathway
- Found in **29-69% of papillary thyroid cancers**
- **100% specific for PTC** — not found in benign thyroid lesions (unlike RAS or RET/PTC)
- Predicts poor outcomes: extrathyroidal invasion, advanced stage, metastasis, relapse
- Associated with **loss of radioiodine avidity** and treatment failures
- Proposed as a specific molecular marker for diagnosis of PTC in indeterminate FNAC specimens

**2. RAS Mutations:**
- Most widely mutated proto-oncogene in human cancers (30% overall)
- Three families: K-RAS, H-RAS, N-RAS
- Found in 20% of papillary and 53% of follicular carcinomas
- Represents an early event in thyroid tumourigenesis — present in some benign lesions too
- Activates both MAPK and PI3K-AKT pathways

**3. RET-PTC Rearrangements:**
- Gene translocations found in **5-40% of adult PTC**, more common in paediatric PTC and radiation-associated cancers
- >10 types of translocation — most common are RET/PTC1 and RET/PTC3
- Activates both MAPK and PI3K-AKT pathways

**4. PAX8/PPARG Rearrangement:**
- Translocation between chromosomes 2 and 3
- Found in up to **60% of follicular thyroid carcinoma** and follicular variant of PTC
- Inhibits tumour suppressor activity of PPARG

**5. RET Germline Mutations (MTC):**
- Chromosome 10q11.2
- Cause hereditary MTC (MEN 2A, MEN 2B, FMTC)
- Genotype-phenotype correlation guides prophylactic thyroidectomy timing

### Tumour Suppressor Genes

**p53 mutations:**
- Found in only 10% of thyroid malignancies
- Predominantly in poorly differentiated and anaplastic histotypes
- Suggests p53 plays a minor role in early thyroid carcinogenesis (unlike many other cancers)
- May contribute to de-differentiation from DTC to anaplastic carcinoma

### Key Signalling Pathways

**MAPK Pathway (RAS → RAF → MEK → ERK):**
- Central pathway in papillary thyroid carcinogenesis
- Activated by BRAF, RAS mutations, and RET/PTC rearrangements
- Regulates cell proliferation and survival

**PI3K-AKT Pathway:**
- More frequently activated in follicular thyroid cancer
- Activated by RAS mutations and PTEN inactivation
- Regulates cell growth, proliferation, and apoptosis

### Clinical Applications of Molecular Testing

| Application | Molecular Test | Clinical Utility |
|-------------|---------------|------------------|
| Indeterminate FNAC (Bethesda III/IV) | BRAF, RAS, RET/PTC panel | Refines malignancy risk; may avoid diagnostic surgery |
| Confirmed PTC | BRAF V600E status | Predicts aggressiveness, RAI resistance, guides treatment intensity |
| FTC suspected | PAX8/PPARG, RAS | Supports diagnosis; cannot distinguish adenoma from carcinoma |
| MTC family screening | RET germline mutation | Determines need for prophylactic thyroidectomy |
| Anaplastic carcinoma | BRAF V600E, p53 | Guides targeted therapy (dabrafenib + trametinib) |

### Future Research Directions

As identified by the British and American Thyroid Associations:
- Molecular profiling of tumours as an aid to risk stratification
- Methods of identifying high-risk radioiodine-resistant tumours early to escalate treatment and reduce recurrence
- The role of the immune response in thyroid cancer and how this may influence lateral neck dissection

> [!abstract] Key Points for Examination
> 1. MIVAT (Miccoli): gas-less, 2-3 cm midline incision, suitable for nodules ≤3 cm, learning curve >50 cases; Henry technique: CO2 insufflation at 8 mmHg, 3 lateral ports, cannot perform total thyroidectomy
> 2. Robotic-assisted thyroidectomy (RAT): da Vinci transaxillary approach pioneered by Chung in Korea, 5-6 cm axillary incision, suitable for hemithyroidectomy up to 6 cm nodules; >3000 patients in Korea by 2013
> 3. RAT advantages: 3D visualization, EndoWrist technology (7 degrees of freedom), tremor filtration, motion scaling; unique complication is brachial plexus dysfunction
> 4. RAT contraindications: thyroid cancer (except microcarcinoma), Graves' disease, BMI >30, shoulder pathology
> 5. TI-RADS scoring is based on 5 ultrasound categories (composition, echogenicity, shape, margin, echogenic foci) — TR5 (≥7 points) has >20% malignancy risk and requires FNAC if ≥1 cm
> 6. Recurrent thyroid cancer: rising thyroglobulin or appearance of thyroglobulin antibodies should prompt USS ± FNAC; systematic evaluation through USS → CT/MRI → PET-CT → I-131 scan
> 7. BRAF V600E mutation is 100% specific for PTC, found in 29-69% of cases, predicts poor outcomes including loss of radioiodine avidity — most important molecular marker in thyroid cancer
> 8. RAS mutations (20% PTC, 53% FTC) represent early tumourigenesis events and are found in benign lesions too — less specific than BRAF
> 9. PAX8/PPARG rearrangement found in up to 60% of FTC — useful molecular marker for follicular thyroid neoplasms
> 10. p53 mutations are rare in early thyroid cancer (10%) but common in anaplastic carcinoma — suggests role in de-differentiation


---

> [!tip] Clinical Pearls
> - The ideal RAT candidate is a thin, narrow-chested female with a nodule ≤4 cm — body habitus is critical for successful transaxillary approach
> - BRAF V600E mutation testing in indeterminate FNAC (Bethesda III/IV) can significantly change management — if positive, it confirms PTC with 100% specificity and may obviate diagnostic hemithyroidectomy
> - Airway obstruction following a post-operative bleed is actually LESS likely with RAT than conventional thyroidectomy because the capacious dissection space allows haematoma dissipation
> - Thyroglobulin washout from FNAC needle of a suspicious neck node increases sensitivity for detecting recurrent PTC — always request this when sampling neck masses in thyroid cancer follow-up
> - The cultural and healthcare economic factors driving RAT adoption in Korea (5× reimbursement, feng shui beliefs about neck scars) do not translate to Western populations — niche indications in the UK
> - Molecular testing is moving from a diagnostic adjunct to a prognostic tool — BRAF-positive PTC with loss of radioiodine avidity may benefit from early escalation to targeted therapies rather than repeated RAI
